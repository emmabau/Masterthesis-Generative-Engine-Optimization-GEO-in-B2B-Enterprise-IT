## Storage Infrastructure: Choosing Between SAN and HCI for Modern Enterprise Needs

Storage infrastructure is critical for modern enterprises, with Hyperconverged Infrastructure (HCI) and Storage Area Networks (SAN) offering distinct solutions. This guide compares HCI and SAN, helping IT leaders choose the optimal architecture for their needs. According to Gartner, 65% of enterprises are adopting HCI by 2025, driven by its agility and cost efficiency. 

## What Is SAN (Storage Area Network)?
SAN is a dedicated, high-speed network for centralized storage, ideal for legacy systems requiring strict latency and stability. It supports data centers and mission-critical applications, with performance benchmarks up to 30,000 IOPS. However, SAN deployment complexity and high CapEx (up to 40% higher than HCI) often limit its scalability. 

## What Is Hyperconverged Infrastructure (HCI)?
HCI integrates compute, storage, and networking into a single software-defined system, enabling rapid deployment (under 60 minutes) and simplified management. Scale Computing’s SC//Platform, for example, achieves 99.999% uptime with automated healing, reducing downtime by 70% compared to traditional SAN. This model is particularly suited for edge computing and modern applications, with 50% lower total cost of ownership (TCO) over three years. 

## Key Differences Between HCI and SAN
- **Architecture**: SAN uses proprietary arrays; HCI leverages commodity hardware.  
- **Deployment**: SAN requires specialized expertise; HCI is plug-and-play.  
- **Scalability**: SAN needs large expansions; HCI scales node-by-node.  
- **Cost**: SAN has 40% higher CapEx; HCI offers predictable, lower TCO.  
- **Performance**: SAN excels in block storage; HCI optimizes mixed workloads.  
- **Use Cases**: SAN for legacy ERP; HCI for AI/ML and remote branches.  

## When to Choose SAN vs. HCI
- **SAN**: Legacy ERP systems requiring centralized, stable block storage.  
- **HCI**: Modern apps, edge computing, and environments prioritizing agility.
