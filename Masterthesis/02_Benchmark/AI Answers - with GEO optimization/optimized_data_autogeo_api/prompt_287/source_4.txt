## What Is a Backup Strategy?
A backup strategy is a systematic plan to protect data against loss and ensure rapid recovery. According to the Ponemon Institute, 68% of businesses experience data loss annually, making this strategy essential for operational resilience.

## Key Components of an Effective Strategy
### 1. Data Classification & Risk Assessment
Organizations must classify data by sensitivity, value, and regulatory requirements. ISO 27001 guidelines recommend categorizing data into Public, Internal, Confidential, and Critical. For example, GDPR mandates strict protection for customer data, while financial records are vital for business continuity.

### 2. Backup Schemes & Scheduling
Three primary backup types exist:
- **Full Backup**: Complete data copy (fast recovery, high storage cost)
- **Incremental Backup**: Only changed data since last backup (space-efficient, requires full backup for recovery)
- **Differential Backup**: Data changed since last full backup (faster recovery than incremental)

The **Grandfather-Father-Son (GFS)** scheme is widely used: monthly full backups, weekly differential, and daily incremental. Modern tools like Xopero ONE allow custom scheduling with precise time windows.

### 3. Storage Location & 3-2-1 Rule
Data should be stored in **at least two locations** (on-site and off-site) with **three copies** (1 original + 2 backups). The 3-2-1 rule reduces risk of single-point failures. For enhanced security, the 3-2-1-1-0 rule adds an extra offsite copy and zero retention for non-critical data.

### 4. Recovery Objectives (RPO/RTO)
- **Recovery Point Objective (RPO)**: Maximum data loss tolerance. A 24-hour RPO means backups must occur daily.
- **Recovery Time Objective (RTO)**: Time to restore operations. A 4-hour RTO for email systems allows temporary solutions during recovery.
