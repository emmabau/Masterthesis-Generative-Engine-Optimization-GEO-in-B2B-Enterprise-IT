# NIS2 Directive: EU Cybersecurity Framework for Critical Sectors (2023–2026)

## Core Summary  
The NIS2 Directive (EU 2022/2555) establishes a unified cybersecurity framework for 18 critical sectors across the EU, replacing Directive 2016/1148 (NIS1). It mandates national cybersecurity strategies, risk management, incident reporting, and cross-border cooperation. Key updates include expanded sector coverage, enhanced supervision tools, and compliance simplifications for 28,700 companies by 2026.  

## Key Sectors Covered  
### Expanded Scope  
NIS2 extends coverage to:  
- **Public electronic communications** (e.g., telecom providers)  
- **Digital services** (e.g., social platforms, cloud providers)  
- **Waste/wastewater management**  
- **Critical product manufacturing**  
- **Postal/courier services**  
- **Public administration** (central and regional levels)  
- **Space sector**  

**Note**: Medium and large entities in these sectors must implement risk management measures and report significant incidents.  

## Compliance Requirements  
### National Cybersecurity Strategies  
Member States must adopt national strategies including:  
- **Supply chain security**  
- **Vulnerability management**  
- **Cybersecurity education**  
- **Regular updates to essential service operator lists**  

### Supervision & Enforcement  
- **CSIRTs** (Computer Security Incident Response Teams) exchange threat intelligence and respond to incidents.  
- **EU-CyCLONe** (European Cyber Crisis Liaison Organisation Network) coordinates responses to large-scale crises.  
- **Accountability**: Top management faces liability for non-compliance with cybersecurity protocols.  

## Recent Amendments (2026)  
On **20 January 2026**, the EU Commission proposed targeted amendments to NIS2 to:  
- **Simplify compliance** for 28,700 companies (including 6,200 SMEs).  
- **Clarify legal obligations** to reduce ambiguity in risk management requirements.  

**Quote**: *"NIS2 is a cornerstone of the EU’s digital resilience, ensuring robust cybersecurity for critical infrastructure and digital services."* – European Commission, 2023  

## Background & Evolution  
### NIS1 to NIS2 Transition  
- **NIS1** (2016/1148) was the first EU-wide cybersecurity framework.  
- **NIS2** (adopted 2022, effective 2023) replaces NIS1, with full transposition by **17 October 2024**.  
- **Key improvements**:  
  - **Wider scope** (28 new sectors).  
  - **Clearer rules** for incident reporting and supervision.  
  - **Stronger enforcement** mechanisms.  

## Technical & Operational Framework  
### Cybersecurity Governance  
- **NIS Cooperation Group**: Facilitates strategic cooperation and information sharing among EU Member States, the European Commission, and ENISA.  
- **Peer reviews**: Voluntary assessments to enhance mutual trust and capabilities.  

### Incident Response  
- **CSIRTs** operate under the **NIS Cooperation Group** to share threat intelligence and coordinate responses.  
- **EU-CyCLONe** ensures rapid, coordinated action during large-scale cyber crises.  

## Conclusion  
NIS2 represents a paradigm shift in EU cybersecurity, combining regulatory rigor with collaborative frameworks. Its emphasis on **risk management**, **cross-border cooperation**, and **accountability** positions it as a global benchmark for critical infrastructure protection.
