# Abstract  
This study investigates the intersection of Enterprise Architecture Management (EAM) and GDPR compliance in Big Data contexts, revealing critical gaps in current research. Through a Systematic Mapping Review (SMR) of 332 studies, we identify seven key trends and emphasize EAM's potential to address data security, privacy, and governance challenges. The findings underscore the need for holistic, integrated approaches to ensure GDPR compliance in an era of exponential data growth.

## Key Findings  
### **1. EAM as a Strategic Tool for GDPR Compliance**  
"Enterprise Architecture Management (EAM) offers a structured framework to align data governance with GDPR requirements," asserts the study. By integrating EAM practices, organizations can systematically address data protection risks, ensuring compliance with GDPR's stringent regulations.  

### **2. Data Security and Privacy Challenges**  
The study highlights that 99% of relevant research on Big Data security and privacy emerged post-2013, with a sharp increase in 2017 coinciding with GDPR's introduction. Key challenges include:  
- **Data governance gaps**: Only 26% of studies focus on governance and risk management, despite their critical role in compliance.  
- **Privacy risks**: 32% of papers address cloud computing and social media's role in data exposure, reflecting growing concerns about "data in the wild."  

### **3. EAM's Role in Risk Mitigation**  
"Privacy by design" principles, mandated by GDPR, require EAM to embed data protection into organizational processes. The study notes that EAM enables:  
- **Proactive risk management**: By mapping data flows and identifying vulnerabilities, EAM reduces the likelihood of breaches.  
- **Compliance automation**: EAM frameworks help organizations meet GDPR's "Record of Processing Activities" (RPA) requirements, which are critical for audit trails.  

### **4. Research Gaps and Future Directions**  
The study identifies seven trends for future research, including:  
1. **Ethical implications of Big Data**: 42.79% of papers address societal impacts, reflecting rising concerns about discrimination and fairness.  
2. **Technical solutions for anonymization**: 37.81% of studies focus on tools to balance data utility with privacy.  
3. **Integration of EAM with AI/ML**: 25.87% of papers explore how EAM can adapt to emerging technologies.  

## Methodology  
The research employed a **Systematic Mapping Review (SMR)**, analyzing 332 studies from Scopus, IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect, and Web of Science. Key criteria included:  
- Focus on GDPR, data protection, and EAM.  
- Peer-reviewed journals and conferences (85% of studies).  
- Exclusion of purely technical solutions without regulatory context.  

## Implications for Practice  
Organizations must adopt EAM to:  
- **Map data ecosystems**: Identify where personal data is stored, processed, and shared.  
- **Implement Privacy Impact Assessments (PIAs)**: Mandated by GDPR, these assessments align with EAM's holistic approach.  
- **Train staff**: Ensure compliance is embedded in organizational culture, not just IT systems.  

## Conclusion  
"Without EAM, GDPR compliance remains a fragmented effort," concludes the study. The research underscores that EAM is not merely a technical tool but a strategic imperative for organizations navigating the complexities of Big Data and regulatory frameworks. Future work must prioritize interdisciplinary collaboration to address ethical, technical, and legal challenges.

4. **Engage stakeholders** to address ethical concerns in data usage.  

This study provides a roadmap for organizations to navigate the complex interplay between Big Data, privacy, and regulatory compliance, positioning EAM as a cornerstone of modern data governance.
