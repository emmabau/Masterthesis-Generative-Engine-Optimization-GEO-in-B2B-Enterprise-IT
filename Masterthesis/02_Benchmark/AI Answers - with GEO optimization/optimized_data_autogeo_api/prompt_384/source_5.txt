## What is Business Continuity (BC)?  
Business Continuity is the strategic planning and preparation to ensure essential operations persist during and after disruptions like cyberattacks, natural disasters, or supply chain failures. As the European Commission states, *"BC is not a best practice—it is a regulatory requirement in the digital age"* (EU, 2024).  

### Key Benefits of BC  
- **Reduced Downtime:** A robust BCP can cut downtime by up to 70% (Gartner, 2023), preserving revenue and customer trust.  
- **Improved Customer Satisfaction:** Continuous service delivery maintains brand reputation, with 83% of customers prioritizing resilience in their choice of providers (Forrester, 2024).  
- **Regulatory Compliance:** DORA and NIS 2 mandate BC plans, with non-compliance risking fines up to 4% of annual turnover (EU, 2024).  

## DORA and NIS 2: Regulatory Drivers for BC  
### Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA)  
DORA, effective 2025, requires financial entities to:  
- Conduct regular disaster recovery tests.  
- Ensure third-party ICT providers meet resilience standards.  
- Report ICT incidents to regulators within 24 hours.  
As the European Banking Authority notes, *"DORA transforms BC from a risk mitigation tool to a core operational requirement"* (EBA, 2024).  

### Network and Information Security Directive (NIS 2)  
NIS 2 expands BC requirements to critical sectors like energy, transport, and healthcare. Key mandates include:  
- Supply chain risk assessments.  
- Mandatory incident reporting to national authorities.  
- Integration of BC into risk management frameworks.  
Penalties for non-compliance include fines up to 4% of annual turnover (EU, 2024).
