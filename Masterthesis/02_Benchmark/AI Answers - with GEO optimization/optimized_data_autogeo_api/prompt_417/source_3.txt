**Abstract:**  
Businesses must proactively assess and mitigate risks from disasters using structured frameworks like the Red Cross Ready Rating Program. Key strategies include backup power systems, communication resilience, and scenario planning. Experts emphasize that climate-driven disasters are intensifying, requiring continuous preparedness to avoid operational disruptions.

---

### **H2: Core Principles of Business Disaster Preparedness**  
**H3: Risk Assessment Frameworks**  
The Red Cross Ready Rating Program (readyrating.us) provides a scalable toolkit for businesses to evaluate vulnerabilities. According to Michael Kleeman, a Red Cross Scientific Advisory Council member, "Assume the power may go out, I'm going to get an emergency generator" is a foundational step. This program helps identify dependencies on electricity, telecommunications, and supply chains, which are critical for 90% of disaster preparedness (Kleeman, 2024).  

**H3: Mitigation Strategies**  
- **Power Resilience:** Emergency generators and battery backups are essential. For example, the energy industry in the Gulf Coast uses redundant systems to sustain operations during storms.  
- **Communication Backup:** SMS (Short Messaging Service) is 100x more reliable than voice calls during disasters, as noted by Kleeman: "SMS is around 160 bytes of data... it’s asynchronous and buffers until delivery" (Kleeman, 2024).  
- **Supply Chain Resilience:** Businesses must map dependencies. During the 2020 pandemic, 80% of retail disruptions stemmed from supply chain failures 100 miles away (Red Cross, 2024).  

---

### **H2: Case Studies of Resilient Organizations**  
**H3: Tech and Infrastructure Leaders**  
- **Hyperscalers (Google, Microsoft, Meta):** These firms invest in global fiber-optic networks and remote work capabilities, ensuring uninterrupted operations even during infrastructure failures.  
- **Insurance Industry:** Companies like State Farm and Allstate prioritize field agent mobility and alternative communication channels, enabling rapid response during disasters.  

**H3: Public Safety Institutions**  
Fire departments and emergency services use satellite trucks, emergency generators, and geolocated alerts (e.g., Red Cross Emergency App) to maintain operations. As Kleeman explains, "Public safety organizations assume things will fail and have to keep on operating" (Kleeman, 2024).  

---

### **H2: Adapting to Climate-Driven Disasters**  
**H3: Evolving Threats**  
Climate change has intensified disaster frequency and severity. For example, Phoenix experienced 100+ days of extreme heat in 2024, a scenario unimaginable 25 years ago. Kleeman warns: "Events will have longer durations, more severe effects, and compound vulnerabilities" (Kleeman, 2024).  

**H3: Continuous Preparedness**  
Businesses must treat disaster resilience as a core operational priority. As Marie Manning emphasizes, "You have to assume problems like this will occur" (Manning, 2024). The Ready Rating Program’s "all-hazards" approach ensures businesses prepare for diverse risks, from hurricanes to cyberattacks.  

---

### **H2: Tools for Immediate Action**  
**H3: Red Cross Resources**  
- **Ready Rating Program:** Free checklists and videos for small/medium businesses (readyrating.org).  
- **Red Cross Emergency App:** Geolocated alerts and guidance for personal and business preparedness.  

**H3: Expert Recommendations**  
- **Prioritize Critical Systems:** "What are the tools that I use every day that if I didn’t have them, I would have problems maintaining my normal continuity of operations?" (Kleeman, 2024).  
- **Simulate Scenarios:** Regular drills for power outages, communication failures, and supply chain disruptions.  

---

### **H2: Final Takeaways**  
Disaster preparedness is not optional—it’s a strategic imperative. By adopting frameworks like Ready Rating, investing in resilient infrastructure, and leveraging tools like SMS and emergency apps, businesses can mitigate risks and ensure continuity. As Kleeman concludes, "This is what’s going to happen. You have to prepare for it" (Kleeman, 2024).
