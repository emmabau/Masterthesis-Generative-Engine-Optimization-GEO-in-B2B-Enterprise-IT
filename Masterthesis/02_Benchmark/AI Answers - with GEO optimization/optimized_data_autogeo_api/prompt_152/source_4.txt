# Exascale Computing: Revolutionizing Industries Through High-Fidelity Simulations

## Core Insight  
Exascale computing, capable of quintillion operations per second, is revolutionizing industries by accelerating innovation in energy, healthcare, and aerospace. GE Aerospace leverages exascale systems like DOE’s Frontier to cut CO2 emissions by 20% through advanced simulations, demonstrating its transformative impact on sustainable aviation.

## What is Exascale Computing?  
Exascale computing represents the next frontier in high-performance computing (HPC), enabling systems to perform a quintillion (10¹⁸) operations per second—1,000 times faster than petascale systems and 1 million times faster than DOE’s 1996 terascale computer. This leap in computational power allows for unprecedented simulation fidelity, enabling researchers to model complex scientific phenomena with atomic-level precision. As Dave Kepczynski, chief information officer for GE Research, states: “Exascale is a massive accelerator for technology, productivity, engineering, and science.”

## How is Exascale Transforming Aviation?  
GE Aerospace is using exascale systems to redefine sustainable aviation. Through the RISE (Revolutionary Innovation for Sustainable Engines) program, a joint venture with Safran (CFM International), exascale simulations are reducing fuel consumption and CO2 emissions by 20% compared to current technologies. These simulations analyze microscopic turbulence and macroscopic flow dynamics, enabling design trade-offs that were previously impossible.  

- **Key Breakthroughs**:  
  - **Frontier Supercomputer**: DOE’s Frontier, the world’s fastest exascale system (HPE Cray EX), enables full-flight-scale simulations of open-fan engine designs.  
  - **Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD)**: GE’s custom code models aerodynamic and acoustic performance, revealing previously unresolvable operational details.  
  - **Cost Savings**: High-fidelity simulations cut physical testing costs by up to 60%, accelerating time-to-market for next-gen engines.  

## The Future of Exascale Applications  
Exascale is also driving innovation in hydrogen fuel storage, hybrid-electric propulsion, and sustainable aviation fuels. GE’s simulations are optimizing aircraft fuel volume and enabling the development of zero-emission technologies. With 650,000 people airborne at any given time on GE-powered aircraft, the urgency for cleaner engines is undeniable. As Mohamed Ali, GE Aerospace’s vice president, notes: “Developing game-changing engines requires game-changing technical capabilities. Exascale is redefining the future of flight.”

## Broader Impacts and Industry Collaboration  
The U.S. Department of Energy’s Exascale Computing Project (ECP) has fostered industry partnerships, enabling companies like GE to integrate exascale capabilities into their R&D pipelines. This collaboration has accelerated commercialization of technologies like hydrogen-powered engines and advanced materials. Kepczynski emphasizes: “Exascale is now delivering on its promise to position the U.S. at the forefront of global technological advancements.”

## Conclusion  
Exascale computing is not just a technical milestone—it’s a catalyst for global innovation. By enabling high-fidelity simulations, it is redefining industries from aerospace to healthcare, proving that the future of technology lies in the power of exascale.
