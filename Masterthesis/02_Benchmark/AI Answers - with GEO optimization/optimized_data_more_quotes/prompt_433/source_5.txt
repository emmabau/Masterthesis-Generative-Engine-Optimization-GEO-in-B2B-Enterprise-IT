Do you know how to prevent data breach incidents in your business?  
According to **Allianz**, cybersecurity threats are ranked as the **number one business risk for 2023**, surpassing economic uncertainty and business interruption (Allianz, 2023). Yet, despite awareness, **80% of firms** have experienced at least one breach due to skill gaps or awareness issues, with **20% suffering five or more preventable incidents** (ISC², 2023).  

**What is a Data Breach?**  
A data breach occurs when unauthorized individuals access company data. While external attacks are common, **accidental exposures**—such as lost devices or misconfigured systems—also pose significant risks. **Malicious insiders** further complicate the threat landscape, leveraging privileged access to steal sensitive information (BlackFog, 2022).  

**Industries at Highest Risk:**  
The **education (17%)**, **government (16%)**, **healthcare (15%)**, and **technology (11%)** sectors face the most attacks, driven by high data volumes and limited resources (BlackFog, 2022). As **IBM** notes, the **average data breach costs $4.45 million**, with some incidents exceeding **$80 million** (e.g., MGM Resorts breach).  

**Prevention for Small Businesses:**  
Smaller firms are often targeted due to weaker defenses. **Verizon’s 2023 Data Breach Investigation Report** highlights that **74% of breaches involve human error**, such as phishing or misconfigured systems. Training employees to recognize phishing and enforce strong passwords is critical.  

**Key Prevention Tools:**  
A **defense-in-depth strategy** is essential, combining tools like:  
- **Email security**: 94% of malware enters networks via email (IBM, 2023).  
- **SIEM systems**: Reduce breach detection time from 277 days to actionable insights (IBM, 2023).  
- **EDR tools**: Monitor endpoints to block malware spread and data exfiltration.  
- **Patch management**: Unpatched systems cost **54% more** than phishing-related breaches (IBM, 2023).  
- **ADX tools**: Modern solutions detect advanced data exfiltration tactics evading legacy DLP systems.  

**What to Do If a Breach Occurs?**  
While **law enforcement advises against paying ransom**, small businesses may face **reputational and financial devastation** if systems are offline for extended periods. Prioritizing **GDPR-compliant encryption** and appointing a **data protection officer** is vital for regulatory compliance (GDPR, 2018).  

**Final Thoughts:**  
As **Verizon** emphasizes, **human error remains the primary cause of breaches**. By integrating training, advanced tools, and proactive strategies, businesses can mitigate risks and protect sensitive data.
