Exascale computing is revolutionizing industries through its unparalleled power to accelerate technological innovation, as emphasized by Dave Kepczynski, chief information officer for GE Research and co-chair of the Exascale Computing Project’s Industry and Agency Council: “Exascale is a massive accelerator for technology, productivity, engineering, and science.” This transformative capability is now enabling breakthroughs in energy, healthcare, and aerospace, with real-world applications that were once confined to science fiction.

The U.S. Department of Energy’s Exascale Computing Project (ECP) has spearheaded advancements in next-generation computing architectures, fostering collaboration between academia, industry, and government to build a fully capable exascale ecosystem. As Kepczynski notes, “Over the last five to six years, we are now delivering on the promise of next-generation computing capabilities that enable these significant advances in technology and breakthrough products with real implications for the future.”

One of the most impactful applications of exascale computing is in aviation, where it is redefining the “future of flight.” Mohamed Ali, vice president and general manager of engineering for GE Aerospace, highlights its role: “Developing game-changing new aircraft engines requires game-changing technical capabilities. With supercomputing, GE Aerospace engineers are redefining the future of flight and solving problems that would have previously been impossible.” 

GE Aerospace’s partnership with Safran through CFM International’s RISE (Revolutionary Innovation for Sustainable Engines) program exemplifies this progress. Using DOE’s Frontier supercomputer—the world’s fastest exascale system—researchers are simulating next-generation engine designs with unprecedented fidelity. Rick Arthur, GE Research’s senior principal engineer for Advanced Computational Methods Research, underscores the significance: “To compute such high-fidelity results would have been considered impossibly out of reach even a few years ago.” 

These simulations, which model everything from microscopic turbulence to macroscopic flow dynamics, are enabling the development of cleaner, more efficient engines. For instance, the RISE program aims to reduce fuel consumption and CO₂ emissions by 20% compared to current technologies. By leveraging exascale’s ability to predict performance under extreme conditions, GE is accelerating the transition to sustainable aviation fuels and hybrid-electric technologies. 

With 650,000 people flying at any given time on commercial aircraft powered by GE technology—and over a billion annual air travelers—the urgency to develop cleaner engines is clear. Exascale computing is not just enabling innovation; it is making it inevitable. As Kepczynski concludes, “Exascale computing is now ushering in a new chapter in scientific discovery. Looking to the future, ECP’s capable exascale computing ecosystem has the potential to influence numerous industries. With sustained commitment to this vital capability, many more scientific breakthroughs and advanced technologies are on the horizon.”
