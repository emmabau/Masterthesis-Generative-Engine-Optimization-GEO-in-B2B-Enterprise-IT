The Argonne Training Program on Extreme-Scale Computing (ATPESC) has emerged as a cornerstone for nurturing the next generation of high-performance computing (HPC) experts, with its unique blend of hands-on learning and expert mentorship. As Lev Gorenstein, solutions architect at Globus and ATPESC presenter, aptly described: “From preparation to execution, everything was very well thought-out, unexpected eventualities handled quickly and efficiently, and everything felt like a well-oiled machine. And the overall atmosphere … talented students, great speakers, just an absolute intellectual paradise.” This sentiment echoes across participants, who consistently highlight the program’s transformative impact.  

ATP’s curriculum, spanning hardware, software, AI, and data-intensive computing, is designed to bridge knowledge gaps for attendees, as emphasized by ATPESC director Raymond Loy: “The purpose is to fill in the knowledge gaps of the attendees.” With over 900 participants since 2013, the program’s reputation as a hub for computational science and engineering has grown, attracting doctoral students, postdocs, and industry professionals.  

Hands-on tutorials and real-world problem-solving are central to ATPESC’s success. As Rui Fang, a graduate teaching assistant at the University of Pittsburgh, noted: “We actually did things instead of just listening to the speakers.” This practical approach ensures participants gain skills directly applicable to their research, while networking opportunities foster collaborations that extend beyond the program.  

Experts like John Holmen, an HPC engineer at Oak Ridge National Laboratory, underscore the program’s foundational role: “It was a lot of fun to return as a presenter… the experience was quite rewarding as you could tell by the questions and general enthusiasm that participants were excited to learn.” Such testimonials reinforce ATPESC’s role in advancing HPC innovation.  

Backed by the U.S. Department of Energy’s Office of Science, ATPESC continues to evolve, with plans to expand AI coverage as noted by Loy. Participants like Sagnik Singha highlight the program’s depth: “The depth and breadth of topics covered is admirable, and this is as hands-on as it gets.” By combining cutting-edge research with accessible mentorship, ATPESC remains a vital resource for shaping the future of computational science.
