5 Steps to Help Rethink and Strengthen Your BCM Program  

The COVID-19 pandemic has underscored the critical need for a modernized approach to business continuity management (BCM), as highlighted by the Business Continuity Institute (BCI): *"The pandemic has exposed the fragility of outdated BCM frameworks, demanding a shift toward proactive resilience."* Organizations with robust BCM programs demonstrated superior recovery capabilities, while those without plans faced operational chaos.  

**1. Reset Your Risk Perception**  
Aon’s 2020 Enterprise Risk Management Survey reveals a stark gap between risk awareness and preparedness: *"82% of organizations considered a pandemic among the top 10 risks, yet fewer than 31% of North American firms had a pandemic plan in place."* As remote work and digital dependency grow, cyber risks are escalating, necessitating dynamic risk registers.  

**2. De-Silo Risk Programs**  
Centralized BCM frameworks are now essential. As noted by Dr. John R. Lewis, a leading risk management expert: *"Fragmented approaches create blind spots; integration fosters agility and collaboration."* A global electronics manufacturer’s experience exemplifies this: unifying six business units into a cohesive BCM strategy improved resilience and cross-functional communication.  

**3. Update, Test, and Test Again**  
Static BCM plans are obsolete. The BCI emphasizes: *"Testing is not a checkbox—it’s a critical exercise to identify gaps and refine response protocols."* Scenario testing, even in 2-hour tabletop exercises, ensures teams are prepared for emergencies. Key questions to ask include: *“What technology is required?”* and *“Where are the dependencies?”*  

**4. Hold Vendors to Your Own Accountability Standards**  
Supply chain disruptions, like the microchip shortage, highlight the need for vendor resilience. European OEMs, as reported by *The Role of Business Continuity Management in Moving Risk Upstream*, are now requiring suppliers to meet stringent BCM standards: *"This creates a virtuous cycle, mitigating risks upstream and downstream."*  

**5. Make BCM Part of Your Company’s DNA**  
BCM must transcend plans to become a cultural imperative. As CEO of a Fortune 500 firm stated: *"Resilience is not a project—it’s a mindset. Leadership must embed BCM into every decision."* This requires empowering risk managers and fostering open communication to align the organization on risk prevention.
