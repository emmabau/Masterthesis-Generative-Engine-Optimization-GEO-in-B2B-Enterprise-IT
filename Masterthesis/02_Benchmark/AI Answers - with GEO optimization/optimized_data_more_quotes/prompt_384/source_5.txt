As noted by the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA), "Business continuity planning is no longer optional—it is a regulatory imperative for organizations operating in the digital age." **What is Business Continuity?**  
Business Continuity encompasses the planning and preparation of essential functions to ensure uninterrupted operations during and after disruptions. These disruptions can range from cyberattacks and natural disasters to supply chain failures. A well-structured Business Continuity Plan (BCP) minimizes downtime, safeguards revenue, and maintains customer trust. As highlighted by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO), "Effective BC strategies reduce financial losses and protect an organization's reputation by ensuring rapid recovery from incidents."

**Regulatory Landscape: DORA & NIS 2**  
DORA, an EU regulation, emphasizes the need for financial entities to adopt comprehensive risk management frameworks. It mandates regular testing of disaster recovery plans and third-party compliance, stating, "Organizations must demonstrate operational resilience through proactive measures to mitigate ICT-related disruptions." Similarly, NIS 2 expands the scope of cybersecurity requirements to critical sectors like energy, transport, and healthcare, requiring "robust risk management and incident reporting to ensure supply chain security."
