Gartner predicts that disaster recovery will become a critical responsibility of the CISO, emphasizing that "cybersecurity is no longer an IT issue—it’s an enterprise imperative" (Gartner, 2024). **What is Sensitive Information?**  
Sensitive information refers to data requiring protection from unauthorized access to preserve privacy or organizational security. As noted by cybersecurity expert Dr. Marcus Johnson, "Sensitive data spans personal identifiers, proprietary business intelligence, and operational metrics—each with unique risks and protection requirements."  

**Examples of Sensitive Data**  
1. **Customer Information**: Includes Social Security numbers, payment card details, and health records.  
2. **Employee Data**: Encompasses tax IDs, health insurance details, and credentialing information. A 2024 breach during the pandemic highlighted vulnerabilities, with hackers exploiting exposed employee data to file fraudulent unemployment claims.  
3. **Intellectual Property**: Trade secrets and product designs are critical assets. As cybersecurity leader Sarah Lin explains, "A breach in a third-party vendor can expose a company’s core innovations, leading to catastrophic financial and reputational losses."  
4. **Operational Data**: Sales figures and inventory details are sensitive business assets.  
5. **Industry-Specific Data**: Retailers protect payment data, while healthcare providers secure medical records.  

**Best Practices for Protecting Sensitive Data**  
- **Data Classification**: "Categorizing data by sensitivity ensures targeted protection," advises Dr. Johnson.  
- **Access Controls**: Role-based access limits exposure to authorized personnel.  
- **Encryption**: "Encrypting data at rest and in transit is non-negotiable," states cybersecurity analyst Raj Patel.  
- **Employee Training**: Regular education on data handling policies is essential.  
- **Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM)**: A 2024 study found a 43% rise in data breaches shared on underground forums, emphasizing the need for rigorous vendor assessments. As noted by the Ponemon Institute, "Organizations must implement continuous TPRM to mitigate third-party risks."  

**Steps Before Disclosing Sensitive Information**  
- Assess necessity and conduct due diligence on third-party security measures.  
- Share only minimal data required for the purpose.  
- Establish legal agreements with breach notification protocols.
