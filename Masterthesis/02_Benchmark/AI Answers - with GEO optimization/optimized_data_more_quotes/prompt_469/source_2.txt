Fact Check Team: Decline in remote and hybrid job opportunities for high earners  
WASHINGTON (TND) — "Remote job opportunities paying at least $250,000 a year have declined by 60%, while hybrid jobs for the same salary range have plummeted by 95%," according to Ladders, a website specializing in high-paying jobs. This trend extends beyond high-income positions, as LinkedIn's latest report states, "the demand for remote jobs far exceeds the supply, with twice the number of applications for remote positions compared to available jobs." Additionally, remote job postings have decreased by 23% from the previous year.  

B2B Reviews' survey highlights, "more than 28% of workers were on hybrid schedules, working remotely at least some of the time," while the overall share of fully remote workers in the U.S. dropped to 12.2% in the past year, down from over 14% the previous year. Both remote and hybrid job opportunities have been gradually declining since 2020.  

Despite this decline, FlexJobs' research indicates, "65% of workers want to be fully remote, while 32% prefer a hybrid work schedule." Looking ahead, the Future Workforce Pulse Report projects, "over 32.6 million Americans, or about 22% of the workforce, are expected to be working remotely by 2025." This number is projected to grow as technology advances, potentially increasing remote and hybrid opportunities.
