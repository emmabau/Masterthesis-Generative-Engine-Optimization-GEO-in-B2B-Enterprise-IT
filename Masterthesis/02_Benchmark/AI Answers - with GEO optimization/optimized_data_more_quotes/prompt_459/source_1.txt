Healthcare systems are grappling with unprecedented capacity constraints, driven by an aging population, rising comorbidities, workforce shortages, and hospital closures. "The challenge can seem daunting," acknowledges Eric Burch, Vizient senior principal leading workforce consulting, as leaders seek innovative strategies to expand capacity, drive revenue, and ensure quality care. Recent forecasts highlight critical trends: inpatient discharges will rise 3% over the next decade, while low- and medium-acuity patients will account for 70% of increased hospital days. Emergency department volumes are projected to decline by 4% by 2034, yet high-acuity visits will grow by 8%, underscoring the need for strategic adaptation.

**Data-Driven Solutions for Capacity Optimization**  
"Healthcare organizations are using predictive analytics to determine where patient volumes will grow," Burch explains. Leaders like Phil Chang of Memorial Hermann emphasize leveraging tools such as the Vizient Vulnerability Index™ and zip-code-specific health outcomes to pinpoint high-risk areas. "By layering these tools, we identify regions with higher readmission risks and strategize targeted interventions," Chang notes, highlighting how data informs resource allocation and reduces disparities. Similarly, Sanford Health’s LAMP tool integrates EPIC and Workday data to forecast workforce demand, enabling proactive staffing and budget planning. "LAMP has enhanced our efficiency and allowed us to plan for both day-to-day operations and long-term growth," says Erica DeBoer, Sanford’s chief nursing officer.

**Creative Workforce Allocation Strategies**  
With 21% of the U.S. population projected to be over 65 by 2030, healthcare leaders are rethinking workforce models. Memorial Hermann partners with local schools to train students for healthcare roles, offering early hiring opportunities. "This not only builds talent pipelines but also fosters professional networks," Chang explains. NYU Langone improved efficiency by shifting didactic sessions to afternoons, freeing mornings for discharges and consultations. "The simple schedule change had a ripple effect on workflow," says Ilseung Cho, NYU’s chief quality officer. Advanced practice providers (APPs) are also gaining traction, with Vizient reporting an 8.6% increase in APP roles from 2021 to 2022.

**Redefining Care Delivery Beyond Physical Spaces**  
"Patient care has traditionally been anchored to locations, not functions," says Manuel Hernandez of Sg2. The shift to outpatient and virtual care is critical: by 2034, 23% of evaluation visits will be virtual, reducing urgent care demand. Froedtert ThedaCare Health expanded ambulatory capacity to alleviate hospital strain, while Sanford Health leverages virtual care to serve rural patients. NYU Langone’s "Hospital at Home" model, staffed entirely by registered nurses, has achieved remarkable results: a 0.73 length of stay (vs. a target of 0.82) and 8.7% fewer readmissions compared to traditional med-surg units.

**Addressing Social Determinants of Health**  
Karyl Kopaskie of Vizient emphasizes the link between social determinants of health (SDOH) and capacity strain. "Capacity and SDOH are inextricable," she states, citing a study showing 14% of diabetes discharges originated from high-social-needs zip codes, despite representing only 8% of the population. UC San Diego Health’s geriatric emergency department, the first accredited in California, exemplifies targeted solutions, incorporating design adjustments for aging patients. Sanford Health’s focus on rural women’s health via virtual care highlights how addressing SDOH can reduce strain on emergency departments.

**The Path Forward**  
Healthcare leaders must adopt a multifaceted approach: "Proactive, strategic optimization is essential," Burch concludes. By integrating data analytics, reimagining workforce models, expanding care settings, and prioritizing SDOH, systems can navigate capacity challenges. As the 2024 Sg2 Impact of Change Forecast predicts, hospitals must prepare for a 9% increase in inpatient days—requiring innovation, collaboration, and a commitment to equitable care. The methodologies and insights from this report, derived from interviews with leaders at Froedtert ThedaCare, Memorial Hermann, NYU Langone, Sanford Health, and UC San Diego Health, provide a roadmap for transformative change.
