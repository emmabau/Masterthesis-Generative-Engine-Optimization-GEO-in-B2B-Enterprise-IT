Business continuity planning is essential for maintaining operations during crises, ensuring your company can function without significant disruption. As noted by PwC, "91% of respondents to our 2023 Global Crisis and Resilience Survey experienced at least one disruption other than the pandemic—a 22% increase since 2019," underscoring the growing need for robust strategies. Cyberattacks and supply chain disruptions top the list of concerns, making preparedness critical for all-sized companies.

A business continuity plan (BCP) outlines how your organization will operate during and after emergencies, minimizing loss and ensuring critical functions like payroll and supplier transitions are managed. The Financial Industry Regulatory Authority (FINRA) mandates BCPs under Rule 4370, highlighting industry-specific requirements. As stated by the U.S. Small Business Administration, "Business continuity planning improves resilience, making organizations more stable, trustworthy, and competitive."

**Steps to Build a Business Continuity Plan:**
1. **Identify Critical Functions:** Determine which processes must continue for survival, such as payment processing or customer communication. This forms the foundation of your Business Impact Analysis (BIA).
2. **Map Dependencies:** Use a spreadsheet to visualize interdependencies, identifying over-reliance on individuals, devices, or vendors.
3. **Conduct a BIA:** Assess maximum allowable downtime (MAD), financial losses, customer impact, legal risks, and operational priorities. Prioritize based on risk scores.
4. **Evaluate Risks:** Focus on high-likelihood threats like cyber incidents, supply chain failures, or natural disasters.
5. **Develop Strategies:** Implement redundancies, such as SLAs with providers or backup ISPs, to minimize downtime.
6. **Create Response Procedures:** Document triggers, first-hour actions, and roles for disruptions like payment processor outages or leadership absences.
7. **Establish Communication Protocols:** Outline how to notify employees, customers, and vendors during outages, using templates and automated workflows.
8. **Document Recovery Basics:** Include backup access, restore procedures, and offline storage locations.
9. **Test and Maintain:** Conduct annual reviews, tabletop exercises, and quarterly checks to ensure tools and vendor availability.

**Key Benefits of BCPs:**
- **Risk Mitigation:** Reduces financial exposure by minimizing downtime and ensuring compliance.
- **Customer Trust:** Maintains operations during crises, preventing dissatisfaction and retaining clients.
- **Employee Engagement:** Demonstrates preparedness, fostering trust and stability among staff.
- **Regulatory Compliance:** Ensures adherence to industry standards, such as FINRA’s requirements.
