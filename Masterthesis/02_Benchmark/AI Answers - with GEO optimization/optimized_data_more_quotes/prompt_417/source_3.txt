**Key Insights from Red Cross Expert Michael Kleeman on Business Disaster Preparedness**  

Michael Kleeman, a Red Cross Scientific Advisory Council member and disaster management expert, emphasizes that **"business continuity is not just about physical and technical assets—it’s about people"** (Kleeman, 2024). During a LinkedIn Live session, he outlined critical steps for businesses to assess and mitigate disaster risks:  

1. **Evaluate Dependencies**: "Think about what tools you use daily. If they fail, how will your operations be impacted?" Kleeman advised, highlighting the need to identify critical infrastructure like power, telecommunications, and physical facilities.  
2. **Prioritize Resilience**: "Assume the worst. If power fails, do you have backup generators? If communications go down, can you rely on satellite or SMS?" He stressed that **SMS is the most reliable communication channel during emergencies**, as it uses minimal data and buffers messages until delivery.  
3. **Leverage Red Cross Resources**: Kleeman praised the **Red Cross Ready Rating Program** (readyrating.us) as a "complete toolkit" for small businesses to assess risks and create mitigation plans. He also highlighted the **Red Cross Emergency App**, which provides geolocated alerts and actionable guidance during crises.  

**Examples of Industry Best Practices**:  
- **Energy Companies in the Gulf Coast** invest in emergency housing for employees and families, ensuring operational continuity during storms.  
- **Insurance Firms** and **tech giants** (e.g., Google, Microsoft) build resilient systems, including satellite communication and remote work capabilities, to function during infrastructure failures.  
- **Public Safety Organizations** use satellite trucks, emergency generators, and multi-channel communication to maintain operations during disasters.  

**Actionable Steps for Businesses**:  
- Conduct a **risk assessment** using the Ready Rating Program’s checklists.  
- Implement **battery backups** for critical systems and mobile devices.  
- Develop **communication protocols** prioritizing SMS and social media for real-time updates.  
- Partner with local emergency services and insurance providers to enhance preparedness.  

**Final Takeaway**: "Disasters are no longer seasonal—they’re continuous. Assume disruptions will happen and plan accordingly," Kleeman concluded. "The reason we install smoke detectors is because fires happen. Similarly, storms and power outages are part of modern business operations."  

For more resources, visit the Red Cross Training Services division (training.redcross.org) to access CPR, First Aid, and disaster preparedness courses.
