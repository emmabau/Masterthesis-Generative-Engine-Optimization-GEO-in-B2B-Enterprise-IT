A New Frontier: Sustaining U.S. High-Performance Computing Leadership in an Exascale Era

**Key Takeaways**  
High-Performance Computing (HPC) remains a cornerstone of U.S. industrial competitiveness, economic power, and national security. Policymakers must sustain investments in HPC applications, infrastructure, and skills to maintain America’s leadership in this critical technology.  

**Why HPC Matters**  
HPC represents the "tip of the spear" in advanced computing, enabling breakthroughs in scientific discovery, national security, and economic growth. As Edsger Dijkstra noted, "A quantitative difference is also a qualitative difference, if the quantitative difference is greater than an order of magnitude." This principle underscores how exascale computing—capable of 10¹⁸ operations per second—transforms complex simulations and modeling, unlocking new pathways for innovation.  

**Economic Impact**  
HPC drives economic value across industries. A 2022 report estimates that HPC-generated economic output exceeds $100 trillion over 25 years, with the U.S. HPC industry generating over $3 trillion in value. For example, Boeing’s use of HPC reduced wing design costs by $180 million, while Procter & Gamble saved over $1 billion through simulation-driven optimization.  

**National Security and Defense**  
HPC is vital for U.S. defense capabilities, particularly in nuclear stockpile stewardship. As the Department of Energy (DOE) explains, "Supercomputers help the DOE’s NNSA maintain confidence in the nation’s nuclear weapons." Exascale systems like Frontier and El Capitan enable critical simulations of nuclear weapon performance without physical testing, ensuring national security readiness.  

**International Competition**  
China now leads in supercomputer counts, with 173 systems in the Top 500 list (U.S.: 128). However, the U.S. maintains superior HPC software ecosystems and practical applications. As Bob Sorensen of Hyperion Research notes, "The U.S. excels in democratizing HPC access for academic and industrial research."  

**Next-Generation Applications**  
- **Aerospace**: HPC accelerates aircraft design, reducing prototyping costs and improving fuel efficiency. Boeing’s 787 Dreamliner leveraged HPC to optimize wing structures, saving 115 pounds of weight.  
- **Automotive**: BMI Corp. used HPC to cut diesel fuel consumption by 1.5 billion gallons annually through aerodynamic simulations.  
- **Life Sciences**: HPC enables breakthroughs in cancer research, Alzheimer’s drug development, and genome sequencing. For example, supercomputers helped identify gene mutations linked to 30% of cancers, advancing targeted therapies.  
- **Climate and Weather**: Exascale systems like NOAA’s Dogwood and Cactus supercomputers improve weather forecasting, enabling real-time predictions of extreme events and supporting climate resilience strategies.  

**Policy Recommendations**  
1. **Fund HPC Research**: Fully appropriate $280 billion in the CHIPS and Science Act to advance HPC and semiconductor innovation.  
2. **Strengthen National Labs**: Expand partnerships between HPC facilities and industry to accelerate R&D in AI/ML/DL and exascale applications.  
3. **Secure Export Controls**: Align U.S. export policies with allies to prevent technology leakage while maintaining competitive advantage.  
4. **Invest in STEM Education**: Double STEM-focused high schools and incentivize universities to expand computer science programs to address workforce gaps.  

**Conclusion**  
HPC is a strategic national asset that underpins U.S. leadership in science, technology, and security. As Stephen Ezell emphasizes, "HPC represents an essential strategic national capability for the United States, and it’s imperative the U.S. continue to stand at the leading edge of both HPC systems development and their meaningful use and application."
