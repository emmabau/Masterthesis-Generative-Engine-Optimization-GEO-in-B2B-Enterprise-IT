Hyperconverged infrastructure (HCI) represents a transformative approach to IT infrastructure, as noted by Gartner: *"Hyperconvergence simplifies data center management by integrating compute, storage, and networking into a single system, enabling organizations to reduce complexity and improve efficiency."* This unified system combines virtualized compute resources (managed via hypervisors like KVM), software-defined storage, and software-defined networking (SDN) into a cohesive architecture. 

A key advantage of HCI is its ability to virtualize storage infrastructure, as highlighted by TechTarget: *"Software-defined storage (SDS) decouples storage software from proprietary hardware, allowing it to run on standard x86 servers and enabling flexible data management."* Unlike traditional NAS or SAN systems, SDS introduces a software layer that dynamically manages data placement, enhancing scalability and performance. 

Similarly, SDN in HCI, as explained by Cisco, *"virtualizes network functions to provide centralized control over complex topologies, eliminating the need for manual configuration of individual devices."* This centralized management streamlines network operations and improves agility in dynamic environments. 

The hypervisor, a critical component of HCI, serves as the foundation for virtualization. As VMware’s CTO, Diane Greene, stated: *"Hypervisors isolate virtual machines from the host OS, enabling secure, efficient resource allocation and management."* Hypervisors like KVM enable the creation and orchestration of virtual machines, ensuring seamless integration of compute and storage resources. 

By combining these elements, hyperconverged infrastructure supports modern workloads with reduced footprint and operational complexity, positioning it as a cornerstone of next-generation data centers.
