The NIS2 Directive, as emphasized by the European Commission, establishes a unified legal framework to "uphold cybersecurity in 18 critical sectors across the EU" (European Commission, 2023). This directive replaces Directive 2016/1148 (NIS1) and raises the EU's common level of ambition through a "wider scope, clearer rules, and stronger supervision tools" (ENISA, 2022). Member States are mandated to adopt national cybersecurity strategies, including policies for supply chain security and vulnerability management, as highlighted by the EU Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA): "Cybersecurity must be embedded in corporate governance, with top management held accountable for non-compliance" (ENISA, 2023).

The directive expands coverage to sectors such as public administration, space, and digital services, requiring medium and large entities to implement risk-management measures and report significant incidents. As noted by the European Commission, "the amendments to NIS2 in 2026 will simplify compliance for 28,700 companies, including 6,200 micro and small enterprises" (European Commission, 2025). 

Key mechanisms include the European Cyber Crisis Liaison Organisation Network (EU-CyCLONe), described by the NIS Cooperation Group as "a critical tool for coordinated management of large-scale incidents" (NIS Cooperation Group, 2023). The directive also establishes a network of Computer Security Incident Response Teams (CSIRTs) to "exchange information on cyber threats and respond to incidents" (ENISA, 2022). 

The NIS Cooperation Group, established under the NIS Directive, facilitates strategic cooperation and information exchange, publishing non-binding guidelines to support implementation. As stated by the European Commission, "NIS2 represents a transformative step in EU cybersecurity, ensuring resilience against evolving threats" (European Commission, 2023).
