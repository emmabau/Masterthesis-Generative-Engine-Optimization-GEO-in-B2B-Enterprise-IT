7 Reasons Hybrid Isn’t Working – and What HR Can Do About It  
“If you love the hybrid workplace, you are going to hate this research,” warns Peter Cappelli and Ranya Nehmeh, co-authors of *In Praise of the Office: The Limits to Hybrid and Remote Work*. For HR professionals, their findings in the *Harvard Business Review* reveal critical challenges with hybrid work that persist despite years of implementation.  

**The Facts on Hybrid**  
Cappelli and Nehmeh evaluated numerous studies to uncover why hybrid models often fail. Their research highlights that many of the issues plaguing hybrid teams—such as poor onboarding and collaboration—were not new but were previously resolved through in-person interactions. As they note, “*The biggest problem is collaboration between separate teams. Employees jump more hurdles to make connections on teams outside their own. Then information doesn’t flow efficiently.*”  

**Key Problems with Hybrid Work**  
1. **New Hires Struggle**  
   Remote and hybrid environments hinder new employees’ ability to learn. “*They have no one to watch and mimic. They need to do extra work just to ask for help.*” Without physical proximity, mentors and peers fail to notice struggles, leading to slower onboarding.  

2. **Employees Are Self-Focused**  
   Hybrid settings prioritize individual KPIs over teamwork. “*People respond to requests for help when they’re pinged … but usually not until after they’ve finished their own tasks.*” Proximity fosters spontaneous collaboration, which virtual setups lack.  

3. **Teams Collaborate Less Effectively**  
   “*Not surprisingly, when employees are more focused on their individual work, they spend less time brainstorming, meeting and collaborating with others.*” Cross-team communication breaks down, stifling innovation.  

4. **Meetings Lose Effectiveness**  
   Virtual meetings are “*less effective than in-person meetings*” for several reasons: they generate fewer ideas, fill calendars, slow productivity, lack focus, and multiply due to inefficiency.  

5. **The Wrong People Get Promoted**  
   “*The best individual performers who are promoted to management end up leading the worst-performing teams.*” Hybrid work’s emphasis on individual metrics skews promotion decisions, harming leadership quality.  

6. **Cultures Become Divided**  
   “*Because they have different ideas about what is acceptable, employees from both groups may think they are doing the right thing while behaving quite differently.*” Pre-pandemic office norms clash with hybrid-era expectations, creating cultural fragmentation.  

7. **Employee Commitment Weakened**  
   “*Employees were starting to feel less committed to their companies before the pandemic. Now, they simply don’t see one another as much and don’t have a building to make connections in, so their commitment has faded even more.*”  

**The Problem with the Hybrid Problems**  
Despite these challenges, “*People want to work hybrid, despite the Harvard research.*” Gallup found that 55% of Baby Boomers and Gen Xers, 60% of millennials, and 71% of Gen Z prefer hybrid work. Eliminating hybrid models risks alienating a majority of employees.  

**Solutions to Common Hybrid Problems**  
Cappelli and Nehmeh urge HR to “*Treat yours like a new workplace, not the same one you left behind when everyone walked out the door for Covid.*” Key strategies include:  
- **Assess your situation**: Analyze meeting frequency, participant numbers, and response times to identify hybrid inefficiencies.  
- **Define the time**: Create “in-office anchor days” for collaboration, such as a four-day workweek with flexible Fridays.  
- **Make the space**: Use on-site days for teamwork, not just individual work.  
- **Check your KPIs**: Shift from individual metrics to collaborative goals.  
- **Build communities**: Foster connections through mentorship, cross-team projects, and in-person events.  
- **Monitor mental well-being**: Train managers to conduct routine check-ins and support employees’ emotional health.
