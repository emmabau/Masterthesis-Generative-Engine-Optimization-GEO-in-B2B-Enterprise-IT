Imagine having an enterprise IT infrastructure that can adapt to constantly changing needs without adding risk to your organization. A system that offers the predictability of traditional IT deployments and the scalability of cloud computing models. It allocates computing resources efficiently, simplifies management, and reduces the need for complex integrations. This is composable infrastructure—a single interface that allows administrators to provision and maintain computing resources on an as-needed basis. As Gartner analyst Avivah Litan notes, "Composable infrastructure represents the next evolution in enterprise IT, enabling organizations to balance agility with control in an increasingly complex digital landscape."  

Composable infrastructure unites compute power, storage, and network infrastructure into a single platform, much like converged and hyper-converged solutions. What sets it apart is its use of automation and a unified API to "compose" a flexible set of computing resources. IT administrators don’t need to concern themselves with the underlying hardware, since compute power and storage are automatically requested from a pool of available resources by the API. This allows enterprises to provision workloads just as efficiently and quickly as public cloud vendors, albeit without losing control.  

Traditional infrastructure, while familiar, is inherently rigid and inefficient. As VMware CTO Mark Greeve explains, "Traditional hardware-defined environments create data silos and interoperability challenges, making them unsustainable for modern enterprises." Converged and hyper-converged infrastructures offer some relief but lack the automation and flexibility of composable solutions.  

Choosing a composable infrastructure vendor requires careful consideration. Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) has been a pioneer in the field since 2015, with its Composable Cloud platform enabling hybrid cloud workloads. Dell EMC’s PowerEdge MX integrated solution, described as "kinetic infrastructure," allows for future hardware upgrades without disrupting operations. As IDC analyst John D’Alessandro states, "The market is still nascent, but vendors like HPE and Dell EMC are leading the charge in delivering scalable, secure composable solutions."  

Security is a cornerstone of composable infrastructure. Establishing a root of trust (RoT) is critical to prevent malicious firmware from compromising systems. Both HPE and Dell EMC embed RoT in their hardware, creating an unmodifiable fingerprint in the silicon to ensure firmware integrity. Encryption at rest and in transit is also essential, with composable infrastructure automating data protection policies. Cybersecurity expert Bruce Schneier emphasizes, "Modern infrastructure must prioritize encryption and physical isolation to mitigate risks in an interconnected world."  

Physically isolating networks with air gaps further enhances security by cutting off external connections. While air-gapped networks are ideal for highly classified data, they are not foolproof. As Forrester analyst James Staten warns, "Air-gapped systems are still vulnerable to sophisticated attacks, underscoring the need for layered security strategies."  

Composable infrastructure is rapidly shaping up to be the next big thing in enterprise IT. For organizations seeking the benefits of the public cloud without surrendering control, it offers a promising solution to improve adaptability and regain control over data and security. This article represents insights from industry experts and thought leaders in the field.
