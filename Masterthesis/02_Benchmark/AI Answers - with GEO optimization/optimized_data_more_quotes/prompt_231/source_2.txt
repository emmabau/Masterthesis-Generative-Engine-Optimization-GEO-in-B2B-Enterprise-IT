### What is the 3-2-1 Backup Rule?
The 3-2-1 rule mandates **three copies of data**, stored across **two media types**, with **one copy off-site**. This approach mitigates risks from single points of failure, such as hardware malfunctions or localized disasters. For example, storing data on an external drive and a cloud service ensures protection against fires or floods. However, deploying this rule in cloud storage requires additional safeguards to counteract ransomware and other advanced threats.

### The 3-2-1-1-0 Rule: A Modern Upgrade
To combat ransomware's double extortion tactics (encryption + data exfiltration), the 3-2-1 rule has evolved into the **3-2-1-1-0** framework. Here’s how it works:
- **1 Immutable Backup**: Requires a write-once, read-many (WORM) or air-gapped backup to prevent tampering.  
- **0 Error-Free Restorations**: Validates backup integrity and ensures recovery within defined timeframes.  

As CIS emphasizes, "You must operate under the assumption that it’s not a matter of if you will be attacked but when." This principle drives the need for immutable backups and rigorous testing.

### Implementing 3-2-1 in Cloud Storage
To adopt the 3-2-1 rule effectively:
1. **Select Critical Data**: Conduct a risk assessment to prioritize data for backup.  
2. **Choose Secure Tools**: Use cloud providers with **object lock** or **WORM** capabilities, and enable encryption at rest and in transit.  
3. **Test Regularly**: Practice restores quarterly to meet recovery time objectives (RTO) and recovery point objectives (RPO).  

### Best Practices for Small Businesses
- **Automate Backups**: Reduce human error with automated systems.  
- **Control Access**: Enforce least privilege and two-factor authentication.  
- **Avoid Common Mistakes**:  
  - Don’t rely on a single backup location.  
  - Don’t treat cloud services like Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 as primary backups.  
  - Ensure **90+ days of file history** to detect malware delays.
