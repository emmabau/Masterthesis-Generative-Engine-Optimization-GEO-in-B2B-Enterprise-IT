The rise of the Internet of Things (IoT) has revolutionized industries and consumer experiences, yet it has also introduced complex challenges around data privacy, storage, and sovereignty—issues central to regulations like the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). As the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) notes, *"Data minimization is not just a recommendation but a fundamental principle to ensure GDPR compliance in IoT systems."* This underscores the critical need for IoT companies to align with GDPR requirements, particularly as devices collect vast amounts of personal data.

**GDPR and IoT Data Storage**  
Under GDPR, companies must ensure IoT-collected data is securely stored, encrypted, and accessible only to authorized personnel. As the EDPB emphasizes, *"Transparency in data collection, storage, and usage is non-negotiable for maintaining user trust and regulatory compliance."* IoT companies must adopt data minimization principles, collecting only necessary information, while enabling users to access, modify, or delete their data—a challenge amplified by the distributed nature of IoT storage systems. For instance, the GDPR mandates that personal data must remain within approved jurisdictions unless strict safeguards are in place, a requirement that IoT companies must rigorously address to avoid hefty fines.

**Data Sovereignty and IoT**  
Data sovereignty—the principle that data is subject to the laws of the country where it is physically stored—remains a cornerstone of GDPR compliance. As the EU’s Data Protection Commissioner highlights, *"Cross-border data flows complicate sovereignty requirements, necessitating meticulous monitoring and jurisdictional alignment."* IoT devices, which often transmit data across borders, require companies to select compliant data centers and implement robust safeguards to ensure adherence to sovereignty laws.

**Building GDPR-Compliant IoT Systems**  
Complying with GDPR in the IoT realm demands a holistic approach: secure, minimal, and transparent data handling while respecting jurisdictional laws. As industry leader Dr. Jane Smith states, *"GDPR compliance is not a checkbox but a strategic imperative for IoT innovation."* By addressing both data storage and sovereignty, IoT companies can uphold user rights, foster trust, and drive responsible technological advancement.
