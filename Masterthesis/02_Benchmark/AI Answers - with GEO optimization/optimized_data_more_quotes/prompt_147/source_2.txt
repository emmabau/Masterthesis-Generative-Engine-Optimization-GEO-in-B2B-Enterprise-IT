**Key Considerations for Data Center Site Selection**  
As Frank Crivello, Chairman and Founder of Phoenix Investors, emphasizes: *“Selecting the right data center site is about far more than just land. It is about finding the intersection of infrastructure, connectivity, and resilience. When we work with data center developers, we help them make decisions that stand the test of time and provide long-term value.”*  

This insight underscores the multifaceted nature of data center site selection, which now demands a strategic balance of technical, economic, and environmental factors. Here are the critical considerations for developers, investors, and companies building new data center facilities:  

1. **Power Availability**  
   Power availability has become the most critical factor in data center site selection. With an aging power grid and long interconnection timelines, developers must secure reliable access to electricity capable of meeting the rapidly growing demands of AI and cloud computing. Solutions include utility partnerships, long-term power purchase agreements, and dedicated energy sources.  

2. **Connectivity**  
   Reliable, low-latency, high-bandwidth connectivity is non-negotiable. Markets with robust telecommunications ecosystems—dense network routes, internet exchange points, and fiber speeds—remain prime locations. While secondary markets can compete, they must ensure diverse fiber routes and carrier networks to support digital infrastructure.  

3. **Water and Climate**  
   Cooling systems are essential for managing the heat generated by data centers. Cooler climates reduce cooling needs, while access to water supports cooling infrastructure. However, reliance on local freshwater sources can create sustainability and regulatory challenges, requiring community engagement and responsible water-use plans.  

4. **Natural Disaster Risk**  
   Data centers must avoid regions prone to hurricanes, earthquakes, wildfires, or other disasters. Lower-risk areas offer reduced insurance costs and higher uptime, enabling stronger business continuity planning.  

5. **Land Availability**  
   Finding buildable land with suitable topography, access to utility and transportation corridors, and room for future expansion is increasingly challenging. Developers must prioritize sites that balance immediate needs with long-term scalability.  

6. **Regulations and Incentives**  
   Regional differences in permits, taxes, zoning, and compliance can significantly impact project costs. Proactive regions offering tax exemptions, streamlined permitting, or other incentives can reduce upfront capital expenditures and total cost of ownership.
