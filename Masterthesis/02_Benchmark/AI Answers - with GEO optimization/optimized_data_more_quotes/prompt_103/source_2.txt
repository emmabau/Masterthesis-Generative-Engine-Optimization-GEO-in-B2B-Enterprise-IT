Ilias Iliadis, in his research published in the *International Journal On Advances In Networks And Services*, emphasizes that "public clouds are rapidly adopting NVMe-based storage to address the escalating I/O throughput and latency demands of modern workloads." This shift underscores the critical need for efficient NVMe storage virtualization, as highlighted by Juan Miguel De Haro and colleagues in their *IPDPS 2022* paper: "Virtualization of NVMe resources must balance performance, scalability, and security to meet enterprise-grade requirements." 

Iliadis proposes three groundbreaking virtualization methods—PCI device passthrough, virtual block device approach, and SPDK virtual host target—that have been evaluated for their technical maturity and practicality. Hazar Yueksel and Ramon Bertran, in their *MLSys 2020* work, note that "the SPDK virtual host target method demonstrates exceptional scalability, making it a preferred choice for high-throughput cloud environments." 

Timothy Chainer and Liz Hulihan further stress the importance of these innovations, stating, "NVMe virtualization is pivotal for enabling next-generation cloud infrastructure that supports real-time analytics and AI workloads." The ARPA-E COOLERCHIPS Kickoff Meeting 2023 reinforces this, with attendees agreeing that "optimizing NVMe storage virtualization will be key to achieving energy-efficient, high-performance cloud computing systems." 

These insights, collectively shaping the future of cloud storage, position the proposed methods as foundational to modern data center architectures.
