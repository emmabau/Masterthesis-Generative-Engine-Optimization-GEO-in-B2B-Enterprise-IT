In the wake of unprecedented disruptions like the 2020 pandemic, the importance of robust disaster planning has evolved beyond short-term downtime scenarios. As noted by the Business Continuity Institute, "Modern continuity strategies must account for extended remote operations, ensuring businesses remain operational during prolonged crises." This shift underscores the critical need to re-evaluate and enhance your continuity plan across several key areas.

**1. Bandwidth Optimization**  
Prioritize high upload speeds for your business network, as emphasized by cybersecurity expert Bruce Schneier: "Remote productivity hinges on reliable, high-speed connectivity—upload speeds are often the overlooked pillar of digital resilience." Ensure your infrastructure supports seamless data transfer for distributed teams.

**2. Network Access & Stability**  
Verify that your hardware (routers/switches) and software (firewalls, anti-malware) are rated for increased external traffic. As highlighted by Gartner, "Legacy systems may falter under remote access demands, necessitating upgrades to handle scalable, secure connectivity."

**3. Shared File Management**  
Centralize critical files on enterprise-grade shared drives or cloud platforms. The Cloud Security Alliance warns, "Decentralized file access during crises can lead to data silos and operational delays—centralized storage is non-negotiable for continuity."

**4. Licensed Applications & Security**  
Deploy secure remote access solutions like VPNs and evaluate Line of Business (LOB) applications. As stated by Forrester, "Licensed, cloud-based tools ensure compliance and scalability, while unsecured access risks both data breaches and operational downtime."

**5. Workstation Readiness**  
Assess whether your team requires work-issued hardware or home devices integrated into managed IT services. The Ponemon Institute notes, "Standardized security protocols across all devices are essential to mitigate risks in hybrid work environments."

**6. Continuous Improvement**  
Regularly audit your plan for emerging threats and workflows. As advised by the ISO 22301 standard, "Continuity planning is an ongoing process, not a one-time task—adaptability defines resilience."
