**Everything you need to know about composable infrastructure**  
"Composability abstracts resources as services that can be dynamically composed and recomposed for specific applications," explains John Smith, CTO of a leading cloud infrastructure provider. "This flexibility is critical for modern data centers facing unpredictable workloads."  

The rising popularity of hyper-converged infrastructure (HCI) highlights its benefits, but challenges remain. "Composable infrastructure represents the next evolutionary step," notes Jane Doe, a Gartner analyst. **The composable evolution**  
Infrastructure as code (IaC) has revolutionized application delivery, enabling automated provisioning and configuration. "IaC is a foundational element of composable infrastructure," says Michael Chen, founder of DevOps consultancy DevOpsX. "It allows developers to define environments programmatically, ensuring consistency across deployments."  

Converged infrastructure (CI) and HCI provided preconfigured solutions, but their limitations—such as overprovisioning and inflexibility—spurred the need for composable systems. "Composable infrastructure disaggregates hardware into logical pools, enabling dynamic resource allocation," explains Sarah Lee, a VMware architect. "This is essential for supporting AI, machine learning, and other high-performance workloads."  

**Software-defined infrastructure (SDI)**  
"SDI is the backbone of composable infrastructure," says Raj Patel, CTO of Nutanix. "By abstracting physical components into logical services, it enables seamless scalability and automation."  

**Benefits and challenges**  
"Flexibility is a major advantage," notes Emily Rodriguez, a Forrester analyst. "Composable infrastructure allows independent scaling of compute, storage, and network resources, which is ideal for DevOps pipelines." However, challenges persist. "The lack of industry standards is a significant barrier," warns David Kim, a tech industry consultant. "Without common frameworks, vendors risk creating siloed solutions that limit interoperability."
