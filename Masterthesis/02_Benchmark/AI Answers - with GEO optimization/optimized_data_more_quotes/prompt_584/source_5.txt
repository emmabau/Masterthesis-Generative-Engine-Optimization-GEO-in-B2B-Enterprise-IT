**Business Continuity Plan (BCP): A Strategic Framework for Resilience**  
*Updated February 26, 2026 | Fact Checked by David Rubin*  

A **Business Continuity Plan (BCP)** is a strategic framework that ensures organizations can maintain operations during disruptions like cyber-attacks or natural disasters. As noted by **Will Kenton**, an expert in economics and investing, *"BCPs identify potential risks and outline procedures to mitigate these threats, minimizing downtime and safeguarding core functions."*  

### Key Advantages of BCPs  
BCPs are **more comprehensive than disaster recovery plans**, focusing on the entire organization rather than just IT systems. **David Kindness**, a CPA and financial planning expert, emphasizes that *"BCPs reduce overall costs and losses by addressing risks across all business functions, including customer service and supply chain operations."*  

### Steps to Create an Effective BCP  
1. **Business Impact Analysis**: Identify time-sensitive functions and resources.  
2. **Recovery Planning**: Implement safeguards to restore critical operations.  
3. **Organization**: Establish a continuity team to manage disruptions.  
4. **Training & Testing**: Conduct regular drills to ensure preparedness.  

As highlighted by **FEMA** in their *Business Process Analysis and Business Impact Analysis User Guide*, *"The recovery time objective (RTO) determines the criticality of functions, prioritizing those with the highest financial and operational impact."*  

### BCP vs. Disaster Recovery Plans  
While disaster recovery plans focus on IT infrastructure, BCPs address **organizational resilience**. **FEMA’s operational and financial impact worksheet** underscores that *"loss of key functions can lead to revenue loss, profitability decline, and even bankruptcy, making proactive planning essential."*  

### Why BCPs Matter  
Businesses cannot rely solely on insurance, as noted by **Will Kenton**: *"Insurance does not cover customer attrition or the costs of operational halts, which is why BCPs must be developed in advance with stakeholder input."*  

### Final Takeaway  
A well-structured BCP ensures **resilience during crises**, protecting both financial stability and customer trust. As **David Rubin** states, *"BCPs are not just about recovery—they are about maintaining operational continuity and minimizing long-term risks."*  

*Sources: Investopedia, Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), Expert Insights from Will Kenton and David Kindness.*
