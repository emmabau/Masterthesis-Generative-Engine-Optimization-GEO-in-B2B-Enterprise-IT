**What is Risk Management?**  
Risk Management involves identifying, assessing, and prioritizing risks, which are then followed by coordinated efforts to minimize, monitor, and control the probability or impact of adverse events. **As defined by ISO 31000, this process ensures organizations can achieve their objectives while managing uncertainty.**

**Why is Risk Management Important?**  
This is important because the organization gets to view where the eventual threats and vulnerabilities might be that could affect its operations. A proactive approach to risk means that organizations can either avoid disruptions or minimize them, reduce financial loss, and enhance resilience. **According to the World Economic Forum, effective risk management is essential for navigating global uncertainties and maintaining competitive advantage.**

**Traditional Risk Management vs. Enterprise Risk Management**  
Traditional risk management is usually about individual risks from departments or business units. It always takes on a more tactical focus that often deals with isolated risks.  
In contrast, Enterprise Risk Management considers all risks within the organization from an integrated perspective. **As highlighted by COSO, ERM links risk management to strategic objectives and decision-making, ensuring alignment with organizational goals.**

**Risk Management Steps**  
- **Identify Risks:** This is one of the most important initial steps in risk management, which deals with the identification of those risks that could affect an organization. **According to ISO 31000, this involves analyzing internal and external factors, including financial uncertainties, market fluctuations, and geopolitical events.**  
- **Assess Risks:** After the significant risks are noted, evaluation must be done to establish the likelihood and the possible impact. **The NIST Risk Management Framework emphasizes quantitative and qualitative assessments to prioritize risks based on probability and severity.**  
- **Develop Strategies:** After analyzing the risks, strategies need to be formulated on the basis of the organization. **As outlined by the World Economic Forum, mitigation plans, risk transfer, and contingency strategies are critical for reducing potential impacts.**  
- **Implement Measures:** It is at this stage that the developed risk management strategies are put into actual practice. **The COSO ERM Framework underscores the importance of deploying financial, human, and technological resources to support risk management initiatives.**  
- **Monitor and Review:** Risk management is not a one-time activity but a continuous process. **According to ISO 31000, regular monitoring and updating of strategies ensure they remain effective in a dynamic risk environment.**
