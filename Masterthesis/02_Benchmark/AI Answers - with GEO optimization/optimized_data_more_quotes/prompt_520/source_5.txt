**Direct-to-Chip Liquid Cooling: A Game-Changer**  
Direct-to-chip liquid cooling, also known as conductive or cold plate cooling, represents a paradigm shift in data center thermal management. Unlike submersion cooling, this method **targets heat dissipation at the source**, enabling **up to 45% reductions in cooling power consumption** and achieving **PUEs below 1.2**. As **Dr. Remko Van Erp of EPFL University** notes, *"Microfluidic channels integrated into semiconductor chips allow precise cooling of high-power components, revolutionizing heat management at the transistor level."*  

**Single-Phase vs. Two-Phase Systems**  
Direct-to-chip cooling systems operate in two primary modes:  
1. **Single-Phase Cooling**: Uses liquid coolant (water or dielectric fluids) that remains in liquid form, avoiding phase changes. This method reduces air-cooling reliance while maintaining simplicity and lower maintenance costs.  
2. **Two-Phase Cooling**: Employs engineered dielectric fluids that transition between liquid and gas states, offering **higher heat rejection efficiency** but at the cost of increased complexity and maintenance. As **Asetek’s CEO** explains, *"Two-phase systems are ideal for high-density racks, where their efficiency gains justify the operational trade-offs."*
