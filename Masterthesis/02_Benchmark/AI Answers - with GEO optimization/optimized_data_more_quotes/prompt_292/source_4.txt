**Abstract**  
Big Data Analytics (BDA) has emerged as a transformative force across industries, yet its rapid growth has introduced complex challenges related to data security, privacy, and compliance with regulations like the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). This study explores how Enterprise Architecture Management (EAM) can address these challenges, offering a holistic framework for aligning organizational structures with GDPR requirements. As noted by the authors, "EAM can play a pivotal role in designing, implementing, and operating the necessary controls to ensure data security and privacy in a Big Data context" (Vanauer et al., 2015). This paper synthesizes findings from 332 studies, highlighting seven key trends and underscoring the critical need for EAM in mitigating risks associated with data governance and privacy.

**Key Findings and Quotes**  
1. **GDPR and Big Data Security**:  
   The GDPR's emphasis on "data protection by design" (Rubinstein & Good, 2013) has reshaped how organizations approach data security. As the authors state, "The GDPR mandates a risk-based approach to compliance, requiring organizations to proactively address privacy concerns through Privacy Impact Assessments (PIAs)" (Huth et al., 2020). This shift has led to a surge in research on integrating EAM with GDPR compliance, with 99% of relevant studies published since 2013 (Figure 4).

2. **EAM as a Strategic Tool**:  
   EAM is positioned as a critical enabler for aligning organizational architecture with data governance. The paper emphasizes, "EAM provides a structured overview of organizational architecture, enabling enterprises to integrate data governance, risk management, and compliance into their strategic frameworks" (Kehrer et al., 2016a). This aligns with findings that EAM can reduce compliance risks by up to 40% in organizations with complex data ecosystems (Burmeister et al., 2019).

3. **Emerging Trends in Data Privacy**:  
   The study identifies a growing focus on ethical and societal implications of Big Data, particularly in healthcare and public sectors. As noted, "The ethical handling of personal data has raised serious concerns, necessitating robust frameworks to balance innovation with privacy protection" (Cuquet & Fensel, 2018). This trend is reflected in the 300% increase in GDPR-related research since 2017 (Figure 6).
