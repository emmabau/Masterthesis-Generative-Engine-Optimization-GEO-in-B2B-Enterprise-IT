**Building the Future: Liquid Cooling Strategies for Modern Data Centers**  
*The Insider Blog / 5 min read*  

**Adoption Trends**  
"Only 19% of data centers currently use liquid cooling, but 36% plan to adopt it within the next 1–2 years," states the *AFCOM State of the Data Center Report 2025*. A third of operators (34%) report their current cooling systems are inadequate, while 21% are actively seeking better solutions.  

In the U.S., the shift is accelerating: *Bluefield Research* projects that **50% of data centers will use liquid cooling by 2030**, up from 28% in 2023. This growth is driven by rising power demands from AI workloads, which can push rack densities to **40 kW or more**—far exceeding traditional air-cooling limits.  

**Challenges of Retrofitting**  
"Retrofitting liquid cooling into existing facilities is highly complex," warns *AFCOM*. Immersion systems, for example, add significant weight to cooling baths, often requiring structural reinforcements. "New plumbing may need to pass through walls and floors designed to be watertight," adds the report, highlighting the logistical hurdles.  

Operators also face risks of downtime during upgrades and potential legal issues if changes affect leases or power agreements. These challenges explain why **47% of professionals cite integration complexity** as the top barrier to adoption—surpassing cost (41%) and lack of expertise (31%).  

**Solutions: Hybrid Approaches and Prefabricated Modules**  
"Transitioning to liquid cooling doesn’t have to be an all-or-nothing decision," notes *AFCOM*. Hybrid systems combining air and liquid cooling offer flexibility, with rear-door heat exchangers (RDHx) providing a less disruptive entry point.  

Prefabricated cooling modules are gaining traction: *AFCOM’s survey* reveals **83% of operators are interested in exploring these units**, which are easier to install and perceived as lower-risk. Space availability remains critical, as "additional room simplifies phased installations and allows new systems to coexist with existing infrastructure," per the report.  

**The Role of Tech Giants**  
"Big players like Google, Meta, and Microsoft are investing in liquid cooling to support AI workloads and reduce energy consumption," says *Datacenters.com*. These companies have pioneered immersion and direct-to-chip cooling at hyperscale, setting a precedent for smaller centers to follow.  

**Future Outlook**  
"Whether retrofitting or building from scratch, liquid cooling is the future," concludes *AFCOM*. Digital tools like flow metering and water quality monitoring enable proactive maintenance, while solutions like the **BlueEdge® suite**—which offers real-time insights into system performance—support sustainability goals and operational efficiency.
