The U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) Final Rule (28 C.F.R. Part 202), effective **April 8, 2025**, imposes strict restrictions on access to U.S. sensitive personal data and government-related data for collaborators or contractors in "countries of concern" or visitors from these countries. MIT researchers must comply with these regulations **beginning October 6, 2025**, as outlined in the DOJ rule. As emphasized by the DOJ, the rule aims to "block or limit transfers or accessing of certain government-related data and 'bulk' sensitive U.S. personal data to identified countries of concern on national security grounds" (DOJ, 2025).

**Key Countries of Concern**  
The DOJ’s countries of concern include:  
- China (including Hong Kong and Macau)  
- Russia  
- Iran  
- North Korea  
- Cuba  
- Venezuela  

**Critical Definitions**  
- **Sensitive personal data** includes health, financial, geolocation, biometric, and "human 'omic data" (genomic, epigenomic, proteomic, transcriptomic).  
- **Bulk thresholds** apply to data exceeding specific limits (e.g., ≥100 U.S. persons for genomic data, ≥10,000 for health data).  
- **Government-related data** includes location data tied to U.S. government facilities.  
- **Covered persons** are foreign entities or individuals designated by the DOJ or meeting specific criteria (e.g., entities 50% owned by countries of concern).  

**Prohibited and Restricted Transactions**  
- **Prohibited**: Data brokerage, vendor agreements, or employment agreements involving countries of concern or covered persons accessing bulk human 'omic data or biospecimens.  
- **Restricted**: Transactions under vendor, employment, or investment agreements with countries of concern, requiring security controls.  
- **Exempt**: Personal communications, U.S. government activities, medical research under regulatory frameworks, and certain financial/telecom services.  

**Compliance Actions for MIT Researchers**  
1. **Pause work** with collaborators/contractors from countries of concern (e.g., China, Russia, Iran) and contact **research-compliance-help@mit.edu** immediately.  
2. **Avoid sharing** bulk U.S. human 'omics data or biospecimens with countries of concern (prohibited under DOJ rules).  
3. **Review data use agreements** to include DOJ-compliant security and non-transfer clauses.  
4. **Audit compliance** annually, ensuring foreign vendors and non-MIT collaborators cannot access regulated data.  

**Critical Reminder**  
As stated by the DOJ, "access to certain data is restricted and in some cases prohibited for collaborators or contractors in countries of concern" (DOJ, 2025). Researchers must **email research-compliance-help@mit.edu** before sharing large datasets with foreign persons in countries of concern, as some cases may require security controls and audits.  

For further guidance, consult the DOJ’s full rule and MIT’s compliance protocols to ensure adherence to national security regulations.
