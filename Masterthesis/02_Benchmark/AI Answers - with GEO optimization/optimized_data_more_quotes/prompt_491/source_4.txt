Did you know that a recent IDG research study revealed 45 percent of IT decision makers do not understand the value of composable infrastructure? Even seasoned data center professionals are still grappling with its definition, evolution, and benefits. As Dave Fafel, WEI’s Chief Architect, explains in the *Real Tech with WEI* video: “Composable infrastructure, sometimes referred to as ‘infrastructure as code,’ is a software-defined solution that goes beyond simply converging or hyperconverging hardware, compute, and storage into a single integrated unit.” This paradigm shift virtualizes the entire IT infrastructure, treating physical compute, storage, and network devices as services managed via a single application. 

**What Is Composable Infrastructure?**  
Composable infrastructure eliminates the need to configure hardware for specific applications, allowing infrastructure to be managed by software commands. As *Infrastructure for Dummies* states, “The unified API increases productivity and control across the data center by integrating and automating infrastructure operations and applications. It provides a single interface to discover, search, inventory, configure, provision, update, and diagnose the composable infrastructure. A single line of code fully describes and can provision the infrastructure required for an application, eliminating time-consuming scripting of hundreds of calls to low-level tools and interfaces.”  

**Top 10 Business Advantages of Composable Infrastructure**  
1. **More Value/Less Cost**  
   Composable infrastructure reduces CapEx by leveraging fluid pools of dynamic resources and lowers OpEx through streamlined administration.  

2. **Simplicity**  
   A single infrastructure model across the enterprise reduces complexity, training costs, and technical silos.  

3. **Efficiency**  
   Continuous IT service delivery aligns workloads with business demands, enabling operational efficiency gains.  

4. **Software-Driven Tools for “Software Defined Everything”**  
   Developers programmatically control composable infrastructure via a RESTful API, abstracting hardware constraints and enabling rapid resource composition.  

5. **Agility**  
   Resource disaggregation allows flexible, “frictionless” scaling, eliminating siloed groups that impede operations.  

6. **Flexibility**  
   Composable infrastructure supports virtual machines, bare-metal, and containerized environments, enabling hybrid adoption strategies.  

7. **Business Acceleration**  
   Template-driven provisioning reduces infrastructure provisioning times, positioning IT as a strategic asset rather than a bottleneck.  

8. **Modernization**  
   Composable infrastructure aligns with Agile and DevOps methodologies, enabling instant adaptability for modern application workloads.  

9. **Automatic Integration**  
   New devices are instantly recognized and added to resource pools, enabling seamless hardware-software synergy.  

10. **Frictionless Operations**  
    Automated updates, diagnostics, and resource management ensure seamless operations without disrupting IT workflows.  

**Conclusion**  
Composable infrastructure represents a transformative step toward software-defined infrastructure, enabling enterprises to scale efficiently and meet evolving challenges. As IDC notes, “Companies must adopt solutions to support transitions to infrastructures that deliver new capacity for next-generation applications while maintaining mission-critical IT workloads. The benefit of composable is that it aggregates compute, storage, and fabric into shared pools of resources that can be allocated on demand to orchestrate and deliver resources to meet service demands.”
