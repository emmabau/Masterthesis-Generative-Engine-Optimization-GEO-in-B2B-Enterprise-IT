### **What Is a Budget?**  
A budget is a spending plan that uses your past month’s expenses to predict your future spending. It’s not just about cutting costs—it’s about creating a sustainable financial plan. Over time, budgeting becomes a habit, much like brushing your teeth.  

**Key Takeaways**  
- A budget tracks your spending and aligns it with your financial goals.  
- It ensures your savings stay on track for the future.  
- Budgeting helps you avoid overspending, build long-term goals, and manage risks.  

---

### **How to Make a Budget**  
1. **Track Your Spending**  
   Review your receipts and credit card statements for 2–3 months to understand your average monthly spending. Categorize expenses (e.g., groceries, gifts) and keep categories simple.  

2. **Separate Needs and Wants**  
   Divide expenses into **needs** (housing, transportation) and **wants** (entertainment, luxury items). Discretionary spending refers to funds left after covering essentials.  

3. **Set a Budget Goal**  
   Choose a budget framework like:  
   - **50/30/20**: 50% for needs, 30% for wants, 20% for savings/debt.  
   - **70/20/10**: 70% for essentials and discretionary spending, 20% for investments/savings, 10% for debts.  
   A common rule of thumb: Housing costs should not exceed 28% of your gross income.  

4. **Adjust Spending**  
   Compare your actual spending to your budget. If you overspend on wants, cut back on non-essential purchases. Ask: *Is this purchase worth it? Could I go without?*  

5. **Remind Yourself of Long-Term Goals**  
   Stay motivated by focusing on your financial goals—retirement, home purchases, or education. This helps you resist temptations to overspend.  

6. **Revise Your Budget Regularly**  
   A budget is a living document. Review it every 3–6 months to adjust categories, goals, or priorities. Ask: *Do I still need this category? Can I cut back?*

### **Why You Should Make a Budget**  
1. **Achieve Long-Term Goals**  
   A budget maps out your financial path. Whether you’re saving for a home or graduate school, it helps you allocate funds strategically.  

2. **Avoid Overspending**  
   Credit card debt rose to $7,321 per borrower in Q1 2025. A budget ensures you spend within your means and avoid debt traps.  

3. **Simplify Retirement Savings**  
   Automate contributions to retirement accounts (e.g., 401(k), IRA). For example, Trina deferred $1,958.33 monthly to max her 2025 401(k) contribution, ensuring consistent savings.  

4. **Build an Emergency Fund**  
   Aim for 3–6 months of living expenses in an emergency fund. Start small—$50 weekly—and use budgeting tools like YNAB to track progress.  

5. **Analyze Spending Habits**  
   A budget reveals patterns, like overspending on dining out. Use this insight to reallocate funds toward savings or investments.

### **The Bottom Line**  
A budget is a personal spending plan that balances income and expenses. It fosters financial stability, helps you save for emergencies, and keeps you on track for long-term goals. Sticking to a budget ensures your money works for you, not against you.
