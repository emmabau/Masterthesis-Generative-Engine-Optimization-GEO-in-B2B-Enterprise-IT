**Five Direct-Impact Benefits of Direct Liquid Cooling Data Centers**  
By Sanna Makinen  

Air cooling has long been the standard for data center thermal management, but rising rack power densities are pushing its limits. Verne Finland is pioneering a transformative shift by integrating direct liquid cooling (DLC) into its Pori data center, marking a critical step toward sustainable, high-performance computing. As AI and high-performance computing (HPC) demands surge, traditional air cooling systems struggle to keep pace with escalating heat generation. DLC offers a game-changing solution to enhance efficiency, reduce costs, and future-proof data center infrastructure.  

### **Why Liquid Cooling is Essential for Modern Data Centers**  
The exponential growth of AI workloads and complex computational tasks has created an urgent need for advanced cooling strategies. With the AI data center market projected to reach $50 billion by 2027, operators must adopt innovative cooling methods to meet rising power demands. Pairing DLC with air cooling can significantly improve power usage effectiveness (PUE), cut operational costs, and reduce energy consumption. Liquid cooling not only optimizes energy use but also positions data centers as leaders in environmental sustainability.  

### **How Direct Liquid Cooling Works**  
DLC employs cold plates to directly transfer heat from IT equipment to a liquid coolant, which is then removed via a heat exchanger. This method is far more efficient than air cooling, enabling precise temperature control and reducing reliance on energy-intensive airflow systems. Many data centers are now adopting DLC as a primary cooling solution, either as a supplement or full replacement for traditional air cooling.  

### **Five Key Benefits of Direct Liquid Cooling**  
1. **Energy Efficiency**  
   DLC’s superior heat dissipation capabilities are critical for managing high-density workloads. As AI demands grow, liquid cooling becomes essential to handle escalating rack power densities. Unlike air cooling, which struggles with thermal conductivity limitations, DLC ensures optimal cooling efficiency while minimizing excessive airflow requirements.  

2. **Improved Performance**  
   Modern CPUs and GPUs generate intense heat, making air cooling insufficient. DLC maintains optimal operating temperatures, preventing performance degradation from overheating. Verne Global’s implementation of Dell Technologies’ DLC3000 rack solution, capable of cooling up to 80kW+ per rack, exemplifies how DLC enhances compute efficiency.  

3. **Cost Savings**  
   DLC’s high heat transfer capacity reduces energy costs and long-term operational expenses. By extending hardware lifespan and minimizing maintenance, it offers a cost-effective solution for high-performance, process-intensive applications.  

4. **Environmental Sustainability**  
   DLC supports heat reuse initiatives, repurposing captured heat for district heating systems and reducing reliance on fossil fuels. Combined with renewable energy sources, DLC enables data centers to achieve exceptional sustainability, as seen in Verne’s Nordic data centers in Iceland and Finland.  

5. **Scalability and Future-Proofing**  
   DLC enables efficient data center expansion and supports AI readiness. By increasing rack density, it optimizes space usage while accommodating the most demanding AI and HPC workloads. This scalability ensures data centers remain adaptable to evolving technological needs.
