**Understanding NIS2 Requirements**  
The NIS2 Directive introduces stricter cybersecurity rules for EU organizations, applying to essential and important entities. Compliance is now a legal obligation, with penalties for non-compliance. Key areas include risk management, corporate accountability, incident reporting, and business continuity.  

**NIS2 Implementation Deadline**  
NIS2 was set to be transposed into national law by October 17, 2024, but several EU countries missed the deadline. While some nations, like Germany, are still finalizing legislation, organizations must act now. Enforcement is expected to begin once national laws are passed, with delays risking financial penalties, operational risks, and pressure from partners.  

**Who Must Comply with NIS2?**  
NIS2 applies to a broader range of industries than previous regulations. Key categories include:  
- **Essential Entities**: Energy, transport, financial services, healthcare, water supply, digital infrastructure, public administration, and space industry. These face stricter oversight and enforcement.  
- **Important Entities**: Food production, postal services, chemical industry, manufacturing, digital services, research institutions, and waste management. Compliance is mandatory but with slightly less regulatory scrutiny.  

**Key NIS2 Requirements**  
1. **Risk Management**: Implement robust security measures, including multi-factor authentication (MFA), data encryption, network security, and incident response planning.  
2. **Corporate Accountability**: Senior management must oversee cybersecurity strategies, undergo training, and take responsibility for failures.  
3. **Incident Reporting**: Report significant incidents within 24 hours, submit a full report within 72 hours, and provide a final remediation report within one month.  
4. **Business Continuity**: Develop plans for system recovery, crisis response teams, and secure backups to ensure operational resilience.

**Consequences of Non-Compliance**  
- **Fines**: Essential entities face up to €10 million or 2% of global revenue; important entities may be fined up to €7 million or 1.4% of revenue.  
- **Personal Liability**: Executives may face legal action, fines, or temporary bans from management roles.  
- **Operational Risks**: Cyberattacks, supply-chain breaches, and data leaks can lead to financial losses, reputational damage, and business disruption.  

**NIS2 vs. Other Regulations**  
- **GDPR**: Focuses on data protection, while NIS2 secures critical infrastructure.  
- **DORA**: Applies to financial services, aligning with NIS2 but with sector-specific rules.  
- **ISO 27001**: A voluntary framework covering 70% of NIS2 requirements but lacking mandatory incident reporting.
