Abstract
Big Data Analytics (BDA) has emerged as a transformative force across industries, offering significant value through advanced data processing capabilities. However, its rapid growth has introduced complex challenges related to data security, privacy, and compliance, particularly under the framework of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). This study explores how Enterprise Architecture Management (EAM) can serve as a strategic approach to address these challenges, ensuring alignment with GDPR requirements while fostering organizational coherence and value creation. Through a systematic mapping review (SMR) and thematic analysis, we identify key research trends, gaps, and opportunities for integrating EAM into Big Data contexts to enhance data governance, risk management, and privacy protection.

Introduction
The proliferation of Big Data has revolutionized business operations, enabling organizations to derive insights from vast, complex datasets. Yet, this growth has also heightened concerns about data security and privacy, especially with the implementation of GDPR in 2018. GDPR mandates stringent measures for data protection, requiring organizations to embed privacy by design and ensure transparency in data processing. This paper investigates the intersection of BDA, GDPR compliance, and EAM, highlighting how EAM can provide a holistic framework for managing data risks, governance, and regulatory alignment. By analyzing existing research, we aim to uncover how EAM practices can mitigate security vulnerabilities, enhance data governance, and support GDPR compliance in Big Data environments.

Background
Big Data Analytics involves processing diverse, high-volume datasets to uncover patterns and insights, driving innovation across sectors like healthcare, finance, and marketing. However, the collection and use of personal data raise significant privacy concerns, particularly under GDPR, which enforces strict rules on data handling, consent, and breach notification. The GDPR's emphasis on data protection by design and privacy impact assessments (PIAs) underscores the need for integrated approaches to data governance. Enterprise Architecture Management (EAM) offers a structured method to align organizational resources with strategic goals, making it a promising candidate for addressing the multifaceted challenges of Big Data compliance.

Methodology
This study employs a Systematic Mapping Review (SMR) to analyze existing literature on Big Data security, privacy, and GDPR compliance. The SMR process includes defining mapping research questions (MRQs), conducting targeted searches across academic databases, screening relevant studies, and classifying findings using thematic analysis. The research focuses on studies that explore EAM's role in addressing GDPR requirements, emphasizing data governance, risk management, and privacy protection. The results are synthesized to identify trends, gaps, and opportunities for further research in this domain.

Results
The analysis reveals that while EAM has been explored in the context of data governance, its application to GDPR compliance in Big Data environments remains underdeveloped. Key findings include: (1) A growing body of research on data security and privacy challenges in Big Data, with increasing focus on GDPR since 2017; (2) Limited exploration of EAM as a comprehensive solution for GDPR compliance, despite its potential to integrate technical, organizational, and legal aspects; (3) Emerging trends such as ethical considerations in Big Data, data minimization, and privacy-enhancing technologies (PETs) as critical areas for future research. These insights highlight the need for more systematic integration of EAM into Big Data strategies to meet regulatory and operational demands.

Discussion
The study underscores the importance of aligning EAM practices with GDPR requirements to ensure robust data governance and risk management. While EAM provides a structured approach to organizational alignment, its application to Big Data compliance requires further refinement to address specific challenges like data minimization, transparency, and accountability. The findings suggest that EAM can play a pivotal role in embedding privacy by design principles, fostering cross-functional collaboration, and enabling proactive risk mitigation. However, gaps in existing research highlight the need for more empirical studies on EAM's effectiveness in real-world Big Data scenarios, particularly in diverse organizational contexts.

Conclusion
This study contributes to the growing discourse on leveraging EAM to address the complexities of Big Data security and GDPR compliance. While EAM offers a strategic framework for managing data risks and governance, its full potential in this context remains underexplored. Future research should focus on developing scalable EAM models tailored to Big Data environments, integrating ethical and regulatory considerations, and evaluating their impact on organizational compliance and performance. By bridging the gap between technical innovation and regulatory compliance, EAM can help organizations navigate the evolving landscape of Big Data while safeguarding privacy and data protection.
