Abstract
This study explores the intersection of digital transformation (DT) and cybersecurity, emphasizing the critical role of cybersecurity in ensuring business resilience during DT implementation. DT involves integrating IT solutions into organizational processes, which can significantly alter various aspects of an organization. Emerging technologies like AI, big data analytics, blockchain, and cloud computing drive global DT adoption but also introduce new cybersecurity risks. This review highlights the importance of understanding cybersecurity threats during DT to prevent disruptions from malicious activities or unauthorized access. Cybersecurity is vital for protecting digital assets, and our systematic literature review using PRISMA methodology reveals that while DT enhances efficiency, it also presents challenges like data breaches and cyberattacks. We propose a staged cybersecurity readiness framework to help organizations prepare for DT. Keywords: digital transformation, cybersecurity, information technology

1. Introduction
Digital transformation refers to adopting digital solutions in business processes, leading to significant organizational changes. This shift impacts user experience, operations, markets, and customer relationships. The accelerated technology adoption during the COVID-19 pandemic highlighted both opportunities and challenges. Emerging technologies like AI, big data, blockchain, and cloud computing are critical enablers of DT. While these technologies offer benefits, they also create cybersecurity risks. Organizations must prioritize cybersecurity to secure their DT tools and ensure business continuity. Cybersecurity measures like encryption, authentication, and employee awareness are essential to protect against cyber threats. Cyberattacks have escalated, necessitating comprehensive understanding and mitigation strategies to safeguard sensitive information and business processes.

Cybersecurity costs and crimes are rising globally. Haislip et al. noted that cybersecurity breach costs are underestimated, with impacts extending beyond targeted organizations. Garg identified seven benefits of cybersecurity investment, including protecting intellectual property and minimizing customer turnover. Lee's risk management framework emphasizes continuous improvement in cybersecurity practices. The NIST Cybersecurity Framework is widely used, but its lack of cost-benefit analysis is addressed by the Gordon–Loeb model. Krutilla et al. enhanced this model by incorporating depreciation costs. Simon and Omar highlighted the need to consider both coordinated and uncoordinated attacks in cybersecurity investments. Uddin et al. found that cybersecurity weaknesses impact organizational growth, particularly in banking. Curti et al. noted rising cyberattacks in the governmental sector, leading to increased operational costs.

This paper presents a systematic literature review (2019–2023) on DT's impact on the business sector and cybersecurity implications. We propose a cybersecurity readiness framework for organizations pursuing DT. The findings will help practitioners and researchers understand current trends and guide future research. The paper is organized into sections detailing methodology, results, discussion, and conclusion.

4. Discussion
Advanced technologies like IoT, blockchain, and 5G enable secure business processes. Machine learning helps identify malicious traffic. However, these technologies introduce new risks requiring proactive security measures. Governments play a key role in developing cybersecurity policies. Human factors like performance degradation are critical in cybersecurity. Our review proposes a four-level cybersecurity readiness framework for DT implementation.

5. Conclusions
This review underscores cybersecurity's critical role in DT. DT transforms business processes but introduces new risks requiring robust cybersecurity measures. The pandemic highlighted vulnerabilities in rapid digitalization. Organizations must prioritize cybersecurity to ensure successful DT transitions. Implementing encryption, cyber insurance, and employee training can mitigate risks. Future research should focus on comprehensive cybersecurity knowledge throughout DT processes to address emerging threats effectively.
