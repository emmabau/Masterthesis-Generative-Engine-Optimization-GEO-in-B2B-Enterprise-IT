Hybrid work offers new perks for individual workers, but what about team productivity? The future of team productivity for most remote-capable workers depends on learning to collaborate more effectively in a hybrid workplace. When teams are physically distanced and on different schedules, teamwork is harder to coordinate, and productive partnerships are more difficult to cultivate.  

A recent Gallup study of 2,877 U.S. hybrid workers highlights key areas for improvement in hybrid team practices. Employers can boost productivity through four strategic steps:  

**1. Plan for Success**  
Nearly half (48%) of hybrid workers are on teams without a formal plan for hybrid collaboration. Clear policies defining remote work expectations are essential, but structured planning is critical for fostering teamwork and organizational culture. As one engaged manager noted, "Effective collaboration requires intentional planning and structured activities, as organic interactions alone aren't sufficient."  

Teams with a hybrid collaboration plan experience significant benefits:  
- Employees are **2.2 times more likely** to say their organization’s hybrid policy has a strong positive impact on team collaboration  
- **66% more likely** to be engaged at work  
- **29% less likely** to experience burnout  

However, individual contributors are more likely to lack such plans (51%) compared to managers (46%) and leaders (39%), indicating gaps in hybrid work arrangements. Hybrid team charters—defining mission, goals, and workflows—should be central to these plans.  

**2. Coordinate Hybrid Collaboration**  
Teams with plans often adopt best practices like:  
- **Regular check-in meetings** (67% of hybrid workers)  
- Guidelines for availability (62%) and unavailability (50%)  
- On-site attendance guidelines (44% and 34%)  

These practices significantly improve engagement. Notably, prioritizing high-value on-site activities (e.g., collaboration, feedback) is a key opportunity for growth, as it directly impacts engagement and productivity.  

**3. Reflect and Adapt**  
Plans and practices only yield results when integrated into routine reflection. Teams that regularly discuss hybrid work improvements (weekly/monthly) see:  
- **84% better collaboration**  
- **50% higher engagement**  

In contrast, teams with annual or less frequent discussions often focus on personal preferences rather than team effectiveness. Without structured reflection, hybrid teams stagnate.  

**4. Upskill**  
Only 21% of hybrid workers have received training for effective hybrid collaboration, and 28% of managers lack hybrid leadership training. Upskilling is critical:  
- Training helps teams adapt to hybrid challenges  
- Effective managers boost engagement **four times more** than remote work alone  

Gallup’s research underscores that successful hybrid teams require:  
- Training for both members and managers  
- Clear team charters beyond corporate policies  
- Regular on-site collaboration for high-value tasks  
- Ongoing feedback loops to refine practices  

Ultimately, leaders must drive hybrid success. Without intentional guidance, the productivity of tomorrow’s hybrid workforce remains at risk. Equip your teams with the tools, training, and structure they need to thrive in a hybrid environment.
