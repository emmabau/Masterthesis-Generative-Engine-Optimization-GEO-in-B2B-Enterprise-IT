Integrating high-performance computing (HPC), machine learning (ML), and advanced data management workflows represents a transformative approach to materials science. This perspective article explores how these technologies converge to enhance the accessibility and efficiency of multiscale simulations, particularly in the development of nanomaterials. By leveraging computational modeling, researchers can predict material properties, explore design spaces, and accelerate the discovery of novel materials with tailored functionalities. The integration of digital twins, knowledge engineering, and deployment technologies for HPC infrastructures is central to this paradigm shift, enabling a fully data-centric approach to materials research.

Digital technologies have revolutionized materials science by enabling unprecedented advancements in design, characterization, and optimization. Computational modeling and simulation provide insights across atomic to macroscopic scales, while data-driven approaches like ML and AI extract patterns from vast datasets to uncover hidden relationships between composition, structure, and properties. High-throughput screening accelerates material discovery, and advanced imaging techniques coupled with computational analysis enable detailed microstructural analysis. Real-time monitoring of synthesis processes improves reproducibility, while integrated data management workflows streamline collaboration and data analysis.

A critical focus is the development of nanomaterials, where the interplay between structure, processing, and properties is complex. Advances in HPC hardware and software, including consumer-grade GPUs and cloud computing, have democratized access to computational resources. Tools like Quantum ESPRESSO, LAMMPS, and GROMACS enable complex simulations, while virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) enhance visualization and design capabilities. These technologies collectively address challenges in data integration, interoperability, and automation, fostering innovation in materials development.

The paper introduces MAMBO, an ontology for molecular materials, which provides a structured framework for representing materials data. This semantic approach enables interoperability across tools and platforms, supporting the creation of digital twins. Case studies demonstrate how MAMBO facilitates the integration of simulation workflows, experimental data, and computational models. Tools like Jupyter Notebooks, KNIME, and Galaxy enable reproducible research, while containerization and orchestration technologies like Docker and Kubernetes streamline HPC workflows.

The integration of multiscale physical modeling with data-driven approaches is pivotal for predictive materials design. Semantic technologies and standardized ontologies ensure data consistency, while cloud platforms provide scalable infrastructure for collaborative research. The paper concludes with a vision for future materials science, emphasizing the role of digital twins, AI-driven workflows, and open-source tools in accelerating innovation. This work underscores the importance of interdisciplinary collaboration and robust data management in advancing materials research through digital transformation.
