Key Red Flags to Watch For:

1. **No Customized Quoting**: 
   - Warning signs: Proposals arrive without understanding your business needs, growth plans, compliance requirements, or risk tolerance. 
   - Good providers spend 30-60 minutes understanding your business before proposing solutions. They ask critical questions like, "What happens if your site goes down at 2pm on Tuesday?" to determine your support requirements.

2. **Vague Promises vs. Specific Services**:
   - Example: "Comprehensive security hardening" vs. "We’ll implement fail2ban, configure UFW firewall, disable root SSH access, set up automated security patches, and conduct quarterly vulnerability scans."
   - Ask providers to explain their services in plain language. A provider who can't articulate this may lack depth or be hiding critical details.

3. **"Unlimited Support" at Low Prices**:
   - Industry data shows genuine managed services typically cost €150-€400 per user/month. 
   - €100-300/month: Monitoring-only (e.g., automated alerts without full support).
   - €500-1,000/month: Shared attention with slower response times.
   - €1,500-3,000/month: Real partnership with monthly reviews and proactive recommendations.
   - €5,000+/month: Dedicated team for complex infrastructure.

4. **No Clear "What’s NOT Included"**:
   - Honest proposals explicitly state exclusions (e.g., application debugging, feature development) and extra costs (e.g., compliance audits, emergency weekend work). Vague "support" packages risk hidden limitations.

5. **Guaranteed Uptime Without Audit**:
   - Providers can't guarantee uptime on systems they don’t control. A realistic approach is: "After auditing your setup, we’ll recommend changes to achieve 99.9%+ uptime."

Questions That Cut Through the Noise:
1. "Walk me through what happens if my site goes down at 2am on Saturday?"
2. "How many other clients do you currently support?"
3. "Can you provide a reference from a company similar to mine?"
4. "What’s NOT included in this price that catches clients off guard?"
5. "What’s the most common infrastructure mistake you see companies my size making?"
