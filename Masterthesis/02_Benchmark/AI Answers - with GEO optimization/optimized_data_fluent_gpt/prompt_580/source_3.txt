Next generation of exascale computing experts are being shaped through immersive learning experiences at Argonne National Laboratory’s Argonne Training Program on Extreme-Scale Computing (ATPESC). This annual program, hosted by the U.S. Department of Energy’s Argonne Leadership Computing Facility (ALCF), equips early-career scientists with critical skills to harness the power of supercomputing for computational science and engineering. 

Participants, including doctoral students, postdoctoral researchers, and industry professionals, engage in hands-on tutorials and lectures covering hardware, software, AI, machine learning, and data-intensive computing. The program’s curriculum is designed to bridge knowledge gaps, offering practical insights into the complexities of high-performance computing (HPC) and its applications in fields like physics, fluid dynamics, and renewable energy. 

Testimonials highlight the program’s transformative impact: “The atmosphere was an intellectual paradise—talented students, great speakers, and a well-oiled machine of learning,” noted Lev Gorenstein, a solutions architect at Globus. Attendees praised the balance of theoretical instruction and real-world problem-solving, with many citing the networking opportunities and mentorship as key takeaways. 

ATPESC’s interdisciplinary approach fosters collaboration across disciplines, with participants exploring topics from computational fluid dynamics to quantum computing. The program’s adaptability ensures it stays relevant, with plans to expand AI-focused content as the field evolves. 

Supported by the DOE’s Office of Science, ATPESC has trained over 900 researchers since 2013, preparing them to drive innovation in scientific discovery. As John Holmen, an HPC engineer at Oak Ridge National Laboratory, noted: “Returning as a presenter was rewarding—seeing how foundational this experience is for attendees.” 

By combining expert-led instruction with access to cutting-edge facilities, ATPESC remains a cornerstone in cultivating the next generation of HPC leaders, bridging academia, industry, and national laboratories in advancing computational science.
