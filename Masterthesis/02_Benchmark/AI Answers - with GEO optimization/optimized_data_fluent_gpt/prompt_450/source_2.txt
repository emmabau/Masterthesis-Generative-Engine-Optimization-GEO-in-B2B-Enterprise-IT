jj281 raised an important question about potential contradictions in decompression settings. Let's break this down:  

**Decompression Before Storage**  
The recommendation to decompress backup data blocks before storage is intentional. While it may seem counterintuitive to add CPU overhead on the Gateway and Proxy, the benefits far outweigh the costs. Modern compression algorithms like Veeam's "Optimal" setting (LZ4) are designed for high throughput and low CPU usage. A single decompression thread can handle GB/s of data, making this a negligible cost in most environments.  

**Why Compression Matters**  
In environments with 1GbE networks, compression can effectively multiply inbound bandwidth by 2-3x. This is critical because network bandwidth often becomes the bottleneck, not CPU. The overhead of transmitting uncompressed data includes 2x more data, 2x more CPU interrupts, and increased memory copying—all of which consume resources. For most deployments, the bandwidth savings justify the CPU trade-off, especially when CPU capacity is available.  

**DDBoost and Deduplication**  
Inline deduplication (via DDBoost) operates independently of Veeam's dedupe engine. If Veeam dedupe is enabled, it pre-processes data to eliminate exact duplicates before sending them to DDBoost. This reduces the workload on DDBoost and minimizes bandwidth usage. For example:  
- **Without Vee, DDBoost** analyzes all blocks (e.g., Block3 in the example) and processes them.  
- **With Vee dedupe**, Block3 is already identified as a duplicate of Block1 and never reaches DDBoost.  

While Veeam dedupe is CPU-light, disabling it simplifies workflows and avoids unnecessary metadata generation. This can slightly reduce DDBoost's CPU and bandwidth usage during job startup, though the impact is typically minor.  

**Key Takeaway**  
Compression and deduplication are powerful tools for optimizing backups, but their value depends on your environment. If CPU is constrained, disabling compression/dedupe may help. However, in most cases, the bandwidth savings and efficiency gains make these features worth retaining. Always test and tailor settings to your specific infrastructure.
