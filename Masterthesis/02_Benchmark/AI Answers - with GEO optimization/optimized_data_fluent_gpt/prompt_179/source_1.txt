What does business continuity look like when millions of dollars — or lives — are on the line?  
This article explores 7 real-world business continuity examples, showcasing how organizations recover from outages, cyberattacks, and operational disruptions.  

**Key Takeaways from Business Continuity Examples**  
- **Recovery Timelines**: Ranged from 6 hours to 3+ months, depending on backup architecture and testing discipline.  
- **Financial Impact**: Ranged from $17M to $100M+ when continuity planning was weak or reactive.

**What Most People Mean by “Business Continuity Plan Examples”**  
Most searches for business continuity plan examples aren’t just about stories—they’re about actionable frameworks. Here’s a simple structure to follow:  

| **Section**               | **What to Define**                                                                 |  
|---------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|  
| Critical Systems & Processes | Identify systems, applications, and workflows your business depends on.           |  
| Recovery Time Objective (RTO) | Define how quickly each system must be restored after a disruption.               |  
| Recovery Point Objective (RPO) | Determine how much data loss your business can tolerate.                          |

**Business Continuity Plan Examples & Failures**  
1. **CrowdStrike Global IT Outage (July 2024)**  
   - **Category**: SaaS / Software Supply Chain Failure  
   - **Event**: A faulty update caused 8.5M Windows devices to crash, disrupting 750+ U.S. hospitals.  
   - **Impact**: Delta Air Lines canceled 7,000+ flights, facing a $500M impact due to legacy system dependencies. Competitors like American Airlines recovered faster due to robust failover systems.  
   - **Lesson**: Business continuity requires not just IT recovery but operational resilience (e.g., crew scheduling systems).  

2. **Change Healthcare Ransomware Attack (February 2024)**  
   - **Category**: Ransomware  
   - **Event**: A ransomware attack disrupted 15B healthcare transactions, freezing billing and prescriptions for weeks.  
   - **Impact**: Providers without manual cash-flow reserves or alternative clearinghouses faced near-insolvency.  
   - **Lesson**: Plan for “paper downtime” procedures to mitigate extended outages.  

3. **AT&T Nationwide Cellular Outage (February 2024)**  
   - **Category**: Infrastructure Failure  
   - **Event**: A configuration error disrupted 220M customers, including 9-1-1 emergency systems.  
   - **Impact**: Users with dual-SIM devices or failover routers maintained connectivity, while others faced total downtime.  
   - **Lesson**: Diversify carrier dependencies for critical operations.  

4. **TSMC Earthquake Response (April 2024)**  
   - **Category**: Natural Disaster (Success Story)  
   - **Event**: A 7.4-magnitude earthquake struck Taiwan, home to the world’s largest semiconductor manufacturer.  
   - **Impact**: TSMC resumed 70% of chip production within 10 hours due to seismic infrastructure and regular drills.  
   - **Lesson**: Physical continuity planning (e.g., seismic dampers, evacuation drills) is critical in high-risk zones.  

5. **CDK Global Cyberattack (June 2024)**  
   - **Category**: Supply Chain Ransomware  
   - **Event**: A ransomware attack disrupted 15,000+ car dealerships, halting access to customer records and contracts.  
   - **Impact**: Dealerships reverted to “pen and paper” methods to continue sales.  
   - **Lesson**: Analog contingency plans are vital for high-tech failures.  

6. **Jaguar Land Rover “Digital Siege” (September 2025)**  
   - **Category**: Ransomware / Manufacturing Supply Chain  
   - **Event**: A ransomware attack bridged corporate IT and OT networks, crippling factory operations.  
   - **Impact**: Production halted for 4 weeks, costing £200M, and triggered supplier shutdowns.  
   - **Lesson**: Network segmentation and air-gapped backups are essential to prevent cascading failures.  

7. **MGM Resorts Cyberattack (September 2023)**  
   - **Category**: Cybersecurity / Operational Breakdown  
   - **Event**: A ransomware attack crippled MGM’s systems, causing $100M in losses.  
   - **Impact**: Competitor Caesars paid a $15M ransom to avoid operational downtime.  
   - **Lesson**: Balance ransom costs vs. downtime and reputational damage.

**Common Business Continuity Failures**  
- **No formal BCP**: Forces real-time decisions during crises.  
- **No risk assessment**: Fails to prioritize protective investments.  
- **No BIA**: Lacks quantification of downtime impacts (revenue, reputation, etc.).  
- **Overreliance on single systems**: Single points of failure (e.g., SaaS, WAN) cause cascading disruptions.  
- **Untested backups**: Failures during recovery due to unverified restore procedures.  
- **Undefined ownership**: Slows response times and creates reactive recovery.

**Sample Business Continuity Plan Outline**  
- **Objective**: Define key goals for specific systems/business units.  
- **Contact Info**: Include roles for continuity planning and disaster recovery.  
- **Risk Assessment**: Identify disaster scenarios and their likelihood.  
- **Business Impact Analysis**: Quantify financial and operational impacts.  
- **Preventative Measures**: Outline procedures to minimize disaster risks.  
- **Disaster Response Plan**: Document immediate protocols for disruptions.  
- **Continuity Systems**: Specify tools and procedures for maintaining operations.  
- **Backup Locations**: Identify secondary resources (e.g., backup servers, offices).  
- **Communication Plan**: Define how to distribute information during outages.  
- **Testing Schedule**: Plan regular tests for recovery procedures.  
- **Gaps & Recommendations**: Address limitations in current planning.  
- **Review Schedule**: Update the plan annually or after major changes.  

**ACTION PLAN: Business Continuity Implementation Checklist**  
- Define critical systems and set RTO/RPO targets.  
- Assign owners for decisions, communication, and recovery tasks.  
- Run tabletop tests for ransomware, SaaS outages, and network failures.  
- Document runbooks to avoid improvisation during outages.  
- Review and update the plan annually or after major tech/vendor changes.  

**Frequently Asked Questions**  
- **What is a business continuity plan example?**  
  A plan might include ransomware response procedures, backup restoration, and remote work protocols. It typically includes risk assessments, impact analyses, communication strategies, and recovery steps.  

- **What are the key components of a business continuity plan?**  
  Core components include risk assessment, business impact analysis, RTO/RPO definitions, communication protocols, disaster recovery strategies, and regular testing.  

- **What is the difference between business continuity and disaster recovery?**  
  Business continuity focuses on maintaining operations during disruptions, while disaster recovery focuses on restoring IT systems and data. Disaster recovery is part of a broader continuity strategy.  

- **What is a real-life example of business continuity?**  
  During the COVID-19 pandemic, businesses with tested continuity plans shifted to remote work, maintaining services despite major disruptions.  

- **How long does it typically take to recover from a disruption?**  
  Recovery time varies, but organizations with mature continuity strategies often restore critical operations within 24–72 hours.
