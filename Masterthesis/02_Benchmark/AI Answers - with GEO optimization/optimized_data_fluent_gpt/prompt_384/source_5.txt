Business Continuity (BC) is a critical strategy that ensures organizations can maintain essential operations during and after disruptions. In today’s digital landscape, where cyberattacks, natural disasters, and supply chain failures pose significant risks, BC has evolved from a best practice to a regulatory necessity. This article explores the core principles of BC, its alignment with key regulations like DORA and NIS 2, and how organizations can build resilience through proactive planning.

**What is Business Continuity?**  
Business Continuity encompasses the planning, preparation, and execution of strategies to minimize downtime, protect brand reputation, and safeguard revenue streams during disruptions. Whether caused by cyber threats, natural disasters, or operational failures, a robust Business Continuity Plan (BCP) ensures seamless service delivery and customer trust. Key benefits include:  
- **Reduced Downtime:** Swift recovery from disruptions minimizes financial losses.  
- **Enhanced Customer Satisfaction:** Consistent service delivery maintains brand loyalty.  
- **Regulatory Compliance:** DORA and NIS 2 mandate operational resilience, making BC a legal requirement for financial institutions and critical infrastructure sectors.  
- **Preparedness:** Anticipating risks and testing response protocols ensures readiness for any scenario.  

**DORA and NIS 2: Regulatory Drivers for BC**  
1. **DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act):**  
   - Targets financial entities, requiring them to identify, mitigate, and monitor ICT risks.  
   - Mandates regular testing of disaster recovery and BC plans, third-party compliance, and incident reporting.  
   - Emphasizes the integration of BC into risk management frameworks to combat cyber threats.  

2. **NIS 2 (Network and Information Security Directive):**  
   - Expands cybersecurity requirements to sectors like energy, transport, health, and digital infrastructure.  
   - Requires organizations to report significant incidents, secure supply chains, and ensure third-party compliance.  
   - Positions BC as a cornerstone of operational resilience, particularly for critical services.
