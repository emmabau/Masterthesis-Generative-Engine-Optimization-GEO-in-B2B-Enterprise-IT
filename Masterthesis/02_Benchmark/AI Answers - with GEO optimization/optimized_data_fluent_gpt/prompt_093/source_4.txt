we  
A versatile pronoun denoting the first-person plural, "we" serves multiple grammatical and contextual roles across languages and cultures.  

**Core Definitions**  
- **Nominative/Subject Pronoun**: "We" refers to the speaker(s) and/or those included in the discourse (inclusive "we"). Example: *"We are here to arrest you."*  
- **Objective Pronoun**: "Us" functions as the object of verbs or prepositions. Example: *"They saw us."*  
- **Possessive Pronoun**: "Our" indicates ownership. Example: *"This is our house."*  
- **Reflexive Pronoun**: "Ourselves" refers back to the subject. Example: *"We hurt ourselves."*  

**Key Contexts**  
1. **Inclusive "We"**: Includes the listener. Example: *"We need to talk, you and me."*  
2. **Exclusive "We"**: Excludes the listener. Example: *"We are here to arrest you."*  
3. **Editorial/Institutional "We"**: Represents an organization or authority. Example: *"We will state our reasons for publishing the name."*  
4. **Royal "We"**: Used by monarchs. Example: *"We are Queen of these Isles."*  
5. **Colloquial "We"**: Informal, often patronizing. Example: *"How are we feeling today?"*  

**Etymological Roots**  
- Traces back to Proto-Indo-European *wéy*, evolving through Proto-Germanic *wīz* and Old English *wē*.  
- Cognates include Scots *wee*, Dutch *wij*, German *wir*, and Swedish *vi*.
