Quick Definition: IoT security refers to the technologies and processes businesses use to protect their connected devices from cyber threats. Every internet-enabled device is vulnerable to hacking, which can lead to data breaches, malware distribution, or even physical harm. As IoT adoption grows across industries, securing these devices has become a shared responsibility between manufacturers and end users. 

Key IoT Security Risks:
1. **Weak Authentication**: Default passwords are often weak or hardcoded, making devices easy targets. Hackers can exploit these to access networks or join botnets. Manufacturers should enforce strong default passwords and multi-factor authentication.
2. **Low Processing Power**: Many IoT devices lack the computational power to run advanced security features like firewalls or encryption, leaving them exposed to attacks.
3. **Legacy Assets**: Older devices not designed for cloud connectivity may lack modern security protocols, making them risky to integrate into IoT ecosystems.
4. **Shared Network Access**: IoT devices often share networks with other devices, creating vulnerabilities. A compromised device can act as a gateway to sensitive data or other connected systems.
5. **Inconsistent Security Standards**: The absence of universal IoT security guidelines complicates secure device design and machine-to-machine communication.
6. **Lack of Encryption**: Unencrypted data transmissions expose credentials and sensitive information to interception.
7. **Missing Firmware Updates**: Undetected software flaws can create security gaps, requiring remote or physical updates to address vulnerabilities.
8. **Gaps Between Networks and Cloud**: Data traveling between IoT devices and cloud systems often passes through public networks, increasing interception risks.
9. **Limited Device Management**: Businesses struggle to monitor or isolate compromised devices, risking data breaches and unauthorized access.
10. **Physical Vulnerabilities**: Devices in public spaces are susceptible to tampering, such as SIM card theft or direct hardware attacks.

Notable IoT Security Breaches:
- **Mirai Botnet (2016)**: Hackers exploited unsecured IoT devices to launch a massive DDoS attack, disrupting major websites like Netflix and Twitter. The botnet used 145,607 devices, highlighting the scale of IoT vulnerabilities.
- **Target Credit Card Breach (2013)**: Attackers accessed Target’s network through a third-party HVAC vendor’s IoT sensors, stealing millions of credit card details.
- **St. Jude Medical Pacemakers (2017)**: Researchers demonstrated how pacemakers could be hacked to alter heart rates, underscoring the life-threatening risks of insecure medical devices.
- **Jeep Cherokee Hack (2015)**: Cybersecurity experts remotely controlled a Jeep’s engine, brakes, and steering, exposing the risks of connected vehicles.

IoT Security Solutions:
- **Physical Security**: Use tamper-resistant components like eSIMs (soldered to circuit boards) to protect critical data and reduce physical tampering risks.
- **Remote Access Security**: Implement protocols to lock SIM functionality to specific devices and remotely disable connections during breaches.
- **Private Networks**: Deploy secure private networks (e.g., Virtual Private Networks) to prevent data from traversing public internet infrastructure.
- **Abnormality Detection**: Monitor network activity in real time to identify and respond to threats, such as unauthorized access or data leaks.
- **Encrypted Data Transfer**: Use protocols like TLS and X.509 certificates to secure data transmissions between devices and servers.
- **Network-Based Firewalls**: Protect data at the network level, blocking malicious traffic before it reaches devices.
- **Limited Connectivity Profiles**: Restrict device access to only essential functions (e.g., no unnecessary SMS or voice capabilities) to minimize attack surfaces.

Secure IoT Deployment:
IoT security requires a combination of robust technologies and best practices. Manufacturers must embed security at the design stage, while users must maintain vigilance in updating firmware and managing device access.
