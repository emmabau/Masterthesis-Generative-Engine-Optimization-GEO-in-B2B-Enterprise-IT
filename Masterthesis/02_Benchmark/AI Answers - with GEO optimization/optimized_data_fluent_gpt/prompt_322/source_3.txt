**How to Create a Business Continuity Plan: A Practical Guide**  
A business continuity plan (BCP) is a structured framework to maintain essential operations during disruptions and recover them systematically. This guide focuses on actionable steps, not just policy exercises, to help organizations build a plan that works in real-world scenarios.  

**What a Business Continuity Plan Should Do**  
A strong BCP should:  
1. **Identify critical functions**: Determine which operations are most vital and the consequences of their disruption.  
2. **Define response and recovery strategies**: Outline operational workarounds, technology recovery priorities, communication protocols, and dependencies.  
3. **Ensure usability**: Keep the plan tested, maintained, and updated to remain credible and actionable over time.  

**Key Steps to Build an Effective Plan**  
1. **Start with Risk Assessment and Business Impact Analysis (BIA)**  
   - Identify hazards, vulnerabilities, and scenarios.  
   - Determine critical functions, dependencies, and acceptable downtime thresholds.  
   - Tools like BCMMetrics help standardize data collection, structure insights, and align teams on priorities.  

2. **Develop Practical Business Continuity Strategies**  
   Focus on core areas:  
   - **Technology**: Backups, restoration sequencing, and recovery methods.  
   - **Workforce**: Alternate work arrangements and role coverage.  
   - **Site/Facility**: Alternate locations and access disruption plans.  
   - **Suppliers**: Vendor and logistics dependencies.  
   - **Communication**: Clear escalation paths and stakeholder updates.  
   BCMMetrics supports strategy development by aligning details with BIA findings and streamlining updates.  

3. **Define Roles, Governance, and Communication**  
   - Assign ownership: Who maintains the plan, approves changes, and coordinates responses?  
   - Establish review triggers, testing schedules, and evidence retention protocols.  
   - A clear ownership model ensures accountability and audit readiness.  

4. **Test, Maintain, and Improve the Plan**  
   - Regularly test assumptions and update the plan as people, systems, or priorities change.  
   - Use incidents and drills to refine strategies.  
   - Maintain visibility in the maintenance process to sustain credibility.
