Typical disaster planning often focuses on short-term downtime, such as a few days to weeks. However, the 2020 pandemic highlighted the critical need for plans enabling remote work over extended periods to maintain business continuity. Re-evaluate these key aspects of your continuity plan to future-proof your operations:

**1. Bandwidth Optimization**  
Prioritize high upload speeds for your business network. While download speeds matter for users, upload speeds directly impact productivity as data is transferred from your network to employees' devices.

**2. Network Resilience**  
Ensure all hardware (routers/switches) and software (firewalls, antivirus, anti-malware) are updated and rated for handling increased remote traffic. What works for internal use may not scale for remote access.

**3. Centralized File Management**  
Store files accessed by multiple users on a shared network drive or enterprise-grade cloud platform. Implement user-level access controls for sensitive data.

**4. Licensed Application Access**  
Provide secure remote access via VPN or similar tools. Audit "business-critical" applications to ensure sufficient licensed versions are available. Maintain an inventory of authorized cloud programs, users, and access levels.

**5. Remote Workstation Readiness**  
Evaluate hardware needs for remote work. Determine if work-issued laptops or desktop-level performance is required. Consider adding home devices to your managed IT services for consistent security and support.

**6. Post-Pandemic Continuity Gaps**  
Reflect on lessons learned during the health crisis. Identify any additional requirements for your continuity plan.
