Product integrations are essential for B2B companies to attract clients, retain them, and expand into new markets—whether by region, industry, or company size. However, our State of Product Integrations report, based on surveys of 260 product managers and engineers, reveals that most B2B organizations are failing to implement strategies that fully leverage these opportunities.  

**Limited Integration Offerings**  
While most B2B companies recognize the value of integrations, they often fall short in execution. A staggering 68% offer fewer than 10 integrations, which is insufficient given the vast ecosystem of SaaS vendors across categories like CRM, HRIS, and ticketing. Offering just seven to nine integrations neglects the majority of clients’ software ecosystems, leading to frustration and client attrition.  

**Overextending Across Categories**  
Many companies attempt to cover multiple software categories in their integration strategies, but this approach dilutes their ability to meet client needs. For example, a company offering CRM, HRIS, project management, and ticketing integrations with only eight total capabilities can only deliver two integrations per category on average—leaving most client requirements unmet.  

**Slow Development Cycles**  
A concerning 71% of organizations take three weeks or more to build a single integration. While this may suffice for startups, it creates backlogs as companies scale, frustrating engineers, customer teams, and clients alike.  

**Performance Challenges**  
Even after development, integrations often fail to meet client expectations. Over half (55%) cite performance issues as a major challenge, with common pain points including unreliable data sync, slow speeds, infrequent updates, or security gaps. Additional hurdles include outdated API documentation (38%) and costly third-party partnerships (43%).  

**Resource-Intensive Maintenance**  
Organizations spend significant time on reactive maintenance rather than proactive improvement. Half monitor applications for version changes, 48% troubleshoot integration issues, and 46% fix broken integrations caused by external API changes—draining resources and delaying innovation.  

**Looking Ahead: 2024 Integration Ambitions**  
With 50% of companies aiming to build 15+ integrations and 19% targeting 30+, the need for transformation is urgent. Organizations must prioritize scaling integration efforts through increased engineering investment, third-party tooling, or hybrid strategies. As 2024 approaches, the focus will be on delivering robust, scalable integration solutions that meet evolving client demands.
