**What is Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery (BCDR)?**  
BCDR is a strategic framework that combines Business Continuity (BC) and Disaster Recovery (DR) to mitigate risks and ensure uninterrupted operations during crises. BC focuses on preparing for disruptions by maintaining critical functions, while DR addresses restoring IT systems and data after an incident. Together, they form a holistic approach to risk management, enabling organizations to recover swiftly and maintain stakeholder confidence.  

**Key Components of BCDR**  
1. **Business Continuity Plan (BCP):**  
   - A documented strategy outlining how to sustain operations during disruptions.  
   - Includes risk assessments, business impact analysis, and roles for disaster declaration and response.  
   - Addresses critical functions, communication protocols, and recovery priorities.  

2. **Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP):**  
   - A detailed roadmap for restoring IT infrastructure and data post-disaster.  
   - Defines recovery time objectives (RTO) and recovery point objectives (RPO) to minimize downtime and data loss.  
   - Involves backup systems, recovery sites, and post-crisis cleanup procedures.  

**Why BCDR Matters**

**BCDR Standards and Frameworks**  
Adherence to global standards ensures robust strategies:  
- **ISO 22301:** Guidelines for business continuity management systems.  
- **NIST SP 800-34:** Contingency planning for federal systems.  
- **FINRA Rule 4370:** Mandates for financial institution continuity plans.

- **BC vs. DR:** BC focuses on maintaining operations, while DR restores IT systems.  
- **Business Resilience vs. Continuity:** Resilience is the ability to recover, while continuity ensures uninterrupted operations.
