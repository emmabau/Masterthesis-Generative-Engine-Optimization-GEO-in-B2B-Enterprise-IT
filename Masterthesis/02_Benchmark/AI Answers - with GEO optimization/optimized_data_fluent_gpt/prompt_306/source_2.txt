To persuade others effectively, it's crucial to accept "no" gracefully. If your boss feels pressured to say "yes" to avoid negative feelings, they may become more likely to say "no" over time. By demonstrating resilience to rejection, you create space for them to make decisions based on what's truly best. 

While openness to feedback is valuable, it's important to recognize that most product feedback ultimately results in a "no." This is because generating ideas is easier than turning them into reality. There are numerous reasons why proposals might be rejected, including:

- **Political Reasons**: Internal company politics can block certain technologies or solutions. For example, a proposal might require political capital that the team lead lacks or chooses to allocate elsewhere.  
- **Legal Constraints**: In some cases, explainability becomes a critical factor. For instance, a Machine Learning solution might improve quality but compromise transparency, which could be legally problematic.  
- **Hidden Assumptions**: Conflicting technical proposals often stem from unspoken assumptions about user behavior, data, or system requirements. Identifying these assumptions is key. For example, one team prioritized uptime due to its financial impact, while another project overlooked downtime entirely, leading to different technical solutions.  
- **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: Even if a solution is technically sound, its feasibility depends on factors like scalability, maintenance costs, and team expertise. Fancy tools may require specialized skills or increase operational complexity.  
- **Other Factors**: There are always additional reasons that may not fit into these categories.  

To navigate this, first accept that feedback is valuable, but don’t feel obligated to act on every suggestion. Focus on refining your communication skills to advocate for your ideas effectively.  

**Short-Term Strategies**:  
1. Schedule a private meeting with your Product Owner (PO) to discuss your proposals.  
2. Share specific examples of past rejections (e.g., "I proposed X three times, and you consistently said 'no'").  
3. Ask for actionable steps to improve your proposals. For example: "What can I do to make this idea more compelling?"  
4. Emphasize collaboration: "I want to contribute creatively—how can we align on this?"  

Remember, even if your idea is valid, how you present it matters. Effective communication is a skill that can be developed through practice, reading, or training. Prioritizing clarity and empathy in your approach will increase your chances of gaining support.
