Corporate Security Governance

Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) has evolved to address ethical, reputational, employee safety, cyber, business continuity, and terrorism risks. These expanded concerns have driven security programs to implement broader internal controls that ensure effective governance practices guiding corporate strategy and operations. A mature security program establishes a governance framework that promotes adherence to standards, reduces external risks, and ensures compliance with company policies.

Key Tenets of Security Governance:
- **Operational Risk Leadership Advisory Council (ORLAC):** While corporate security often lacks a seat at the ERM table, an ORLAC can bridge this gap. This cross-functional council reports to the ERM committee, enabling operational leaders to collaborate on risk strategy. Members include Corporate & Cyber Security, IT, Legal, HR, Internal Audit, Risk, Supply Chain, EH&S, and Procurement. The council prioritizes operational risk management, identifies redundancies, and facilitates cross-functional evaluation of mitigation strategies for emerging and residual threats.
- **Security Policies:** Clear, documented policies are critical for managing risk. These should define control hierarchies, involve stakeholders in development, and include ownership, scope, and enforcement details. Avoid technical jargon unless defined, and store policies in a searchable, access-controlled database. Use standardized formats with version control, review schedules, and escalation clauses for exceptions.
- **Risk-Based Security Standards:** Align with industry regulations (e.g., Food Safety Modernization Act, NERC-CIP, GDPR) and standards like ISO 37000, ISO 22301, and NFPA 730. Certification to these standards can enhance credibility, differentiate the organization, and improve insurance positioning. Align internal policies with external frameworks to manage third-party risks effectively.
- **Assessment & Audit Program:** Establish an independent audit team to evaluate security controls, compliance, and alignment with business goals. Conduct risk-based audits of third-party suppliers, considering factors like geographic location, ownership, and operational dependency.
- **Metrics for Governance:** Define measurable indicators to assess adoption and enforcement of security governance. Integrate these metrics into risk policies, supplier contracts, and performance evaluations.

Emerging Trends in Security Governance:
- **AI & Automation:** Leverage machine learning for real-time risk detection and automate compliance checks to enhance efficiency and decision-making.
- **Hybrid Work Environments:** Address risks from decentralized workforces by securing home networks, managing endpoints, and mitigating insider threats through identity management and clear policies.
- **ESG Integration:** Align security programs with Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) criteria to improve transparency, meet regulatory requirements, and appeal to socially conscious stakeholders.
