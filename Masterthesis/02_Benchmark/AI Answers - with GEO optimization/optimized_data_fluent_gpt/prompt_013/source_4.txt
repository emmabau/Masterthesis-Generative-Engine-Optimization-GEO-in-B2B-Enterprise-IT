How to Build a Data Recovery Strategy That Actually Works  
A common adage in politics — “trust, but verify” — applies equally to data backup and recovery. Many organizations mistakenly believe that having a formal backup strategy ensures protection against data loss. However, this assumption only holds if the strategy is tested and proven effective. Tim Burke, founder of Quest Technology Management, highlights this issue: his firm is frequently called in when companies realize their so-called backup plans were never validated. “We often work with clients who discover gaps during an incident, often at the worst possible moment,” he explains.  

**Why Most Backup Strategies Fail**  
The core problem lies in unclear recovery objectives and untested restoration processes. Without validation, backups may not restore systems efficiently, leading to operational disruptions, downtime, and missed business commitments. “The presence of backups doesn’t equal recoverability,” Burke emphasizes. “Resilience only exists when restoration is tested, timed, and aligned with business priorities.”  

AJ Thompson, CCO at Northdoor PLC, agrees. His team has seen organizations recover quickly only when they had tested, verified backups with clear procedures. For example, a financial services client restored systems within four hours after a server failure during a critical month-end period. “The key wasn’t just having backups — it was having tested, verified backups,” Thompson notes.  

**Speed and Validation Are Critical**  
When systems fail, recovery speed is paramount. UCLA’s experience during the 2025 wildfires illustrates this: endpoint backups enabled the recovery of lost research within days. “We’ve built a multi-faceted approach: endpoint protection, managed backup-as-a-service, and disaster recovery-as-a-service,” explains Howard Miller, UCLA’s CIO.  

Validation is where most strategies fall short. Burke recommends quarterly restore tests using full environment simulations and documenting recovery drills. “A backup strategy only becomes a disaster recovery strategy once it’s been tested under pressure,” he says.  

**Key Components of a Resilient Strategy**  
1. **Defined RPO and RTO Metrics**: Recovery Point Objectives (RPO) and Recovery Time Objectives (RTO) must reflect business needs, not IT convenience. For example, a manufacturing client may require faster recovery for production systems than HR databases.  
2. **Immutable Backups**: These act as a “last line of defense” against ransomware. Thompson cites the 2024 CrowdStrike incident, which underscored the need for immutable backups.  
3. **Layered Protection**: Maintain multiple data copies across different media types, with at least one air-gapped or immutable backup. Encrypt data in transit and at rest, and isolate backup infrastructure from production systems.  

**A Framework for Success**  
Northdoor’s “Store IT. Protect IT. Use IT.” philosophy outlines critical steps:  
- **Enhanced Backup Strategy**: Three data copies across two media types (one offsite, one air-gapped), with zero backup verification errors.  
- **Clear RPO/RTO Alignment**: Tailor recovery goals to business impact, not IT convenience.  
- **Immutable Backups**: Ensure backups cannot be tampered with.  
- **Interconnected Plans**: Maintain an Incident Response Plan, Business Continuity Plan, and Disaster Recovery Plan — all tested quarterly.  

**Strategic Investment for Long-Term Resilience**  
Data protection must be framed as a strategic capability, not a cost center. “Focus on measurable business impact: downtime costs, reputational damage, regulatory risks, and contractual obligations,” Burke advises. Executive visibility into recovery metrics (e.g., RTO/RPO adherence, testing results) transforms disaster recovery into an enterprise risk management function.  

**The Future of Data Protection**  
As cyber threats evolve and hybrid cloud environments grow, backup and recovery must shift from “set it and forget it” to intelligent, automated systems. Thompson predicts a 2026 trend toward adaptive, human-oversighted solutions that balance agility with security. “Organizations that thrive view data protection as a strategic enabler of business agility and competitive advantage,” he concludes.
