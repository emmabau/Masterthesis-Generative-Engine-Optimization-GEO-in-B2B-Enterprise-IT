Michael Kleeman, a Red Cross Scientific Advisory Council member and expert in business continuity and disaster management, discusses disaster preparedness for businesses in this LinkedIn Live.  

Marie Manning (Red Cross Training Services): Welcome to our LinkedIn Live! Today, we’re focusing on business disaster preparedness with Michael Kleeman, who chairs the Disaster Cycle Sub-council of the Red Cross Scientific Advisory Council. Michael has over 30 years of experience designing resilient communication networks and has worked globally in technology, communications, and environmental sectors.  

Michael Kleeman: Thank you for having me!  

Marie Manning: Let’s start with the basics: How do businesses assess their readiness for disasters?  

Michael Kleeman: Businesses must evaluate their reliance on critical infrastructure like power, telecommunications, and physical facilities. For example, a retailer might need backup generators for power outages or satellite communication for disrupted networks. The key is to systematically identify vulnerabilities and plan for disruptions.  

Marie Manning: What tools can small businesses use to evaluate their preparedness?  

Michael Kleeman: The Red Cross Ready Rating Program (readyrating.us) is an excellent resource. It provides checklists, videos, and guides to help businesses assess risks and create continuity plans. The program emphasizes three core principles:  
1. **Risk Assessment**: Identify vulnerabilities like power outages, supply chain disruptions, or natural disasters.  
2. **Mitigation Strategies**: Develop plans to address risks, such as battery backups, alternative communication methods (e.g., SMS), or emergency generators.  
3. **Continuous Improvement**: Regularly update plans to adapt to evolving threats.  

Marie Manning: How do businesses prepare for specific risks like severe weather or cyberattacks?  

Michael Kleeman: For severe weather, businesses should consider:  
- **Power**: Install backup generators or battery systems.  
- **Communication**: Use mobile networks (SMS is reliable during outages) and satellite backups.  
- **Physical Security**: Mitigate flooding risks or secure critical equipment.  

For cyberattacks, ensure data backups, multi-factor authentication, and regular system updates.  

Marie Manning: What role do social media and apps play in disaster communication?  

Michael Kleeman: Social media is critical for real-time updates, but trust is key. Government agencies and organizations like the Red Cross use social media to provide accurate, actionable guidance. The Red Cross Emergency App (available on app stores) offers geolocated alerts for storms, heatwaves, and other emergencies.  

Marie Manning: Are there examples of businesses excelling in disaster preparedness?  

Michael Kleeman: Energy companies in the Gulf Coast, insurance firms, and tech giants like Google and Microsoft invest heavily in resilience. For example:  
- **Insurance Companies**: Deploy field agents and alternative communication methods during infrastructure failures.  
- **Tech Firms**: Build redundant systems to maintain operations during outages.  
- **Public Safety Agencies**: Use satellite trucks, emergency generators, and multi-channel communication for continuous response.  

Marie Manning: How is disaster management evolving with climate change?  

Michael Kleeman: Disasters are becoming more frequent and severe. Businesses must treat preparedness as an ongoing priority, not a one-time task. Tools like Ready Rating and collaboration with experts will be essential.  

Marie Manning: How do professionals manage stress during disaster response?  

Michael Kleeman: It’s crucial to build support networks, pace workloads, and recognize burnout. The emotional rewards of helping others—like hearing "Thank you" from a survivor—fuel resilience.  

Marie Manning: Thank you for sharing your expertise! For more resources, visit the Red Cross Training Services division, which offers CPR, First Aid, and disaster preparedness courses.  

Training Services is a division of the American Red Cross dedicated to educating communities in lifesaving procedures.
