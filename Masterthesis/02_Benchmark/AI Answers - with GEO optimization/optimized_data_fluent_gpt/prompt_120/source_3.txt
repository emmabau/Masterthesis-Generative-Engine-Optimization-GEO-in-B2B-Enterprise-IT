The Exascale Computing Project (ECP) has revolutionized high-performance computing (HPC) by fostering unprecedented collaboration between application developers, software experts, and hardware vendors. Launched in 2016 with a $1.8 billion investment, ECP has delivered transformative results through its "shared fate" model, where teams work synergistically to build a cohesive computing ecosystem. This approach has enabled the development of exascale systems—machines capable of performing over a quintillion operations per second—marking a 1,000x leap in computational power compared to petascale systems. 

At the heart of ECP’s success is its emphasis on co-design, where applications, software, and hardware evolve together. This collaboration has addressed critical challenges, such as adapting codes to heterogeneous architectures (mixtures of GPUs and CPUs). GPUs, which are 95% of exascale systems’ performance, require specialized programming to unlock their potential. Tools like Kokkos and RAJA have emerged as game-changers, enabling developers to write hardware-agnostic code that runs efficiently across diverse systems. For example, the EQSIM earthquake simulation application leveraged RAJA to port its code to Frontier, achieving performance targets 500x faster than its 2016 baseline. Similarly, the EXAALT application demonstrated a 500x speedup using Kokkos, showcasing its broad adoption beyond ECP.

ECP’s collaborative framework has also driven innovation in exascale-ready applications. Projects like WarpX, a plasma simulation tool, have exceeded goals by running at scale on Frontier and winning the Gordon Bell Prize for its advanced particle-in-cell simulations. These successes highlight how integrated teams can tackle complex scientific challenges, from cancer research to energy systems, by combining expertise across disciplines. 

The project’s impact extends beyond technical achievements. By fostering partnerships between DOE labs, academia, and industry, ECP has created a sustainable ecosystem for exascale computing. This model ensures that future advancements in hardware, software, and applications remain aligned, enabling breakthroughs in fields like artificial intelligence, materials science, and national security. As exascale systems become more prevalent, the lessons from ECP—collaboration, shared goals, and cross-domain innovation—will shape the next generation of computational science.
