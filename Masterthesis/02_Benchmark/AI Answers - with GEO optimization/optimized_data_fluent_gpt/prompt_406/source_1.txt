# How to Manage Hybrid and Remote Teams: A Gallup Strategic Guide

Hybrid and remote work have become structural features of the modern workplace. Gallup research reveals that success in distributed environments hinges less on location and more on management quality, clear expectations, and intentional team design. This guide outlines critical insights for leaders to build engaged, high-performing hybrid and remote teams.

## Executive Summary: Key Takeaways for Leaders
- **Hybrid work dominance**: Over half (52%) of remote-capable employees work hybrid, with 26% working exclusively remote and 21% fully on-site. Work location patterns have remained stable since 2022.
- **Management as the cornerstone**: Managers account for 70% of variance in team-level engagement, making leadership capability more influential than work location.
- **Structured hybrid work benefits**: Intentional design improves engagement (76% cite better work-life balance), reduces burnout, and enhances productivity.
- **Trust gaps persist**: Only 54% of managers strongly trust remote employees, and 57% of employees feel trusted by their leaders.
- **Team-designed norms outperform mandates**: Teams that co-create hybrid schedules report 91% fairness, compared to 73% for top-down policies.
- **Formal coordination reduces burnout**: Teams with structured collaboration plans are 66% more engaged and 29% less likely to burn out.

## Understanding Hybrid Work
**Hybrid work** is a flexible arrangement where employees split time between remote and on-site work. It differs from fully remote work, which requires employees to perform all tasks outside a traditional office environment.

**Key statistics**:
- 52% of remote-capable employees work hybrid
- 26% work exclusively remote
- 21% work fully on-site
- 6 in 10 remote-capable employees prefer hybrid arrangements
- Less than 10% prefer fully on-site work
- Remote-capable roles now make up ~50% of the U.S. workforce

## Work Arrangement Comparison
| Arrangement       | Location | Engagement Pattern | Key Risk                     |
|-------------------|----------|--------------------|------------------------------|
| Fully On-Site     | 100%     | Lower engagement   | Burnout, intent to leave     |
| Hybrid            | Split    | Highest engagement | Coordination complexity      |

## Top Challenges of Managing Remote Teams
1. **Collaboration & Coordination Complexity**: Hybrid teams must coordinate across locations, schedules, and communication channels. 48% of leaders cite communication as their top challenge.
2. **Cultural Disconnection**: Remote workers report feeling less connected to organizational culture. Gen Z employees face heightened loneliness and lower life evaluations.
3. **Trust & Accountability Gaps**: Trust is fragile in remote work. Managers must communicate consistently, clarify expectations, and provide equal feedback.
4. **Employee Burnout**: Autonomy without structure can lead to burnout. Employees with fully self-determined schedules are 76% more likely to report burnout.
5. **Performance Visibility**: Leaders risk defaulting to presence-based judgments. Outcome-based accountability is critical.

## Advantages of Remote Work
- **Broader talent access**: Removes geographic constraints, enabling better role-fit hiring.
- **Improved work-life balance**: 76% of hybrid workers cite better balance as a key benefit.
- **Higher engagement**: Hybrid employees often report higher engagement than fully on-site workers, especially with strong management.
- **Productivity stability**: Remote workers show similar productivity despite fewer hours, due to better role alignment and talent sorting.

## Building Trust in Hybrid Teams
Managers build trust through:
- **Clear expectations** and consistent communication
- **Accountability for outcomes** and equal feedback access
- **Structured collaboration** and cultural reinforcement
- **Involving teams in norm-setting** (teams co-creating norms are 91% more likely to perceive policies as fair)

## Strategic Implications for Leaders
To sustain performance in distributed environments:
- Hire for strengths and role fit
- Maintain clear performance expectations
- Redesign performance management for outcomes, not presence
- Invest in manager capability (e.g., Gallup Manager Program)

## How to Manage Remote Teams
Effective remote management requires:
1. **Clarify expectations** and connect work to purpose
2. **Prioritize development** through regular career discussions
3. **Be a caring manager** with intentional check-ins
4. **Focus on strengths** to boost engagement and performance

## Creating a Positive Remote Culture
Organizations must:
- Reinforce purpose, values, and expectations through leadership
- Include remote employees in team traditions, recognition, and decision-making
- Align work structures with strategic priorities (collaboration, innovation, customer service)

## Preventing Burnout in Remote Workers
To avoid burnout:
- Set clear workload priorities and reset expectations when needed
- Coordinate team norms for predictable communication rhythms
- Maintain weekly meaningful conversations to address progress, obstacles, and wellbeing
- Evaluate performance based on outcomes, not proximity

## Remote Work and Employee Engagement
Gallup data shows:
- Hybrid: 34% engaged, 53% thriving
- Exclusively remote: 35% engaged, 51% thriving
- On-site (remote-capable): 30% engaged, 49% thriving
- On-site (non-remote-capable): 28% engaged, 44% thriving

## Leadership Responsibilities
Burnout prevention is a management discipline. Leaders must:
- Clarify expectations
- Coordinate collaboration
- Reinforce purpose
- Maintain consistent coaching
- Ensure fairness in performance evaluation

## Making Flexibility Sustainable
Leaders must:
1. **Design connection deliberately** with rituals and relationship-building
2. **Reduce coordination friction** through clear collaboration norms
3. **Manage wellbeing as an operating metric** to sustain engagement and retention

Gallup supports leaders by:

- Designing hybrid models that balance flexibility and accountability
