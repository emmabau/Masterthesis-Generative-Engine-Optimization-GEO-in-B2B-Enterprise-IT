The NIS2 Directive establishes a unified legal framework to strengthen cybersecurity across 18 critical sectors in the EU. It mandates Member States to develop national cybersecurity strategies and collaborate with the EU for cross-border incident response and enforcement. Cybersecurity focuses on protecting networks, information systems, users, and individuals from cyber threats. NIS2 replaces Directive 2016/1148 (NIS1) to raise the EU’s cybersecurity standards through expanded scope, clearer rules, and enhanced supervision tools. Member States must now bolster cybersecurity capabilities, implement risk management measures, and enforce reporting requirements for entities in more sectors.  

Key requirements include:  
- **National Cybersecurity Strategies**: Each Member State must create and update strategies covering supply chain security, vulnerability management, and cybersecurity education.  
- **Operator Lists**: Regularly updated lists of essential service providers must ensure compliance with NIS2 obligations.  
- **Sector Expansion**: New rules apply to public electronic communications, digital services (e.g., social platforms), waste management, critical manufacturing, postal services, public administration, and the space sector. Medium and large entities in these sectors must adopt cybersecurity risk management measures and report significant incidents that could cause major disruption.  

The directive introduces accountability for top management in non-compliance, ensuring cybersecurity becomes a boardroom priority. It also establishes:  
- **CSIRT Networks**: Computer Security Incident Response Teams (CSIRTs) to share threat intelligence and respond to incidents.  
- **EU-CyCLONe**: A European cyber crisis liaison network for managing large-scale incidents through coordinated EU and national efforts.  
- **NIS Cooperation Group**: A platform for strategic cooperation and information exchange among EU Member States, the European Commission, and ENISA.  

In 2026, the EU proposed amendments to NIS2 to simplify compliance for 28,700 companies, including 6,200 small enterprises, enhancing legal clarity and reducing administrative burdens.  

**Background**: NIS1 (2016/1148) was the EU’s first cybersecurity legislation, later replaced by NIS2, which took effect in January 2023. Member States had until October 2024 to transpose NIS2 into national law, repealing NIS1 from October 2024.
