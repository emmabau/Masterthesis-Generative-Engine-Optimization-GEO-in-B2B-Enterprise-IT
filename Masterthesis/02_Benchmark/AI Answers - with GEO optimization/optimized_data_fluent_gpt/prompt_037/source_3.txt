5 Steps to Strengthen Your BCM Program in a Post-Pandemic World  

The COVID-19 pandemic has reshaped how organizations approach business continuity management (BCM), highlighting the critical need for resilient strategies to address evolving risks. From natural disasters to cyber threats and supply chain disruptions, BCM is no longer optional—it’s a necessity. Companies with robust BCM frameworks not only weathered the pandemic better but also recovered faster, while those without plans faced chaos. Here’s how to modernize your BCM program for future uncertainties:  

1. **Reassess Risk Perception with Data-Driven Insights**  
   BCM begins with a dynamic risk assessment. While 82% of organizations recognized pandemics as top risks (per Aon’s 2020 Enterprise Risk Management Survey), fewer than 31% had formal pandemic plans. This gap underscores the need for continuous risk recalibration. As remote work and digital reliance grow, cyber threats are becoming more probable and impactful. Regularly update your risk register to reflect emerging threats like supply chain vulnerabilities, geopolitical shifts, and technological dependencies.  

2. **Break Down Silos for Holistic Risk Management**  
   Fragmented BCM efforts create inconsistencies. A global electronics manufacturer, for instance, improved its response by unifying its six business units under a centralized framework. This collaboration not only streamlined operations but also fostered cross-functional communication. Prioritize integrating BCM into all departments, ensuring alignment on shared goals and accountability.  

3. **Test, Update, and Refine Your BCM Plan**  
   Static plans are obsolete. Treat your BCM strategy as a living document, revisiting it annually to reflect changes in personnel, technology, and operations. However, updates alone aren’t enough—scenario testing is vital. For example, during the pandemic, many companies discovered employees lacked remote work tools or knew how to access secure networks. Conduct tabletop exercises with key stakeholders to identify gaps and refine protocols.  

   **Key Testing Questions:**  
   - When was your last BCM test?  
   - What triggers the plan’s activation?  
   - How do you manage a shutdown?  
   - Are critical failure points identified?  
   - Are roles and responsibilities clear?  
   - What dependencies exist?  
   - How will communication occur?  
   - What technology and skills are required?  

4. **Hold Vendors to the Same Accountability Standards**  
   Supply chain disruptions, like microchip shortages and lumber scarcity, have exposed vulnerabilities. To mitigate cascading risks, ensure vendors meet your BCM standards. Leading European OEMs now require suppliers to adhere to rigorous continuity protocols, creating a ripple effect of resilience. Additionally, many insurers now demand BCM plans as part of underwriting, emphasizing their strategic importance.  

5. **Embed BCM into Your Organizational Culture**  
   BCM is more than a plan—it’s a mindset. Embed it into your company’s DNA by securing executive commitment and empowering risk managers to drive cross-functional adoption. Foster open communication channels to address risks proactively. Cultural and structural alignment ensures BCM remains a priority, even during crises.  

By adopting these steps, organizations can build risk resilience, protect operations, and position themselves to thrive in an unpredictable world.
