Healthcare systems are grappling with tight margins and capacity constraints, driven by factors such as an aging population, complex illnesses, workforce shortages, and hospital closures. These challenges are intensifying in emergency departments and inpatient care settings, requiring innovative strategies to address capacity crunches. Leaders from five top healthcare providers—Froedtert ThedaCare Health, Memorial Hermann Health System, NYU Langone, Sanford Health, and UC San Diego Health—shared key strategies to overcome these obstacles.  

**Data-Driven Decision Making**  
Data and analytics are critical for optimizing care delivery. Providers are leveraging predictive analytics and tools like the Vizient Vulnerability Index™ to forecast patient volumes, identify high-risk areas, and allocate resources effectively. For example, Memorial Hermann uses predictive dashboards to manage patient flow and improve outcomes, while Sanford Health’s LAMP tool streamlines workforce planning by integrating electronic health records and HR data. These insights enable proactive strategies to manage capacity and labor needs.  

**Creative Workforce Solutions**  
Workforce shortages remain a major challenge, with projections indicating a 21% rise in the U.S. population aged 65+ by 2030. Providers are adopting innovative approaches:  
- **Training pipelines**: Memorial Hermann partners with local schools to recruit future healthcare professionals.  
- **Workflow optimization**: NYU Langone shifted resident didactics to afternoons, improving discharge efficiency.  
- **Advanced practice providers (APPs)**: Expanding roles for nurse practitioners and physician assistants has shown promise in addressing staffing gaps.  
- **AI tools**: Virtual assistants, automated task managers, and centralized scheduling systems are reducing burnout and improving operational efficiency.  

**Redefining Care Delivery**  
The shift from inpatient to outpatient and virtual care is reshaping healthcare. For instance:  
- **Ambulatory expansion**: Froedtert ThedaCare Health leverages underutilized outpatient spaces to relieve hospital pressure.  
- **Virtual care**: Sanford Health uses telehealth to serve rural patients, while NYU Langone’s Hospital at Home model reduces inpatient stays by 10% and readmissions by 25%.  
- **Home-based care**: This trend is expected to grow by 28% by 2034, requiring investments in technology, remote monitoring, and partnerships.  

**Addressing Social Determinants of Health (SDOH)**  
SDOH significantly impact capacity and outcomes. Vizient data reveals that patients from high-social-needs communities have 70% higher inpatient discharge rates for diabetes and 83% higher rates for high-risk pregnancies. Targeted interventions, such as geriatric-specific emergency departments and virtual OB services, are critical to reducing strain on hospitals.  

**Key Takeaways**  
- **Data analytics** enables predictive planning and resource allocation.  
- **Workforce innovation** through training, AI, and APP integration is essential.  
- **Reimagining care models**—including virtual and home-based services—can alleviate inpatient pressure.  
- **SDOH-focused strategies** improve equity and reduce capacity strain.  

The healthcare industry must adopt bold, data-informed strategies to navigate these challenges, ensuring sustainable growth and quality care for evolving patient needs.
