New Research Reveals Sharp Decline in Remote and Hybrid Job Opportunities for High Earners  

Washington, D.C. — A recent study highlights a significant drop in remote and hybrid job opportunities for high-wage earners. According to Ladders, a platform focused on high-paying roles, remote jobs offering at least $250,000 annually have fallen by 60%, while hybrid roles in the same salary range have dropped by 95%. This trend extends beyond high-income positions: LinkedIn reports that remote job demand far outpaces supply, with twice as many applicants as available roles. Remote job postings also declined by 23% year-over-year.  

The shift is reflected in workforce trends. Last year, 12.2% of U.S. workers were fully remote, down from over 14% the prior year, per B2B Reviews. Hybrid arrangements, where workers split time between home and office, now account for over 28% of roles. Both remote and hybrid opportunities have steadily declined since 2020.  

Despite these declines, worker preferences remain strong. FlexJobs research shows 65% of workers favor fully remote roles, while 32% prefer hybrid setups. Looking ahead, the Future Workforce Pulse Report projects that 32.6 million Americans—about 22% of the workforce—will work remotely by 2025. This growth is expected to accelerate as technology advances, potentially reviving remote and hybrid opportunities.
