Composable infrastructure represents a transformative approach to modern data center architecture, enabling organizations to dynamically allocate compute, storage, and networking resources based on real-time workload demands. According to cio-wiki.org, this model treats data center resources as fluid pools rather than static silos, allowing IT teams to tailor infrastructure configurations for specific applications with unprecedented agility. When integrated with software-defined storage (SDS), composable infrastructure creates a highly scalable, cloud-native environment that maximizes resource utilization and operational efficiency.

**Key Components of Composable Infrastructure**  
1. **Composable Hardware**: Aggregates compute (virtualized or bare metal servers), storage, and network resources into shared pools, enabling on-demand allocation.  
2. **Composable Software**: SDS decouples storage hardware from management layers, allowing dynamic allocation of storage resources across applications. This integration ensures storage scales seamlessly with compute and network demands.  
3. **High-Speed Interconnects**: Technologies like NVMe-oF and NVMe/TCP ensure low-latency, high-bandwidth I/O operations, critical for AI/ML, real-time analytics, and transactional workloads.  
4. **Composable APIs**: Enable programmable access to resources, fostering automation and flexibility in managing infrastructure.

SDS further optimizes costs by abstracting storage resources, reducing reliance on dedicated hardware.
