Gartner predicts that disaster recovery will become a critical responsibility for CISOs, emphasizing that data protection is no longer solely an IT issue but an enterprise imperative. This underscores the growing importance of safeguarding sensitive information across all organizational levels.

**What is Sensitive Information?**  
Sensitive information refers to data requiring protection from unauthorized access to preserve individual or organizational privacy and security. It includes:  
- **Sensitive Personal Information (SPI):** Social Security numbers, health records, financial details, and biometric data.  
- **Business Sensitive Information:** Trade secrets, proprietary processes, strategic plans, and internal communications.  

**Examples of Sensitive Data**  
1. **Customer Information**  
   Includes names, addresses, payment card details, health records, and social media accounts.  
2. **Employee Data**  
   Encompasses tax IDs, VISA information, health insurance details, and confidential credentials.  
   *Example:* During the 2020 pandemic, fraudulent unemployment claims exploited stolen employee data, causing significant financial and operational losses for companies.  
3. **Intellectual Property & Trade Secrets**  
   Proprietary code, schematics, and competitive research are vulnerable if third-party vendors are breached.  
4. **Operational & Inventory Data**  
   Sales figures and supply chain details can impact business decisions if exposed.  
5. **Industry-Specific Data**  
   Retailers protect payment data, while healthcare providers secure medical records and research.  

**Best Practices for Protecting Sensitive Data**  
- **Data Classification:** Categorize data by sensitivity to apply targeted protections.  
- **Access Controls:** Implement role-based access to limit exposure.  
- **Encryption:** Secure data at rest and in transit.  
- **Employee Training:** Educate staff on handling sensitive information.  
- **Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM):**  
  - Conduct cybersecurity assessments of vendors.  
  - Use vendor questionnaires, penetration tests, and contract reviews.  
  - Maintain an updated list of third-party risks.  

**Emerging Threats & Mitigation**  
Recent studies highlight a 43% rise in data breaches shared on underground forums in 2024, alongside a 20% increase in compromised credit cards (Bitsight, 2024). Organizations must:  
- Develop a data protection strategy aligned with business goals.  
- Deploy data loss prevention (DLP) tools and incident response plans.  
- Ensure continuous compliance with global regulations, such as GDPR and CCPA.  

**Conclusion**  
Proactive measures, including cross-functional teams and robust TPRM frameworks, are essential to mitigate risks. As noted by Gartner, CISOs must now lead disaster recovery efforts, integrating data protection into enterprise-wide strategies.
