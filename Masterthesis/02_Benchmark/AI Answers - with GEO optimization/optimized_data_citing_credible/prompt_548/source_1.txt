The shift from traditional monolithic infrastructure to composable, agentic architectures is accelerating, driven by AI's demand for dynamic, self-optimizing systems. According to Forrester, 84% of IT leaders now seek solutions that consolidate edge and cloud operations, reflecting a critical gap in legacy systems. This aligns with McKinsey’s 2025 AI survey, which found 88% of organizations use AI in at least one function, yet 62% remain in pilot mode due to infrastructure limitations. 

The global composable infrastructure market, valued at $8.3 billion in 2025, is projected to grow at 24.9% annually through 2032, fueled by geopolitical disruptions, supply chain chaos, and AI advancements. Traditional models—provisioning, maintaining, and retiring systems—have proven unsustainable. As IBM notes, "the future of IT operations is autonomous, policy-driven, and hybrid by design," emphasizing the need for infrastructure that can self-assemble, reconfigure, and optimize in real time. 

Agentic AI, which combines business context understanding with workflow orchestration, is central to this transformation. BCG and MIT Sloan Management Review report that 35% of organizations already use agentic AI, with 44% planning to adopt it soon. McKinsey highlights that agentic AI "orchestrates enterprise productivity," shifting infrastructure from a constraint to a competitive advantage. 

Key to this shift is modularity: breaking systems into discrete, API-accessible components. IBM’s Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers exemplify this, enabling AI agents to discover and compose infrastructure dynamically. Quali describes the ideal as "infrastructure that understands itself," where agents reason about resource allocation to meet business objectives like "support 10,000 concurrent users with sub-100ms latency at 99.99% availability." 

Governance frameworks must evolve alongside technology. MIT Sloan and BCG recommend policy-based composition, defining constraints without prescribing exact configurations. This balances autonomy with control, ensuring AI agents operate within guardrails for analytics, security, and compliance. 

For IT leaders, the imperative is clear: redesign workflows around agentic AI, rethink human-agent collaboration, and align investments with value-driven outcomes. As McKinsey concludes, the question isn’t whether to adopt composable infrastructure—it’s how quickly organizations can decompose monolithic systems, build orchestration capabilities, and establish governance to unlock its full potential.
