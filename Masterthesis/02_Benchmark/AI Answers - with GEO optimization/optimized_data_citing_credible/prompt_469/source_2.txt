Fact Check Team: Decline in Remote and Hybrid Job Opportunities for High Earners  
WASHINGTON (TND) — A recent analysis reveals a notable decline in remote and hybrid job opportunities for high-wage earners. According to Ladders, a platform specializing in high-paying roles, remote jobs offering at least $250,000 annually have dropped by 60%, while hybrid positions in the same salary bracket have fallen by 95%. This trend extends beyond high-income roles: LinkedIn reports that remote job demand far outstrips supply, with twice as many applicants as available positions. Remote job postings also declined by 23% year-over-year.  

The shift is reflected in workforce data. B2B Reviews notes that 12.2% of U.S. workers were fully remote last year, down from over 14% the prior year, while hybrid arrangements—where employees work remotely at least part-time—now account for more than 28% of schedules. Both remote and hybrid opportunities have steadily declined since 2020.  

Despite this decline, worker preferences remain strong. FlexJobs research indicates 65% of workers favor fully remote roles, while 32% prefer hybrid setups. Looking ahead, the Future Workforce Pulse Report estimates that 32.6 million Americans (about 22% of the workforce) will work remotely by 2025, a projection expected to grow as technology advances.
