High-Performance Computing (HPC) stands as a cornerstone of U.S. industrial competitiveness, economic power, and national security readiness. As outlined in the report *A New Frontier: Sustaining U.S. High-Performance Computing Leadership in an Exascale Era*, HPC represents the "tip of the spear" in advanced computing, enabling breakthroughs in scientific discovery, technological innovation, and national defense. Policymakers must sustain investments in HPC applications, infrastructure, and skills to maintain America’s leadership in this critical domain.  

The exascale era marks a transformative milestone, with the U.S. launching the Frontier supercomputer in 2022, capable of executing 1.102 exaflops—1,000 times faster than petascale systems. This leap in computational power unlocks unprecedented opportunities for modeling complex systems, from aerospace engineering to climate science, as noted by the Department of Energy (DOE) and National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA). For instance, HPC accelerates aircraft design by enabling simulations that reduce physical prototyping costs and time, as demonstrated by Boeing’s use of supercomputers to cut wind tunnel tests from 77 to 11 prototypes for the 787 Dreamliner.  

HPC’s economic impact is profound. Hyperion Research estimates that HPC applications have generated over $3 trillion in economic value since 1993, with a return on investment (ROI) of $452 for every $1 invested across industries like defense, manufacturing, and life sciences. For example, Procter & Gamble leverages HPC to optimize product formulations, saving over $1 billion in costs through computational simulations that replace physical experiments. Similarly, the U.S. Department of Energy’s ExaWind initiative aims to harness HPC to optimize wind energy systems, reducing turbine inefficiencies caused by wake effects and increasing energy output by up to 40%.  

National leadership in HPC is vital for security and innovation. The U.S. leads in exascale computing, with 128 supercomputers in the top 500 list as of 2022, though China has narrowed the gap. However, the U.S. edge lies in its focus on software development and accessibility, as highlighted by Hyperion Research. For instance, the Extreme Scale Scientific Software Stack (E4S) democratizes HPC access for researchers, enabling open-source tools to run complex simulations. This contrasts with China’s focus on hardware performance over practical applications, where supercomputers like Tianhe-2 face usability challenges due to limited software support.  

In healthcare, HPC drives advancements in drug discovery and personalized medicine. During the COVID-19 pandemic, supercomputers like Summit at Oak Ridge National Laboratory accelerated vaccine development by simulating drug interactions and identifying potential treatments. Pfizer’s use of HPC reduced the time to develop its antiviral drug Paxlovid, demonstrating how computational models can streamline R&D. Similarly, HPC aids in cancer research, such as the DOE-NCI collaboration to model RAS gene mutations, which are linked to 30% of cancers. These efforts underscore HPC’s role in addressing global challenges, from climate modeling to Alzheimer’s research.  

To maintain leadership, the U.S. must prioritize investments in HPC infrastructure, as outlined in the CHIPS and Science Act of 2022. This includes funding for the Advanced Scientific Computing Research (ASCR) program and expanding regional supercomputing hubs to foster public-private partnerships. As the report concludes, sustained HPC innovation is essential to secure America’s position as a global leader in technology, economic growth, and national security.
