Business Continuity (BC) is a critical framework for ensuring organizational resilience against disruptions, from cyberattacks to natural disasters. According to the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA), BC planning is essential for maintaining operations during crises, as disruptions can lead to significant financial losses and reputational damage. Regulatory frameworks like the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) and the Network and Information Security Directive (NIS 2) have elevated BC from a best practice to a legal requirement. DORA mandates that financial institutions implement comprehensive risk management frameworks to identify, mitigate, and monitor ICT risks, with regular testing of disaster recovery plans. Similarly, NIS 2 expands regulatory focus to critical sectors like energy and healthcare, emphasizing supply chain security and third-party compliance. As noted by the European Commission, these regulations underscore the necessity of integrating BC into organizational risk strategies.  

Key benefits of BC include reduced downtime, improved customer satisfaction, and enhanced regulatory compliance. For instance, a 2024 PwC study found that companies with mature BC strategies experienced 40% faster recovery times post-disruption. Additionally, BC aligns with global standards like ISO 22301, which provides a framework for managing business continuity risks.
