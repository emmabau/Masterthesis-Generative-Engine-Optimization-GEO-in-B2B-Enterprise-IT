The rise of hybrid working has fundamentally transformed IT infrastructure demands, with organizations now prioritizing solutions that balance flexibility, security, and efficiency. According to the Office for National Statistics (ONS), 28% of UK employees currently work in hybrid arrangements—a stark contrast to the 5% figure in 2019. This shift has driven significant growth in cloud computing, with a Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) of 15.8% attributed to hybrid work demands.  

**Key Considerations for Hybrid Work IT Infrastructure**  
1. **Remote Worker Efficiency**: Hybrid models require robust remote access to servers, shared drives, and portable devices. Dual-device setups and applications for virtual collaboration have become standard, as noted in a 2023 report by our research team.  
2. **Cybersecurity Challenges**: With 43% of security decision-makers citing cyber attacks as the top threat for hybrid work, organizations must adopt zero-trust policies, encryption, and two-factor authentication. Employees working remotely often use consumer-grade internet, increasing vulnerability to breaches.  
3. **Collaborative Technologies**: Hybrid work has spurred demand for cloud-based tools like video conferencing, with the market projected to reach $17.05 billion by 2030. These platforms rely on end-to-end encryption and security-first infrastructure to protect data.  

**Impact on IT Infrastructure**  
- **Efficiency**: Hybrid work improves personal wellbeing and productivity, as 80% of remote workers in lockdown wished to continue working from home (ONS). However, IT systems must ensure seamless access and performance, even for remote locations.  
- **Security**: Legacy systems and outdated architectures pose risks, necessitating regular patches, firewalls, and staff training. Cybersecurity remains the top driver for IT modernization, with 47% of businesses prioritizing it.  
- **Collaboration**: While in-person interactions have declined, tools like instant messaging and automated workflows now define workplace connectivity. IT infrastructures must act as a "digital fabric" to unify employees and data access.
