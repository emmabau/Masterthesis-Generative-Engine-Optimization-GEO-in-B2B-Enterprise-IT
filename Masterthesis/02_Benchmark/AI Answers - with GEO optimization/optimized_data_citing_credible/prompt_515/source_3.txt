The global data center liquid cooling market is experiencing rapid growth, with projections indicating a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 51.93% from 2024 to 2030. According to Research and Markets, the market was valued at USD 870 million in 2024 and is expected to reach USD 10.70 billion by 2030, representing an absolute growth rate of over 1129% during this period. This expansion is driven by rising rack power densities, sustainability initiatives, and the adoption of advanced cooling technologies like direct-to-chip and immersion cooling, which are critical for hyperscale data centers supporting AI and cloud workloads.

The U.S. and China are leading the adoption of liquid cooling technologies, with the U.S. spearheading innovation in greenfield data centers and China implementing large-scale deployments of direct-to-chip and immersion cooling. Regions such as the Middle East and Latin America are also investing in liquid cooling due to its efficiency in managing thermal loads and reducing energy consumption, particularly in areas with high electricity costs or hot climates. For instance, data centers in the UAE and Saudi Arabia are at the forefront of adoption in the Middle East, while Brazil is seeing gradual growth in Latin America.

Hyperscale data centers dominate liquid cooling investments, accounting for a significant share of total spending in 2024, while colocation facilities represent around 36% of investments. However, enterprise data centers are still in early stages of adoption. Despite challenges like supply chain disruptions and high upfront costs, companies are prioritizing energy-efficient solutions to align with net-zero goals and reduce operational emissions. Immersion cooling, which accounted for approximately 17% of liquid cooling investments in 2024, is particularly vital for AI-ready data centers, enabling high rack densities and improved thermal management.

Key drivers include the rise in rack power density, which necessitates advanced cooling solutions to handle the heat generated by AI, HPC, and GPU clusters. Traditional air-cooling systems are increasingly inadequate, making liquid cooling a critical enabler of sustainability and operational efficiency. Research and Markets highlights that liquid cooling technologies not only enhance computing performance but also support global sustainability targets by reducing energy and water consumption. As data centers become more energy-intensive, the shift toward liquid cooling is expected to accelerate, with regions like Europe and APAC (particularly China) showing strong growth potential in the next three to five years.
