According to the Komprise 2024 State of Unstructured Data Management report, IT budgets are facing increasing pressure due to data growth, particularly the rapid expansion of unstructured data. For large enterprises, storing, backing up, and replicating data can cost millions annually, especially for disaster recovery plans. Cybersecurity concerns further drive costs, as unused unstructured data often becomes a weak link for ransomware attacks, prompting organizations to maintain redundant copies of data—often at significant expense. Gartner predicts a 23% growth in IT spending on data center systems in 2025, driven largely by AI infrastructure investments and rising costs for recurring spending. Meanwhile, IDC forecasts that worldwide spending on public cloud services will reach $805 billion in 2024, doubling by 2028, with storage representing a significant portion of this growth.

To balance cost-cutting with data management needs, organizations should prioritize optimization over mere cost reduction. According to Komprise, most (80%) of data in a typical organization is cold and rarely accessed, yet it continues to consume expensive storage and backup resources. By offloading this data to cheaper solutions like object storage in the cloud, companies can save at least 70% in costs without disrupting user access. Implementing data tiering—retaining active data on high-performance systems while moving infrequently accessed data to cost-effective storage—can significantly reduce expenses. File tiering further minimizes ransomware risks by avoiding user disruption during data migration.

Cloud storage plays a critical role in cost optimization, offering scalability and flexibility. However, organizations must avoid rehydration costs, which occur when data must be rehydrated from cloud storage to primary systems during vendor transitions. This can lead to egress fees and increased storage costs. While AWS, Microsoft, and Google have eliminated certain egress fees, their policies often apply only to intra-cloud or same-region transfers. Cross-cloud or cross-region data movement still incurs charges, emphasizing the need for careful planning.

CIOs and CXOs can mitigate unexpected egress costs by aligning data workflows with cloud providers’ pricing models, using deduplication, and leveraging content delivery networks (CDNs) for frequently accessed data. Data retention policies also help control costs by systematically removing obsolete data, such as old logs or redundant backups, which reduce the burden on primary storage and lower cybersecurity risks. Data lifecycle management (DLM) automates this process, moving data to cheaper archival tiers as it ages, while ensuring compliance with regulatory requirements.

To maintain flexibility, organizations should avoid vendor lock-in by adopting storage-agnostic solutions that support technologies like QLC-Flash and Storage as a Service (STaaS). As Benjamin Henry, Field CTO at Komprise, notes, the future of storage lies in agility and visibility across diverse storage silos, ensuring data is placed optimally for performance and cost efficiency. By integrating these strategies, businesses can reduce waste, optimize budgets, and align storage investments with strategic priorities.
