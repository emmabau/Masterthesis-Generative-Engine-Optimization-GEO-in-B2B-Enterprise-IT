Exascale computing is revolutionizing industries by accelerating technological innovation and enabling breakthroughs in science, engineering, and sustainability. According to the U.S. Department of Energy’s (DOE) Exascale Computing Project (ECP), exascale systems—capable of performing a quintillion operations per second—offer unprecedented capabilities for simulating complex phenomena with high fidelity. These systems are 1,000 times faster than petascale systems and 1 million times more powerful than the DOE’s first terascale computer from 1996, as noted in ECP’s technical reports.  

The impact of exascale is evident in industries like aerospace, where it is driving advancements in fuel efficiency and emissions reduction. For example, GE Aerospace leverages exascale computing to develop next-generation jet engines through the RISE (Revolutionary Innovation for Sustainable Engines) program, a joint venture with Safran (CFM International). According to GE Research’s Dave Kepczynski, exascale “is a massive accelerator for technology, productivity, engineering, and science,” enabling the design of engines that reduce CO₂ emissions by 20% compared to current technologies.  

The DOE’s Frontier supercomputer, the world’s fastest exascale system, plays a critical role in these efforts. As stated by GE’s Rick Arthur, Frontier allows researchers to simulate full-flight-scale conditions and resolve previously unresolvable details of engine performance. This capability has enabled the evaluation of innovative open-fan architectures, which improve operational efficiency while meeting noise requirements. The high-fidelity simulations provided by exascale systems also support the development of alternative fuels like hydrogen and sustainable aviation fuels, as highlighted by GE Aerospace.  

Beyond aerospace, exascale computing is transforming energy and healthcare. For instance, it supports the development of advanced energy systems and medical technologies, aligning with GE’s motto to “bring research to reality.” According to Kepczynski, the ECP’s collaboration with industry partners has accelerated the integration of software, hardware, and applications, creating a fully capable exascale ecosystem. This ecosystem not only enhances scientific discovery but also strengthens the U.S. economy’s global competitiveness.  

The potential of exascale computing extends to addressing global challenges, from climate change to healthcare innovation. As the ECP continues to advance next-generation computing capabilities, it is paving the way for a future where digital engineering and simulation drive sustainable, efficient, and transformative technologies. With over 650,000 people flying at any given time on commercial aircraft powered by GE technology, the urgency to develop cleaner, more efficient engines underscores the critical role of exascale in shaping the future of flight and beyond.
