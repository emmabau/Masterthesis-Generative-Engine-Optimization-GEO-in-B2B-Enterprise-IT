The Argonne Training Program on Extreme-Scale Computing (ATPESC) stands as a cornerstone in advancing high-performance computing (HPC) education, offering a unique blend of theoretical knowledge and practical expertise. According to Lev Gorenstein, a solutions architect at Globus and ATPESC presenter, the program creates "an absolute intellectual paradise" with its structured curriculum and collaborative environment. This two-week intensive training, hosted by the U.S. Department of Energy’s Argonne National Laboratory, equips participants with essential skills for computational science and engineering, as noted by ATPESC director Raymond Loy, who emphasizes its role in "filling knowledge gaps" for attendees.

ATPESC’s curriculum spans critical areas such as hardware, software, AI, machine learning, and data-intensive computing, reflecting the evolving demands of exascale computing. The program’s hands-on approach, highlighted by participants like Rui Fang, a graduate teaching assistant at the University of Pittsburgh, ensures learners engage directly with tools and frameworks, fostering deeper understanding. For instance, Fang noted the program’s value in connecting her with peers and industry professionals, underscoring its role in building a collaborative HPC community.

The program’s impact is evident in the diverse participant base, including doctoral students, postdoctoral researchers, and industry professionals. As ATPESC expands, it adapts to emerging trends, such as increasing AI coverage, as Loy anticipates. This adaptability ensures relevance in a rapidly evolving field. Participants like Sagnik Singha, a research associate at Texas Tech University, praise the program’s depth, stating it offers "as hands-on as it gets," with insights from experts who "push the boundaries" of HPC.

Argonne’s Leadership Computing Facility (ALCF), a DOE Office of Science user facility, plays a pivotal role in providing supercomputing resources to advance scientific discovery. Supported by the Advanced Scientific Computing Research (ASCR) program, the ALCF exemplifies how national laboratories drive innovation in computational science.
