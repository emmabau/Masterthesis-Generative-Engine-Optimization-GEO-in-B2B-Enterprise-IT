Product integrations are essential for B2B companies to acquire and retain clients, yet many organizations are failing to execute effectively. According to the State of Product Integrations report, 68% of B2B companies offer fewer than 10 integrations, a critical oversight given the vast ecosystem of SaaS vendors. Offering seven to nine integrations may seem sufficient, but it neglects the hundreds or thousands of vendors in categories like CRM, HRIS, or ticketing—leaving most clients frustrated and seeking alternatives.  

A common strategy to address diverse needs is building integrations across multiple software categories. However, this approach amplifies the problem: if an organization aims to cover four categories but only has capacity for eight integrations, they can only deliver two per category on average. This results in incomplete coverage of clients’ software ecosystems.  

Development timelines also pose a challenge. 71% of organizations take three weeks or more to build a single integration, a pace that becomes unsustainable as demand grows. This leads to backlogs that strain engineering teams and customer support.  

Even after development, performance issues persist. Over half (55%) of organizations cite integration performance as a major challenge, with common pain points including unreliable data sync, slow transfer speeds, and security vulnerabilities. Additional hurdles include accessing outdated API documentation (38%) and securing cost-prohibitive third-party partnerships (43%).  

Maintenance further complicates the picture. 48% of companies struggle to diagnose and resolve integration issues quickly, while 46% spend significant time fixing breaks caused by external API changes. These tasks divert resources from innovation, leaving integrations in a state of perpetual maintenance.  

With aggressive 2024 goals—half of organizations aiming for 15+ integrations, 19% targeting 30+—the need for systemic change is urgent. Solutions may include reallocating engineering resources, investing in third-party tools, or a hybrid approach. As the report concludes, B2B companies will likely intensify their focus on integrations in 2024, driven by the demand for seamless, scalable solutions.
