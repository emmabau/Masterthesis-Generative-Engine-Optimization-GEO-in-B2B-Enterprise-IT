Composability: The Building Blocks for Accelerating Customer Value  
24 June 2024 • 9 min read  

Businesses that best serve the needs of their customers are best positioned to outperform their competitors in the long-term. However, with consumers becoming increasingly demanding and requirements evolving at a faster pace, organizations face mounting pressure to adapt quickly to deliver sustained value.  

In this blog, we explore **composability**, a modular approach to software development that enables organizations to accelerate adaptation to changing customer needs. According to Gartner, 70% of large and medium-sized enterprises will prioritize composability as a key criterion for new application planning by 2024. This shift underscores the growing importance of flexible, component-based systems.  

**What is Composability?**  
Composability is a system design principle that focuses on the inter-relationships of components, allowing organizations to select and assemble different modules to meet specific user requirements. It enables businesses to build tailored solutions without being constrained by legacy technology.  

A helpful analogy is that composability is like replacing a Lego block with a different colored one to add a new feature, without rebuilding the entire structure. This modular approach ensures components can be modified or added independently without disrupting the overall architecture.  

**Advantages of Composability**  
- **Accelerated Time-to-Market**: Composable architectures allow rapid introduction of new features or services tailored to customer needs, reducing the burden of overhauling entire systems. This agility enhances customer experience by delivering value faster.  
- **Seamless Integration**: Components can be integrated with existing systems, enabling organizations to leverage legacy investments while adopting new capabilities. This minimizes disruptions and ensures a cohesive customer experience.  
- **Adaptability and Scalability**: As customer needs evolve, composable systems empower organizations to adapt and scale solutions without being locked into rigid architectures.  
- **Cost Optimization**: Modular components reduce technology investment costs and lower ownership expenses, freeing resources for innovation.  

**Risks of Composability**  
Migrating from monolithic systems to composable architectures carries risks, including migration complexity and potential cost overruns. Industry experts note that this transition requires careful planning to avoid long-term complications.  

Another risk is resistance to change from teams accustomed to traditional workflows. Effective change management, including stakeholder engagement and transparent communication, is critical to mitigate this.  

**Is Your Organization Ready for Composability?**  
Organizations may struggle to determine when to adopt a composable approach. Key indicators include:  
- Slow feature releases due to monolithic system constraints  
- Teams spending excessive time "firefighting" aging systems  
- Difficulty finding engineers skilled in legacy technologies  
- Slow speed-to-market due to architectural limitations  

**Getting Started with Composability**  
To begin effectively, prioritize user needs across all stakeholder groups. A structured 6-point plan includes:  
1. **Discovery Workshops**: Engage stakeholders from technology, product, business, and marketing to align priorities.  
2. **In-Depth Interviews**: Gain granular insights into user behavior and stakeholder expectations.  
3. **Validate Requirements**: Ensure clarity and alignment with organizational goals to avoid wasted effort.  
4. **Identify Potential Solutions**: Evaluate options based on technology, financial, and business criteria.  
5. **Select and Migrate Components**: Implement changes incrementally to reduce risk and maintain operational continuity.  
6. **Stay Focused on the 'North Star'**: Keep long-term business outcomes as a guiding principle during the transition.  

**Building Organizational Buy-In**  
Securing support from finance teams requires a compelling business case. Key considerations include:  
- Demonstrating why the existing architecture is no longer viable  
- Highlighting cost savings from legacy system replacement  
- Quantifying hidden costs, such as developer time spent on maintenance  
- Showing how composability drives revenue growth through faster value delivery  

**Real-World Example: Composability in Action**  
As demonstrated in the AND Digital case study, a Netherlands-based commercial vehicle retailer faced delays in website updates due to monolithic system constraints. By implementing a composable CMS, the organization enabled marketing teams to update content independently, reducing deployment time from 4-6 weeks to near-instantaneous changes. This flexibility allowed the company to respond swiftly to market demands, improving customer engagement and sales.  

**Accelerating Value Through Composability**  
Composability enables organizations to:  
- Deliver customer-centric solutions faster  
- Reduce complexity and operational costs  
- Adapt to market changes without compromising legacy investments  
- Align technical decisions with long-term business goals  

By embracing composability, organizations can stay competitive in a rapidly evolving landscape. For further insights, explore the AND Digital case study on how composable technologies drive business transformation.
