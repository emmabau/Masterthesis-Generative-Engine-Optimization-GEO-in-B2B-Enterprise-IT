In modern enterprise data centers, composable infrastructure has emerged as a critical solution to address the growing demand for flexibility and scalability. According to Enterprise Storage Forum, the ability to rapidly configure or provision hardware is essential for preventing bottlenecks, as bandwidth demands vary across workloads. Composable infrastructure enables dynamic resource allocation, improving networking speeds by allowing data center networks to handle both high-performance and standard workloads efficiently. 

A key challenge in composable systems is latency, as data center CPUs may struggle to process data quickly when resources are decoupled from processing units. NVMe over Fabrics (NVMe-oF) addresses this by providing a direct PCIe bus connection between NVMe SSDs and CPUs, enabling faster data access across network fabrics. This innovation is highlighted by Liqid, which offers Matrix—a composable infrastructure solution that integrates Intel Optane persistent memory and NVMe storage into a unified network fabric. 

Composable infrastructure differs from hyperconverged infrastructure (HCI) in its modular approach, allowing resources to be independently scaled. As noted by Jenna Phipps of Enterprise Storage Forum, this shift toward decoupling resources aligns with broader trends in technologies like microservices, where flexibility is prioritized over tightly integrated systems. Additionally, composable infrastructure supports containerization, enabling containers to run seamlessly across bare-metal servers and virtualized environments, as emphasized by storage experts. 

Despite its potential, some researchers question the long-term viability of composable infrastructure, citing challenges in adoption. However, Western Digital advocates for its role in edge computing, where composable systems can enhance data processing capabilities. Vendors like Fungible and Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) are also advancing the field: Fungible uses DPUs to optimize resource allocation, while HPE’s Synergy platform integrates compute, storage, and networking via HPE Composer, supporting hybrid cloud environments. 

For enterprises seeking to leverage composable infrastructure, solutions from Liqid, Fungible, and HPE offer practical pathways to improve data center efficiency. As the technology evolves, its impact on storage, networking, and cloud integration is expected to grow, driven by the need for adaptable, scalable architectures in modern IT ecosystems.
