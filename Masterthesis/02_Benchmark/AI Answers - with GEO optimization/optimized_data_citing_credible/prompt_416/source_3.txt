**Definition**  
An IT security audit company systematically evaluates an organization’s systems, infrastructure, policies, and controls to identify vulnerabilities, assess risk, and verify compliance with standards. Unlike general IT consultants, these firms follow structured methodologies to produce documented findings, risk ratings, and actionable remediation roadmaps.  

**Market Context**  
The demand for independent security audits has surged due to five forces:  
1. **Compliance Is Non-Negotiable** – SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA, and other frameworks are now table stakes for enterprise customers (Source: Atlant Security, 2026).  
2. **Customers Require Proof** – Third-party audit reports are now standard for procurement and vendor security questionnaires.  
3. **Cyber Insurance Demands It** – Insurers require documented audits, risk assessments, and evidence of controls before issuing policies.  
4. **Internal Teams Can’t Audit Themselves** – Independent auditors provide fresh perspectives and cross-industry benchmarking.  
5. **M&A Due Diligence Demands It** – Cybersecurity due diligence is now a standard part of mergers and acquisitions.

**Comparison: IT Security Audit vs. Penetration Test vs. Vulnerability Assessment**  
| Factor | IT Security Audit | Penetration Test | Vulnerability Assessment |  
|-------|------------------|------------------|--------------------------|  
| Purpose | Evaluate overall security posture | Find and exploit vulnerabilities | Identify known vulnerabilities |  
| Scope | Broad (technical + governance) | Narrow (specific systems) | Broad (all systems) |  
| Approach | Manual review + testing | Simulated attacks | Automated scanning |  
| Output | Comprehensive report with remediation | Exploit evidence | List of vulnerabilities |  
| Cost | $10,000–$100,000+ | $5,000–$50,000+ | $2,000–$15,000 |
