**What is High-Performance Computing (HPC)?**  
High-Performance Computing (HPC) refers to the use of supercomputers or computer clusters to solve complex computational problems requiring massive data processing. As defined by the IEEE, HPC systems can perform trillions of calculations per second, making them essential for simulations, modeling, and large-scale data analysis. Unlike standard personal computers, HPC systems are designed to handle tasks such as weather forecasting, molecular dynamics, and real-time analytics, which demand parallel processing capabilities.

**Types of HPC**  
Understanding HPC systems is crucial for leveraging their potential. Key types include:  
- **Traditional HPC**: Utilizes clusters of high-performance computers interconnected via dedicated networks. This model is widely used in academia and research for tasks like climate modeling and genomics.  
- **Distributed HPC**: Leverages geographically dispersed nodes to enhance resource utilization and reduce latency. A 2022 study by the University of California, Berkeley, highlighted its role in improving fault tolerance for global scientific collaborations.  
- **Containerized HPC**: Employs containerization technologies like Docker to package applications and dependencies, enabling consistent execution across environments. According to Docker’s 2023 whitepaper, this approach simplifies scaling and deployment for cloud-based HPC workloads.  
- **Custom HPC Solutions**: Tailored for specific industries, these systems integrate hardware, software, and configurations to address unique challenges. For example, NVIDIA’s HPC solutions for autonomous vehicles combine GPUs with specialized AI frameworks.  
- **HPC on Cloud**: Cloud platforms like AWS and Azure provide scalable HPC resources, allowing organizations to access high-performance capabilities without upfront hardware investments.
