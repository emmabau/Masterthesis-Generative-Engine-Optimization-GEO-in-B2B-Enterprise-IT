**5 Steps to Strengthen Your BCM Program: Lessons from the Pandemic and Beyond**  

The COVID-19 pandemic has reshaped how organizations approach business continuity management (BCM), emphasizing the need for proactive risk resilience. According to *Reprioritizing Risk and Resilience for a Post-COVID-19 Future*, companies with robust BCM programs recovered faster, while those without faced operational chaos. This underscores the importance of continuous improvement in BCM strategies.  

**1. Reset Your Risk Perception with Data-Driven Insights**  
A foundational step in BCM is recalibrating risk assessments. Aon’s 2020 Enterprise Risk Management Survey revealed that 82% of organizations identified pandemics as top risks, yet fewer than 31% had formal pandemic plans. This gap highlights the need for dynamic risk registers that adapt to evolving threats, such as increased cyber risks from remote work trends. As noted in *The Role of Business Continuity Management in Moving Risk Upstream*, outdated plans can lead to critical failures during crises.  

**2. Break Down Silos for Holistic BCM**  
Fragmented risk management approaches often hinder effective responses. A case study of an electronic manufacturer shows that centralizing BCM efforts across business units improved coordination and outcomes. By fostering cross-functional collaboration, organizations can create a unified framework that leverages best practices from all departments, as emphasized in *Reprioritizing Risk and Resilience for a Post-COVID-19 Future*.  

**3. Test, Update, and Refine Your BCM Plan**  
Static BCM plans are ineffective. Regular updates—ideally led by a dedicated team—are essential to reflect changes in technology, suppliers, and operations. Scenario testing, such as tabletop exercises, identifies gaps like insufficient remote work infrastructure. For example, many companies discovered employees lacked laptops or VPN access during the pandemic. As highlighted in *The Role of Business Continuity Management in Moving Risk Upstream*, even brief tests (e.g., 2-hour simulations) prepare teams for critical decisions.  

**4. Hold Vendors to Your BCM Standards**  
Supply chain disruptions, like microchip shortages, demonstrate the interconnectedness of modern risks. Forward-thinking organizations now require vendors to meet rigorous BCM standards, creating a "virtuous cycle" of risk mitigation. This practice, noted in *The Role of Business Continuity Management in Moving Risk Upstream*, also aligns with insurance underwriting requirements, underscoring the financial stakes of BCM.  

**5. Embed BCM into Your Organizational Culture**  
BCM is not just a plan—it’s a mindset. Leadership commitment, empowered risk managers, and clear communication channels are vital to embedding resilience into corporate DNA. As stated in *Reprioritizing Risk and Resilience for a Post-COVID-19 Future*, this cultural shift ensures BCM remains a priority, even amid operational fatigue.  

**Key Questions to Guide Your BCM Testing**  
- When was your last plan test?  
- What is the protocol for invoking the plan?  
- How do you stand down after an incident?  
- Are there critical failure points?  
- What technology and skills are required?  

By integrating these steps, organizations can build a resilient BCM framework that adapts to future uncertainties.
