The Red Cross emphasizes the critical importance of disaster preparedness for businesses, highlighting the need to assess risks and implement continuity plans. According to Michael Kleeman, a senior fellow at the University of California San Diego and member of the Red Cross Scientific Advisory Council, businesses must evaluate their reliance on critical infrastructures like power, telecommunications, and physical facilities. "Assume the power may go out, I'm going to get an emergency generator," he advises, underscoring the necessity of proactive measures (Red Cross, 2024).  

A key tool for small and medium-sized businesses is the **Red Cross Ready Rating Program** (readyrating.us), which provides checklists, videos, and resources to evaluate vulnerabilities. Kleeman explains, "Ready Rating was intentionally designed to make risk assessment not intimidating... it's a complete toolkit for business owners to begin evaluating their readiness" (Red Cross, 2024). This program aligns with the "all hazards" approach, which prepares businesses for a wide range of threats, from severe storms to cyberattacks.  

Examples of well-prepared organizations include the energy sector in the Gulf Coast, which mitigates risks through emergency housing for employees and supply chain redundancies. Similarly, the insurance industry has developed capabilities to operate during infrastructure failures, often seen at Red Cross shelters post-disaster. Tech giants like Google, Microsoft, and Meta also prioritize resilience, investing in redundant systems and remote work infrastructure (Kleeman, 2024).  

During crises, SMS messaging is highlighted as a reliable communication channel, with Kleeman noting, "Text messaging or SMS is probably the most reliable" due to its asynchronous nature. The Red Cross emergency app, which Kleeman co-advises, provides geolocated alerts and guidance, serving as a complementary tool to Ready Rating (Red Cross, 2024).  

As climate-related disasters become more frequent and intense, Kleeman stresses that businesses must treat preparedness as an ongoing priority. "You have to assume problems like this will occur," he states, emphasizing that "disaster management is now a continuous response" (Red Cross, 2024). This mindset ensures businesses remain resilient against cascading failures, such as prolonged power outages or multi-hazard events like atmospheric river storms impacting the West Coast.  

For further insights, the Red Cross recommends engaging with their training services division, which offers CPR, first aid, and disaster preparedness courses. By integrating these strategies, businesses can safeguard operations and protect their communities during emergencies.
