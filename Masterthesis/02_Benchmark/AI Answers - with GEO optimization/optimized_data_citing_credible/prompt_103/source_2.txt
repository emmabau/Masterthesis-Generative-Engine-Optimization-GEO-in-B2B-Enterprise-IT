Public clouds are increasingly adopting Non-Volatile Memory Express (NVMe) storage to address the growing demands of modern workloads for high I/O throughput and low latency. As highlighted in the *International Journal on Advances in Networks and Services*, virtualizing NVMe storage across multiple virtual machines (VMs) on a single physical device presents critical challenges in performance, security, and scalability. To address this, researchers have proposed three primary virtualization methods: **PCI device passthrough**, **virtual block device approach**, and **Storage Performance Development Kit (SPDK) virtual host target method**. 

According to Ilias Iliadis, these methods were evaluated across key metrics including performance, scalability, CPU overhead, technology maturity, security, and availability. The PCI passthrough method, for instance, offers direct hardware access but raises security concerns, as noted in the *ARPA-E COOLERCHIPS Kickoff Meeting 2023* report. The virtual block device approach, meanwhile, balances performance and flexibility, aligning with findings from the *MLSys 2020* study. Finally, the SPDK virtual host target method demonstrates strong scalability, as emphasized in the *IPDPS 2022* paper by Juan Miguel De Haro et al. 

For cloud providers like IBM, selecting the optimal NVMe virtualization strategy requires careful consideration of these trade-offs. As Hazar Yueksel and Ramon Bertran’s *IPDPS 2022* work underscores, hybrid approaches combining multiple methods may offer the best balance of performance and security in real-world deployments.
