Is Your IoT Security Strategy Scalable?  
Zac Amos  

The Internet of Things (IoT) is rapidly expanding, with 16.6 billion active connections by the end of 2023, and 51% of adopters planning to increase their investments in IoT security, according to Gartner. Yet, many organizations overlook the critical need for a scalable cybersecurity strategy. A one-time fix is insufficient, as cyberattacks against IoT devices surged by 87% year-over-year in 2022, reaching over 112 million attacks, as reported by the Ponemon Institute.  

Scalable IoT security requires more than just expanding defenses—it demands compatibility across diverse protocols and software. For instance, 98% of IoT traffic remains unencrypted, making encryption standards a foundational element of any strategy. IT leaders must prioritize interoperability, ensuring new devices align with existing frameworks and support third-party security tools, as emphasized by the Cloud Security Alliance.  

Transparency is another cornerstone. A scalable cybersecurity solution must include an equally scalable asset inventory. Manual tracking is impractical, with 63% of security professionals already experiencing burnout from high workloads, per a 2023 report by the Ponemon Institute. Cloud-based solutions and automated network discovery tools offer scalable, real-time visibility into IoT endpoints, enabling faster vulnerability detection.  

Zero-trust architecture is essential for mitigating risks in complex IoT networks. Traditional perimeter-based models fail to address the growing threat of third-party exposures, with 37% of businesses untracked in this area, according to a 2023 study by Forrester. Zero-trust verifies every connection, reducing the risk of undetected threats. Automation further enhances scalability, enabling tasks like malware scanning, software updates, and breach detection. Automated tools can save organizations an average of $2.22 million by preventing attacks before they escalate.  

Finally, a scalable strategy must evolve with emerging threats. Combining defensive and offensive approaches—such as "purple team" collaboration and automated penetration testing—allows organizations to identify vulnerabilities proactively. Regular reviews and adaptive policies are critical to staying ahead of cybercriminals who outpace business growth in scale and sophistication.  

Building a scalable IoT security strategy is not optional—it’s a necessity. Organizations must act now to protect against vulnerabilities that could cost millions in damages.
