Why the future of cloud storage needs to change — and what that means for us  

The cloud storage landscape is at a critical inflection point. A recent study by Seagate and Recon Analytics reveals that **61% of global organizations expect their cloud storage needs to double or more by 2028**, driven by AI adoption and the exponential growth of "dark data" — data created but never used. This surge in demand is already straining existing infrastructure, with data centers consuming **1–1.5% of the world’s total electricity** (International Energy Agency). If current trends continue, this figure will rise sharply, exacerbating environmental and operational challenges.  

The problem isn’t just about scale. Traditional centralized storage models are inherently unsustainable. Data centers, while efficient for large-scale operations, create single points of failure and attractive targets for cyberattacks. As Recon Analytics highlights, **90% of AI users prioritize security** when building infrastructure, yet many still cling to outdated centralized systems. Distributed cloud storage offers a compelling alternative: leveraging existing devices like home servers and computers to decentralize data storage. This approach reduces energy consumption, lowers costs, and enhances security by spreading data across thousands of nodes.  

But the benefits go beyond sustainability. The IDC reports that **68% of data generated today is dark data**, discarded due to the high cost of long-term storage. Distributed models make it economically viable to retain this data, improving AI training and business intelligence. As one industry leader put it, “Distributed cloud isn’t just smarter — it’s necessary.”
