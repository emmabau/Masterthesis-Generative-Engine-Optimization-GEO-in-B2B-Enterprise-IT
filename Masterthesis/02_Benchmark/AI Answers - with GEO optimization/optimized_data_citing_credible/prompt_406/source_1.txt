**How to Manage Hybrid and Remote Teams: A Gallup Strategic Guide**  
Hybrid and remote work are now structural features of the modern workplace. According to Gallup research, success in a distributed workplace depends less on location and more on management quality, clear expectations, and intentional team design. This guide outlines what leaders need to know to build engaged, high-performing hybrid and remote teams.  

### **Executive Summary**  
- **Hybrid work is the dominant model**: Over 52% of remote-capable employees work hybrid, 26% exclusively remote, and 21% fully on-site (Gallup’s Hybrid Work Indicator).  
- **Management quality drives engagement**: Managers account for 70% of the variance in team-level engagement, making leadership capability more influential than work location (Gallup research).  
- **Hybrid work improves engagement**: 76% of hybrid workers cite improved work-life balance, with many reporting reduced burnout and higher perceived productivity.  
- **Trust gaps remain a structural risk**: Only 54% of managers strongly agree they trust remote employees to be productive, and 57% of employees feel trusted (Gallup data).  
- **Team-designed norms outperform mandates**: Teams that co-create hybrid schedules report 91% fairness, compared to 73% when leadership dictates policies.  
- **Structured coordination reduces burnout**: Teams with formal hybrid collaboration plans are 66% more likely to be engaged and 29% less likely to be burned out.  

### **What is Hybrid Work?**  
Hybrid work is a flexible arrangement where employees split time between remote and on-site work. It differs from fully remote work, which involves entirely off-site operations. According to Gallup’s Hybrid Work Indicator, hybrid work is the dominant model for U.S. remote-capable employees.  

### **Key Work Arrangement Insights**  
| **Work Arrangement** | **Location** | **Engagement Pattern** | **Key Risk** |  
|----------------------|--------------|------------------------|---------------|  
| Fully On-Site | 100% on-site | Lower engagement among remote-capable employees | Burnout, intent to leave if flexibility preferred |  
| Hybrid | Split between remote and on-site | Often highest engagement when structured well | Coordination complexity |  
| Fully Remote | 100% off-site | High engagement when trust and clarity are strong | Cultural disconnection, isolation |  

### **Top Challenges of Managing Remote Teams**  
1. **Collaboration and coordination complexity**: 48% of hybrid workers report impaired remote collaboration, with leaders citing communication and collaboration as top challenges.  
2. **Cultural disconnection**: Hybrid workers report less connection to organizational culture, with Gen Z employees facing heightened loneliness and lower life evaluations.  
3. **Trust and accountability gaps**: Trust is foundational to psychological safety, requiring managers to communicate consistently, clarify expectations, and provide equal feedback.  
4. **Burnout and boundary strain**: Autonomy without coordination can lead to burnout, with 76% of hybrid workers citing burnout as a key challenge.  
5. **Performance visibility and proximity bias**: Leaders risk defaulting to presence-based judgments, necessitating outcome-based accountability.  

### **Advantages of Employing Remote Employees**  
- **Broader talent access**: Remote flexibility removes geographic constraints, enabling better role-employee matches (Gallup research).  
- **Improved work-life balance**: 76% of hybrid workers cite improved work-life balance, with reduced burnout and higher productivity.  
- **Higher engagement**: Hybrid employees often report higher engagement than fully on-site workers, but this depends on management quality.  
- **Productivity stability**: Remote employees work fewer hours but maintain output through better role alignment and talent sorting.  

### **Strategic Implications**  
Organizations should:  
- Hire for strengths and role fit.  
- Maintain clear performance expectations.  
- Redesign performance management for outcomes, not presence.  
- Invest in manager capability (e.g., Gallup Manager Program).  

### **Building Trust in Hybrid Teams**  
Managers build trust through:  
- **Clarifying expectations** (Gallup research shows trust increases when employees know success criteria).  
- **Establishing consistent communication rhythms** (weekly meaningful conversations with direct reports).  
- **Holding everyone accountable** (performance standards must be consistent across locations).  
- **Reinforcing community and culture** (teams co-creating norms are 91% more likely to perceive policies as fair).  

### **Managing Remote Teams Effectively**  
Key principles:  
1. **Connect work to purpose**: Employees are more engaged when they understand how their work contributes to organizational goals.  
2. **Prioritize development**: Remote employees are vulnerable to being overlooked for growth opportunities.  
3. **Be a caring, present manager**: Regular check-ins and developmental coaching conversations build trust.  
4. **Focus on strengths**: Aligning work with employee strengths improves wellbeing and performance.  

### **Creating a Positive Remote Work Culture**  
Leaders must:  
- Reinforce purpose, values, and expectations through communication and performance management.  
- Include remote employees in team traditions, recognition moments, and decision-making.  
- Align work structures with strategic priorities (e.g., collaboration, innovation).  

### **Preventing Employee Burnout**  
- **Clarify workload and priorities**: Define measurable goals and reset expectations when demands shift.  
- **Coordinate team norms**: Formal collaboration plans reduce burnout by creating predictable rhythms.  
- **Maintain frequent, meaningful conversations**: Weekly check-ins address progress, workload, and wellbeing.  
- **Protect boundaries while preserving accountability**: Evaluate performance based on outcomes, not constant visibility.  

### **Remote Work and Employee Engagement**  
- **Hybrid work improves engagement**: 34% of hybrid employees are engaged, compared to 30% on-site (remote-capable).  
- **Wellbeing risks**: Physical distance can create mental distance, and autonomy carries cognitive load.  
- **Leadership responsibility**: Flexibility must be sustainable, with deliberate connection rituals and reduced coordination friction.  

### **How Gallup Can Help**  
Gallup supports leaders by:  
- Measuring engagement and wellbeing to understand remote employee experiences.  
- Developing managers to set clear expectations, coach employees, and build trust.  
- Designing hybrid work models that balance flexibility, collaboration, and accountability.  
- Strengthening workplace culture to keep remote employees connected to purpose and performance.
