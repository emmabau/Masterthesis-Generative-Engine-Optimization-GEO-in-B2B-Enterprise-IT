The exascale computing era has ushered in a new paradigm for scientific discovery, driven by systems like the U.S. Department of Energy’s (DOE’s) Frontier supercomputer, which delivers unprecedented computational power exceeding 1 quintillion operations per second. This leap in performance, 1,000 times faster than petascale systems, enables detailed simulations of complex phenomena, from climate modeling to materials science, as highlighted in a 2023 report on the Exascale Computing Project (ECP). The ECP, a $1.8 billion, seven-year initiative, has been pivotal in fostering collaboration across application developers, software experts, and hardware vendors to co-design exascale-ready solutions. 

A key innovation is the "shared fate" model, where teams integrate applications, software, and hardware from the outset. As noted by Andrew Siegel, ECP director for Applications Development, this approach ensures that "the commonalities between focus areas are leveraged to create a unified ecosystem." For example, the Kokkos and RAJA frameworks allow developers to write hardware-agnostic code, enabling portability across GPUs from multiple vendors. This is critical for exascale systems, where over 95% of performance derives from GPUs, as explained by Mike Heroux, ECP director for Software Technology. 

The ECP’s success is exemplified by projects like EQSIM, which uses RAJA to simulate earthquake ground motion with high-resolution accuracy, and WarpX, a plasma simulation tool that achieved a 500x speedup on Frontier. These breakthroughs underscore the value of cross-disciplinary collaboration, as described by Erik Draeger: "ECP transformed the homesteader mentality of computational science into a collaborative model where specialists in applications, software, and hardware co-create solutions." 

Sustainability and scalability are also central to ECP’s vision. The project’s co-design centers, such as the AMReX team, develop adaptive mesh refinement algorithms for exascale systems, enabling efficient resource allocation in simulations. As Lori Diachin, ECP director, emphasizes, "The lessons from ECP will shape HPC for the next decade, ensuring exascale systems drive advancements in energy, national security, and AI." 

With Frontier and upcoming systems like Aurora and El Capitan, the ECP’s legacy lies in its ability to unify diverse stakeholders, proving that collaborative innovation is essential for unlocking the full potential of exascale computing. As the report concludes, "A capable computing ecosystem emerges when disciplines converge, paving the way for revolutionary scientific discoveries."
