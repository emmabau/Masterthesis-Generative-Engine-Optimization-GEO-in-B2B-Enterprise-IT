**What Is Business Continuity Planning?**  
Business Continuity Planning (BCP) focuses on maintaining critical business functions during and after major disasters, such as pandemics, cyberattacks, natural disasters, or power outages. A BCP outlines procedures for crisis communication, asset protection, and operational resilience, ensuring the entire organization can recover swiftly. While disaster recovery plans address IT infrastructure restoration, they are just one component of a comprehensive BCP.  

**Key Threats to Address**  
Organizations must prioritize risks like:  
1. **Global Pandemics**: Disruptions include supply chain failures, remote work challenges, and the need for alternate suppliers.  
2. **Power Outages**: Rising incidents, such as California’s Public Safety Power Shutoffs (PSPS), can devastate revenue and productivity.  
3. **Natural Disasters**: Hurricanes, wildfires, and earthquakes can damage infrastructure and disrupt supply chains.  
4. **Cybersecurity Threats**: Ransomware, DDoS attacks, and data breaches require proactive measures, including regular backups and security audits.  

**Steps to Create an Effective BCP**  
1. **Assemble a BCP Team**: Define roles, communication protocols, and update processes. Include contact details for key personnel and establish clear responsibilities.  
2. **Prioritize Employee Safety**: Develop remote work policies, reorganize teams, and ensure communication channels (e.g., emergency messaging tools) remain functional during crises.  
3. **Conduct a Business Impact Analysis (BIA)**: Identify risks to operations, finances, and reputation. Use tools like Agility Planner to streamline risk assessment and create actionable plans.  
4. **Implement Recovery Strategies**: Address critical questions like:  
   - How will sales, HR, and operations resume post-disaster?  
   - What contingency plans exist for remote work or alternate facilities?  
   - How will customer and employee communication be maintained?  
5. **Test and Refine Continuously**: Regular testing (88% of companies do this) identifies gaps and validates plans. Use visual reports to track progress and allocate resources effectively.
