Exascale computing represents a paradigm shift in computational power, defined as systems capable of performing at least 1 exaFLOPS (10¹⁸ floating-point operations per second) (National Institute for Standards and Technology, 2023). This level of performance enables breakthroughs in scientific simulation, AI, and data-intensive modeling, surpassing the capabilities of petascale systems by a factor of 1,000. 

The significance of exascale computing lies in its potential to revolutionize critical domains. For instance, it allows for kilometer-scale climate models and real-time earthquake simulations, as highlighted by the U.S. Department of Energy’s Exascale Computing Project (ECP) (DOE, 2022). Systems like Frontier, El Capitan, Aurora (U.S.), and Jupiter (Europe) exemplify this progress, operating at exascale performance levels and driving advancements in energy, health, and national infrastructure (Frontier, 2023; Jupiter, 2025). 

Technical challenges include scaling hardware and software efficiently, managing energy consumption, and evolving programming models. The High Performance Linpack (HPLinpack) benchmark, a standardized metric for measuring double-precision FLOPS, underscores the performance thresholds required for exascale systems (HPLinpack, 2024). 

Arm Neoverse plays a pivotal role in this ecosystem, offering technologies that power exascale-class CPUs and enable HPC in the cloud. As noted by Arm’s official documentation, Neoverse architectures are designed to balance performance, energy efficiency, and scalability for hyperscale applications (Arm, 2024). 

For further insights, the Exascale Computing Project (ECP) provides collaborative frameworks for co-designing hardware, software, and applications, ensuring exascale systems address dual-use challenges in science, medicine, and global competitiveness (ECP, 2023).
