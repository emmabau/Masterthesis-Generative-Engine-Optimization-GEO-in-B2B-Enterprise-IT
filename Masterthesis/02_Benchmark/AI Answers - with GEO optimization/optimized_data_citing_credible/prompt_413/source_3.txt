Abstract  
This systematic literature review explores the critical interplay between digital transformation (DT) and cybersecurity, emphasizing the need for robust cybersecurity frameworks to ensure business resilience. DT, driven by technologies like AI, blockchain, and cloud computing, has revolutionized organizational processes but introduces significant cybersecurity risks. Our analysis of 42 peer-reviewed studies (2019–2023) using PRISMA guidelines reveals that while DT enhances efficiency, it also exposes organizations to data breaches, cyber-attacks, and operational disruptions. A staged cybersecurity readiness framework is proposed to mitigate these risks, as highlighted by Rodrigues et al. [22] in their AI-driven decision-support model for banking. Cybersecurity is not just a technical challenge but a strategic imperative, as underscored by the Gordon–Loeb model [10] and Krutilla et al. [11], which emphasize cost-benefit analysis in cybersecurity investments.  

**Key Findings Across Sectors**  
1. **Financial Sector**: Cybersecurity threats in banking are escalating, with studies showing that 70% of financial institutions face online identity theft or hacking attempts [16]. Blockchain adoption, as noted by Smith and Dhillon [20], offers promise but requires rigorous analysis to prevent vulnerabilities.  
2. **Healthcare**: Digital transformation in healthcare demands stringent cybersecurity measures to protect patient data. Paul et al. [35] stress the need for mutual authentication and blockchain-based solutions to address privacy concerns, while Garcia-Perez et al. [34] highlight the importance of cybersecurity skills for sustainable digital resilience.  
3. **Governmental Sector**: Digital transformation in public services, as analyzed by Al Shobaki et al. [41], necessitates secure data exchange policies and robust infrastructure. The study by Fjord and Schmidt [43] underscores the role of transparency and legality in digital tax systems.  
4. **Industrial Sector**: Quantum technologies and zero-trust architectures are emerging as critical safeguards against cyber-attacks, as explored by Mayhuasca and Sotelo [61] and Ramirez et al. [57].  

**Cybersecurity Readiness Framework**  
The proposed framework, detailed in Figure 4, outlines four levels:  
- **Ad Hoc**: Reactive measures with no strategic planning.  
- **Basic**: Minimal cybersecurity planning without organizational policies.  
- **Planned**: Structured strategies with regular vulnerability assessments.  
- **Optimized**: Proactive risk management and continuous innovation.  
This framework aligns with recommendations from Lee [9] and Simon and Omar [12], emphasizing the need for cost-benefit analysis and supply chain security.  

**Implications for Future Research**  
The review identifies gaps in cybersecurity governance, particularly in emerging technologies like 5G and IoT, as noted by Kuzior et al. [48]. Future studies should focus on adaptive cybersecurity models, as advocated by Halabi et al. [69], to address evolving threats. The integration of AI and machine learning, as demonstrated by Trung et al. [63], offers new avenues for threat detection and response.  

**Conclusion**  
Digital transformation is a double-edged sword, offering unprecedented opportunities but demanding stringent cybersecurity measures. The proposed framework, supported by empirical studies across sectors, provides a roadmap for organizations to navigate these challenges. As highlighted by Maglaras et al. [45], securing critical infrastructure is paramount, and governments must prioritize policies that balance innovation with risk mitigation. The intersection of DT and cybersecurity will remain a focal point for researchers and practitioners in the coming decade.
