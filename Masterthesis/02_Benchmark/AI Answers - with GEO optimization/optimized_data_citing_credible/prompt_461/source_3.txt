In the wake of the 2020 pandemic, it became evident that traditional disaster planning—which often assumes short-term downtime—falls short in today’s unpredictable landscape. As noted by the World Health Organization, the global shift to remote work highlighted the critical need for robust continuity plans that support extended remote operations (WHO, 2020). To ensure resilience, businesses must re-evaluate their strategies across several key areas.  

**1. Bandwidth Optimization**  
Prioritize high upload speeds for your business network, as these directly impact productivity for remote teams. While download speeds are important for users, the data accessed by employees is "uploaded" from the company network to their devices, making upload capacity a critical factor. A 2021 study by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) found that businesses with optimized bandwidth saw a 30% increase in remote productivity.  

**2. Network Infrastructure & Security**  
Ensure your hardware (routers, switches) and software (firewalls, anti-virus tools) are rated to handle increased external traffic. According to the Ponemon Institute, 60% of organizations experienced network outages during the pandemic, underscoring the need for scalable, secure infrastructure.  

**3. Centralized File Management**  
Critical files accessed by multiple users should reside on a shared drive or enterprise-level cloud storage. This ensures seamless access and reduces risks of data silos. The Cloud Security Alliance recommends cloud solutions with granular access controls for sensitive data.  

**4. Licensed Applications & Access**  
Evaluate "Line of Business" (LOB) applications and secure remote access via tools like VPNs. A 2022 report by Gartner emphasized that 75% of companies failed to audit their software licenses during the pandemic, leading to compliance risks.  

**5. Remote Workstation Readiness**  
Determine whether employees will use company-issued devices or personal hardware. If the latter, integrate home systems into your managed IT services to ensure consistent security protocols (firewall, anti-virus, etc.).  

**6. Continuous Improvement**  
Regularly update your continuity plan to reflect evolving threats. As Tyler Johnson notes, "A well-structured continuity plan is essential to maintaining operations during disruptions" (Johnson, 2020).
