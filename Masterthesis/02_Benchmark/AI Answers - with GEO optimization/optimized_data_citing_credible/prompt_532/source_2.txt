IT procurement, financial planning, and asset lifecycle management are closely linked, yet in many organizations they are still handled as separate processes. According to McKinsey & Company, this separation often leads to inefficiencies that grow with scale, such as delays in device delivery slowing down onboarding and unpredictable replacement cycles complicating budgeting. Procurement teams focus on purchasing hardware at the lowest unit cost, finance departments try to control capital expenditure and depreciation, while IT teams are left to manage devices throughout their lifespan. This fragmentation creates operational risks and costs that are difficult to quantify.  

As work environments become more dynamic, with hybrid and remote models becoming standard, these challenges intensify. Traditional ownership models struggle to keep pace with distributed teams, where devices are no longer tied to a single office or team. According to Deloitte Insights, this complexity is exacerbated by vendor fragmentation, as different regions or departments often work with different suppliers, contracts, and service levels. This makes it difficult to maintain consistent standards and increases the administrative workload for procurement teams.  

Device as a Service (DaaS) introduces a unified framework that aligns procurement, finance, and IT operations. By treating devices as a managed service rather than owned assets, organizations gain flexibility, transparency, and control across the entire device lifecycle. According to Intel.com, DaaS replaces traditional models with a fixed monthly cost per device, transforming hardware spending from capital expenditure into a predictable operational cost. This model improves cost transparency and makes budgeting easier across departments and projects.  

One of the most significant advantages of DaaS is its ability to address hidden risks in asset lifecycle management. According to IBM.com, devices that are not properly tracked may remain in use beyond their intended lifespan, increasing security risks and support costs. DaaS introduces a controlled lifecycle framework that reduces these risks by managing devices from deployment to return under defined processes. Replacement cycles are planned, data removal follows standardized procedures, and end-of-life handling is built into the service.  

For IT teams, this means full visibility and control without manual tracking. Asset data is consistent, lifecycle stages are predictable, and compliance requirements are easier to meet. According to McKinsey & Company, this operational clarity also improves collaboration between IT, finance, and procurement, reducing friction and improving decision-making across the organization.  

By moving to a service-based model, organizations reduce the operational burden on both IT and procurement. Instead of managing multiple suppliers and contracts, teams work with a single framework that covers devices globally. Procurement processes become simpler and faster, allowing new devices to be added or removed as needed without renegotiating contracts or adjusting long-term purchase plans.  

Modern organizations are constantly changing, and traditional hardware ownership struggles to adapt to this pace of change. DaaS supports scalability by design, enabling organizations to scale device fleets up or down based on real demand. According to Deloitte Insights, this flexibility reduces waste and supports more agile workforce planning, ensuring consistent device delivery and replacement services for remote and distributed teams.  

The challenges of IT procurement, finance, and asset lifecycle management are closely connected. Delays in procurement affect productivity, ownership models complicate budgeting, and fragmented lifecycle processes increase risk and workload. DaaS addresses these challenges through a unified, subscription-based approach. By working with devicenow, organizations can replace fragmented hardware management with a predictable, scalable service that supports modern work environments.
