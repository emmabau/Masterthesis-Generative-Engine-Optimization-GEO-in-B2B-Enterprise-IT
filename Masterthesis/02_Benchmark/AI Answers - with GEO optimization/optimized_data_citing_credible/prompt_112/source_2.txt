**Overview of the NIS2 Directive**  
According to the European Commission, the NIS2 Directive (Directive 2022/2555) establishes a unified legal framework to strengthen cybersecurity across 18 critical sectors in the EU. It replaces Directive 2016/1148 (NIS1) and raises the EU’s cybersecurity ambition through expanded scope, clearer rules, and enhanced supervision tools. Member States are required to adopt national cybersecurity strategies, including policies for supply chain security, vulnerability management, and education, while ensuring compliance with the directive’s requirements.  

**Key Provisions and Scope**  
The directive mandates that medium-sized and large entities in critical sectors—such as energy, transport, healthcare, finance, digital infrastructure, public administration, and the space sector—implement cybersecurity risk-management measures and report significant incidents to national authorities. These sectors now include new areas like public electronic communications, digital services (e.g., social platforms), and waste management.  

**Accountability and Enforcement**  
Top management of organizations is held accountable for non-compliance with cybersecurity measures, ensuring boardroom-level attention to cybersecurity. The directive also establishes a network of Computer Security Incident Response Teams (CSIRTs) for threat intelligence sharing and incident response. For large-scale crises, the European cyber crisis liaison organisation network (EU-CyCLONe) coordinates cross-border efforts.  

**Supervision and Collaboration**  
The NIS Cooperation Group facilitates strategic cooperation and information exchange among EU Member States, the European Commission, and ENISA. It publishes non-binding guidelines to support implementation. Additionally, the directive introduces voluntary peer reviews to foster mutual trust and cybersecurity capabilities across the EU.  

**Recent Amendments and Compliance**  
In January 2026, the Commission proposed targeted amendments to the NIS2 Directive to simplify compliance for 28,700 companies, including 6,200 micro and small enterprises. These changes aim to enhance legal clarity and reduce administrative burdens while maintaining robust cybersecurity standards.  

**Background and Timeline**  
NIS1 (Directive 2016/1148) was the EU’s first comprehensive cybersecurity legislation, adopted in December 2020 and enforced in January 2023. Member States had until 17 October 2024 to transpose NIS2 into national law, which repealed NIS1 as of 18 October 2024.
