**Key Implications for Businesses:**
1. **Board-Level Cybersecurity Accountability**  
   NIS2 mandates that executives bear direct responsibility for cybersecurity, shifting from policy approval to operational oversight.    Regulators now prioritize real-time incident response over static compliance checks. For example, the directive requires continuous risk assessments, active vulnerability management, and real-time visibility into critical assets (ENISA, 2023). 3. **Expanded Scope Beyond Traditional Sectors**  
   NIS2 applies to 18 critical sectors, including healthcare, energy, transport, and finance, and covers companies with over 50 employees or 10 million euros in annual revenue. This includes many previously unregulated entities, with supply chain risks now explicitly addressed (EU NIS2 Regulation, 2022).

4. **Mandatory Incident Disclosure Frameworks**  
   Under NIS2, organizations must disclose incidents immediately and provide detailed reports within days. This requires seamless coordination between technical, legal, and executive teams, as well as forensic capabilities to meet reporting deadlines (ENISA, 2023).

Organizations treating NIS2 as a checkbox exercise risk falling behind.
