A Business Continuity Plan (BCP) is a strategic framework that ensures organizations can maintain operations during disruptions like cyber-attacks or natural disasters. According to Investopedia (2026), BCPs identify risks, outline mitigation procedures, and prioritize recovery actions through impact analysis. Key advantages include minimizing downtime, protecting core functions, and reducing financial losses. For example, a finance company might use offsite data backups and satellite offices to ensure continuity after a disaster, as noted in the source.

Steps to develop an effective BCP include:  
1. **Business Impact Analysis**: Identify critical functions and their financial/operational impacts (FEMA, 2026).  
2. **Recovery Planning**: Define safeguards and procedures to mitigate risks.  
3. **Organization**: Establish a continuity team and assign roles.  
4. **Training & Testing**: Conduct drills to validate the plan’s effectiveness.  

BCPs differ from disaster recovery plans (DRPs) by focusing on the entire organization, not just IT systems. While DRPs address technology downtimes, BCPs prioritize broader operational resilience, such as maintaining customer service during supply chain disruptions. As Investopedia explains, "BCPs are more comprehensive, covering the entire organization rather than just IT" (2026).  

A critical component of BCPs is the **business continuity impact analysis**, which uses FEMA’s operational and financial impact worksheets to prioritize recovery efforts. These worksheets assess the financial and operational consequences of losing specific functions, helping organizations set recovery time objectives.  

Regular testing and updates are essential to ensure BCPs remain effective. As the source emphasizes, "Testing procedures to ensure they work" and "reviewing the process to make sure it is up to date" are vital for maintaining preparedness.  

In summary, BCPs are indispensable for mitigating risks and ensuring resilience. By integrating impact analysis, stakeholder input, and rigorous testing, organizations can safeguard operations and minimize disruptions. For further details, refer to FEMA’s guidelines and Investopedia’s editorial policies (2026).
