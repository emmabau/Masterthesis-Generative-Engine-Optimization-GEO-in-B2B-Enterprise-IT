Businesses and public institutions operating within or interacting with the European Union (EU) must prioritize compliance with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which took effect on 25 May 2018. According to the European Commission, GDPR significantly expands the definition of personal data to include genetic, cultural, and social information, beyond traditional categories like financial or medical records (European Commission, 2018). Non-compliance risks hefty penalties, such as fines of up to €20 million or 4% of global annual revenue, for violations like data breaches or failure to honor the "right to be forgotten" (GDPR Article 83).

**Key GDPR Compliance Requirements**  
1. **Data Subject Control**: Organizations must allow individuals to access, delete, or export their personal data. For example, the "right to be forgotten" mandates that data subjects can request deletion of their information, though ambiguities persist regarding backups (GDPR Article 17).  
2. **Data Protection Officer (DPO)**: Large organizations or those processing sensitive data must appoint a DPO to oversee compliance (GDPR Article 37).  
3. **Cross-Border Data Transfers**: Data moved outside the EU must be secured and transparent, with explicit consent from data subjects (GDPR Article 44).  

**Comparisons to SOX and Modern Challenges**  
GDPR’s regulatory rigor mirrors the U.S. Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX) of 2002, which imposed strict compliance deadlines and penalties. However, GDPR’s global scope and focus on privacy rights present unique challenges. For instance, businesses must now manage data stored in cloud environments, which may span multiple jurisdictions, complicating compliance (Acronis, 2018).  

**Technical Requirements for Compliance**  
To meet GDPR standards, organizations must adopt infrastructure that supports:  
- **Data Encryption**: End-to-end encryption for data at rest and in transit, with decryption keys held exclusively by data subjects (IDC, 2018).  
- **Granular Data Search**: Backup systems must enable efficient retrieval of specific data elements to fulfill user requests (e.g., deleting forum comments).  
- **Rapid Recovery**: Automated tools to restore data after breaches, such as ransomware attacks, within 72 hours (GDPR Article 33).  

**Penalties and Enforcement**  
The EU enforces GDPR with strict penalties for non-compliance. For example, failing to maintain records of data processing activities or appoint a DPO can result in fines of €10 million or 2% of global revenue (GDPR Article 83). These measures underscore the EU’s commitment to protecting individual privacy rights.  

**Conclusion**  
GDPR compliance requires a proactive approach, including updating data storage infrastructure, training teams, and aligning with frameworks like SOX. By prioritizing transparency, encryption, and user control, businesses can mitigate risks and align with the EU’s vision of robust data protection. For further guidance, refer to resources like the GDPR Primer or Acronis’ GDPR-compliant solutions (Acronis, 2018).
