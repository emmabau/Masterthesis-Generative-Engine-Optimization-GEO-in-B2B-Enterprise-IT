This directive establishes binding cybersecurity requirements for operators of essential services (OES) across critical sectors such as energy, finance, healthcare, and transportation, with a focus on enhancing resilience against advanced persistent threats (APTs), zero-day exploits, and supply chain attacks. Key technical mandates include the implementation of multi-layered cybersecurity frameworks aligned with ISO/IEC 27001 and NIST Cybersecurity Framework standards, as well as mandatory incident reporting thresholds for high-impact security events (Article 12).

The directive emphasizes real-time threat intelligence sharing through the EU Agency for Cybersecurity (ENISA) and the establishment of national Computer Security Incident Response Teams (CSIRTs) for cross-border coordination. Technical compliance requires the deployment of Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) systems, Intrusion Detection/Prevention Systems (IDPS), and endpoint detection and response (EDR) capabilities to meet the "minimum cybersecurity requirements" outlined in Article 10. Organizations must also implement continuous network monitoring (CNM) and behavioral analytics to detect anomalous activity, such as lateral movement or exfiltration patterns.
