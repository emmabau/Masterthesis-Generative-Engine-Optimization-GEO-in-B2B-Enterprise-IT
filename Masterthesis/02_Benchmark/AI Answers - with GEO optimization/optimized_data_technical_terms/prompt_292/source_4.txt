Abstract
Context
Big Data Analytics (BDA) represents a transformative IT practice with profound implications across organizational functions, necessitating robust frameworks for data security, privacy, and compliance. The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) introduces stringent requirements for data protection, challenging organizations to integrate privacy-by-design principles into their operational models. Enterprise Architecture Management (EAM) emerges as a critical approach to align organizational resources with strategic objectives, offering a holistic framework for addressing GDPR compliance in BDA contexts. This study investigates the intersection of EAM, data governance, and GDPR through a Systematic Mapping Review (SMR) of existing literature, identifying gaps and opportunities for future research.

Objective
This research aims to: (1) survey the state-of-the-art in security, privacy, and protection of big data, with a focus on GDPR and Data Protection Directive (DPD) frameworks; (2) evaluate the potential of EAM to address GDPR compliance challenges in BDA environments; and (3) identify emerging trends and research gaps in this domain.

Method
A Systematic Mapping Review (SMR) methodology was employed, combining systematic literature review (SLR) techniques with Template Analysis for thematic coding. Search strings were constructed using keywords from the six mapping research questions (MRQs), targeting Scopus, IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect, and Web of Science databases. Inclusion criteria prioritized peer-reviewed journals, conferences, and grey literature, while excluding non-English publications and non-technical solutions.

Results
The SMR identified 332 relevant studies published between 2013 and 2020, with a sharp increase in GDPR-related research post-2017. Key findings include: (1) limited exploration of EAM's role in GDPR compliance; (2) growing emphasis on data governance, risk management, and privacy-by-design principles; (3) challenges in integrating EAM with technical solutions for data protection; and (4) emerging trends in ethical AI, data provenance, and cross-border data flows. The study highlights the need for more research on EAM's systemic integration with GDPR requirements and its impact on organizational resilience.

Conclusions
While EAM shows promise in addressing GDPR challenges, its application in BDA contexts remains underexplored. Future research should focus on developing EAM frameworks that incorporate GDPR compliance as a core component, leveraging systematic approaches to data governance, risk mitigation, and privacy-enhancing technologies. This study provides a foundation for advancing research on the intersection of EAM, BDA, and regulatory compliance.

Introduction
Big Data Analytics (BDA) has revolutionized organizational operations, enabling insights from vast, heterogeneous datasets. However, this transformation introduces significant security and privacy risks, particularly under the GDPR's stringent requirements. The GDPR mandates data minimization, purpose limitation, and accountability, necessitating organizational-wide changes to data handling practices. Enterprise Architecture Management (EAM) offers a structured approach to align IT infrastructure with strategic goals, making it a potential solution for GDPR compliance in BDA contexts. This study investigates how EAM can address GDPR challenges through a systematic review of existing literature, identifying gaps and opportunities for future research.

Research Methodology
The study employed a Systematic Mapping Review (SMR) methodology, combining systematic literature review (SLR) techniques with Template Analysis for thematic coding. Search strings were constructed using keywords from the six mapping research questions (MRQs), targeting Scopus, IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect, and Web of Science databases. Inclusion criteria prioritized peer-reviewed journals, conferences, and grey literature, while excluding non-English publications and non-technical solutions. The search process involved three strings: (1) BDA and GDPR/DPD frameworks; (2) BDA, EAM, and GDPR/DPD; and (3) BDA and EAM without explicit security/privacy focus. The resulting 332 studies were analyzed using Template Analysis to identify thematic patterns and research trends.

Key Findings
The SMR revealed: (1) A surge in GDPR-related research post-2017, with 99% of studies published from 2013 onwards; (2) Dominance of journals like Computer Law & Security Review and conferences like IEEE Big Data Congress in publishing relevant work; (3) Focus on data governance, risk management, and privacy-by-design principles; (4) Limited exploration of EAM's role in GDPR compliance; and (5) Emerging trends in ethical AI, data provenance, and cross-border data flows. The study highlights the need for more research on EAM's systemic integration with GDPR requirements and its impact on organizational resilience.

Discussion
The findings underscore the growing importance of EAM in addressing GDPR challenges in BDA contexts. While EAM offers a holistic framework for aligning IT infrastructure with strategic goals, its application in GDPR compliance remains underexplored. Future research should focus on developing EAM frameworks that incorporate GDPR compliance as a core component, leveraging systematic approaches to data governance, risk mitigation, and privacy-enhancing technologies. This study provides a foundation for advancing research on the intersection of EAM, BDA, and regulatory compliance.
