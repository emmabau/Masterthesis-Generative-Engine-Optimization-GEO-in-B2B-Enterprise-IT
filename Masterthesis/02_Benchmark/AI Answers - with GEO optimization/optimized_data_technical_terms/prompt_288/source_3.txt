Exascale Deep Learning for Climate Analytics: A Technical Breakdown of Segmentation Architectures and System Innovations

Abstract
This study presents a novel approach to extreme weather pattern detection using advanced deep learning architectures. We demonstrate the scalability of Tiramisu and DeepLabv3+ networks on exascale systems, achieving sustained throughput of 21.0 PF/s (Tiramisu) and 325.8 PF/s (DeepLabv3+) with 5300 P100 GPUs and 27360 V100 GPUs respectively. The use of FP16 Tensor Cores enables a peak throughput of 1.13 EF/s and sustained 999.0 PF/s for DeepLabv3+ at 27360 GPUs. Our work introduces system-level optimizations for data staging, hierarchical all-reduce communication, and algorithmic innovations in loss functions and gradient optimization.

I. Technical Justification
The application of segmentation architectures to climate datasets enables state-of-the-art weather pattern masks with precision exceeding 98.2% for background class identification. The use of mixed-precision training (FP16) combined with Tensor Cores achieves a 2.3x speedup compared to FP32. Our system-level innovations include:
- Distributed data staging with 8-threaded parallel I/O achieving 11.98 GB/s bandwidth
- Hierarchical all-reduce with NCCL and MPI hybridization for 90.7% parallel efficiency
- Weighted loss functions with inverse square root class frequencies for class imbalance mitigation
