[edit]Etymology 1
[edit]From Middle English *we*, derived from Old English *wē* (“we”), which traces to Proto-West Germanic *wiʀ, Proto-Germanic *wīz, *wiz (“we”), and ultimately Proto-Indo-European *wéy (“we (plural)”). Cognate with Scots *wee, we (“we”), North Frisian *we (“we”), West Frisian *wy (“we”), Low German *wi (“we”), Dutch *we, wij (“we”), German *wir (“we”), Danish, Swedish, and Norwegian *vi (“we”), Icelandic *vérr, við (“we”), Avestan *vaēm, Sanskrit *vayám.

Pronoun
[edit]we (first-person plural personal pronoun nominative case, objective case *us*, possessive determiner *our*, possessive pronoun *ours*, reflexive *ourselves*, reflexive singular *ourself*)
- Denotes inclusion of the speaker(s)/writer(s) in a group, excluding the person(s) being addressed (exclusive we).
- Example: "We, the liberal and progressive people of the world, are at war with the far right to save the earth." (Paul Mason, *The Guardian*, 2017)
- Inclusive we: "We need to have a talk, you and me."
- Editorial we: "We will in due course state our reasons for publishing the name of the accused." (Hong Kong Legislative Council, 2021)
- Institutional we: "As the government, we have a duty to ensure public safety."
- Royal we: "We are Queen of all these Isles." (Royal pronoun, reflexive *ourself*)
- Colloquial inclusive we: "Good evening everyone. How are we all tonight?"
- Nurse's we: "Hello Mrs Miggins. How are we feeling this morning?"
- Third-person reference: "I've just been to see Mrs Miggins. And how is she? I'm afraid we aren't too good today."
- Expository we: "To solve the equation, we first collect all the terms in x on one side."
- Self-referential we: "Now then ... let me see ... I hope I'm doing this right ... if we just connect these two wires together ..."
- Regional variant: "We (the administration) propose to make the following amendments..." (CMAB C4/9/1, 2021)
- Anaphoric we: "We live and learn." "We still have much to learn about quantum mechanics."
