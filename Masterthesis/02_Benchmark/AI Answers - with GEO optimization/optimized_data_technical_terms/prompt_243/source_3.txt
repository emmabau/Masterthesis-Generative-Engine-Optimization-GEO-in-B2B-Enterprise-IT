This perspective article explores the convergence of HPC, ML, and data-centric methodologies to enhance materials discovery, emphasizing the role of digital twins, knowledge engineering, and cloud-based infrastructures. The proposed framework leverages HPC clusters, ML algorithms (e.g., deep learning, reinforcement learning), and semantic technologies (e.g., ontologies, knowledge graphs) to create interoperable, reproducible workflows for materials design and characterization.

Key innovations include the development of the MAMBO ontology (Materials and Molecules Basic Ontology), a semantic framework enabling structured representation of molecular systems, simulations, and experimental data. MAMBO integrates concepts like molecular aggregates, computational methods (e.g., density functional theory (DFT), molecular dynamics (MD)), and property prediction models, facilitating semantic interoperability across multiscale simulations. This ontology supports the integration of heterogeneous data sources, including experimental databases (e.g., Materials Project, NOMAD) and computational results from quantum chemistry codes (e.g., Quantum ESPRESSO, LAMMPS).

The paper highlights the critical role of cloud computing in materials research, with platforms like Materials Cloud and JupyterHub enabling scalable access to HPC resources and collaborative workflows. Tools such as Jupyter Notebooks, KNIME, and Galaxy provide interactive environments for data analysis, simulation, and machine learning model training, while containerization technologies (Docker, Singularity) ensure reproducibility across heterogeneous computing environments. These tools are essential for implementing AI-driven workflows, such as predicting charge transport properties in amorphous molecular materials through hybrid DFT-Monte Carlo simulations.

The integration of digital twins in nanomaterials development is exemplified by workflows combining molecular dynamics simulations (e.g., GROMACS) with machine learning models trained on high-throughput datasets. This approach enables predictive modeling of material properties, such as electronic coupling in molecular aggregates, with computational efficiency gains of up to 100x compared to traditional methods. The paper also addresses challenges in HPC resource management, demonstrating how Kubernetes orchestration and containerized workflows can optimize GPU utilization for AI-driven materials discovery.

By establishing a semantic infrastructure for materials data, this work paves the way for standardized, FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) data practices. The proposed framework aligns with emerging trends in quantum computing and edge AI, positioning materials science at the forefront of digital transformation. These advancements are critical for accelerating the discovery of novel nanomaterials with tailored properties, such as high thermal conductivity or enhanced mechanical strength, through data-driven design and simulation.
