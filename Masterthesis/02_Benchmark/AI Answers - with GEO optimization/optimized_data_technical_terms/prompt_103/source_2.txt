Ilias Iliadis, in his research published in the *International Journal on Advances in Networks and Services*, presents a comprehensive analysis of NVMe storage virtualization techniques in public cloud infrastructures. Modern public clouds are increasingly adopting Non-Volatile Memory Express (NVMe) storage to address the escalating demands of I/O throughput and sub-millisecond latency for workloads such as real-time analytics and AI training. The paper evaluates three NVMe virtualization paradigms:  
1. **PCIe Device Passthrough**: Enables direct assignment of physical NVMe controllers to virtual machines (VMs), minimizing latency via hypervisor-level DMA bypass. This method achieves near-metal performance but requires strict resource partitioning and is limited by PCIe topology constraints.  
2. **Virtual Block Device (VBD) Emulation**: Utilizes hypervisor-managed virtualization layers to abstract NVMe devices, offering flexibility but introducing ~15-20% overhead in IOPS (Input/Output Operations Per Second) compared to passthrough.  
3. **SPDK Virtual Host Target (VHT)**: Leverages the Storage Performance Development Kit (SPDK) to implement user-space NVMe drivers, enabling RDMA-enabled protocols and achieving 20-30% lower latency than traditional VM-based solutions.  

The study benchmarks these methods on IBM Cloud's NVMe infrastructure, highlighting trade-offs between performance, security (e.g., secure virtualization frameworks), and scalability across 100+ VMs. Key findings include the superiority of SPDK-VHT in high-concurrency scenarios and the critical role of QoS mechanisms in mitigating resource contention.  

Additional foundational work includes:  
- *IPDPS 2022* (Juan Miguel De Haro et al.): Advances in distributed NVMe-over-Fabrics (NVMe-oF) architectures.  
- *MLSys 2020* (Hazar Yueksel et al.): Optimization of NVMe storage for machine learning workloads.  
- *ARPA-E COOLERCHIPS Kickoff Meeting 2023* (Timothy Chainer et al.): Energy-efficient NVMe design for cloud-scale systems.  

These contributions collectively define the state-of-the-art in NVMe virtualization, offering actionable insights for cloud providers and developers seeking to optimize storage performance in virtualized environments.
