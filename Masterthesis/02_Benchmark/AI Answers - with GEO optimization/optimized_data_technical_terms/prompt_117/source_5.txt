Abstract
Modern buildings face escalating demands for energy efficiency optimization, driven by global sustainability goals and the need to reduce carbon footprints. This study investigates critical parameters for energy management systems (EMS) in smart buildings, leveraging IoT and AI-driven analytics. By analyzing three reference buildings with distinct operational contexts, we demonstrate how integrating real-time occupancy data, environmental sensors, and predictive modeling can achieve up to 23% energy savings. Our framework incorporates non-intrusive load monitoring (NILM), semantic ontologies, and adaptive control strategies to optimize thermal comfort while minimizing energy waste. Key contributions include a novel approach to contextualized energy management, validated through empirical experiments in academic, industrial, and commercial settings.

1. Introduction
Buildings account for 39% of global energy consumption, with HVAC systems alone responsible for 50% of this usage [1]. The EU Directive 2010/31/EU mandates energy performance improvements through standardized metrics like CEN/EN 15251 [2]. Our work addresses the gap in holistic energy management by integrating IoT-enabled sensors, machine learning algorithms, and semantic technologies to create adaptive EMS. This approach enables real-time optimization of energy distribution, occupant comfort, and system efficiency, as demonstrated through case studies in university campuses, smart offices, and industrial facilities.

2. Related Work
Existing solutions often focus on isolated subsystems, neglecting the interdependencies between occupancy patterns, environmental conditions, and energy consumption. While tools like EnergyPlus [3] provide simulation capabilities, they lack real-time adaptability. Recent advances in semantic web technologies [4] and edge computing [5] offer new avenues for contextual awareness. Our work extends these by proposing a hybrid system combining NILM, ontological data integration, and reinforcement learning for dynamic energy optimization.

3. Methodology
The proposed framework employs a multi-layered architecture:
- **Data Acquisition Layer**: Integrates heterogeneous sensors (temperature, occupancy, power consumption) with NILM techniques for appliance-level disaggregation
- **Semantic Processing Layer**: Utilizes ontologies (OWL, RDF) for contextual data fusion and rule-based decision making
- **Control Optimization Layer**: Implements adaptive control algorithms (PID, RBF networks) for real-time energy management

Key innovations include:
- Context-aware energy distribution algorithms
- Occupancy prediction models using particle filters
- Ontology-driven semantic interoperability for multi-system integration

4. Experimental Validation
Three case studies were conducted:
1. **University Campus**: Achieved 20% reduction in HVAC energy use through occupancy-aware scheduling
2. **Smart Office**: Demonstrated 23% energy savings using adaptive lighting and thermal control
3. **Industrial Facility**: Reduced peak load by 18% through predictive maintenance and load shifting

Technical metrics include:
- 40.5% HVAC energy contribution in monitored buildings
- 0.89 correlation between occupancy levels and energy consumption
- 1.5m localization accuracy for occupant tracking

5. Results and Discussion
The system's ability to dynamically adjust to changing conditions is evidenced by:
- 14-30% energy savings in controlled environments
- 23.12% average reduction in cooling and lighting energy
- 95% data fusion accuracy through semantic integration

These results validate the effectiveness of context-aware energy management systems in achieving sustainability goals while maintaining occupant comfort. The framework's modular design enables scalability across different building types and operational contexts.

6. Conclusion
This work presents a comprehensive approach to energy-efficient building management through the integration of IoT, AI, and semantic technologies. The validated framework demonstrates significant energy savings across diverse building types, offering a scalable solution for achieving global sustainability targets. Future work will focus on expanding the system's capabilities through edge AI and federated learning for distributed energy management.
