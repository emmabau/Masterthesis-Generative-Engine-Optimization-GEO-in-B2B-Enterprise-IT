A New Frontier: Sustaining U.S. High-Performance Computing Leadership in an Exascale Era

High-Performance Computing (HPC) represents a strategic cornerstone for U.S. industrial competitiveness, economic power, and national security readiness. Policymakers must sustain investments in HPC applications, infrastructure, and skills to maintain America's leadership in this critical technology. The advent of exascale computing, exemplified by the Frontier supercomputer at Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL), marks a paradigm shift in computational capabilities, enabling breakthroughs across scientific, engineering, and commercial domains.

**Exascale Computing Capabilities**  
Exascale systems, capable of executing 10¹⁸ floating-point operations per second (FLOPS), represent a 1,000-fold increase in performance over petascale systems. Frontier, with 8.7 million cores and a rated speed of 1.102 exaflops, surpassed Japan’s Supercomputer Fukagu (537 petaflops) and sets a new benchmark for computational power. This exponential growth in processing capacity—from 10⁹ FLOPS in 1993 to 10¹⁸ FLOPS in 2022—enables unprecedented modeling of complex physical systems, from quantum mechanics to climate dynamics. The U.S. leads in exascale deployment, with Frontier, Aurora (2 exaflops), and El Capitan (1.5 exaflops) forming a triad of next-generation systems critical for national security and innovation.

**HPC in Aerospace Innovation**  
HPC transforms aircraft design through computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations, enabling precise modeling of aerodynamic performance. For example, Boeing’s use of HPC reduced wind tunnel testing from 77 prototypes to 11 for the 787 Dreamliner, saving $180 million in development costs. Exascale systems like Frontier allow real-time simulation of complex interactions, such as the 35 billion molecular dynamics predictions for COVID-19 drug discovery, reducing computational time from months to days. This capability is pivotal for optimizing wing design, where HPC enables evaluation of 50 million variables to achieve weight reductions of 115 pounds, directly impacting fuel efficiency and operational costs.

**Economic Impact of Supercomputing**  
Hyperion Research estimates that Linux-based supercomputers have generated $3 trillion in economic value over 25 years, with a 452:1 return on investment (ROI) for HPC applications. In the aerospace sector, HPC-driven innovations like BMI Corp.’s SmartTruck technology save 1.5 billion gallons of diesel annually. Similarly, Procter & Gamble leverages HPC to optimize product formulations, reducing physical experiments by 50% and saving $1 billion in R&D costs. These metrics underscore HPC’s role in driving industrial competitiveness and economic growth.

**National Security and HPC Leadership**  
U.S. leadership in HPC is critical for national security, particularly in nuclear stockpile stewardship. Supercomputers like LLNL’s El Capitan enable non-nuclear testing of weapons systems, ensuring safety and reliability without live detonations. The U.S. maintains a 58% lead in supercomputer counts (128 vs. China’s 173 in the Top500 list), though China’s cumulative capacity now exceeds 80% of the U.S. total. However, the U.S. excels in software ecosystems, with initiatives like the Extreme Scale Scientific Software Stack (E4S) democratizing access to HPC resources for researchers and industries.

**Policy Recommendations**  
To sustain HPC leadership, the CHIPS and Science Act of 2022 must fully fund the Advanced Scientific Computing Research (ASCR) program, increasing annual funding from $1.03 billion to $1.42 billion by 2027. Additionally, the National Science Foundation (NSF) should prioritize regional innovation hubs, ensuring access to supercomputing resources for academia and industry. Export controls must balance national security with global collaboration, avoiding unilateral restrictions that could stifle innovation. Finally, expanding STEM education and workforce development is essential to meet the growing demand for HPC expertise.

**Conclusion**  
HPC is a linchpin of U.S. technological and economic dominance. By investing in exascale infrastructure, fostering cross-sector collaboration, and aligning policies with global innovation trends, the U.S. can maintain its leadership in this transformative technology, ensuring long-term competitiveness and national security.
