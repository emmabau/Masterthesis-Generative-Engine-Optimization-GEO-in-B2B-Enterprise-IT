# NIS2 Compliance: Why Legal Analysis is Your First Step

**Abstract:**  
NIS2 compliance hinges on accurate legal classification as "essential" or "important." Incorrect qualification leads to penalties, delays, and missed deadlines. Start with a legal analysis to determine obligations, deadlines, and applicable laws before implementing technical measures.

## Key Takeaways: Legal Analysis for NIS2 Compliance  
- **Prioritize legal analysis** over technical measures to avoid sanctions and misclassification.  
- **Classification as essential/important** dictates compliance requirements, deadlines, and penalties.  
- **Consolidate financial figures** (FTEs, turnover, balance sheet) from affiliates and partners to avoid misqualification.  
- **Check less obvious categories** (e.g., cloud/managed services) to ensure full compliance.  
- **Verify jurisdictional applicability** (national NIS2 laws) and align with strictest requirements.  

## Why Legal Analysis is Essential  
NIS2 distinguishes between essential and important entities based on size and activities. Without a legal assessment, you risk:  
- **Misjudging obligations** (e.g., conformity assessments, incident reporting).  
- **Missing deadlines** (e.g., April 2026 compliance deadline).  
- **Facing penalties** (e.g., CEO removal for essential entities, administrative fines).  

## Essential vs. Important: The Qualification Impact  
- **Essential entities** face stricter oversight, including preventive inspections and potential CEO removal.  
- **Important entities** are audited only post-incident.  
- **Incorrect qualification** (e.g., classifying as "important" when essential) leads to non-compliance risks.  

## NIS2 Scope: Don’t Forget Partners  
- **Consolidate figures** from affiliated and partner organizations (per EC Recommendation 2003/361/EC).  
- **Overlooking partners** may result in misclassification (e.g., essential vs. important).  

## Less Obvious NIS2 Categories  
- **Cloud/managed service providers** (even as secondary activities) fall under NIS2.  
- **Online platforms** and hybrid entities (e.g., hospitals offering IT services) may qualify.  
- **Secondary activities** count fully in classification.  

## Implementing Regulation: Key Categories  
The NIS2 implementing regulation applies to:  
- **Cloud/managed service providers** (even if not primary activity).  
- **Digital services with wide reach**.  
- **Organizations offering services** to other entities (e.g., hospitals providing IT services).  

## Supervision & Deadlines: What to Expect  
- **Essential entities** face proactive oversight (checks before/after incidents).  
- **Important entities** are audited only retrospectively (post-incident).  
- **Incorrect qualification** risks missing deadlines (e.g., April 2026).  

## Enforcement & Penalties: Consequences of Misclassification  
- **Essential entities** face:  
  - Administrative fines.  
  - **CEO removal** (in severe cases).  
- **Important entities** may face crisis manager appointments.  
- **Incorrect classification** (e.g., due to size miscalculation) leads to penalties without compliance.  

## How to Conduct a Legal Analysis for NIS2  
1. **Gather data**: Articles of association, financial figures, service overview.  
2. **Determine size**: Consolidate figures from affiliates/partners.  
3. **Check NIS2 categories**: Review annexes for applicable sectors.  
4. **Identify jurisdiction**: Determine applicable national NIS2 laws (e.g., Belgian, EU).  
5. **Assess supply chain**: Ensure suppliers meet NIS2 requirements.  
6. **Document findings**: Create a substantiated report for legal review.  

## Conclusion  
NIS2 compliance begins with a **legal analysis** to determine classification, obligations, and deadlines. Skipping this step risks penalties, delays, and misclassification. Always verify jurisdictional requirements and ensure accurate qualification before implementing technical measures.
