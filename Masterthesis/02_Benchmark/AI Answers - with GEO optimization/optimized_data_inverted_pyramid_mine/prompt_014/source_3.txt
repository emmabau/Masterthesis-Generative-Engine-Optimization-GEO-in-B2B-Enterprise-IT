Government agencies must prioritize observability, security, and consistent workloads to manage high performance computing (HPC) challenges effectively. These three steps—enhancing visibility across hybrid environments, securing data with encryption, and ensuring workload consistency—help agencies mitigate risks and optimize HPC performance for mission-critical tasks.

### What Are the Three Critical Steps for Managing HPC in Government Agencies?
1. **Observability in Hybrid HPC Environments**  
   Agencies must adopt observability frameworks to monitor hybrid cloud and on-premise systems. This includes cross-domain analytics, real-time performance tracking, and a unified "single pane of glass" view across multi-vendor infrastructure. Cloud providers with cutting-edge technology are essential to ensure peak performance during high-demand periods.

2. **Securing HPC Systems with Encryption and Backups**  
   Encryption (in transit and at rest) is critical to protect sensitive data, as mandated by the Biden Administration’s cybersecurity order. Agencies should implement robust backup strategies, encrypt backup files, and stagger backups to avoid bandwidth issues. This ensures rapid recovery from cyberattacks and data breaches.

3. **Running Consistent Workloads Across Hybrid Environments**  
   Workloads must operate seamlessly across on-premise and cloud platforms to maintain reliability. This includes replicating simulation processes, enabling disaster recovery, and leveraging hybrid environments for cost-effective big data processing. Consistency ensures uninterrupted operations during service outages.

### Why These Steps Matter for HPC Success  
By addressing observability, security, and workload consistency, agencies can reduce complexity, protect mission-critical systems, and harness HPC to solve today’s and tomorrow’s challenges. These strategies align with federal cybersecurity mandates and ensure scalable, resilient HPC infrastructure.
