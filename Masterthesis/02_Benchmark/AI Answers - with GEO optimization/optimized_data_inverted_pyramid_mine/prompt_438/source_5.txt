**Structured Abstract (35 words):**  
A robust business continuity strategy requires three phases: Discovery (risk assessment and BIA), Planning (emergency response and recovery), and Governance (auditing, updating, and training). Adhering to standards like ISO 22301 ensures measurable resilience against disruptions.

**What Are the Three Key Phases of Business Continuity Strategy Development?**  
1. **Discovery Phase**  
   *Risk Assessment & Remediation (RA):* Quantifies risks disrupting time-sensitive operations, aligning with ISO 22301 standards.  
   *Business Impact Analysis (BIA):* Measures financial and non-financial impacts of disruptions, guiding recovery strategies.  

2. **Planning Phase**  
   *Emergency Management & Response:* Defines initial stabilization protocols for incidents.  
   *Crisis Management & Communications:* Coordinates internal/external recovery efforts with leadership structures.  
   *Business Restoration & Recovery:* Documents strategies to restore operations within recovery time objectives (RTOs).  

3. **Governance Phase**  
   *Plan Auditing:* Evaluates plan effectiveness and maturity levels.  
   *Plan Updating:* Ensures compliance with policies and evolving risks.  
   *Training & Exercising:* Prepares stakeholders through simulations and role-specific drills.  

**Why Are These Phases Critical for Organizational Resilience?**  
The Discovery phase establishes risk thresholds and BIA metrics, while Planning integrates response and recovery frameworks. Governance ensures adaptability, with audits and training maintaining plan relevance. Together, they align with ISO 22301 and DRII best practices to mitigate financial, reputational, and operational risks.
