# Structured Abstract  
Optimizing data center cooling requires six key practices: understanding equipment cooling needs, installing servers correctly, maintaining clear aisles, using blanking panels, setting accurate alarms, and collaborating with colocation providers. These steps improve airflow management, reduce energy use, and ensure hardware longevity while supporting sustainability goals.

## What Are the 6 Best Practices for Data Center Cooling?  
### 1. Understand Equipment Cooling Needs  
Know the kilowatt draw per cabinet to determine airflow requirements. This enables efficient cooling and prevents overheating.  

### 2. Install Servers Correctly  
Position servers with cold aisles on one side and hot aisles on the other. Incorrect orientation causes fans to draw hot air, risking equipment damage and thermal imbalances.  

### 3. Keep Aisles and Cabinets Clear  
Remove debris and organize cables to avoid airflow blockages. Perforated tiles and cluttered cables can impede cooling, leading to overheating.  

### 4. Install Blanking Panels and Side Panels  
Use blanking panels for empty rack units and side panels on cabinets to block hot air from entering servers, enhancing cooling efficiency.  

### 5. Set Alarms Correctly  
Calibrate temperature alarms to avoid nuisance alerts or missed critical issues. Align settings with equipment specs and SLAs for optimal monitoring.
