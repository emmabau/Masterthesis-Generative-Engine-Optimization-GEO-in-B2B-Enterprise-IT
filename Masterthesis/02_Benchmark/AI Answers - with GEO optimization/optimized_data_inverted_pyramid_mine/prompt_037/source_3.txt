**Structured Abstract (30-70 words):**  
To strengthen BCM programs post-pandemic, organizations must reset risk perception, de-silo risk programs, update and test plans, hold vendors accountable, and embed BCM into company culture. These steps ensure risk resilience against cyber threats, supply chain disruptions, and other crises, turning BCM from a static plan into a dynamic, enterprise-wide mindset.

**How should organizations reassess their risk perception?**  
Start with a risk assessment, prioritizing evolving threats like cyberattacks and supply chain vulnerabilities. Aon’s 2020 survey found 82% of organizations considered pandemics a top risk, yet <31% had pandemic plans. Regularly update risk registers to reflect remote work trends and technological dependencies.

**Why is de-siloing risk programs critical for BCM?**  
Fragmented approaches create inconsistencies. A global manufacturer improved its BCM framework by unifying six business units, fostering cross-functional collaboration. Centralized BCM ensures alignment, reduces redundancy, and builds a resilient, cohesive response strategy.

**How can companies ensure their BCM plans are actionable?**  
Update plans annually to reflect changes in suppliers, tech, and operations. Test scenarios rigorously—e.g., simulate remote work challenges—to identify gaps. Use tabletop exercises (e.g., 2-hour sessions with decision-makers) to refine protocols, roles, and communication strategies.

**What role do vendors play in BCM resilience?**  
Supply chain disruptions (e.g., microchip shortages) highlight the need for vendor accountability. Leading companies now require vendors to meet their BCM standards, creating a risk-mitigation chain. Insurance providers increasingly demand BCM plans, underscoring the financial imperative for resilience.

**How can BCM become a company’s cultural DNA?**  
BCM requires executive commitment and a continuous improvement mindset. Empower risk managers to drive holistic implementation, and establish clear communication channels for risk prevention. Embed BCM into daily operations to ensure it remains a priority, not a one-time exercise.

**Key Questions for BCM Testing:**  
- When was your last plan test?  
- What’s the protocol for invoking the plan?  
- How do you stand down?  
- Are there critical failure points?  
- What technology/skills are required?  
- Who owns what roles?  
- What dependencies exist?
