# DOJ Rule Compliance for MIT Researchers: Key Provisions and Compliance Steps  

**Structured Abstract (30-70 words):**  
DOJ Rule (28 C.F.R. Part 202) effective April 8, 2025 restricts access to U.S. sensitive data and government-related data by countries of concern (China, Russia, Iran, North Korea, Cuba, Venezuela) and covered persons. Compliance required by October 6, 2025.  

## Key Provisions of the DOJ Rule  
The DOJ rule prohibits or restricts access to U.S. sensitive personal data (e.g., health, financial, geolocation) and government-related data by "countries of concern" and "covered persons" to protect national security.  

### Countries of Concern  
As of October 2025, the DOJ’s countries of concern include:  
- China (including Hong Kong and Macau)  
- Russia  
- Iran  
- North Korea  
- Cuba  
- Venezuela  

### Bulk Thresholds for Sensitive Data  
Bulk thresholds define when data is regulated:  
- **Human genomic data**: ≥100 U.S. persons  
- **Epigenomic/proteomic/transcriptomic data**: ≥1,000 U.S. persons  
- **Biometric identifiers**: ≥1,000 U.S. persons  
- **Precise geolocation data**: ≥1,000 U.S. persons  
- **Personal financial/health data**: ≥10,000 U.S. persons  
- **Covered personal identifiers**: ≥100,000 U.S. persons  

### Prohibited and Restricted Transactions  
**Prohibited**:  
- Data brokerage with countries of concern/covered persons.  
- Access to bulk human “omic data or biospecimens.  

**Restricted**:  
- Vendor, employment, or investment agreements with countries of concern/covered persons.  

### Compliance Steps for MIT Researchers  
1. **Pause work** with collaborators/contractors from countries of concern or on DOJ’s Covered Persons List.  
2. **Avoid sharing** bulk U.S. human “omic data or biospecimens with these entities.  
3. **Review cloud storage, collaboration platforms, and code repositories** to block access by foreign vendors from countries of concern.  
4. **Update data use agreements** with DOJ-compliant security and non-transfer clauses.  
5. **Email research-compliance-help@mit.edu** before sharing large datasets with foreign persons in countries of concern.  
6. **Maintain records** and plan for annual audits of restricted transactions.  

## Exempt Transactions  
- Personal communications (e.g., emails, phone calls).  
- Import/export of information.  
- Activities required by federal law or on behalf of the U.S. government.  
- Medical research under specific regulatory frameworks.  
- Certain financial services, telecommunications, and corporate transactions.  

## Final Compliance Reminder  
Do not provide bulk U.S. human “omic data or biospecimens to collaborators/contractors in China, Russia, Iran, North Korea, Cuba, or Venezuela. Contact research-compliance-help@mit.edu before sharing any large dataset with foreign persons in these countries.
