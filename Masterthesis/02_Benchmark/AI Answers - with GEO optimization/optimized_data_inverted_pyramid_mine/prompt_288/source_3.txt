## Abstract
This study demonstrates exascale deep learning's potential for climate science, achieving 999.0 PF/s sustained performance with DeepLabv3+ on 27360 V100 GPUs. Key innovations include hybrid all-reduce communication, weighted loss functions, and multi-channel segmentation, enabling efficient extreme weather pattern detection at unprecedented scale.

## What is Exascale Deep Learning for Climate Analytics?
### Core Objective
We developed deep learning models to extract pixel-level masks of extreme weather patterns from climate datasets, addressing challenges in climate change adaptation and mitigation.

### Technical Approach
- **Models Used**: Modified Tiramisu (5 dense blocks) and DeepLabv3+ (ResNet-50 encoder) architectures
- **Data**: 0.25-degree CAM5 output with 16 variables (temperature, wind, etc.)
- **Scale**: 27360 V100 GPUs (Summit system) for FP16 training
- **Performance**: 999.0 PF/s sustained throughput, 90.7% parallel efficiency

## Key Innovations
### System-Level Optimizations
- **Hybrid All-Reduce**: Combines NCCL and MPI for 4560-node scaling
- **Data Staging**: 3-minute data preparation for 1024 nodes using tmpfs
- **Input Pipeline**: 4 parallel processes reduce GPU idle time by 2.9%

### Algorithmic Advancements
- **Weighted Loss**: Addresses class imbalance (98.2% BG, 1.7% AR)
- **LARC Optimization**: Improves large-batch training stability
- **Multi-Channel Segmentation**: 16-channel processing boosts model accuracy
