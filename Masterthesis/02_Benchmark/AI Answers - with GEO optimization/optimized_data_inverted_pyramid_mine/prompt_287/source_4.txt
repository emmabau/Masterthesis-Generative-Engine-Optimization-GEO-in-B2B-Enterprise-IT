**What Is a Backup Strategy?**  
A backup strategy is a systematic plan to protect data against loss and ensure rapid recovery. It involves data classification, backup scheduling, storage location selection, and defining recovery objectives (RPO/RTO). Effective strategies minimize downtime and align with regulatory requirements like GDPR.

**Key Components of a Backup Strategy**  
1. **Data Classification**  
   - Categorize data by sensitivity, value, and criticality (e.g., Public, Internal, Confidential, Critical).  
   - Use criteria like availability, compliance, and replaceability to prioritize protection.  

2. **Backup Scheduling**  
   - Choose between full, incremental, and differential backups.  
   - Implement standardized schemes like the Grandfather-Father-Son (GFS) model for reliability.  

3. **Storage Location**  
   - Combine onsite (local servers, NAS) and offsite (cloud) storage for redundancy.  
   - Follow the 3-2-1 rule (3 copies, 2 locations, 1 offsite) or advanced rules like 3-2-1-1-0 for enhanced security.  

**Recovery Objectives: RPO vs. RTO**  
- **Recovery Point Objective (RPO):** Defines data loss tolerance (e.g., 24-hour RPO means losing up to 24 hours of data).  
- **Recovery Time Objective (RTO):** Specifies restoration time (e.g., 4-hour RTO for email systems).  
- Align these metrics with business needs and regulatory requirements.  

**Best Practices for Backup Implementation**  
- **Automate Backups:** Reduce human error and ensure compliance with strict schedules.  
- **Protect Endpoints and Cloud Environments:** Secure desktops, laptops, and SaaS apps (e.g., Microsoft 365) under the Shared Responsibility Model.  
- **Monitor and Test Backups:** Regularly verify data integrity and conduct recovery drills.  
- **Encrypt and Secure Backups:** Use end-to-end encryption, role-based access, and immutable storage to prevent tampering.
