## Key NIS2 Compliance Summary  
NIS2 mandates stricter cybersecurity rules for EU organizations, with penalties up to €10M for non-compliance. Essential entities (energy, healthcare, finance) and important entities (IT services, manufacturing) must implement risk management, report incidents within 24/72/30 days, and ensure leadership accountability. Automation tools and structured checklists can streamline compliance.  

## Who Must Comply with NIS2?  
### Essential Entities (Strictest Requirements)  
- Energy, transport, financial services, healthcare, water supply, digital infrastructure, public administration, space industry  
### Important Entities (Obligations Apply)  
- Food production, postal services, chemical industry, manufacturing, digital services, research institutions, waste management  

## Key NIS2 Requirements  
### 1. Risk Management  
- Multi-factor authentication (MFA), data encryption, network security, incident response plans, third-party vendor assessments  
### 2. Corporate Accountability  
- Leadership must approve cybersecurity strategies, undergo training, and bear liability for failures  
### 3. Reporting Obligations  
- 24-hour early warning for major incidents, 72-hour full report, 30-day final remediation report  
### 4. Business Continuity  
- System recovery procedures, crisis response teams, and secure backups  

## 10 Essential Measures for NIS2 Compliance  
1. Conduct regular risk assessments and update security policies  
2. Implement encryption for data at rest and in transit  
3. Develop a structured incident response framework  
4. Enforce secure procurement and system operation practices  
5. Provide cybersecurity training for employees  
6. Establish strong access control and asset management  
7. Maintain business continuity plans with up-to-date backups  
8. Adopt advanced authentication and secure communication protocols  
9. Assess third-party security and enforce supply chain compliance  
10. Automate risk assessments and incident reporting  

## How to Achieve NIS2 Compliance  
1. Confirm organizational scope (essential/important entities)  
2. Perform a gap analysis to identify cybersecurity weaknesses  
3. Develop a risk-management strategy with regular audits  
4. Strengthen governance by assigning a security officer and training executives  
5. Establish clear incident response and reporting processes  
6. Secure supply chains through vendor assessments and contractual obligations  
7. Prepare for audits by maintaining comprehensive documentation  

## Consequences of Non-Compliance  
- Fines up to €10M (essential entities) or €7M (important entities)  
- Executive liability, including potential bans from management roles  
- Operational risks, reputational damage, and financial losses from cyberattacks  

## NIS2 vs. Other Regulations  
- **GDPR**: Focuses on data privacy, while NIS2 secures critical infrastructure  
- **DORA**: Financial sector-specific, aligns with NIS2 but adds sector rules  
- **ISO 27001**: Voluntary framework covering ~70% of NIS2 requirements
