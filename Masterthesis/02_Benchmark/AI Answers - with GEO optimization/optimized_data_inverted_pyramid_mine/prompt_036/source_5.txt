# Is €800/Month Reasonable for Server Management?  
**Bottom Line:** €800/month is reasonable if you have 2-5 servers, get personalized support, and have monthly check-ins. Avoid providers with vague promises, low client counts, or hidden costs.  

## Key Red Flags to Watch For  
### Red Flag #1: Unasked Questions  
Providers that skip understanding your downtime costs, growth plans, or compliance needs are selling packages, not solving problems.  

### Red Flag #2: Vague Promises  
Avoid terms like “comprehensive security.” Ask for specifics: “Will you implement fail2ban, configure UFW, and conduct quarterly scans?”  

### Red Flag #3: “Unlimited Support” at Low Prices  
Providers claiming “unlimited support” for €100-200/month likely manage 100+ clients. Real attention costs €500-€3,000/month.  

### Red Flag #4: No Clear Exclusions  
Good providers state what’s extra (e.g., compliance audits, emergency fixes). Vague “support” means hidden costs.  

### Red Flag #5: Guaranteed Uptime Without Audit  
Providers promising 99.9% uptime without auditing your setup are making unkeepable promises.  

## Evaluating Proposals: A Checklist  
### Questions to Ask  
1. **What happens if your site goes down at 2am?**  
2. **How many clients do you support?** (Over 50 = you’re a number)  
3. **Can you provide a reference from a company like mine?**  
4. **What’s NOT included in this price?**  
5. **What’s the most common mistake for my size?**  

### Pricing Framework  
- **€100-300/month:** Monitoring-only (e.g., smoke detectors, no fire department).  
- **€500-1,000/month:** Shared support (response times in hours).  
- **€1,500-3,000/month:** Real partnership (monthly reviews, proactive fixes).  
- **€5,000+/month:** Dedicated team (for 20+ servers or strict compliance).  

## Real-World Example: Sarah’s Decision  
Sarah, a CEO with 20 employees, chose a €850/month provider with 18 clients, specific services, and monthly reviews. They prevented a €30K data loss and improved her team’s workflow.  

## Conclusion: Choosing the Right Partner  
The best providers don’t sell everything—they tell you what you need, what you don’t, and why. Avoid pressure tactics and generic answers. A true partner takes ownership of your infrastructure while you focus on growth.
