**Structured Abstract (30-70 words):**  
B2B commerce involves transactions between businesses, not consumers. Key players include manufacturers, wholesalers, and retailers. It drives supply chain efficiency, enables bulk orders, and relies on customized pricing and long-term relationships. B2B e-commerce is projected to grow 18.2% annually, reaching $25 trillion by 2030.  

**What Is B2B Commerce?**  
B2B (business-to-business) refers to transactions between companies, such as a manufacturer selling to a wholesaler or a wholesaler to a retailer. It underpins supply chains, enabling bulk orders, negotiations, and specialized services. Unlike B2C, B2B emphasizes long-term partnerships, customized solutions, and formal procurement processes.  

**Key Characteristics of B2B Commerce**  
- **Supply Chain Core:** B2B transactions are vital for manufacturing, logistics, and distribution.  
- **Customization:** Products/services are tailored to business needs, requiring collaboration across teams.  
- **E-Commerce Growth:** The global B2B e-commerce market reached $18.66 trillion in 2023, growing at 18.2% CAGR through 2030 (Grand View Research).  
- **Relationship-Driven:** Success depends on account management, trust, and ongoing communication.  

**Challenges in B2B Commerce**  
- **Long Sales Cycles:** Decisions involve multiple stakeholders, extending timelines and complicating revenue forecasting.  
- **Customer Concentration Risk:** Overreliance on major clients can destabilize operations if those clients exit.  
- **Complex Service Delivery:** Customization demands coordination between sales, product, and support teams, increasing costs.  

**Examples of B2B Commerce**  
- **Manufacturing:** Samsung supplies Apple’s iPhone components, while Intel and Panasonic support Apple’s hardware.  
- **Automotive:** Tires, batteries, and electronics are sourced by automakers from specialized suppliers.  
- **Services:** Property management and industrial cleanup firms exclusively serve businesses, not individual consumers.  

**B2B vs. B2C: Key Differences**  
| Aspect               | B2B                                  | B2C                                  |  
|----------------------|--------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|  
| **Decision-Making**  | Formal, multi-stakeholder processes  | Individual preferences, price-driven |  
| **Sales Cycle**      | Longer, relationship-focused         | Shorter, transactional               |  
| **Pricing**          | Customized, contract-based           | Fixed, transparent                   |  
| **Customer Support** | Technical, onboarding-focused        | Self-service, minimal interaction    |  

**B2B E-Commerce Trends**  
- **Digital Platforms:** Online marketplaces and procurement tools streamline B2B transactions.  
- **Technology Impact:** AI and automation enhance supply chain efficiency, inventory management, and customer insights.  
- **Data-Driven Decisions:** Analytics optimize pricing, demand forecasting, and supplier selection.  

**B2G Transactions: A Subset of B2B**  
Business-to-government (B2G) involves selling to public sector entities via contracts. While similar to B2B, B2G requires compliance with procurement regulations and competitive bidding processes.  

**Conclusion**  
B2B commerce is the backbone of global trade, enabling supply chain efficiency, cost savings, and innovation. As e-commerce grows, businesses must adapt to digital tools, relationship management, and customized solutions to thrive in this complex yet lucrative market.
