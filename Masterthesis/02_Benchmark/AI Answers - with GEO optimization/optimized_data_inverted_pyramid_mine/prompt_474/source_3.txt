**Structured Abstract:**  
This document outlines the fiscal year 2005 budget requests and recommendations for the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) and related agencies, emphasizing the need for adequate funding to support agricultural research, conservation programs, and food safety initiatives. Key priorities include the Market Access Program (MAP), Wildlife Habitat Incentives Program (WHIP), and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA)’s role in ensuring food safety and combating threats like Bovine Spongiform Encephalopathy (BSE).

**Key Recommendations and Budget Priorities**  
### **1. USDA Budget Requests and Funding Needs**  
The USDA’s 2005 budget request includes significant funding for the **Market Access Program (MAP)** at $140 million, as authorized by the 2002 Farm Bill. This program is critical for U.S. agricultural exports, particularly in the wine and beef industries, which face high tariffs and trade barriers. The **Wildlife Habitat Incentives Program (WHIP)** is also recommended for a $100 million increase to support habitat restoration and technical assistance for farmers.  

### **2. Conservation and Environmental Programs**  
The **Conservation Security Program (CSP)** requires a 2.5% allocation from the Environmental Quality Incentives Program (EQIP) to fund the Colorado River Salinity Control (CRSC) Program. This initiative is vital for maintaining water quality in the Colorado River, which supports millions of people in the U.S. and Mexico. The **Grassland Reserve Program (GRP)** and **Wetlands Reserve Program (WRP)** also need sustained funding to meet conservation goals and address salinity issues.  

### **3. Food Safety and Animal Health Initiatives**  
The **Food and Drug Administration (FDA)** plays a central role in ensuring food safety, particularly in combating **Bovine Spongiform Encephalopathy (BSE)**. The FDA’s **BSE Surveillance Program** and **rapid testing initiatives** are essential to prevent the spread of the disease and maintain consumer confidence. The **Animal and Plant Health Inspection Service (APHIS)** also faces challenges in managing wildlife diseases like **Avian Influenza** and **Chronic Wasting Disease (CWD)**, requiring increased funding for surveillance and control measures.  

### **4. Research and Education Programs**  
The **National Research Initiative (NRI)** and **McIntire-Stennis Cooperative Forestry Program** are highlighted as critical for advancing agricultural research and sustainable forestry practices. The **Renewable Resources Extension Act (RREA)** is recommended for full funding to support educational programs for private landowners, addressing issues like land use, soil health, and wildlife conservation.  

### **5. International Trade and Export Support**  
The **Market Access Program (MAP)** is essential for expanding U.S. agricultural exports, particularly in emerging markets like China and South America. The **Foreign Agricultural Service (FAS)** is urged to provide robust support for export initiatives, including trade promotion and market development activities. The **International Trade Agreement (CAFTA)** also requires attention to ensure U.S. agricultural competitiveness.  

### **6. Rural Development and Infrastructure**  
The **Rural Development Programs** under the USDA, including the **Rural Housing Service (RHS)** and **Rural Business-Cooperative Service (RBSC)**, are critical for supporting rural communities. The **Broadband Loan Program** and **Telecommunications Initiatives** are recommended to improve connectivity and economic opportunities in rural areas.  

### **7. Public Health and Safety Measures**  
The **FDA’s Food Safety Initiatives** and **CDC’s Role in Disease Surveillance** are emphasized to address public health threats like **Avian Influenza** and **BSE**. The **Emergency Food Assistance Program (TEFAP)** and **Commodity Supplemental Food Program (CSFP)** are also highlighted for their role in combating hunger and ensuring food security.  

**Conclusion**  
The 2005 budget requests and recommendations underscore the importance of sustained funding for USDA and FDA programs to address agricultural, environmental, and public health challenges. Prioritizing initiatives like MAP, WHIP, and BSE surveillance will ensure the long-term viability of U.S. agriculture and food safety.
