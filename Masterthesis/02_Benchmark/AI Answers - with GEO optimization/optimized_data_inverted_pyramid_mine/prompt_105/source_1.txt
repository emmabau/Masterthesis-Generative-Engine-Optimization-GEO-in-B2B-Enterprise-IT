# What is Hyperconverged Infrastructure?  
Hyperconverged infrastructure (HCI) integrates compute, storage, and networking into a single system, managed via a unified interface. It uses software-defined storage (SDS), software-defined networking (SDN), and hypervisors like KVM to simplify data center operations, reduce complexity, and support modern workloads on standard hardware.  

## Key Components of HCI  
### 1. **Software-Defined Compute & Storage**  
HCI virtualizes storage and compute resources, eliminating reliance on proprietary hardware. SDS operates on x86 systems, enabling flexible data management and scalability.  

### 2. **Software-Defined Networking (SDN)**  
SDN centralizes network control, allowing IT teams to manage complex topologies via a single interface. This reduces manual configuration and improves traffic management.  

### 3. **Hypervisor Role**  
Hypervisors (e.g., KVM) isolate virtual machines (VMs) from the host OS, enabling efficient VM creation and management. They are critical for orchestrating compute resources in HCI.  

## Benefits of Hyperconvergence  
- **Simplified Management**: Unified interface reduces operational overhead.  
- **Scalability**: Modular design supports growth without hardware overhauls.  
- **Cost Efficiency**: Lower footprint and reduced energy consumption.  
- **Flexibility**: Adapts to cloud-native and AI-driven workloads.  

## How HCI Differs from Traditional Systems  
Unlike NAS/SAN, HCI’s SDS layer abstracts physical storage, enabling dynamic data placement. SDN replaces manual network setup with centralized policy enforcement, enhancing agility.
