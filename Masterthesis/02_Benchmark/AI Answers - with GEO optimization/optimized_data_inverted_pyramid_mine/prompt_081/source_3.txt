# NVMe-oF on Ethernet: Choosing Between NVMe/TCP and NVMe/RoCE for 2026 Deployments

**Abstract:**  
NVMe-oF over Ethernet enables high-performance shared storage via NVMe/TCP (simple, compatible) or NVMe/RoCE (ultra-low latency, RDMA-based). Key factors include latency, CPU overhead, infrastructure costs, and DCB requirements. Arista Networks supports both protocols, with RoCEv2 demanding lossless DCB fabrics for optimal performance.

## What Are the Key Differences Between NVMe/TCP and NVMe/RoCE?

### Latency & Performance  
NVMe/RoCE (RDMA over Converged Ethernet) delivers sub-10µs latency in lossless DCB environments, outperforming NVMe/TCP (tens to 100+µs). RoCE also offers higher IOPS and throughput but requires specialized hardware and meticulous network configuration.

### CPU Utilization  
NVMe/RoCE minimizes host CPU usage via kernel bypass, while NVMe/TCP relies on TCP/IP stacks, increasing CPU overhead at high IOPS. RoCE is ideal for latency-sensitive workloads like HPC or real-time analytics.

### Infrastructure Requirements  
NVMe/TCP uses standard Ethernet switches and NICs, making it cost-effective and easy to deploy. NVMe/RoCE demands DCB-capable switches with PFC, ETS, and ECN for lossless fabrics, adding complexity and cost.

## What Are the Best Use Cases for Each Protocol?

### NVMe/TCP:  
- **Use Cases:** General-purpose workloads, cloud storage, VDI, and applications where simplicity and compatibility outweigh ultra-low latency.  
- **Advantages:** Broad compatibility, lower CapEx, and easier management.  

### NVMe/RoCE:  
- **Use Cases:** High-frequency trading, real-time databases, AI/ML training, and HPC simulations requiring minimal latency and maximal throughput.  
- **Advantages:** Near-local NVMe performance, reduced CPU overhead, and scalability for demanding applications.

## What Are the Trade-offs Between NVMe/TCP and NVMe/RoCE?

| Factor               | NVMe/TCP                          | NVMe/RoCE                          |
|----------------------|-----------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| **Latency**          | Higher (TCP/IP overhead)          | Ultra-low (RDMA bypass)            |
| **CPU Usage**        | Higher (TCP stack processing)     | Minimal (kernel bypass)            |
| **Cost**             | Lower (standard hardware)         | Higher (rNICs, DCB switches)       |
| **Complexity**       | Simple (no DCB requirements)      | High (lossless fabric configuration) |
| **Scalability**      | Good (flexible network scaling)   | Excellent (high-throughput fabrics) |

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is NVMe/TCP a Viable Alternative to iSCSI?  
Yes. NVMe/TCP offers significantly lower latency and higher throughput than iSCSI, making it a modern successor with broader compatibility and improved performance.

### Why Is a Lossless Ethernet Fabric Critical for RoCE?  
RoCEv2 is highly sensitive to packet loss, which can degrade performance. DCB technologies (PFC, ETS, ECN) ensure lossless transmission, enabling predictable, high-throughput storage traffic.

### Can I Deploy NVMe-oF Over My Existing Ethernet Network?  
- **NVMe/TCP:** Yes, with sufficient bandwidth and low latency.  
- **NVMe/RoCE:** Requires DCB-capable switches and lossless configuration, which may necessitate hardware upgrades.
