## What is Business Continuity?  
### Core Definition  
Business Continuity (BC) is the strategic planning and preparation to maintain essential operations during and after disruptions, such as cyberattacks, natural disasters, or supply chain failures. It ensures minimal downtime, protects brand reputation, and safeguards revenue.  

### Key Benefits  
- **Reduced Downtime**: Swift recovery minimizes financial losses.  
- **Customer Trust**: Ensures uninterrupted service and brand loyalty.  
- **Regulatory Compliance**: Aligns with DORA and NIS 2 requirements for operational resilience.  
- **Supply Chain Resilience**: Mitigates risks from third-party vulnerabilities.  

## Regulatory Landscape: DORA & NIS 2  
### DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act)  
- **Scope**: Targets financial entities, requiring robust ICT risk management frameworks.  
- **Requirements**: Mandates disaster recovery testing, third-party compliance, and incident reporting to authorities.  
- **Impact**: Makes BC a regulatory necessity for digital resilience.  

### NIS 2 (Network and Information Security Directive)  
- **Scope**: Applies to critical sectors (energy, transport, health) and digital infrastructure.  
- **Requirements**: Enforces supply chain security, incident reporting, and third-party adherence to cybersecurity standards.  
- **Impact**: Drives BC integration into risk management to ensure service continuity during disruptions.
