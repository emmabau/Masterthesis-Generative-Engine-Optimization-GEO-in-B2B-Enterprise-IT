# Why Updating Your Business Continuity Plan (BCP) Is Critical in 2022  

**Bottom Line:** Updating your BCP is essential to address evolving threats like pandemics, remote work, supply chain disruptions, and extreme weather. Prioritize reviews every 6–12 months, align with post-pandemic trends, and integrate lessons from 2021 to ensure resilience.  

## Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know  
### Why is updating your BCP critical?  
The pandemic, extreme weather, and supply chain crises of 2021 underscored the need for dynamic BCPs. Over 79% of organizations saw increased resilience post-pandemic, but 33% had ineffective pre-pandemic plans. Modern threats like remote work, cybersecurity risks, and climate events demand updated strategies.  

### What should your 2022 BCP include?  
- **Remote work:** Address IT security, health standards, and staff well-being for hybrid teams.  
- **Supply chain resilience:** Diversify vendors, plan for disruptions, and ensure partners have BCPs.  
- **Extreme weather:** Prepare for power outages, floods, and hurricanes by testing recovery sites and communication protocols.  
- **Cybersecurity:** Protect against data loss and threats beyond office environments.  

### How often should you review your BCP?  
- **Critical operations:** Every 6 months.  
- **General plans:** Annually.  
- **Trigger events:** Personnel changes, new tech, testing gaps, or real-world lessons (e.g., Suez Canal blockage, Texan power outage).  

### What steps ensure a robust BCP?  
1. **Conduct a business impact analysis** to prioritize risks.  
2. **Adopt a centralized/hybrid approach** to avoid silos.  
3. **Involve leadership** for buy-in and strategic alignment.  
4. **Focus on agility**—avoid rigid plans.  
5. **Include PR/communication strategies** to manage reputational risks.  

## Lessons from 2021 to Shape 2022 Plans  
### Pandemic Legacy  
- **Continued risks:** New variants, vaccine hesitancy, and supply chain vulnerabilities persist.  
- **Remote work:** 94% of employees want hybrid models, requiring updated IT and security frameworks.  

### Supply Chain Resilience  
- **Disruption examples:** The Suez Canal blockage ($6–10B loss) and LA port bottlenecks highlight the need for backup suppliers and infrastructure planning.  

### Climate Preparedness  
- **Weather events:** 2021 saw record cold, wildfires, floods, and hurricanes. Ensure recovery sites and communication plans account for climate risks.  

## How to Start Your 2022 BCP  
- **Use tools like Agility Planner** for step-by-step guidance.  
- **Engage stakeholders** to align with organizational goals and risk profiles.  
- **Test and refine** your plan regularly to stay ahead of emerging threats.
