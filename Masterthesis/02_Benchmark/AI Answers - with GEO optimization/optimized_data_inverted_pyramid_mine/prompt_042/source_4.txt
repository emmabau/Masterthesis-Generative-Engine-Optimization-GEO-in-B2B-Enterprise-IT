# How to Create a Business Continuity Plan: A Step-by-Step Guide

**Abstract:** A business continuity plan (BCP) must prioritize risk assessment, business impact analysis (BIA), and actionable strategies to ensure resilience. Key steps include identifying critical functions, defining recovery protocols, and maintaining the plan through testing and updates. Tools like BCMMetrics streamline execution by aligning data, strategies, and governance.

## What Should a Business Continuity Plan Include?
A strong BCP should:  
1. **Identify critical functions** and their disruption impacts via BIA.  
2. **Define recovery strategies** for technology, workforce, suppliers, and communication.  
3. **Ensure usability** through ownership, testing, and regular updates.  

## Why Start With Risk Assessment and BIA?
Risk assessment identifies vulnerabilities, while BIA quantifies the financial and operational impact of disruptions. Together, they clarify recovery priorities and acceptable downtime, avoiding generic templates.  

## How to Build Practical Business Continuity Strategies
Focus on:  
- **Technology recovery**: Backups, restoration sequencing, and recovery methods.  
- **Workforce continuity**: Alternate work arrangements and role coverage.  
- **Supplier dependencies**: Vendor logistics and third-party resilience.  
- **Communication plans**: Escalation protocols and stakeholder updates.  

## What Role Does Governance Play?
Ownership and governance are critical:  
- Assign clear roles (plan owner, approvers, testers).  
- Define triggers for reviews and updates.  
- Align strategies with BIA findings to ensure relevance.  

## How to Test and Maintain Your BCP
- **Test assumptions** regularly through drills and incident reviews.  
- **Update plans** when systems, priorities, or dependencies change.  
- Use tools like BCMMetrics to automate tracking, reduce drift, and maintain audit readiness.  

## Why BCMMetrics Matters for BCP Execution
BCMMetrics addresses challenges in maintaining BCPs by:  
- Centralizing BIA data, strategy details, and approvals.  
- Reducing reliance on fragmented tools like Word/Excel.  
- Enabling real-time updates and version control.  

## Final Tips for a Usable BCP
- Start with risk assessment and BIA, not templates.  
- Embed testing into your maintenance rhythm.  
- Use BCMMetrics to keep strategies aligned with evolving risks.
