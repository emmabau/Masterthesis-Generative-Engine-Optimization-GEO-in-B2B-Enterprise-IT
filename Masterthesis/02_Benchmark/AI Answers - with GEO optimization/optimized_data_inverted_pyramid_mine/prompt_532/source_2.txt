# Structured Abstract  
Device as a Service (DaaS) unifies IT procurement, finance, and asset lifecycle management, reducing inefficiencies through a subscription model. It offers predictable costs, lifecycle control, and scalability for modern work environments.  

## Why Traditional IT Procurement and Asset Management Fail  
IT procurement, financial planning, and asset lifecycle management are siloed, leading to delays, budgeting challenges, and operational risks. Procurement focuses on unit costs, finance on capital expenditure, and IT on device lifecycle, creating fragmented processes that hinder agility.  

## Challenges of Hybrid Work and Vendor Fragmentation  
Hybrid and remote work models strain traditional ownership frameworks, while vendor fragmentation complicates procurement. Disconnected supplier contracts, inconsistent service levels, and manual tracking increase administrative burdens and compliance risks.  

## How Device as a Service (DaaS) Solves These Challenges  
DaaS replaces fragmented hardware management with a unified, subscription-based model. Fixed monthly fees transform capital expenditure into predictable operational costs, aligning spending with actual usage. Lifecycle management is centralized, ensuring secure device handling, proactive maintenance, and compliance.  

## Benefits of DaaS for Modern Organizations  
DaaS reduces operational friction by eliminating multi-vendor complexity, enabling scalable device fleets and global consistency. Finance teams gain cost transparency, IT teams achieve lifecycle predictability, and employees receive seamless device support regardless of location.  

## Conclusion and Sources  
Device as a Service addresses integration gaps in IT, finance, and asset management, offering agility and cost control. For organizations seeking to adapt to dynamic work environments, DaaS provides a scalable, transparent solution.
