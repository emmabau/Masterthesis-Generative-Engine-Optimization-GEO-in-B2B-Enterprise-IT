# Composable Platforms: Enabling Business Value Through Flexible Stakeholder Collaboration

**Abstract**  
Composable platforms enable diverse personas to access tailored capabilities without over-investing in secondary areas, enhancing flexibility and business value. This article explains how different stakeholders leverage platform flexibility to meet technical and business needs, with actionable insights for optimizing platform design.

## What is a Composable Platform?  
A composable platform is an integrated collection of capabilities defined by stakeholder needs, enabling modular configuration to address technical and business goals. Unlike monolithic systems, it allows teams to select and combine components (e.g., build tools, artifact storage) to deliver value without adopting all features.  

### Key Characteristics  
- **Modular Architecture**: Components like CI/CD pipelines, observability tools, and security scanners can be combined or used independently.  
- **Persona-Centric Design**: Tailored for Builders, Enablers, Developers, and Customers, each with distinct requirements.  
- **Loose Coupling**: Components remain interoperable while allowing customization, enabling features like automatic SBOM generation.  

## How Do Personas Shape Platform Design?  
### Builders  
Focus on infrastructure flexibility, supporting on-premise and cloud environments. They define foundational configurations (networking, security) before handing off to other teams.  

### Enablers  
Expand platform capabilities by integrating application-specific tools (e.g., databases, interconnectivity) to meet developer and business customer needs. Prioritize scalability and governance.  

### Developers (End-Users)  
Leverage pre-configured CI/CD pipelines and third-party tools to accelerate workflows. Benefit from reduced lead times and access to diverse workloads.  

### Customers  
Indirectly benefit from platform flexibility through improved reliability, faster time-to-market, and cost efficiency. Drive prioritization of composable features aligned with business goals.  

## Why Composability Matters for Business Success  
- **Scalability**: Modular components adapt to evolving technical and market demands.  
- **Efficient Onboarding**: Teams adopt only necessary capabilities, reducing stakeholder investment in secondary areas.  
- **Competitive Advantage**: Enables rapid innovation by aligning platform capabilities with specific business deliverables.  

### Example Use Cases  
- A development team uses build and scan capabilities while storing artifacts externally.  
- MLOps teams integrate observability tools with cloud-native platforms for real-time monitoring.  

## How to Optimize for Composable Platforms  
1. **Define Persona-Specific Requirements**: Align platform capabilities with stakeholder priorities (e.g., developers need CI/CD tools; customers value reliability).  
2. **Prioritize Interoperability**: Ensure components can work together seamlessly (e.g., automatic image signing across tools).  
3. **Iterate Based on Feedback**: Continuously refine components to reflect evolving business needs.
