## What is Composable Infrastructure?  
A composable infrastructure system is a modular architecture where services are developed, deployed, and maintained as independent components. These modules are orchestrated to build complex systems, unlike monolithic systems that operate as single units.  

### Key Advantages  
- **Versatile:** Reconfigurable for diverse use cases  
- **Adaptable:** Individual modules can be swapped without system-wide disruptions  
- **Resilient:** Failing modules do not compromise the entire system  
- **Scalable:** Modules scale independently based on demand  

## Why Composable Over Monolithic?  
Monolithic systems lock businesses into rigid architectures, making integrations, data migrations, and scalability efforts costly and slow. Composable systems, rooted in first principles, enable innovation by breaking problems into foundational components.  

### First Principles Mindset  
- **Deepens system understanding**  
- **Creates alternative solutions**  
- **Drives breakthrough ideas**  
This mindset allows composable systems to evolve beyond traditional boundaries, such as "headless" architectures that decouple frontend/backend experiences.  

## Core Characteristics of Composable Systems  
1. **Minimal Dependencies:** Loosely coupled modules with limited internal knowledge  
2. **Independent Scaling:** Modules scale without affecting the entire system  
3. **Integration-Driven:** Communication via APIs and protocols  
4. **Governance Layer:** Manages security, compliance, and error handling across modules  

## Real-World Examples  
- **Composable CDP:** Unifies customer data from sales, marketing, and support  
- **Composable Commerce:** Decouples frontend and backend for optimized e-commerce  
- **Headless CRM:** Separates customer communication processes for flexible support apps  
- **Composable ERP:** Integrates financial microservices (e.g., invoicing, payroll) with legacy systems
