# Structured Abstract  
This study investigates the intersection of Big Data analytics, GDPR compliance, and Enterprise Architecture Management (EAM). Using Systematic Mapping Review (SMR), we find limited research on EAM's role in addressing GDPR challenges, highlighting a critical gap. Our analysis identifies seven key trends and underscores the need for holistic, architecture-driven approaches to data governance and privacy in Big Data contexts.

## Methodology  
### Systematic Mapping Review (SMR)  
We conducted an SMR to analyze 332 papers published between 2013–2020, focusing on data security, privacy, and GDPR compliance. Search strings combined terms like "Big Data," "GDPR," "EAM," and "data protection," targeting journals, conferences, and interdisciplinary databases.  

## Key Findings  
### Data Security & Privacy Challenges  
- **Volume & Complexity**: Big Data's scale and unstructured nature pose risks for privacy and security.  
- **Regulatory Gaps**: GDPR's emphasis on "privacy by design" remains underexplored in technical implementations.  
- **Ethical Concerns**: Emerging trends highlight risks of discrimination, re-identification, and ethical misuse of data analytics.  

### EAM's Potential in GDPR Compliance  
- **Holistic Framework**: EAM offers a structured approach to align data governance, risk management, and compliance across organizational units.  
- **Case Studies**: Research shows EAM can streamline GDPR requirements like Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) and Record of Processing Activities (RoPA).  
- **Implementation Barriers**: SMEs and complex systems face challenges in adopting EAM due to resource constraints and fragmented governance.  

## Research Gaps & Future Directions  
### Unexplored Areas  
1. **Technical Integration**: Few studies address how EAM integrates with existing data protection technologies (e.g., encryption, anonymization).  
2. **Ethical Frameworks**: Lack of systematic analysis on balancing innovation with ethical data use in Big Data analytics.  
3. **Global Compliance**: Limited research on EAM's adaptability to non-EU data protection regulations.  

### Recommendations  
- **Interdisciplinary Collaboration**: Merge EAM with legal, ethical, and technical frameworks to address GDPR's multifaceted requirements.  
- **Standardized Metrics**: Develop benchmarks for measuring EAM effectiveness in GDPR compliance.  
- **Policy Advocacy**: Promote EAM as a core component of data governance strategies for organizations handling sensitive data.  

## Conclusion  
This study reveals EAM's untapped potential as a strategic tool for GDPR compliance in Big Data environments. While current research is fragmented, the growing complexity of data ecosystems necessitates a shift toward architecture-driven, holistic solutions. Future work should prioritize scalable EAM models that balance innovation with regulatory rigor, ensuring both compliance and ethical responsibility in data practices.
