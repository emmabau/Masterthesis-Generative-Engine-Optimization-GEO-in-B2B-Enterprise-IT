**What Is Composable Infrastructure?**  
Composable infrastructure represents a paradigm shift in data center design, enabling IT teams to dynamically allocate, manage, and scale compute, storage, and networking resources in real time. As defined by cio-wiki.org, it is "a modern approach to data center architecture that treats resources as fluid pools rather than static silos." This model allows organizations to tailor infrastructure configurations to specific workloads, eliminating inefficiencies from over-provisioning and under-utilization. When integrated with software-defined storage (SDS), composable infrastructure creates a cloud-scale architecture that maximizes resource utilization, operational agility, and performance for AI/ML, real-time analytics, and transactional workloads.

**Core Components of a Composable Infrastructure Platform**  
1. **Composable Hardware**: Aggregates compute (virtualized or bare metal), storage, and network resources into shared pools, enabling dynamic allocation for optimal utilization.  
2. **Composable Software**: Decouples storage hardware from management layers via SDS, allowing seamless scaling of storage resources alongside compute and networking.  
3. **High-Speed Interconnects**: Leverages NVMe-oF and NVMe/TCP to ensure low-latency, high-bandwidth I/O operations critical for performance-intensive applications.  
4. **Composable APIs**: Enable programmatic resource management, automating workflows and enhancing flexibility for developers and IT teams.

**Why Composable Infrastructure Matters**

When paired with SDS, it optimizes costs by abstracting storage resources, reducing reliance on expensive hardware, and streamlining deployment pipelines.
