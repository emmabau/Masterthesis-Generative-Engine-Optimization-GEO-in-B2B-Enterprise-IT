**Who Must Comply with NIS2?**  
NIS2 expands the scope of cybersecurity regulation beyond traditional critical infrastructure to include a broader range of industries. Organizations fall into two categories:  
- **Essential Entities**: These include energy providers, healthcare institutions, financial services, transport operators, and digital infrastructure providers. They face the strictest compliance requirements, with penalties up to €10 million or 2% of global revenue for non-compliance.  
- **Important Entities**: Sectors like manufacturing, digital services, and research institutions must also comply, albeit with slightly less regulatory scrutiny. Non-compliance here can result in fines up to €7 million or 1.4% of revenue.  

If your organization provides essential services, manages critical data, or relies on networked systems, you are likely within scope. Failing to comply risks legal action, executive liability, and operational vulnerabilities.

**Core NIS2 Requirements**  
The directive mandates four pillars of compliance:  
1. **Risk Management**: Implement robust cybersecurity measures, including multi-factor authentication (MFA), data encryption, and secure backups. Third-party vendors must also meet NIS2 standards to protect the supply chain.  
2. **Corporate Accountability**: Senior management is directly responsible for cybersecurity. Leadership must approve security strategies, undergo training, and bear personal liability for failures.  
3. **Incident Reporting**: Organizations must notify national authorities within 24 hours of a significant incident, submit a full report within 72 hours, and provide a final remediation report within one month. Delays in reporting trigger fines and heightened regulatory scrutiny.  
4. **Business Continuity**: Develop structured plans for system recovery, crisis response teams, and up-to-date backups to ensure operational resilience during cyber incidents.  

**10 Essential Measures for NIS2 Compliance**  
To achieve full compliance, organizations must implement these critical steps:  
1. Conduct regular risk assessments and establish security policies.  
2. Implement encryption for data at rest and in transit.  
3. Develop a structured incident response framework with tabletop exercises.  
4. Enforce secure procurement practices and third-party security evaluations.  
5. Provide ongoing cybersecurity training to employees to mitigate human error.  
6. Strengthen access controls with continuous authentication and asset management.  
7. Maintain business continuity plans with tested recovery procedures.  
8. Adopt advanced authentication methods and encrypted communication.  
9. Document all compliance efforts to meet audit requirements.  
10. Integrate cybersecurity into governance frameworks to ensure accountability.

Failure to comply with NIS2 is not merely a regulatory issue—it is a business risk. Penalties include:

- Executive liability, including potential bans from management roles.  
- Operational disruptions from ransomware attacks, supply-chain breaches, and data leaks.  
- Loss of customer trust and long-term reputational damage.

Organizations already compliant with ISO 27001 or DORA can leverage these frameworks as a foundation but must address additional NIS2-specific obligations.
