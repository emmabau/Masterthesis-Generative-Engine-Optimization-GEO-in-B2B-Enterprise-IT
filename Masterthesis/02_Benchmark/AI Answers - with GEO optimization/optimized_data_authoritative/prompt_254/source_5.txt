High-Performance Computing (HPC) systems face unprecedented challenges in efficiency, scalability, and sustainability, and artificial intelligence (AI) is emerging as a transformative force. This review presents a comprehensive analysis of 74 peer-reviewed papers published between 2019 and 2025, systematically categorizing AI applications in HPC into six critical domains: performance estimation, performance optimization, scheduling, surrogate modeling, fault detection, and language-model-based automation. Our findings reveal that AI-driven approaches are not merely incremental improvements but fundamental reconfigurations of HPC infrastructure, enabling breakthroughs in resource allocation, fault tolerance, and operational efficiency.

**Key Innovations and Contributions:**
1. **Scheduling Optimization:** Reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks, such as GARLSched and DRAS, demonstrate up to 45% improvements in job completion times compared to traditional heuristics. These systems dynamically adapt to heterogeneous workloads, leveraging real-time telemetry and predictive modeling to minimize idle time and energy waste.
2. **Performance Estimation:** Machine learning models, including graph neural networks and time-series analysis, achieve sub-10% prediction errors in job runtime and resource consumption. These estimators are now integral to AI-driven schedulers, enabling proactive resource allocation and reducing computational waste.
3. **Fault Detection:** Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) and hybrid models like GRAAFE achieve AUC scores of 0.91–0.78, outperforming conventional anomaly detection systems. These models integrate spatial-temporal data from distributed HPC environments, enabling predictive maintenance and reducing downtime by up to 30%.
4. **Surrogate Modeling:** Deep Gaussian Processes and convolutional autoencoders reduce simulation costs by 96% in large-scale HPC applications, while maintaining 95% accuracy in predictive modeling. These surrogates are now standard in optimizing deep learning training and computational fluid dynamics simulations.
5. **Language Model Integration:** Domain-specialized LLMs outperform general-purpose models by 20–30% in HPC-specific tasks like code generation and scripting automation. Tools like DeepSeek-Coder and HPCGPT demonstrate 1.5–2.8× speedups in inference and data-race detection, establishing LLMs as essential components of modern HPC workflows.

**Critical Challenges and Future Directions:**
- **Interoperability:** The absence of standardized APIs and data formats hinders the integration of AI components across HPC systems. Our analysis of 2024–2025 studies highlights the urgent need for FAIR (Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable) frameworks to enable seamless collaboration.
- **Scalability:** RL-based schedulers face limitations in handling massive action spaces, requiring innovations in action masking and hybrid heuristic-RL architectures. The paper proposes a unified LLM-OS framework that integrates scheduling, fault detection, and performance optimization into a single adaptive system.
- **Security:** AI-enhanced HPC systems introduce new vulnerabilities, including model poisoning and adversarial attacks. Our review underscores the necessity of robust MLOps pipelines and secure LLM-OS architectures to mitigate risks in distributed environments.

This work establishes a foundational roadmap for AI integration in HPC, emphasizing the convergence of machine learning, optimization, and domain-specific knowledge. By addressing both technical and systemic barriers, this review provides actionable insights for researchers, engineers, and policymakers seeking to harness AI’s full potential in HPC ecosystems. The methodologies and findings presented here are indispensable for advancing sustainable, intelligent, and scalable HPC systems in the exascale era.
