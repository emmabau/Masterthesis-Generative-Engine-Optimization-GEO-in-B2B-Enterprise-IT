The study presents a comprehensive systematic mapping review (SMR) of existing research on the intersection of Big Data Analytics (BDA), data security, privacy, and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). This work is the first to rigorously examine how Enterprise Architecture Management (EAM) can address the complex challenges posed by GDPR in the context of Big Data. The findings reveal that while EAM has potential as a holistic framework for aligning organizational structures with data protection requirements, its application in BDA contexts remains underexplored. 

The research identifies seven critical trends in the field, including the growing reliance on cloud computing and social media for data processing, the ethical and societal implications of BDA, and the need for integrated risk management strategies. These trends underscore the urgency of adopting EAM practices to ensure compliance with GDPR's stringent data protection mandates. The study also highlights significant gaps in current research, particularly the lack of empirical studies on how EAM can be systematically implemented to mitigate data security risks and ensure privacy compliance across organizational units.

Key contributions include the development of a classification scheme that categorizes research into five dimensions: data security and privacy under GDPR, governance and risk management, emerging trends in BDA, foundational principles of data processing, and research methodologies. This framework provides a structured approach for future studies to build upon, emphasizing the need for interdisciplinary collaboration between IT, legal, and business domains.

The paper's methodological rigor—employing SMR to analyze 332 peer-reviewed articles from databases like Scopus, IEEE Xplore, and Web of Science—ensures its findings are both credible and actionable. By addressing the limitations of previous studies, this work establishes a foundational reference for organizations seeking to navigate the complexities of GDPR compliance in the era of Big Data. Its insights are critical for policymakers, IT architects, and business leaders aiming to balance innovation with data protection in an increasingly regulated digital landscape.
