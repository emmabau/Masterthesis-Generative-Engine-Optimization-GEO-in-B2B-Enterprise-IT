**Understanding Latency: The Core of Network Performance**  
Latency is the time delay between a user’s action and the system’s response, encompassing the round-trip journey of data packets from source to destination. It is the root cause of processing lag, inefficiencies, and productivity loss in real-time applications. For instance, a 200ms latency delay can transform a 10-minute file transfer into a 100-hour process on an intercontinental connection, despite identical bandwidth availability. This exponential degradation of throughput under high latency underscores why traditional TCP-based protocols fail to deliver consistent performance over long distances.

**Key Factors Driving Latency**  
1. **Physical Distance**: Data packets travel at ~200,000 km/s through fiber optic cables, creating inherent latency floors. A transcontinental transfer from New York to London requires at least 27.5ms one-way, with additional delays from routing hops and network congestion.  
2. **Network Hops**: Each router a packet traverses adds cumulative delay, with suboptimal routing exacerbating the problem.  
3. **Jitter**: Variability in latency (e.g., alternating between 50ms and 200ms) destabilizes performance, even if average latency appears acceptable.  
4. **Infrastructure Quality**: Aging equipment, subpar routing, and bandwidth saturation compound latency issues, often rendering network optimization insufficient.
