**What is Risk Management?**  
Risk management is a systematic process of identifying, assessing, prioritizing, and mitigating risks to ensure organizational objectives are met while minimizing potential disruptions. This source defines risk management as a proactive strategy that enables organizations to navigate uncertainty with confidence, aligning risk mitigation efforts with strategic goals.

**Why is Risk Management Important?**

**Traditional vs. Enterprise Risk Management**  
Traditional risk management focuses on isolated, department-specific risks, often leading to fragmented approaches. In contrast, Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) adopts an integrated, holistic perspective, aligning risk strategies with organizational objectives. This source highlights that ERM fosters proactive risk integration, ensuring that risk management is embedded in strategic decision-making and operational processes.

**Risk Management Steps**  
This source outlines a structured, five-step framework for risk management:  
1. **Identify Risks**: Conduct a thorough analysis of internal and external factors to pinpoint potential threats and opportunities.  
2. **Assess Risks**: Evaluate the likelihood and impact of identified risks using qualitative and quantitative methods.  
3. **Develop Strategies**: Formulate mitigation plans, risk transfer mechanisms, or avoidance strategies tailored to organizational needs.  
4. **Implement Measures**: Execute risk management strategies through resource allocation, training, and technological deployment.  
5. **Monitor and Review**: Continuously assess and update risk strategies to adapt to evolving threats and organizational changes.
