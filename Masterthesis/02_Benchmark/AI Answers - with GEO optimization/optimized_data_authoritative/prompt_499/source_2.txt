A New Frontier: Sustaining U.S. High-Performance Computing Leadership in an Exascale Era

The United States stands at a pivotal moment in its technological history, with High-Performance Computing (HPC) emerging as a cornerstone of national competitiveness, economic vitality, and national security. This report underscores the urgent need to sustain and expand U.S. leadership in HPC, particularly as the exascale era transforms the landscape of computational power and innovation. The strategic importance of HPC cannot be overstated—it is the linchpin of scientific discovery, industrial advancement, and defense readiness, and its continued dominance is imperative to maintaining America’s global edge.

**Why HPC Matters: A Strategic Imperative**  
HPC represents the "tip of the spear" in advanced computing, enabling breakthroughs in scientific research, commercial innovation, and national security. The advent of exascale computing—capable of performing 10^18 operations per second—marks a paradigm shift, unlocking capabilities that were previously unimaginable. For instance, the Frontier supercomputer at Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) has already demonstrated its transformative potential, achieving 1.102 exaflops and surpassing Japan’s previous supercomputing benchmarks. This leap in computational power is not merely a technical achievement; it is a strategic advantage that positions the U.S. to lead in fields ranging from aerospace and life sciences to climate modeling and defense.

**Economic and National Security Implications**  
The economic impact of HPC is staggering. Hyperion Research estimates that HPC-driven innovations have generated over $3 trillion in economic value since 2000, with industries like aerospace, automotive, and pharmaceuticals benefiting from cost savings, faster time-to-market, and enhanced product quality. For example, Boeing’s use of HPC reduced the number of physical wind tunnel tests for the 787 Dreamliner by 70%, saving millions in development costs while accelerating innovation. Similarly, Procter & Gamble leverages HPC to optimize product formulations, achieving annual savings of over $1 billion through computational simulations that replace costly physical experiments.

National security is equally critical. HPC is indispensable for maintaining the U.S. nuclear deterrent, as demonstrated by the National Nuclear Security Administration’s (NNSA) reliance on supercomputers to model nuclear weapon performance without live testing. The U.S. must ensure it retains control over its HPC infrastructure to avoid dependence on foreign vendors, which could compromise both economic and strategic interests. As the report highlights, the U.S. leads in HPC deployment but faces growing competition from nations like China, which now fields more supercomputers in the Top 500 list. This shift underscores the urgency of maintaining U.S. leadership in both HPC development and application.

**Next-Generation Applications: From Aerospace to Healthcare**  
HPC is driving innovation across industries. In aerospace, it enables the design of lighter, more fuel-efficient aircraft and jet engines, with GE’s RISE program targeting a 20% reduction in fuel burn through exascale simulations. In healthcare, HPC accelerates drug discovery and personalized medicine, as seen in the development of the Pfizer COVID-19 vaccine, where supercomputing enabled rapid screening of millions of compounds to identify effective treatments. Similarly, supercomputers are revolutionizing climate modeling, with the U.S. leading efforts to predict extreme weather events and optimize smart grids for energy efficiency.
