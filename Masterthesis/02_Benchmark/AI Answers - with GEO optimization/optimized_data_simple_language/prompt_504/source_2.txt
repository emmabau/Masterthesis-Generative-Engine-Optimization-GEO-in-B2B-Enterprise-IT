Hybrid work can boost individual perks, but team productivity requires intentional strategies. Gallup research with 2,877 U.S. hybrid workers highlights four key steps to improve collaboration:

1. **Create a Team Plan**  
   Over half of hybrid workers lack a clear plan for effective collaboration. A structured plan helps teams align goals, reduce confusion, and boost engagement. Teams with plans see:  
   - 2.2x more likelihood of reporting positive collaboration impacts  
   - 66% higher employee engagement  
   - 29% lower burnout rates  
   Plans should include team goals, communication norms, and shared expectations. Managers and leaders often lack these plans, creating gaps in hybrid teamwork.

2. **Set Clear Collaboration Guidelines**  
   Regular check-ins (67% of teams) are most common, but guidelines for availability (62%) and unavailability (50%) have the biggest impact on engagement. Less common practices include on-site schedules (44%) and prioritizing in-office activities (29%). Teams that prioritize collaboration during in-person time see the greatest benefits.

3. **Reflect and Adapt Regularly**  
   Teams with ongoing discussions (84% of hybrid workers) improve collaboration and engagement. Weekly/monthly check-ins help build rapport and solve issues. Teams that only discuss hybrid work once a year miss opportunities to refine practices and address challenges.

4. **Invest in Training**  
   Only 21% of hybrid workers receive training for effective collaboration. Managers need training too—28% of hybrid managers are trained to lead teams. Gallup finds that effective managers boost engagement fourfold, regardless of remote work arrangements.

**Key Takeaway:** Hybrid teams succeed when leaders create plans, set clear guidelines, foster ongoing reflection, and prioritize training. Without these steps, teams risk stagnation. As one worker noted, "Regular meetings help build collaboration and trust." Leaders must actively shape hybrid work to maximize productivity.
