The paper explores the intersection of Big Data Analytics (BDA), data privacy, and the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), highlighting critical challenges and opportunities for Enterprise Architecture Management (EAM) in ensuring compliance. Key insights include:

**Core Challenges**  
- **Data Security & Privacy Risks**: BDA's scale and complexity introduce significant risks, including unauthorized access, data breaches, and misuse of personal information. GDPR's strict requirements for lawful data processing and transparency add pressure on organizations to adopt robust safeguards.  
- **EAM's Role**: EAM offers a holistic framework to align data governance, risk management, and compliance efforts across organizational units. However, research gaps persist in integrating EAM practices with GDPR requirements in BDA contexts.  

**Key Findings**  
1. **Limited EAM Research**: Few studies explicitly link EAM to GDPR compliance in BDA, despite its potential to address systemic risks through strategic alignment of technology, processes, and policies.  
2. **Seven Emerging Trends**:  
   - Ethical and societal impacts of BDA (e.g., discrimination, fairness).  
   - Cloud computing and social media's role in data proliferation.  
   - Privacy-by-design principles and data minimization.  
   - Governance frameworks for unstructured data.  
   - Risk management in cross-border data flows.  
   - Legal and technical challenges in anonymization.  
   - Integration of EAM with data protection impact assessments (DPIAs).  

**Methodology**  
A Systematic Mapping Review (SMR) analyzed 332 studies (2013–2020) to identify trends, gaps, and solutions. Techniques like Template Analysis helped categorize research themes, revealing EAM's underutilization in GDPR contexts.  

**Implications**  
- **For Organizations**: EAM can streamline GDPR compliance by embedding data protection into business processes, reducing risks of non-compliance and reputational damage.  
- **For Researchers**: Future work should focus on scalable EAM frameworks, ethical BDA practices, and tools for real-time risk monitoring.
