Business continuity planning ensures your company can keep running during crises by prioritizing critical processes. This guide explains how to create a BCP for small businesses, covering risk assessment, impact analysis, and testing strategies.

**What is a Business Continuity Plan?**  
A BCP outlines how your business operates during emergencies to minimize disruption. It defines roles for tasks like payroll and supplier switching, and includes regular testing to ensure preparedness.  

**Why Every Business Needs a BCP**  
- 91% of businesses faced disruptions (up 22% since 2019), with cyberattacks and supply chain issues being top concerns.  
- Required by industries like finance (FINRA Rule 4370) and critical for compliance, customer trust, and financial stability.  

**9 Steps to Build a Business Continuity Plan**  
1. **Identify Critical Functions**  
   List essential processes (e.g., payment processing, customer communication) that keep your business operational.  

2. **Map Dependencies**  
   Create a spreadsheet showing what each function relies on (tools, staff, vendors, data). Highlight single points of failure.  

3. **Conduct a Business Impact Analysis (BIA)**  
   Score risks based on:  
   - Maximum allowable downtime (MAD)  
   - Financial loss per hour/day  
   - Customer impact (low to high)  
   - Legal risks (e.g., data breaches)  
   - Operational priority  

4. **Assess Likely Risks**  
   Prioritize threats like cyberattacks, internet outages, or supply chain disruptions using likelihood and impact scores.  

5. **Develop Continuity Strategies**  
   Plan backups for critical systems (e.g., SLAs with vendors, backup ISPs, generators). Focus on minimizing downtime.  

6. **Create Response Procedures**  
   Define actions for high-impact scenarios (e.g., payment processor failure):  
   - Triggers (e.g., staff illness)  
   - First-hour steps (e.g., switch to backup tools)  
   - Roles for communication, finance, and vendor management  

7. **Establish Communication Protocols**  
   Prepare templates for notifying employees, customers, and vendors during outages. Use automated workflows for speed.  

8. **Document Backup & Recovery**  
   Outline how to access online backups, restore files, and prioritize recovery steps. Include offline storage locations.  

9. **Test, Train, and Update**  
   Conduct annual drills and quarterly checks. Track recovery time metrics (RTA) and update plans as technology or teams evolve.  

**Key Differences: BCP vs. Disaster Recovery**  
- **BCP**: Focuses on keeping operations running during disruptions (e.g., using backups).  
- **Disaster Recovery (DRP)**: Restores technology and infrastructure post-crisis.  

**Benefits of a BCP**  
- Increases resilience against recessions, natural disasters, or power outages.  
- Reduces financial risks, prevents customer dissatisfaction, and improves employee retention.  
- Ensures compliance with industry regulations and legal obligations.
