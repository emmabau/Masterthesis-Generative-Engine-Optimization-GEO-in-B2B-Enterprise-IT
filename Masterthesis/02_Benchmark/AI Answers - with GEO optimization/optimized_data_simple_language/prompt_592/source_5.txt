B2B companies face significant challenges in product integration strategies, according to a recent survey of 260 product managers and engineers. Here are the key issues:

**1. Limited Integration Offerings**  
Most B2B companies (68%) offer fewer than 10 integrations, which fails to address the vast ecosystem of SaaS vendors (hundreds to thousands in categories like CRM, HRIS, or ticketing). This narrow approach leaves most clients frustrated, driving them to seek alternatives.

**2. Overextending Across Categories**  
Attempting to cover multiple software categories (e.g., CRM, HRIS, project management) while limited in integration capacity leads to under-serving each category. For example, offering 8 integrations across 4 categories results in only 2 per category, missing critical client needs.

**3. Slow Development Cycles**  
71% of organizations take 3+ weeks to build a single integration. This pace becomes unsustainable as demand grows, creating backlogs that strain engineers and customer teams.

**4. Performance and Partnership Challenges**  
- 55% struggle with integration performance (e.g., unreliable data sync, security gaps).  
- 38% face outdated or restricted API documentation.  
- 43% find third-party partnerships cost-prohibitive (fees up to tens of thousands annually).

**5. Maintenance Overhead**  
Companies spend more time fixing broken integrations (46%) or diagnosing issues (48%) than improving existing ones. Monitoring version changes, handling discontinued features, and resolving API changes outside their control consume critical resources.

**6. 2024’s Aggressive Integration Goals**  
Half of B2B companies aim to build 15+ integrations, while 19% target 30+. To meet these goals, organizations must prioritize:  
- Allocating more engineering resources  
- Investing in third-party tools  
- Combining both strategies  

The urgency to scale integrations in 2024 underscores the need for transformative changes in how B2B companies approach product integration strategies.
