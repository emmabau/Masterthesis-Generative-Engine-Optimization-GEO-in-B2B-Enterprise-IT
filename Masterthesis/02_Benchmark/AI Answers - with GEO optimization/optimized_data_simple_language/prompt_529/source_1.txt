Compliance Program Metrics That Boards Actually Want to See  
Understanding which metrics matter most for board reporting and how to present them effectively  

Boards want to know if your compliance program is working — not just existing. Here’s how to show real impact with data that answers three key questions:  
1. Are we exposed to risks?  
2. Are we protecting the organization?  
3. Can we prove it under scrutiny?  

**Why Activity Metrics Fall Short**  
Numbers like training completions or hotline call volumes only show "busyness," not effectiveness. The DOJ’s updated Corporate Enforcement Policy focuses on whether programs are "working in practice" — meaning outcomes, not outputs.  

**The 4 Key Metrics That Matter**  
Use these four dimensions to build a clear, strategic narrative for your board:  

1. **Culture & Engagement**  
   - *Reports per 100 employees*: 1.4–3.6 reports annually signal a healthy speak-up culture. Higher rates often mean trained intake specialists.  
   - *Identified caller rate*: 50%+ shows trust in anonymity. 75%+ indicates strong psychological safety.  
   - *Reporter satisfaction*: 90%+ means the reporting process reinforces trust.  
   - *Risk assessment participation*: 80–90% completion shows engagement, while lower rates suggest survey fatigue or poor communication.  

2. **Detection & Response**  
   - *Hotline abandonment rate*: <1% is best. Industry average is 15–19%.  
   - *First response time*: Under 2 hours shows operational readiness.  
   - *Case resolution time*: Shrinking times (without cutting corners) prove efficiency.  
   - *Substantiation rate*: Balance this with case volume to avoid over- or under-reporting.  

3. **Risk Mitigation**  
   - *Disclosure completion rates*: Low rates = unmanaged risk.  
   - *Corrective action completion*: Shows whether issues are resolved after investigations.  
   - *Repeat offense rate*: Declining rates = behavioral change. Rising rates = unresolved root causes.  
   - *Exclusion screening coverage*: Critical for healthcare orgs. Gaps = legal exposure.  

4. **Program Maturity**  
   - *Risk heat map trends*: Show how risk profiles shift over time.  
   - *Intake channel diversity*: Multiple channels (hotline, web, SMS) = accessibility.  
   - *Year-over-year trends*: Trends tell a story — snapshots don’t.  

**How to Present Metrics to Boards**  
- **Start with risk**: Open with top risks addressed, not just numbers.  
- **Use benchmarks**: Compare your data to industry standards (e.g., 2% abandonment vs. 15–19% average).  
- **Answer "so what?"**: Explain how metrics impact risk, reputation, or outcomes.  
- **Keep it visual**: Use dashboards, heat maps, and color-coded indicators.  
- **Be honest**: Share gaps and plans — boards trust transparency.  

**Common Mistakes to Avoid**  
- **Vanity metrics**: "10,000 training completions" means nothing without showing behavior change.  
- **Data silos**: Use centralized platforms to connect hotline, case, and disclosure data.  
- **Infrequent reporting**: Quarterly updates (with real-time dashboards) keep compliance strategic.  
- **Ignoring qualitative data**: Combine numbers with insights from interviews or risk assessments.  

**Building a Metrics Framework**  
1. Align metrics to your top 5 risks.  
2. Set baselines (even imperfect data is better than none).  
3. Automate data collection to avoid errors.  
4. Set targets and review quarterly.  
5. Tell a story: Risk → actions → evidence → next steps.  

**Key Takeaways**  
- Activity metrics = existence. Effectiveness metrics = value.  
- Focus on culture, detection, risk, and maturity.  
- Context matters: Benchmark against industry standards and show trends.  
- Centralized data = clearer narratives.  
- Be candid: Share gaps and plans.  

**FAQs**  
- *How often to report?* Quarterly minimum. Annual deep-dive reports are ideal.  
- *Most important metric?* Reports per 100 employees (paired with identified caller rate and substantiation rate).  
- *How to benchmark?* Use ECI or compliance tech providers’ anonymized data.  
- *What if the board doesn’t ask?* Proactively show maturity — even before they request it.  
- *How do metrics relate to DOJ evaluations?* They prove programs "work in practice" under scrutiny.
