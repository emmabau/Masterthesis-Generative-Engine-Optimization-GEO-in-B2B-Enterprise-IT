High-Performance Computing (HPC) is a cornerstone of U.S. competitiveness in science, technology, and national security. As the world enters the exascale era—marked by supercomputers capable of one quintillion calculations per second—HPC is unlocking breakthroughs in aerospace, healthcare, energy, and more. This report highlights why HPC matters, its economic impact, and how the U.S. can maintain its leadership.

**Why HPC Matters**  
HPC enables solving complex problems that were once impossible. For example:  
- **Scientific Discovery**: Exascale systems simulate everything from atomic interactions to climate patterns, accelerating breakthroughs in medicine, energy, and materials science.  
- **National Security**: HPC powers nuclear stockpile stewardship, cybersecurity, and defense simulations, ensuring the U.S. maintains a strategic edge.  
- **Economic Growth**: HPC drives innovation in industries like aerospace, automotive, and pharmaceuticals, creating jobs and boosting productivity.  

**Key Applications of HPC**  
1. **Aerospace**: HPC designs fuel-efficient aircraft and jet engines, reducing costs and improving safety. For example, Boeing uses HPC to cut wind tunnel testing by 70%, saving millions in R&D.  
2. **Healthcare**: Supercomputers accelerate drug discovery, like Pfizer’s use of HPC to develop COVID-19 vaccines in record time.  
3. **Energy**: HPC optimizes wind farms and smart grids, enhancing renewable energy efficiency.  
4. **Manufacturing**: Companies like Procter & Gamble use HPC to design better products, saving over $1 billion in costs.  

**Economic Impact**  
- HPC generates **$3 trillion in economic value** globally, with U.S. firms realizing **$452 in revenue for every $1 invested**.  
- The HPC industry itself is worth **$34.8 billion**, with projected growth to **$90 billion by 2026**.  

**Policy Recommendations**  
1. **Invest in HPC Infrastructure**: Expand funding for exascale systems and ensure access for researchers and industries.  
2. **Strengthen National Leadership**: Prioritize U.S. manufacturing of HPC systems to avoid reliance on foreign suppliers.  
3. **Support Workforce Development**: Train more scientists and engineers to meet growing demand for HPC expertise.  
4. **Foster Public-Private Partnerships**: Encourage collaboration between academia, industry, and government to drive innovation.  

**Conclusion**  
HPC is not just a tool—it’s a strategic asset. By investing in HPC, the U.S. can lead in scientific discovery, economic growth, and national security, ensuring its position as a global innovator.
