NIS2 Compliance: A Guide for Businesses

What is NIS2?
The Network and Information Security Directive 2 (NIS2) is the EU's updated cybersecurity framework, replacing the 2016 NIS directive. It strengthens cybersecurity for critical sectors by requiring strict risk management, incident reporting, and supply chain security. Non-compliance can result in fines up to €10 million or 2% of global turnover for essential entities, and €7 million or 1.4% for important entities.

Who Must Comply?
NIS2 applies to:
- **Essential Entities**: Energy, transport, finance, health, public administration, digital infrastructure (large organizations with >250 employees or €50M+ turnover)
- **Important Entities**: Food, manufacturing, digital services, chemicals, postal services (medium-sized with >50 employees or €10M+ turnover)
- Non-EU businesses serving EU customers also must comply

Key Requirements
1. **Risk Management**: Conduct regular cybersecurity risk assessments and implement security controls
2. **Incident Reporting**: Report major incidents within 24/72 hours and submit detailed reports within 1 month
3. **Supply Chain Security**: Assess and secure third-party vendors
4. **Business Continuity**: Develop disaster recovery plans with defined recovery time objectives
5. **Access Controls**: Use multi-factor authentication and least-privilege access
6. **Encryption**: Protect data in transit and at rest

Penalties for Non-Compliance
- Essential entities: Up to €10M or 2% of global turnover
- Important entities: Up to €7M or 1.4% of global turnover
- Fines apply for delayed reporting, poor security practices, or management negligence

How to Achieve Compliance
1. Conduct a gap analysis to identify security weaknesses
2. Implement a risk management framework with board-level oversight
3. Train employees on cybersecurity best practices
4. Use encryption, access controls, and multi-factor authentication
5. Develop incident response plans and conduct regular drills
6. Maintain secure backups and test recovery processes
