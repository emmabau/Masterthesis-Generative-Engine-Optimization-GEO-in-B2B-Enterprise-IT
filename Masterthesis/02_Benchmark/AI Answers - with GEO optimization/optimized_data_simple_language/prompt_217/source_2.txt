The US Department of Energy (DOE) launched the Exascale Computing Project (ECP) in 2016 to build software infrastructure for next-generation supercomputers capable of over 10^18 operations per second. This initiative, extending through 2022, creates a unified software stack for DOE's exascale and pre-exascale systems, shaping the future of US high-performance computing (HPC). The ECP unites two DOE programs: the Office of Science Advanced Scientific Computing Research and the National Nuclear Security Administration Advanced Simulation and Computing. Over 100 teams of scientists and engineers from national labs, universities, and industry collaborate on developing applications, software, and hardware for exascale systems.

Key webinars explore critical HPC topics:
- **Developing Vascular Digital Twins**: Amanda Randles (Duke University) discusses shifting healthcare from reactive to proactive care using digital twins, moderated by Michela Taufer (University of Tennessee Knoxville).
- **HPC Evolution & Future Trends**: Jack Dongarra (University of Tennessee) examines how HPC has transformed over the past decade, emphasizing the need for new software libraries and algorithms to handle dynamic, distributed computing environments.
- **HPC in Industrial Design**: Brian Mitchell (GE Global Research) shares insights on using HPC to design aircraft engines and power generation systems, highlighting the balance between hardware/software solutions and cost-benefit analysis.
- **Performance & Scalability**: Salman Habib and Rajeev Thakur (Argonne National Lab) present the HACC framework, demonstrating how to design applications for diverse supercomputer architectures while maintaining high performance.
- **GPU Acceleration**: Mark Ebersole (NVIDIA) explains how GPUs enable parallel computing across devices from smartphones to supercomputers, covering CUDA, OpenACC, and future programming challenges.
- **Parallel Execution Models**: Bill Gropp (University of Illinois) explores how programming approaches impact scalability and reliability in both small-scale and large-scale parallel systems.

These webinars provide practical insights into HPC's role in solving complex problems, from healthcare to energy, while addressing the technical challenges of exascale computing. All sessions are archived at webinar.acm.org, and SIGHPC welcomes topic suggestions for future webinars at communications@sighpc.org.
