Q&A from TechTalk: Exascale Computing and HPC Insights

**1. Software Technology (ST) Efforts**  
The ECP's ST efforts cover programming languages, math libraries, compilers, debuggers, and open-source vs. closed-source tools. For detailed insights, refer to the [ECP ST Capability Assessment Report](https://www.exascaleproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/ECP-ST-CAR.pdf).

**2. Transitioning to HPC**  
To switch to HPC, leverage ECP training courses, online resources like Coursera, and job openings at DOE labs and universities. Attend conferences like SC19 or contact ECP leadership for guidance.

**3. System Architectures**  
The "diverse system architectures" mentioned refer to hardware designs (e.g., CPU vs. co-processors) and software stacks. Architectural diversity includes hardware, software, and programming models.

**4. Computer Graphics Tools**  
For graphics tools, animation controls, and data storage, consult the [ECP ST Capability Assessment Report](https://www.exascaleproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/ECP-ST-CAR.pdf).

**5. HPC Programming Languages**  
Common languages include C++, Fortran, Python, and CUDA. C++ is most prevalent, but ECP supports multiple languages through "language-agnostic" software stacks.

**6. Open-Source Tools for Non-DOE Users**  
Most ECP tools are open-source, accessible via the [ECP website](https://www.exascaleproject.org) and the [ST Capability Assessment Report](https://www.exascaleproject.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/ECP-ST-CAR.pdf).

**7. Application Portfolio Selection**  
Applications were chosen based on their alignment with DOE missions and national interests. They aim to solve broad scientific problems while demonstrating exascale readiness.

**8. Benefits for the HPC Community**  
Even without exascale access, developers benefit from tools designed for laptops/desktops, which emulate exascale hardware for efficient software testing.

**9. HPC Career Stability**  
HPC careers are stable, with long-term investments in DOE facilities like TACC, which has operated for over 25 years.

**10. Parallel Computing Approaches**  
ECP focuses on distributed memory for large-scale simulations and shared memory for multi-threaded tasks, balancing computational and communication efficiency.

**11. HPC in Education**  
ECP applications can be used in advanced courses, but simpler "proxies" are recommended for introductory classes. Contact ECP leadership for tailored resources.

**12. Data Organization in MPP Grids**  
Directed graphs (DAGs) are practical for task-based models in MPP systems. For deeper insights, reach out to ECP Software Technology leaders.

**13. Energy Efficiency in HPC**  
Reducing energy use focuses on minimizing data movement (which consumes 20–100x more power than computation). ECP prioritizes "communications-avoiding" software.

**14. GPU-Driven Software**  
ECP software adapts to GPU availability, optimizing compute kernels for hardware-specific performance.

**15. Building Exascale Stacks Without Hardware Specs**  
ECP collaborates with DOE HPC facilities and vendors to anticipate hardware trends, ensuring software aligns with upcoming exascale systems.

**16. Hardware and OS Integration**  
ECP software works with robust OSes and hardware, allowing deep integration with vendors where it enhances performance or efficiency.

**17. Strong Scaling Benchmarks**  
ECP provides strong-scaling data to help researchers optimize simulations, from continuum models to quantum calculations.

**18. Job Opportunities in HPC**  
Explore DOE lab websites, HPC center pages, and SIGHPC for job listings. Contact [vicechair@sighpc.org](mailto:vicechair@sighpc.org) for guidance.

**19. Fault Tolerance Metrics**  
The "<1 fault/week" metric (MTTI) reflects hardware-software resilience. ECP aims for reliable systems through combined fault-tolerant designs.

**20. Modern Fortran in Exascale**  
Fortran remains critical for legacy DOE applications, with the 2018 standard (COARRAYs) supporting parallelism. However, compiler availability is a challenge.

**21. RAJA vs. Kokkos**  
RAJA and Kokkos both enable performance portability. For details, see [Kokkos - Performance Portability](https://www.exascaleproject.org/project/exalearn-co-design-center-for-exascale-machine-learning-technologies/).

**22. Engaging with ECP on Redfish Standards**  
NSF-funded projects can collaborate with ECP’s hardware team via John West or ECP leadership for data center control standards.

**23. ECP Hardware Details**  
Visit DOE ASCR, ASC, or HPC facility websites (e.g., NERSC, OLCF) for hardware specifics.

**24. Benefits Beyond DOE**  
ECP technologies benefit universities, companies, and researchers through open standards and collaborative frameworks.

**25. ExaML vs. Industry ML**  
ExaML complements industry frameworks by enhancing them with exascale-specific capabilities, avoiding duplication.

**26. ISV Collaboration**  
ISVs (e.g., CAE, CFD) partner with ECP to integrate tools into their products, avoiding direct competition.

**27. ECP and Chameleon**  
ECP plans to deploy tools on Chameleon for testing and collaboration with NSF facilities like TACC.

**28. Performance Portability**  
ECP prioritizes performance portability across DOE systems, using abstractions like Kokkos and RAJA to ensure broad compatibility.

**29. Exascale for National Challenges**  
Exascale systems enable faster simulations, better statistical accuracy, and reduced model assumptions, aiding decisions on climate, economics, and more.
