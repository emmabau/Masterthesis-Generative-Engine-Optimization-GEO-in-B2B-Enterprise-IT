# NIS2 Compliance: A Step-by-Step Legal Analysis Guide  

## Key Takeaways  
- Start with a **legal analysis** to determine if your organization is classified as "essential" or "important" under NIS2.  
- Your classification directly impacts your obligations, deadlines, and penalties.  
- Always **include partner and subsidiary figures** when calculating your organization’s size.  
- Check for **hidden NIS2 categories** like cloud services, managed services, or online platforms.  
- Verify if the **implementing regulation** applies to your organization.  
- Incorrect classification leads to fines, delays, or even CEO removal.  

## Why Legal Analysis is Critical  
The NIS2 Directive requires organizations to meet strict cybersecurity standards based on their classification. A **legal analysis** ensures you:  
- Understand your **size** (FTEs, turnover, balance sheet) and how it affects your classification.  
- Identify which **NIS2 activities** apply to your business.  
- Determine the **correct national law** (e.g., Belgium, Germany, etc.) and handle overlapping regulations.  
- Know if you must undergo a **conformity assessment** or face penalties for non-compliance.  
- Plan for **supervision** (pre-incident checks for essential entities vs. post-incident audits for important entities).  
- Avoid **severe penalties**, including fines or executive removal for essential entities.  

## Essential vs. Important: The Classification Impact  
Your organization’s **classification** determines everything:  
- **Essential entities** face stricter requirements, including mandatory audits and potential CEO removal.  
- **Important entities** are only audited after incidents or non-compliance signs.  
- **Incorrect classification** (e.g., treating an essential entity as important) risks missed deadlines and penalties.  

## Hidden NIS2 Categories  
Don’t overlook less obvious sectors:  
- **Cloud service providers**  
- **Managed service providers (MSPs)**  
- **Online platforms**  
Even secondary activities (e.g., a hospital’s IT department offering services to other healthcare providers) count fully.  

## Consolidation: Don’t Miss This Step  
When calculating your organization’s size:  
- **Include figures** from affiliated and partner organizations (e.g., FTEs, turnover, balance sheet).  
- Failing to consolidate can misclassify your organization as "important" instead of "essential," leading to incorrect compliance steps.  

## Implementing Regulation: What You Need to Know  
The NIS2 implementing regulation adds technical requirements for specific categories:  
- Cloud service providers  
- MSPs offering IT services  
- Digital services with wide reach  
Even if your main activity isn’t listed, offering these services to others may trigger compliance.  

## Supervision and Deadlines  
- **Essential entities**: Face audits before and after incidents. Key deadline: **April 2026**.  
- **Important entities**: Only audited after incidents or non-compliance signs.  
- Incorrect classification risks missing deadlines and penalties.  

## Penalties for Non-Compliance  
- **Essential entities**: Fines, administrative measures, or CEO removal.  
- **Important entities**: Crisis managers or fines.  
- Incorrect classification (e.g., due to size miscalculations) can lead to being treated as "essential" without meeting requirements.  

## How to Conduct a Legal Analysis  
Follow these steps to ensure compliance:  
1. **Gather data**: Articles of association, financial figures, service overview.  
2. **Calculate size**: Use EC Recommendation 2003/361/EC and include partner figures.  
3. **Check NIS2 categories**: Review annexes for applicable sectors (including indirect activities).  
4. **Determine jurisdiction**: Identify which national NIS2 law applies (e.g., Belgium, Germany).  
5. **Assess supply chain**: Ensure suppliers meet NIS2 standards to avoid compliance risks.  
6. **Document findings**: Create a legal report and verify with a legal expert.  

## Final Tips  
- **Don’t skip the legal analysis**—it’s the foundation of your NIS2 strategy.  
- Use **checklists** and **legal experts** to avoid mistakes.  
- Stay updated on **national NIS2 transposition** (e.g., some countries may not have implemented the directive yet).  
- Secure **certifications** (e.g., ISO 27001, CyberFundamentals) to demonstrate compliance.  

By prioritizing legal analysis, you avoid costly errors and ensure your organization meets NIS2 requirements effectively.
