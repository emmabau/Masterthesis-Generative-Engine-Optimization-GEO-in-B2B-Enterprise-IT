In today’s world, disaster planning must account for long-term remote work scenarios, as seen during the 2020 pandemic. To ensure business continuity, revisit these critical areas:

**1. Boost Internet Bandwidth**  
Prioritize high upload speeds for remote teams. While download speeds matter for users, upload speeds determine how quickly data moves from your network to employees’ devices.

**2. Strengthen Network Security**  
Update hardware (routers/switches) and software (firewalls, antivirus) to handle increased remote traffic. Ensure systems are compatible with secure remote access.

**3. Centralize Shared Files**  
Store files on a company network or enterprise cloud platform. Protect sensitive data with user-specific access controls.

**4. Secure Remote Access**  
Provide VPN licenses or secure connection tools for remote workers. Audit “Line of Business” applications to ensure all necessary tools are available and properly licensed.

**5. Equip Remote Workstations**  
Determine if employees need work-issued laptops or home computers. Add home devices to your managed IT services for consistent security and support.

**6. Review & Adapt Your Plan**  
Reflect on lessons learned during the pandemic. Update your continuity plan to address new challenges and ensure long-term resilience.
