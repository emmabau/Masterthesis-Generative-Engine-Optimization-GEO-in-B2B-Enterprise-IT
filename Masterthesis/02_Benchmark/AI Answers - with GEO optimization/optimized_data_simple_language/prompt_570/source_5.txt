What is Exascale Computing?  
Exascale computing refers to supercomputing systems capable of performing at least 1 exaFLOPS (10¹⁸ floating-point operations per second). This level of power enables breakthroughs in scientific research, AI development, and data analysis, surpassing the capabilities of petascale systems.  

Why Exascale Computing Matters  
Exascale computing transforms how we solve complex global challenges. It powers advanced simulations for weather forecasting, healthcare, drug discovery, and physics research. Key benefits include:  
- **Scientific breakthroughs**: Enables kilometer-scale climate models, real-time earthquake simulations, and quantum-mechanical calculations.  
- **Frontline systems**: Supercomputers like Frontier, El Capitan, Aurora (U.S.), and Jupiter (Europe) now operate at exascale speeds.  
- **Strategic importance**: Critical for national security, energy innovation, and infrastructure resilience.  
- **Technological progress**: Drives advancements in hardware efficiency, parallel computing, and software engineering.  

How Exascale Computing Works  
- **Hardware**: Uses millions of multi-core CPUs and accelerators (GPUs, AI units) connected via high-speed networks.  
- **Software**: Breaks tasks into parallel processes, optimized with tools like the Exascale Computing Project (ECP).  
- **Resilience**: Designed to handle hardware failures with error correction and energy-efficient cooling.  
- **Specialized use**: Tailored for AI, simulations, real-time analytics, and scientific modeling.  

Key Features of Exascale Computing  
- **Performance**: At least 1 exaFLOPS, 1,000x faster than petascale systems.  
- **Measurement**: Standardized using 64-bit double-precision FLOPS (tested via HPLinpack benchmark).  
- **Infrastructure**: Built on energy-efficient, scalable architectures for high-speed data processing.  
- **Collaborative development**: Projects like the U.S. DOE’s ECP align hardware, software, and applications.  
- **Global competition**: Nations invest in exascale systems for science, climate modeling, medicine, and more.
