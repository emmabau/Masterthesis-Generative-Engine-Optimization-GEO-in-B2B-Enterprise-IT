The Internet of Things (IoT) has transformed industries and consumer experiences, but it also brings significant challenges, especially around data privacy and compliance. At TagoIO, we recognize the critical need to align IoT systems with regulations like the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), which protects user rights and builds trust. Here’s how IoT companies can address key compliance issues:

**1. Secure and Transparent Data Handling**  
GDPR requires IoT data to be stored securely, encrypted, and accessible only to authorized users. To comply:  
- **Collect only necessary data** (data minimization) to reduce risks.  
- **Clearly inform users** about what data is collected, where it’s stored, and how it’s used.  
- **Give users control** over their data, including the right to delete it.  

**2. Data Sovereignty Challenges**  
Data sovereignty—data being subject to the laws of the country where it’s stored—is central to GDPR. IoT complicates this because devices often transmit data across borders. To stay compliant:  
- **Store data in approved jurisdictions** (e.g., EU countries) or use strict safeguards for cross-border transfers.  
- **Monitor data flows** to ensure compliance with GDPR’s strict rules on data location.  

**3. TagoIO Solutions for GDPR Compliance**  
TagoIO helps companies meet these requirements with features like:  
- **TagoDeploy**: Deploy IoT systems in EU countries (e.g., Ireland, Germany) to ensure data stays within compliant regions.  
- **User Data Deletion**: Automatically delete all user data when a user requests “Delete my account.”  
- **Transparent Controls**: Manage data access, encryption, and storage settings through intuitive tools.  

By prioritizing secure, minimal, and transparent data practices, IoT companies can protect user privacy, avoid fines, and build trust in a global digital landscape.
