The NIS2 Directive (Directive 2022/2555) is the EU's updated cybersecurity framework, replacing the older NIS1 directive (2016/1148). It strengthens cybersecurity across 18 critical sectors, including energy, transport, healthcare, finance, and now expanded areas like public communications, digital services, waste management, and the space sector.  

**Key Requirements for Member States:**  
- Develop national cybersecurity strategies with focus on supply chain security, vulnerability management, and education.  
- Maintain updated lists of essential service providers and ensure compliance with NIS2 rules.  
- Implement risk management measures and report major cyber incidents to national authorities.  

**New Enforcement Tools:**  
- **EU-CyCLONe:** A network for managing large-scale cyber crises through coordinated EU and national efforts.  
- **CSIRTs:** Cybersecurity teams that share threat intelligence and respond to incidents.  
- **Accountability Measures:** Top management is now directly responsible for cybersecurity compliance.  

**Recent Updates (2026):**  
The EU proposed amendments to simplify compliance for 28,700 companies, including 6,200 small businesses, reducing administrative burdens while maintaining security standards.  

**Collaboration Frameworks:**  
- **NIS Cooperation Group:** Facilitates information sharing and strategic cooperation among EU nations, the European Commission, and ENISA.  
- **Peer Reviews:** Encourages voluntary assessments to build trust and improve cybersecurity capabilities across the EU.  

By expanding coverage, clarifying rules, and fostering cross-border collaboration, NIS2 aims to create a stronger, unified cybersecurity defense for Europe’s critical infrastructure and digital services.
