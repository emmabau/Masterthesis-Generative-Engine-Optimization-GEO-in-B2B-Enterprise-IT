6 Steps to Build a Robust Business Continuity Plan  

Every minute your business is offline costs money and damages your reputation. Downtime can lead to lost revenue, customer dissatisfaction, and even industry-wide scrutiny, as seen in the Colonial Pipeline ransomware incident. To avoid these risks, create a business continuity plan that includes data backup and disaster recovery strategies. Here’s how to do it effectively:  

**1. Identify Your Risks**  
List all potential threats to your operations, such as cyberattacks, natural disasters, power outages, or human error. A team effort ensures you cover every vulnerability.  

**2. Analyze Business Impact**  
Determine how long your business can afford to be offline (Recovery Time Objective or RTO) and how much data you can afford to lose (Recovery Point Objective or RPO). Use tools like the Ready.gov business impact analysis worksheet to guide this process.  

**3. Prioritize Critical Systems**  
Map out your network, hardware, and software dependencies to identify mission-critical systems. This helps you focus recovery efforts on what matters most.  

**4. Implement a Reliable Backup Strategy**  
Adopt a 3-2-1-1 backup plan: Keep three copies of your data across two storage types, with one copy offsite (cloud or secure storage) and one in immutable storage. This defends against ransomware and ensures data resilience.  

**5. Create a Disaster Recovery Plan**  
Outline steps to restore operations, including technologies needed to meet your RTO and RPO goals. Options include file-based recovery, virtual machine recovery, or cloud-based solutions.  

**6. Test Your Plan Regularly**  
Regular testing ensures your plan works when needed. Tools like Arcserve Cloud Services allow you to simulate a failover with a single click.
