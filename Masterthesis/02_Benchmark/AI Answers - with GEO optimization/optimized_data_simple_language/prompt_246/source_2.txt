The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), effective May 25, 2018, is a critical privacy law for businesses operating in the European Union (EU) and globally. Here’s a simplified guide to key requirements and implications:

**Key GDPR Requirements**  
1. **Scope**: Applies to all companies worldwide that handle EU citizens’ data, including those with EU customers or partners.  
2. **Data Subject Rights**:  
   - **Right to be Forgotten**: Users can request deletion of their personal data.  
   - **Data Access & Control**: Users must easily access, edit, delete, or export their data.  
   - **Data Portability**: Users can transfer their data between services in a usable format.  
3. **Compliance Obligations**:  
   - **Data Protection Officer (DPO)**: Required for large organizations or those processing sensitive data.  
   - **Data Breach Reporting**: Notify EU authorities of breaches within 72 hours.  
   - **Storage Location**: Store personal data within the EU or ensure secure cross-border transfers.  

**Understanding Roles**  
- **Controller**: Business deciding how to use EU residents’ data (e.g., online retailers, healthcare providers).  
- **Processor**: Third-party handling data on behalf of controllers (e.g., cloud storage providers).  

**Technical Requirements for Compliance**  
- **Data Encryption**: Encrypt data at rest and in transit, with decryption keys controlled by data subjects.  
- **Backup & Recovery**:  
  - Search backups to locate specific data quickly.  
  - Restore data rapidly in case of breaches, hardware failure, or errors.  
- **Data Storage**:  
  - Allow users to specify where data is stored (e.g., EU-based servers).  
  - Support granular deletion and modification of personal data.  

**Penalties for Non-Compliance**  
- Fines up to €20 million or 4% of global revenue (whichever is higher) for severe violations.  
- Fines up to €10 million or 2% of revenue for lesser breaches (e.g., poor record-keeping).  

**Key Terminology**  
- **Data Subject**: Any EU citizen whose data is processed.  
- **Personal Data**: Any information identifying an individual (e.g., names, IP addresses, social media posts).  
- **Data Breach**: Unauthorized access, loss, or theft of personal data.  

**Best Practices for Businesses**  
- Use automated tools for compliance tracking and breach monitoring.  
- Prioritize privacy-by-design principles (e.g., pseudonymization).  
- Regularly review data storage and processing practices to align with GDPR.
