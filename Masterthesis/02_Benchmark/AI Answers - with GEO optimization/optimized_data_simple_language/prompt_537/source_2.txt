Corporate Security Governance: A Modern Approach

Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) has evolved to address ethical, reputational, employee safety, cyber threats, and business continuity risks. These expanded concerns require security programs to implement robust internal controls that support governance practices guiding corporate strategy and operations.

A mature security program includes:
- **Operational Risk Leadership Advisory Council (ORLAC):** A cross-functional team reporting to the ERM committee that enables collaboration between departments like Cyber Security, IT, Legal, HR, and Supply Chain. This council helps prioritize operational risks, remove redundancies, and address emerging threats through shared expertise.
- **Security Policies:** Clear, documented guidelines that define how risks are managed. Policies should include ownership details, stakeholder roles, and avoid unnecessary jargon. Store them in searchable databases with version control and regular reviews.
- **Risk-Based Standards:** Align with regulations like the Food Safety Modernization Act, NERC-CIP, and GDPR, as well as standards like ISO 37000 (ethics governance) and ISO 27002 (information security). Certification to these standards can enhance credibility and support third-party risk management.
- **Audit Programs:** Independent audits ensure security controls align with business goals. Third-party audits should assess suppliers based on risk factors like geographic location and operational dependency.
- **Metrics:** Track adoption and enforcement of security programs using measurable indicators. Include these metrics in supplier contracts and project evaluations.

**Emerging Trends in Security Governance:**
1. **AI and Automation:** Machine learning detects risks in real time, while automation streamlines compliance checks and log reviews. These tools improve efficiency and enable data-driven decisions.
2. **Hybrid Work Security:** Address risks from decentralized teams by securing home networks, managing endpoint devices, and mitigating insider threats through identity management and clear policies.
3. **ESG Integration:** Align security programs with Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) goals to enhance transparency, meet regulations, and appeal to socially conscious stakeholders.
