Fact Check Team: Remote and Hybrid Job Opportunities for High Earners Are Declining  

New research reveals a sharp drop in remote and hybrid job opportunities for high earners. Ladders, a platform for high-paying jobs, reports that remote roles paying $250,000+ annually have fallen by 60%, while hybrid roles in the same range have dropped by 95%.  

This trend extends beyond high-income roles. LinkedIn highlights that remote job demand far outstrips supply, with twice as many applicants as available positions. Remote job postings also declined by 23% year-over-year.  

The number of U.S. workers working remotely has decreased. Last year, 12.2% of workers were fully remote—down from 14% the prior year. Over 28% worked hybrid schedules, but both remote and hybrid opportunities have been declining since 2020.  

Despite fewer options, most workers still prefer remote or hybrid setups. FlexJobs finds 65% want full-time remote work, and 32% prefer hybrid. The Future Workforce Pulse Report predicts 22% of the U.S. workforce (32.6 million people) will work remotely by 2025, with growth expected as technology advances.
