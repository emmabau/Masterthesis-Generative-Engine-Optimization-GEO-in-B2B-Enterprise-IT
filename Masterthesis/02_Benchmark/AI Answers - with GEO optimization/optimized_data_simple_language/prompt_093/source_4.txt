we  
A versatile pronoun meaning "we" in English, with rich linguistic roots and diverse usage across languages.  

**Key Points:**  
1. **Origins:**  
   - From Old English *wē*, linked to Proto-Germanic *wīz* and Proto-Indo-European *wéy*.  
   - Cognate with Scots *wee*, Dutch *wij*, German *wir*, and Scandinavian *vi*.  

2. **Pronunciation:**  
   - /wiː/ (Received Pronunciation) or /wi/ (General American).  
   - Homophones: *oui* (French "yes"), *Wii* (brand name).  

3. **Usage:**  
   - **Inclusive "we":** Includes the listener (e.g., "We need to talk, you and me").  
   - **Exclusive "we":** Excludes the listener (e.g., "We are here to arrest you").  
   - **Royal "we":** Used by monarchs (e.g., "We are Queen of these Isles").  
   - **Colloquial "we":** Friendly or patronizing tone (e.g., "How are we feeling today?").  

4. **Variations in Other Languages:**  
   - **Dutch:** *wij* (subject) vs. *we* (object).  
   - **French:** *nous* (subject) vs. *nous* (object).  
   - **Tok Pisin:** *we* (plural, "we/us").  
   - **Japanese:** *ware* (archaic) or *ware wa* (modern).  

5. **Derived Terms & Phrases:**  
   - "We are the champions" (song lyric).  
   - "We live and learn" (idiom).  
   - "Houston, we have a problem" (famous phrase).  

6. **Notes:**  
   - In English, *we* does not inflect adjectives for gender.  
   - The word appears in over 100 languages, reflecting its universal role in communication.
