5 Steps to Strengthen Your BCM Program in a Changing Risk Landscape  

The COVID-19 pandemic highlighted the importance of business continuity management (BCM) by showing how prepared organizations recover faster from crises like pandemics, cyberattacks, or supply chain disruptions. Here’s how to modernize your BCM strategy:  

1. **Update Your Risk Assessment**  
   Many companies underestimated pandemic risks, with 82% of organizations identifying it as a top risk in 2020—but fewer than 31% had a pandemic plan. Risks are evolving: remote work and tech reliance now increase cyberattack risks. Regularly review and update your risk register to reflect new threats.  

2. **Break Down Silos**  
   Fragmented BCM efforts lead to confusion. A manufacturer improved its response by uniting six business units to share insights and create a unified plan. Centralizing BCM fosters collaboration and ensures consistent preparedness across the organization.  

3. **Test, Refresh, and Improve**  
   BCM plans must be dynamic, not static. Update them annually to reflect changes in suppliers, technology, and operations. Testing is critical: even a 2-hour tabletop exercise with key leaders can identify gaps (e.g., lack of remote work tools during the pandemic). Ask:  
   - When was your last test?  
   - What’s the protocol for activating the plan?  
   - How will you communicate during a crisis?  
   - What tech and skills are required?  

4. **Demand BCM Standards from Vendors**  
   Supply chain risks are growing—microchip shortages and lumber scarcity disrupted industries. Ensure vendors meet your BCM standards to avoid cascading risks. Leading companies now require vendors to align with their resilience goals, creating a safer ecosystem.  

5. **Embed BCM into Your Culture**  
   BCM isn’t just a plan—it’s a mindset. CEO support and clear communication channels are vital to embedding risk resilience into your company’s DNA. Prioritize continuous improvement to stay agile in uncertain times.  

By adopting these steps, organizations can build stronger, more adaptable BCM programs that withstand future disruptions.
