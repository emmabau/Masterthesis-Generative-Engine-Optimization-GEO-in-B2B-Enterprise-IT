7 Key Challenges of Hybrid Work & How HR Can Fix Them  

Hybrid work isn’t working for many companies—here’s why and what HR can do to address it.  

**The Big Picture**  
Research by Harvard Business Review experts Peter Cappelli and Ranya Nehmeh reveals hybrid work creates seven major issues, many of which existed pre-pandemic but were resolved through in-person interactions.  

**Top 7 Hybrid Work Problems**  
1. **New Hires Struggle**  
   Remote/hybrid teams make onboarding harder. New hires lack mentors to guide them, leading to slower learning and extra work to get help.  

2. **Employees Focus on Themselves**  
   Remote workers prioritize individual goals over teamwork. Without face-to-face interactions, collaboration drops, and help requests are delayed.  

3. **Teams Collaborate Less**  
   Reduced in-person interaction leads to poor cross-team communication. Brainstorming and meetings become inefficient, and information silos form.  

4. **Meetings Lose Effectiveness**  
   Virtual meetings:  
   - Generate fewer ideas  
   - Fill calendars with frequent, low-productivity sessions  
   - Include more people, slowing focus  
   - Encourage multitasking and wasted time  

5. **Wrong People Get Promoted**  
   Remote work rewards individual performance over leadership skills. Studies show these leaders often create underperforming teams.  

6. **Culture Splits**  
   Pre-pandemic and post-hybrid employees develop different cultural norms, leading to misunderstandings and reduced cohesion.  

7. **Employee Commitment Drops**  
   Less in-person interaction weakens connections to the company, increasing turnover.  

**Why Hybrid Isn’t Working**  
Despite research, 55% of Baby Boomers/Gen X and 71% of Gen Z prefer hybrid work. Cutting hybrid risks demotivating employees and driving them to seek new jobs.  

**Solutions for HR**  
1. **Treat Hybrid Like a New Workplace**  
   Use fresh rules and policies to replace outdated in-office practices.  

2. **Set Clear In-Office Schedules**  
   Create “anchor days” for collaboration. For highly collaborative roles, consider a four-day workweek with flexible Fridays.  

3. **Design Spaces for Connection**  
   Use office time for teamwork, not just individual work. Avoid downsizing offices as an excuse for reduced in-person interaction.  

4. **Revise KPIs and Promotions**  
   Reward teamwork and collaboration, not just individual performance.  

5. **Build Cross-Team Communities**  
   Encourage mentorship, reverse mentoring, and social events. Create opportunities for face-to-face interactions across teams.  

6. **Support Mental Well-Being**  
   Train managers to check in regularly and identify burnout. Provide resources to combat isolation in remote work.
