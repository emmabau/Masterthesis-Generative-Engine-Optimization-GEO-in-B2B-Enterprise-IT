Hyperconvergence is a growing trend in IT infrastructure, with strong market growth predicted. Gartner forecasts the hyperconverged infrastructure (HCI) market will reach $2 billion by 2016 and $5 billion by 2019. A 451 Research survey shows 86% of enterprises planning server/storage refreshes will boost HCI investments. 

Key players include Nutanix, Pivot3, Simplivity (leaders), and Dell EMC, VMware, HPE, Cisco, Huawei (strong performers). However, experts caution against overhyping the technology—just because a system is labeled "hyperconverged" doesn't guarantee it delivers all promised benefits.

What is hyperconvergence?  
It combines compute and storage resources using software-defined infrastructure, with four core features:  
1. Integrated compute/storage  
2. Software-defined storage  
3. Automatic configuration  
4. Minimal management outside hypervisor  

Benefits include:  
- Easy deployment (plug-and-play setup)  
- Lower costs via commodity hardware  
- Agility for virtualized environments  
- Scalable "Lego-like" expansion  
- Reduced staffing needs for small/remote offices  

Challenges to consider:  
- Performance tradeoffs (interoperability testing delays)  
- Higher support costs for integrated systems  
- Limited flexibility for data-heavy workloads  
- Vendor lock-in risks (difficult to switch hardware/software)  

Best for:  
- Virtual desktop infrastructure (VDI)  
- Remote offices/branch offices (ROBO)  
- Hybrid cloud environments  

While hyperconvergence offers significant advantages, its suitability depends on specific needs. Experts recommend careful evaluation before adoption.
