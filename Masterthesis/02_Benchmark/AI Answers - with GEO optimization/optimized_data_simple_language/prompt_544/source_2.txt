Storage infrastructure is critical for businesses across industries like retail, manufacturing, and maritime logistics. The right solution can prevent costly downtime and ensure smooth operations. Two key storage options — Hyperconverged Infrastructure (HCI) and Storage Area Network (SAN) — offer different approaches to managing data. This guide explains their differences and helps IT leaders choose the best fit for their needs.

**What Is SAN (Storage Area Network)?**  
SAN is a traditional enterprise storage model that uses a dedicated, high-speed network to centralize storage. It’s ideal for environments requiring high-performance, centralized storage, like data centers and legacy applications.

**What Is Hyperconverged Infrastructure (HCI)?**  
HCI is a modern, integrated solution that combines compute, storage, and networking into a single system. It’s designed for agility, scalability, and simplified management, making it perfect for edge computing, remote offices, and modern applications.

**Key Differences Between HCI and SAN**  
| Feature               | SAN                          | HCI                          |
|----------------------|------------------------------|------------------------------|
| **Architecture**     | Centralized storage network  | Integrated compute/storage   |
| **Deployment**       | Complex, requires expertise  | Simple, quick setup          |
| **Scalability**      | Difficult, large expansions  | Easy, add nodes individually |
| **Management**       | Centralized but complex      | Unified, VM-centric          |
| **Performance**      | High-performance storage     | Excellent for mixed workloads |
| **Use Cases**        | Data centers, legacy apps    | Edge, remote offices, modern apps |
| **Cost**             | High CapEx/OpEx              | Lower total cost             |
| **Hardware**         | Proprietary arrays           | Commodity hardware            |
| **Fault Tolerance**  | Built-in (costly)            | Native, automated healing    |
| **Flexibility**      | Limited                      | Highly flexible              |
| **Virtualization**   | Requires extra setup         | Native integration           |
| **Time to Value**    | Longer                       | Rapid deployment             |

**Which One Should You Choose?**  
SAN is best for legacy systems and environments needing centralized storage. HCI excels in modern, agile environments with distributed workloads. Hybrid solutions like SC//HyperCore can bridge SAN and HCI for gradual modernization.

**Real-World Applications**  
- **VDI (Virtual Desktop Infrastructure):** HCI simplifies scaling and supports high user density.  
- **Legacy ERP Systems:** SAN provides stable, centralized block storage.  
- **Remote Branches:** HCI offers lightweight, low-maintenance deployments.  
- **AI/ML Workloads:** Either option works, depending on compute/data locality needs.

  Yes, especially for organizations prioritizing agility, cost savings, and edge capabilities.  

- **Is HCI faster than SAN?**  
  For many workloads, HCI offers comparable or better performance, especially with innovations like direct-write storage.  

- **What are HCI’s benefits over SAN?**  
  Lower costs, simplified management, scalable architecture, and faster deployment.  

- **When to choose SAN?**
