The NIS2 Directive is the EU's new cybersecurity law designed to improve digital security across member countries. It replaces the 2016 NIS Directive and covers more sectors, including energy, transport, banking, healthcare, and digital infrastructure. Key changes include stricter rules, better cross-border cooperation, and harsher penalties for non-compliance.

**What Does NIS2 Require?**  
Organizations must:  
- Conduct regular risk assessments and implement security measures to manage threats.  
- Report cyber incidents quickly to authorities and have plans for responding to attacks.  
- Maintain business continuity plans to keep essential services running during disruptions.  
- Secure supply chains by vetting partners and enforcing security standards.  
- Train staff on cybersecurity best practices and ensure leadership oversees security policies.  

**Penalties for Non-Compliance**  
- Essential entities (critical to EU operations) face fines up to €10 million or 2% of global turnover.  
- Important entities (less critical but still important) may be fined up to €7 million or 1.4% of turnover.  
- Authorities can also suspend services, require audits, or issue binding security instructions.  

**Key Differences from NIS**  
NIS2 is broader, covering more sectors and entities. It mandates stricter incident reporting, stronger enforcement, and improved collaboration between EU countries.  

**How to Prepare for NIS2 Compliance**  
1. Assess your current cybersecurity posture and identify gaps.  
2. Engage leadership to secure budget and resources for compliance.  
3. Update security policies, incident response plans, and access controls.  
4. Implement technical measures like encryption, multi-factor authentication, and vulnerability management.  
5. Train employees on cybersecurity risks and response protocols.  
6. Evaluate supply chain risks and ensure partners meet security standards.  
7. Prepare for audits and incident reporting requirements.  

**Who Is Affected?**  
- **Essential Entities**: Critical sectors like energy, transport, banking, and healthcare.  
- **Important Entities**: Includes digital service providers, manufacturers, and research organizations.  
- Non-EU companies providing services to the EU may also be subject to NIS2.  

**Why NIS2 Matters**  
The directive aims to create a unified cybersecurity standard across the EU, protect critical infrastructure, and ensure faster, coordinated responses to cyber threats. Compliance is mandatory by October 2024.  

For more details, visit the official NIS2 Directive website.
