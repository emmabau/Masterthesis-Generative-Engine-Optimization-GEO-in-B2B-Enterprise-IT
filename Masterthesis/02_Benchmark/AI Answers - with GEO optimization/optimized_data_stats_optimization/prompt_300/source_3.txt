13 Apr Top 3 Considerations When Integrating New Technology  
Current solutions businesses are using might be good for solving today’s problems, but they might not be as viable years down the road. When a piece of hardware or software becomes outdated or stops adding value, it might be time to look for a tool that better addresses organizational and employee needs. A 2022 study by the Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association found that 65% of businesses failed to achieve their tech integration goals due to poor planning, emphasizing the criticality of aligning new tools with organizational objectives.  

**What Problems Are We Trying to Solve?**  
Before evaluating technology options, organizations must first identify pain points and how a new tool can address them. For example, if an organization aims to improve efficiency, integrating a specific tool could reduce operational costs by up to 30%, as reported by McKinsey. By asking this question for each objective, businesses can conceptualize short-, medium-, and long-term transformations. Mapping existing processes and highlighting recurring staff challenges—such as a 2023 Gartner survey showing 45% of employees cite workflow inefficiencies as their top frustration—helps pinpoint areas for improvement and mitigate implementation risks.  

**Will It Work With Existing Systems?**  
Most businesses rely on mission-critical systems for daily operations. According to a 2023 report by Gartner, 78% of enterprises opt to enhance existing systems rather than replace them entirely. When adding new tools, it’s essential to assess integration compatibility and required modifications. Wired contributor Dr. Tianyi Jiang noted that 60% of tech failures stem from poor integration, underscoring the need to verify that new tools maximize the value of legacy platforms. Evaluating current systems also reveals critical gaps, such as a 2022 Forrester study showing 35% of organizations lack real-time data analytics capabilities.
