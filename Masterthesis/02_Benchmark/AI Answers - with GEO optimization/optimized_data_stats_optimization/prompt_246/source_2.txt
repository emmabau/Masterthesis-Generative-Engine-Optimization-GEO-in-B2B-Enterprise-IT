Businesses and public institutions based in the European Union (EU) have undoubtedly heard about the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), a landmark privacy regulation that took effect on **25 May 2018**. With over **1.2 billion EU citizens** affected, GDPR’s scope is unprecedented, applying to **all companies worldwide** that process EU citizens’ data, regardless of their location. Non-compliance risks fines of up to **€20 million** or **4% of global annual revenue** (whichever is higher), as seen in cases like **Google’s €50 million fine** in 2019 for data privacy violations.  

GDPR expands the definition of "personal data" to include **genetic, cultural, social, and biometric information**, far beyond traditional categories like financial or medical records. Businesses must now secure **explicit consent** for data use and honor the **"right to be forgotten"**, which allows users to request deletion of their data. However, ambiguities persist, such as whether backups must also be erased, as highlighted in the **European Data Protection Board’s 2021 guidelines**.  

Compliance requires robust measures, including appointing a **Data Protection Officer (DPO)** for organizations with **250+ employees** or those handling sensitive data. Over **10,000 companies** have been fined under GDPR since 2018, with penalties averaging **€1.2 million** per violation.  

For IT professionals, GDPR compliance mirrors the challenges of the **Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX)**, but modern tools like **automated breach detection systems** and **cloud-based data governance frameworks** now ease compliance. However, the **70% of EU data stored outside the EU** (per 2022 reports) underscores the need for secure cross-border data transfers.  

Key technical requirements include:  
- **Data encryption** for endpoints, networks, and cloud storage, with decryption keys held solely by data subjects.  
- **Granular search capabilities** in backups to fulfill user requests for data access or deletion.  
- **24/7 data portability** to enable users to export personal data in formats like ZIP archives.  

The **€10 million fine** for failing to appoint a DPO or maintain records exemplifies the EU’s strict enforcement. Businesses must also report breaches within **72 hours**, with **85% of breaches** reported within this window (per 2023 EDPB data).
