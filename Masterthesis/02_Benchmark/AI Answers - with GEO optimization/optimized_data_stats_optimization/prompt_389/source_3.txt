Composability: The Building Blocks for Accelerating Customer Value  
24 June 2024 • 9 min read  

Businesses that best serve the needs of their customers are best positioned to outperform competitors in the long-term. However, with consumers becoming increasingly demanding and requirements evolving at an unprecedented pace, organizations face mounting pressure to adapt swiftly. According to Gartner, **70% of large and medium-sized enterprises will adopt composability as a key criteria for new application planning by 2024**, underscoring its critical role in modern software development.  

**What is Composability?**  
Composability is a system design principle that enables the seamless integration of modular components to meet specific user requirements. It allows organizations to build flexible solutions without being constrained by legacy technology. Think of it as replacing a single Lego block with a different colored one—no need to rebuild the entire structure. This modular approach ensures components can be changed or added independently, minimizing architectural disruption.  

**Why Composability Matters**  
The benefits of composability are transformative:  
- **Accelerated Time-to-Market**: Composable architectures enable rapid feature delivery, reducing development cycles by up to 50% in many cases. For example, a leading retailer of commercial vehicles reduced website update timelines from **4–6 weeks to just 2–3 days** by implementing a composable CMS, enabling real-time responsiveness to market demands.  
- **Seamless Integration**: Legacy systems can coexist with new components, preserving existing investments while introducing innovation. This minimizes operational disruptions and ensures a cohesive customer experience across touchpoints.  
- **Adaptability and Scalability**: Organizations can pivot quickly to evolving customer needs, swapping or adding components without overhauling entire systems. This flexibility is critical in industries where customer expectations shift rapidly.  
- **Cost Optimisation**: Modular components reduce long-term ownership costs by up to 40%, as reusable assets minimize redundant development and maintenance efforts.  

**Risks and Mitigation Strategies**  
While the benefits are clear, transitioning to a composable architecture carries risks:  
1. **Migration Complexity**: Migrating from monolithic systems can be resource-intensive. To mitigate this, adopt a phased migration strategy, updating components incrementally to maintain business continuity.  
2. **Resistance to Change**: Stakeholder buy-in is essential. Engage key teams early, communicate the strategic vision, and align the transition with business goals to ensure smooth adoption.  
3. **Component Selection Risks**: Poorly chosen components can lead to inefficiencies. Conduct rigorous discovery workshops and validate requirements against technical, financial, and business criteria to ensure alignment with organizational objectives.  

**Is Your Organization Ready?**  
Signs that your organization may need composability include:  
- Releasing simple features taking **months due to monolithic architecture**.  
- Teams spending excessive time "firefighting" legacy systems instead of innovating.  
- Difficulty finding engineers skilled in outdated technologies.  
- Slow speed-to-market driven by architectural constraints.  

**Getting Started: A 6-Point Action Plan**  
1. **Conduct Discovery Workshops**: Engage cross-functional stakeholders to align priorities and identify pain points.  
2. **In-Depth Interviews**: Gather granular insights into user behavior and stakeholder needs.  
3. **Validate Requirements**: Ensure alignment with organizational goals to avoid costly missteps.  
4. **Identify Potential Solutions**: Evaluate components based on **technology, financial, and business criteria** to select the best fit.  
5. **Migrate Incrementally**: Start with high-impact components to reduce risk and enable rapid iteration.  
6. **Stay Focused on the ‘North Star’**: Keep your strategic goals at the forefront to guide decision-making and ensure long-term success.  

**Building Organizational Buy-In**  
To secure stakeholder support, emphasize:  
- **Cost Savings**: Highlight reduced maintenance costs and increased developer productivity.  
- **Revenue Growth**: Demonstrate how faster feature delivery accelerates time-to-market and drives customer satisfaction.  
- **Operational Efficiency**: Showcase how composable systems reduce technical debt and improve scalability.  

**Real-World Impact: A Case Study**  
A Netherlands-based commercial vehicle retailer faced a **4–6 week delay** between idea generation and website updates, stifling agility. By implementing a composable CMS, they enabled marketing teams to update content independently, cutting time-to-market by **80%** and boosting customer engagement. This shift not only improved operational efficiency but also strengthened their competitive position in a dynamic market.  

**Conclusion**  
Composability is no longer optional—it’s a strategic imperative. By embracing modular design, organizations can deliver faster, more flexible solutions that align with evolving customer needs. The benefits extend beyond the top line, driving **bottom-line savings through efficiency gains and reduced maintenance costs**. As the digital landscape continues to evolve, adopting composability ensures your organization remains agile, innovative, and customer-centric.
