Hybrid work offers new perks for individual workers, but what about team productivity? A Gallup study of 2,877 U.S. hybrid workers reveals critical insights: **48% of hybrid workers** are on teams without a formal plan for effective collaboration, directly impacting productivity. Organizations can boost hybrid team performance through four evidence-based strategies, backed by data-driven findings.

1. **Plan for Success**  
   Hybrid teams without a structured plan face significant challenges:  
   - Employees lacking a collaboration plan are **2.2 times less likely** to report their hybrid policy has an extremely positive impact on team collaboration.  
   - Teams with a plan see **66% higher engagement** and **29% lower burnout rates**.  
   - Individual contributors (51%) are **more likely than managers (46%)** to lack a plan, highlighting gaps in leadership training.  
   A hybrid team charter—defining mission, goals, and workflows—should be a cornerstone of organizational policy.  

2. **Coordinate Hybrid Collaboration**  
   Best practices for hybrid teams include:  
   - **67% of hybrid workers** benefit from regular check-in meetings, which improve coordination.  
   - Guidelines for availability (62%) and unavailability (50%) have the **greatest impact** on collaboration.  
   - Only **29%** of teams prioritize on-site activities for collaboration, a key opportunity for improvement.  
   Teams that set clear on-site priorities report **1.5x higher engagement** compared to those without such guidelines.  

3. **Reflect and Adapt**  
   Ongoing reflection is critical:  
   - **84% better team collaboration** and **50% higher engagement** result from regular discussions (weekly/monthly) about hybrid teamwork.  
   - Teams without a plan face stark disparities: **75% of members** discuss hybrid strategies once a year or less, leading to fragmented collaboration.  
   As one worker noted, “Weekly in-person meetings build rapport and collaboration opportunities.”  

4. **Upskill**  
   Training gaps hinder hybrid success:  
   - Only **21% of hybrid workers** receive training for effective hybrid collaboration.  
   - Managers fare slightly better (28%), but **4 times higher engagement** is linked to effective leadership.  
   Gallup emphasizes that hybrid teams require **collective upskilling**, starting with manager training.
