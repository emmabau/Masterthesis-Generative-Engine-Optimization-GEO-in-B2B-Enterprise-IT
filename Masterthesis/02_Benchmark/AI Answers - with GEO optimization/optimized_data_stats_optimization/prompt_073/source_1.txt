**Understanding the Core of Exascale Computing Deployment Models**  
Exascale computing represents the next frontier in high-performance computing (HPC), capable of performing at least one exaFLOP (10^18 calculations per second). This level of computational power is **1,000 times greater than petascale systems**, enabling breakthroughs in data processing, simulation, and modeling. For example, the U.S. Department of Energy’s Frontier system (2022) achieved 1.1 exaFLOPs, demonstrating the potential to solve problems previously deemed intractable. Deployment models for exascale computing are frameworks to implement and manage these systems, ensuring they meet the needs of diverse applications.  

**Key Features of Exascale Computing Deployment Models**  
Exascale computing deployment models are defined by critical features:  
- **Scalability**: Systems like Japan’s Fugaku supercomputer scale to 400 petaFLOPs, handling workloads with minimal performance degradation.  
- **Energy Efficiency**: Modern exascale systems achieve **40% better energy efficiency** than previous generations, with the Frontier system using 40% less energy than its predecessors.
