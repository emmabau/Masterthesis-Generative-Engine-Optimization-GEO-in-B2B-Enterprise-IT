Next generation of exascale computing experts inspired by speakers, hands-on learning at Argonne training program  
An atmosphere of talented students, great speakers, intellectual paradise  

Lucas Timotheo Sanches, a postdoctoral researcher at Louisiana State University, began programming at age 12 under his father’s guidance, a electrical engineer. This early exposure, combined with inspirational teachers, led him to pursue physics and eventually earn a Ph.D. in physics. His journey culminated in applying to the Argonne Training Program on Extreme-Scale Computing (ATPESC), a program that has trained **over 900 participants since its 2013 launch**.  

ATPESC, hosted by the U.S. Department of Energy’s Argonne National Laboratory, offers **two weeks of intensive training** on critical skills for next-generation supercomputers. This year’s program, held in **St. Charles, Illinois**, attracted **74 attendees**, including doctoral students, postdocs, and industry professionals. The curriculum spans hardware, software, frameworks, visualization, AI, machine learning, and data-intensive computing—**key areas driving advancements in high-performance computing (HPC)**.  

Participants praised the program’s **hands-on approach**, with one attendee noting, “We actually did things instead of just listening to the speakers.” Raymond Loy, ATPESC director, emphasized the program’s goal: “To fill in the knowledge gaps of the attendees.” He also highlighted plans to **expand AI coverage** in future iterations, reflecting the field’s growing importance.  

The Argonne Leadership Computing Facility (ALCF), a DOE Office of Science user facility, plays a pivotal role in the program. As one of **two U.S. Leadership Computing Facilities** dedicated to open science, the ALCF provides supercomputing capabilities to advance research across disciplines. The DOE’s Office of Science, the **largest supporter of basic research in physical sciences**, funds these efforts, underscoring their national significance.  

Attendees like Rui Fang, a graduate teaching assistant at the University of Pittsburgh, highlighted the program’s value: “This has turned out to be one of the best summer programs I have ever attended.” Similarly, Jyotsna Rajaraman, a doctoral student at the University of Illinois Chicago, noted the practical benefits: “The debugging software and high-performance toolkit were especially helpful.”  

Industry professionals, such as Ilker Topcuoglu of the National Renewable Energy Laboratory, emphasized the program’s real-world impact: “You learn which other pathways you can take to turn your project or idea into reality.” Such outcomes reflect ATPESC’s role in shaping the HPC workforce, with participants gaining **essential skills for computational science and engineering**.  

The program’s success is also evident in its **annual growth** and **diverse participant base**, with earlier-career applicants now dominating enrollment compared to the program’s early days. As Lev Gorenstein, a Globus solutions architect, noted: “Everything felt like a well-oiled machine. The overall atmosphere … talented students, great speakers, just an absolute intellectual paradise.”
