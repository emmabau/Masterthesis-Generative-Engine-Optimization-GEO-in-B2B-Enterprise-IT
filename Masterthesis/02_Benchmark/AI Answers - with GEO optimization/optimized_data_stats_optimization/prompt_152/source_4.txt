Exascale computing is revolutionizing industries with its unparalleled processing power, enabling breakthroughs in energy, healthcare, and aerospace. These systems, capable of performing **1 exaflop (10^18 operations per second)**—**1,000 times faster than petascale systems** and **1 million times faster than DOE’s first terascale computer (1996)**—are transforming scientific research and technological innovation. For instance, the **Frontier supercomputer**, located at Oak Ridge National Laboratory, is the world’s fastest system, delivering **1.1 exaflops** and enabling simulations that were previously deemed "impossibly out of reach."  

In aerospace, GE Aerospace leverages exascale computing to address critical challenges. Their **RISE (Revolutionary Innovation for Sustainable Engines)** program, a joint venture with Safran via CFM International, aims to reduce fuel consumption and CO₂ emissions by **20%** compared to today’s most efficient engines. Using Frontier, researchers simulate **full-flight-scale conditions** with high-fidelity computational fluid dynamics (CFD) models, revealing previously unresolvable details of jet engine performance. For example, **650,000 people** are airborne at any given time on commercial aircraft powered by GE technology, underscoring the urgency of improving fuel efficiency. Last year, over **1 billion people** traveled by air, making sustainable aviation a global imperative.  

Exascale’s impact extends beyond emissions. It accelerates the development of **hydrogen fuel storage**, **sustainable aviation fuels**, and **hybrid-electric technologies**. GE’s simulations now enable **years of design optimization** before physical prototypes are built, cutting development costs and time-to-market. As Dave Kepczynski, GE Research’s CIO, notes: “Exascale is a massive accelerator for technology, productivity, engineering, and science.”  

The U.S. Department of Energy’s Exascale Computing Project (ECP) has been pivotal, fostering collaborations that have advanced **next-gen computing architectures** and **software-hardware integration**. With exascale, industries gain the tools to tackle complex problems—from **microscopic turbulence analysis** to **macroscopic system performance**—driving innovation in energy, healthcare, and aerospace. As the world’s fastest supercomputer, Frontier, continues to push boundaries, exascale computing is not just a tool but a catalyst for a sustainable, technologically advanced future.
