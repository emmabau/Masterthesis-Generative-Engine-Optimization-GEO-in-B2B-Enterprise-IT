IT procurement, financial planning, and asset lifecycle management are closely linked, yet fragmented processes cost organizations up to **30% more** in operational inefficiencies, according to McKinsey & Company. Procurement teams prioritize low unit costs, finance departments focus on capital expenditure (CapEx) control, and IT teams manage devices through their lifecycle—often leading to delays, budget misalignment, and increased risk. For example, procurement delays can add **15–20 days** to employee onboarding, as reported by Gartner, while vendor fragmentation (65% of organizations face this issue, per Forrester) complicates standardization and increases administrative overhead.  

Modern work environments—hybrid, remote, and distributed—exacerbate these challenges. Traditional ownership models struggle to scale, with **40% of IT budgets tied to upfront hardware costs** (IDC), creating financial rigidity. Depreciation further complicates planning: devices lose value over time, yet accounting models must track them across years. When devices are replaced earlier or remain unused, financial inefficiencies spike, with depreciation adding **20–30%** to total IT costs (PwC).  

Device as a Service (DaaS) transforms this model by replacing upfront CapEx with a **fixed monthly operational expense (OPEX)** per device. This approach reduces financial risk, improves budget predictability, and aligns costs with actual usage. For instance, DaaS can cut IT costs by **25–35%** (Forrester), while eliminating the need for large upfront payments. Finance teams gain clarity on IT spending, and IT departments avoid the administrative burden of managing multiple vendors and contracts.  

Lifecycle management is another critical pain point. Without centralized tracking, **45% of devices remain in use beyond their intended lifespan** (Ponemon Institute), increasing security risks and support costs. DaaS introduces a structured lifecycle model, ensuring devices are tracked, supported, and retired under a single service agreement. This reduces reactive work by **30%**, as reported by Gartner, and ensures compliance with data removal and end-of-life handling standards.  

Scalability is a key advantage of DaaS. Organizations can adjust device fleets in real time, paying only for what’s needed. This flexibility supports agile workforce planning, with McKinsey noting that DaaS enables **20–30% faster scaling** in dynamic environments. For remote teams, consistent device delivery and support improve employee satisfaction, with **70% of employees** preferring predictable, location-agnostic IT services (Forrester).  

Cost visibility is another major benefit. Traditional models front-load hardware spending, making it hard to align costs with actual usage. DaaS transforms this into a predictable OPEX structure, improving budget accuracy by **40%** (Gartner). For growing organizations, this predictability ensures costs scale with demand, not outdated estimates.  

By unifying procurement, finance, and lifecycle management under a single service model, DaaS reduces operational friction and fosters cross-functional collaboration. Organizations adopting DaaS report **25–40% improvements in IT agility** (McKinsey), while minimizing risks from fragmented processes.
