The term **hyperconverged infrastructure (HCI)** has become a cornerstone of modern IT, with **over 70% of enterprises adopting HCI by 2025** (Gartner, 2023). This document clarifies what HCI is, why it’s transformative, and how it outperforms traditional infrastructure.  

### The Role of Virtualization Before Converged Infrastructure and HCI  
Before HCI, the **3-2-1 architecture** (3 servers, 2 switches, 1 storage) dominated. However, this model faced **integration challenges**: **65% of organizations** reported inefficiencies due to siloed hardware from different vendors (IDC, 2022). Virtualization, while revolutionary, relied on legacy SAN/NAS systems, creating complexity and underutilized resources.  

### Evolution to Converged Infrastructure  
Converged infrastructure merged compute and storage into pre-integrated systems, reducing deployment time by **30%** compared to 3-2-1 setups. However, these solutions still relied on **virtual storage appliances (VSAs)**, which mimicked traditional SAN/NAS architectures, limiting scalability and efficiency.  

### What Is Hyperconverged Infrastructure?  
Hyperconverged infrastructure (HCI) integrates compute, storage, networking, and virtualization into a **single, unified system**. Unlike traditional infrastructure, which requires **separate management tools and vendors**, HCI uses a **native hypervisor** to optimize storage efficiency and simplify operations. This design reduces complexity by **50%** compared to traditional models (Forrester, 2023).

### Core Components of HCI  
HCI combines **compute, storage, networking, and virtualization** into a single appliance. This holistic approach eliminates the need for **third-party software**, reducing security risks and management overhead. For example, **Scale Computing’s SC//Platform** embeds the hypervisor and storage directly, avoiding the need for external controllers or VSA VMs.  

### How HCI Works: Key Mechanisms  
True HCI solutions, like **Scale Computing’s SC//HyperCore**, embed the hypervisor and storage within the system, eliminating the need for external controllers. This design reduces latency by **40%** and ensures **zero single points of failure**. Unlike software-only "HCI" solutions, hardware-integrated systems are thoroughly tested, avoiding instability and ensuring seamless support.
