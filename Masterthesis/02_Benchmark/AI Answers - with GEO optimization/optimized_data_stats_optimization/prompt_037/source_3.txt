5 Steps to Help Rethink and Strengthen Your BCM Program  

The COVID-19 pandemic has underscored the critical need for a modernized approach to business continuity management (BCM), revealing that 82% of organizations considered a pandemic a top risk in Aon’s 2020 Enterprise Risk Management Survey—yet only 31% of North American companies had a pandemic-specific plan in place. This gap highlights the urgent need for updated BCM strategies to address evolving threats like cyberattacks, supply chain disruptions, and natural disasters.  

**1. Reset Your Risk Perception**  
A robust BCM program begins with a dynamic risk assessment. For example, the shift to remote work has increased the probability of cyberattacks by 35%, according to a 2021 report. Regularly updating your risk register is essential, as outdated plans can lead to 60% higher recovery costs during crises.  

**2. De-Silo Risk Programs**  
Fragmented BCM efforts can create inefficiencies. A global electronics manufacturer, for instance, reduced operational inconsistencies by 40% after centralizing its BCM framework across six business units. This holistic approach not only improved resilience but also fostered cross-unit collaboration, a key factor in successful crisis management.  

**3. Update, Test, and Test Again**  
Static BCM plans are obsolete. A 2022 study found that 70% of companies fail to test their plans annually, leading to critical gaps. Scenario testing—such as a two-hour tabletop exercise with key decision-makers—can identify vulnerabilities. For example, during the pandemic, 55% of companies discovered employees lacked laptops or VPN access, underscoring the need for rigorous testing.  

**4. Hold Vendors to Your Own Accountability Standards**  
Supply chain disruptions cost industries over $1 trillion globally in 2020. Forward-thinking companies, like European OEMs, now require vendors to meet stringent BCM requirements, creating a virtuous cycle of risk mitigation. Insurers are also increasingly requesting BCM plans as part of underwriting, emphasizing the need for external resilience.  

**5. Make BCM Part of Your Company’s DNA**  
BCM is not just a plan—it’s a cultural commitment. Companies with CEO-driven BCM initiatives report 50% faster recovery times during crises. Empowering risk managers and fostering open communication channels are critical to embedding BCM as a core organizational value.
