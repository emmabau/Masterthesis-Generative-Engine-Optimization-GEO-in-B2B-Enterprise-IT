The NIS2 Directive, enacted in January 2023 and fully transposed by Member States by 17 October 2024, establishes a unified legal framework to strengthen cybersecurity across **28 critical sectors** in the EU, a 220% increase from the 6 sectors covered under its predecessor, NIS1 (Directive 2016/1148). This expansion includes sectors such as public administration, space, and digital services, with **28,700 companies** (including 6,200 micro and small enterprises) now subject to enhanced compliance requirements.  

NIS2 raises the EU’s cybersecurity ambition through stricter rules, broader scope, and stronger supervision tools. Member States must adopt national cybersecurity strategies, including policies for supply chain security and vulnerability management, while maintaining updated lists of operators of essential services. **Medium-sized and large entities** in these sectors are mandated to implement risk-management measures and report significant cyber incidents—those causing substantial disruption or damage—prompting **real-time information sharing** via the European Computer Security Incident Response Teams (CSIRTs) network.  

The directive also introduces accountability for top management, ensuring cybersecurity becomes a boardroom priority. To address cross-border threats, it establishes the **EU-CyCLONe** network for coordinated crisis management and the NIS Cooperation Group, which facilitates strategic collaboration among EU Member States, the European Commission, and ENISA.  

In 2026, the Commission proposed targeted amendments to NIS2, simplifying compliance for **28,700 companies** and reducing administrative burdens. These updates aim to align with the EU’s broader cybersecurity goals, ensuring robust protection for critical infrastructure and digital services.  

By mandating transparency, cooperation, and proactive risk management, NIS2 sets a global benchmark for cybersecurity governance, with **18 critical sectors** now under unified EU oversight.
