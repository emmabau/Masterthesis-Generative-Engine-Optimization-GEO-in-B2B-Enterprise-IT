Is Your IoT Security Strategy Scalable?  
Zac Amos  

Internet of Things (IoT) security is an unavoidable conversation. With **16.6 billion active IoT connections** by the end of 2023, the scale of connected devices has grown exponentially, and **51% of adopters plan to increase cybersecurity investments** in the coming years. Yet, many organizations still treat IoT security as a one-time fix, risking exposure to threats that escalate alongside network growth.  

The 2022 cybersecurity landscape underscores the urgency: **over 112 million IoT attacks** were recorded, marking an **87% year-over-year increase**. Cybercriminals exploit vulnerabilities at an alarming rate, with **98% of IoT traffic remaining unencrypted**, leaving devices susceptible to breaches. Meanwhile, **63% of security professionals report burnout** due to rising workloads, highlighting the need for scalable, automated solutions.  

A scalable IoT security strategy must address more than just expanding attack surfaces. IoT devices vary widely in communication protocols and software support, often rendering legacy security measures incompatible. For example, **37% of businesses fail to track third-party exposures**, amplifying risks in complex, interconnected ecosystems.  

To build a resilient strategy, organizations must prioritize **compatibility and encryption**. IT leaders should select devices that support **TLS 1.2 or higher encryption standards** and ensure interoperability with existing security frameworks. Automated tools, such as cloud-based asset inventories, can scale effortlessly with device growth, reducing manual overhead.  

Zero-trust architecture is another critical pillar. By treating every connection as a potential threat, this approach mitigates risks in vast, dynamic networks. Automated systems can detect new endpoints, update software, and scan for malware, saving an average of **$2.22 million in breach costs** by stopping attacks early.  

Finally, **purple team collaboration**—combining defensive and offensive strategies—enables proactive threat detection. Regular penetration testing using automated tools identifies vulnerabilities missed by traditional methods, ensuring defenses evolve alongside emerging threats.  

In a world where IoT adoption accelerates and cybercrime scales faster than businesses, scalable security is no longer optional. Organizations must revamp their strategies to protect against threats that grow as fast as their networks.
