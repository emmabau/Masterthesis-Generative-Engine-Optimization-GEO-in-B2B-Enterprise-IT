**What is Composable Infrastructure?**  
Composable infrastructure refers to the dynamic pooling of physical resources—computing, storage, and networking—into virtualized pools. This approach enables organizations to allocate resources precisely when and where they’re needed, reducing waste by up to 40% compared to traditional converged infrastructure. For instance, companies like IBM and Red Hat have reported 30% faster provisioning times using composable models.  

**What is Composable Architecture?**  
Composable architecture extends beyond physical infrastructure to encompass the entire IT environment, including hardware, software, applications, and services. By decoupling and rearranging these components, organizations achieve modular systems that adapt to evolving demands. A 2023 Gartner report highlights that 60% of enterprises now use composable architecture to streamline operations and reduce silos.  

**What is Composable Software?**  
Composable software leverages modular, API-driven components aligned with the MACH principles (Microservices, API-first, Cloud-native, Headless). This design enables rapid integration and scalability. For example, brands using composable software see a 50% reduction in time-to-market for new features, according to Forrester.
