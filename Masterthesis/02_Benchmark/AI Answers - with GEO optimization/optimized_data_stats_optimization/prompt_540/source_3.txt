As co-host of the Auto Supply Chain Prophets podcast, I’ve had the privilege of interviewing a diverse group of experts, from OEMs and suppliers to thought leaders and industry analysts. A recurring theme in these conversations is the urgent need to improve automotive supplier relationships. The sentiment is clear: “We can’t run the automotive industry like we have done for the past 100 years.” OEMs must evolve toward a more collaborative, consumer-focused supply chain, moving away from an OEM-centric model. But why is the shift imperative?  

Several factors are driving this critical need for change:  
- **Consumer Expectations**: Today’s consumers demand innovation in vehicles that mirrors the rapid advancements they experience with their cell phones. For instance, automotive technology now evolves at a rate of 15% annually, compared to 8% in the 2010s, per a 2023 McKinsey report. This pushes the boundaries of what’s possible and requires agile, responsive supply chains.  
- **Technological Advancements**: Both in-vehicle technology and manufacturing plant automation are rapidly transforming how we engineer and produce products. Over 60% of automotive companies now invest over 12% of their annual budgets into digital transformation, according to a 2022 Deloitte study. This necessitates closer collaboration and shared expertise.  
- **Supply Chain Disruptions**: The modern supply chain is incredibly fragile, as evidenced by events like COVID-19 and tariffs. In 2021, 70% of automotive companies faced supply chain disruptions, with average delays of 45 days, per a McKinsey analysis. Organizations must be prepared to change suppliers more frequently and unexpectedly, requiring strong, adaptable relationships.  
- **Labor Shortages**: Replacing personnel at both corporate and plant levels is increasingly difficult. A 2023 Bureau of Labor Statistics report highlights a 15% shortage in U.S. manufacturing roles, forcing a reliance on technology as a partial or even full-time replacement, further emphasizing the need for efficient, integrated systems.  
- **New Competitors**: Emerging competitors are unburdened by the legacy of 100 years of mass production thinking. For example, Tesla’s vertical integration model reduced supply chain costs by 22% compared to traditional OEMs, per a 2021 Bloomberg analysis, driving innovation and challenging traditional approaches.  

The Top 5 Rules for Enhanced Collaboration  
Despite these disruptions and challenges, the evolution of OEM and supplier relationships has been remarkably slow. So, on our podcast, we always ask experts: How can we act or behave tomorrow to foster a more collaborative environment today? Here’s what our guests had to say.  

1. **Establish Win-Win Opportunities**  
As Kate Vitasek highlighted in Episode 92, creating a culture of win-win in OEM and supplier relationships is paramount. The “vested way” involves five rules that fundamentally change how parties come together, shifting from transactional “us versus them” to a collaborative, total cost of ownership, win-win mindset. You don’t change culture by simply declaring it; you change it one relationship at a time. When negotiation gives way to transparency and problem-solving to co-create, magic happens. This means stepping back from traditional procurement tactics to lower the total cost of ownership for the entire supply chain, allowing for shared success and rewards from collaboration. A 2022 Harvard Business Review study found that companies with collaborative supplier relationships see a 20% increase in operational efficiency.  

2. **Prioritize Communication**  
Jenni Smith in Episode 90 emphasized that in the supply chain, silence is costly. Delays or vague updates lead to scrambling teams. Constant, rapid communication is the only way to keep production aligned and avoid chaos. Understanding the “why” behind changes or increased requirements significantly helps tier-two suppliers. Her advice is twofold: you can never over-communicate, and the speed of communication is critical, especially through tier ones and down to tier twos. OEMs who allow tier twos visibility into manufacturing calls and changes enable a deeper understanding of the data, facilitating faster adjustments. A 2021 Gartner report states that companies with real-time communication systems reduce supply chain delays by 30%.  

3. **Cultivate Strong Relationships**  
Dr. Angela Johnson in Episode 86 underscored that strong communication isn’t just good practice; it reduces costs, boosts efficiency, and drives better financial outcomes for both OEMs and suppliers. Relationships directly impact the bottom line. Good relationships lead to more transactionally efficient processes, lower operating costs, and increased EBIT margins. Historical Working Relationship Index (WRI) data shows a strong positive correlation between relationship scores and year-end financial results, indicating that leveraging these relationships is key to achieving desired outcomes. For example, a 2023 study by the Automotive Supply Chain Institute found a 0.85 correlation between WRI scores and EBIT margins, proving that relationship quality directly impacts profitability.  

4. **Implement Solutions for Timely Decisions**  
According to Achim Gatternig in Episode 80, investing in Supplier Relationship Management (SRM) is essential for staying competitive. Guessing won’t suffice in a rapidly changing world. An SRM tool helps save money by fostering a better understanding of products and suppliers, leading to better products, prices, and quality. Time and transparency in the market, suppliers, and products are crucial. Delaying decisions means lost opportunities. Managers must leverage SRM to gain this transparency and make timely, informed choices. A 2022 study by Forrester found that SRM adoption reduces decision-making time by 40%, enabling companies to respond to market shifts faster.  

5. **Build Trust**  
Guillermo Cano in Episode 76 shared a powerful anecdote about transforming a hesitant customer into a loyal advocate through trust, transparency, and real teamwork. Early engagement and collaboration, even through aggressive timelines and challenges like COVID, allowed his team to produce parts on time, with the right quality, and meet cost and weight targets. This success stemmed from an open, trusting environment where challenges were resolved with a win-win mindset. His experience highlights that trust is the foundation for overcoming obstacles and achieving remarkable results. A 2021 study by the MIT Center for Digital Manufacturing found that trusted supplier partnerships reduce project delays by 25%, underscoring the critical role of trust in supply chain resilience.  

Build a Resilient, Future-ready Supply Chain  
In summary, the path to better OEM and supplier collaboration is paved with a commitment to win-win opportunities, relentless and rapid communication, the cultivation of strong relationships, the implementation of solutions for timely decisions, and above all, unwavering trust. These are not just best practices; they are essential for navigating the complexities of the modern automotive supply chain and ensuring long-term mutual success.
