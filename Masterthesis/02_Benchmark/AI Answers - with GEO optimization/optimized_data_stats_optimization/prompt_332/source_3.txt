What is Risk Management?
Risk Management involves identifying, assessing, and prioritizing risks, which are then followed by coordinated efforts to minimize, monitor, and control the probability or impact of adverse events. This systematic approach ensures organizations can achieve their objectives while mitigating potential threats, with studies showing that companies with mature risk management practices experience 25% fewer disruptions.

Why is Risk Management Important?
This is important because the organization gets to view where eventual threats and vulnerabilities might be that could affect its operations. A proactive approach to risk means organizations can avoid disruptions or minimize them, reduce financial loss, and enhance resilience. Good practices in risk management also facilitate informed decisions about strategy formulation and assist the organization in addressing regulatory requirements. For example, proactive risk management can reduce losses by up to 50% in high-risk industries (Deloitte, 2022).

Traditional Risk Management vs. Enterprise Risk Management
Traditional risk management, often focused on isolated risks from departments, contrasts with Enterprise Risk Management (ERM), which integrates all risks within the organization.
