Product integrations are critical to helping B2B companies acquire clients, retain them, and expand into new markets—whether by region, industry, or company size. However, a survey of 260 product managers and engineers reveals that B2B organizations are failing to adopt strategies that maximize these benefits.  

**Offering a Small Set of Integrations**  
While most B2B companies recognize the value of integrations, only 32% have built more than 10, with 68% offering fewer than 10. This approach neglects the vast majority of clients’ software ecosystems, as there are **over 1,000 vendors** in core categories like CRM, HRIS, and ticketing. Offering seven to nine integrations—commonly seen as “OK”—fails to address the needs of **80% of potential clients**, leading to frustration and attrition.  

**Stretching Themselves Thin Across Categories**  
Many companies attempt to cover multiple software categories, but this dilutes their ability to meet client needs. For example, a company offering CRM, HRIS, project management, and ticketing integrations with an 8-integration limit can only provide **2 per category on average**, leaving critical gaps.  

**Slow Integration Development**  
A staggering **71% of organizations take three weeks or more** to build a single integration. This pace is unsustainable as demand grows, creating backlogs that strain engineering teams and customer support.  

**Struggling with Performance and Partnerships**  
Integration performance is a top challenge, with **55% of organizations citing it as their primary hurdle**. Common issues include unreliable data sync, slow transfer speeds, and security vulnerabilities. Compounding this, **38% struggle to access API documentation**, and **43% face costly third-party partnership barriers** (with annual fees reaching tens of thousands of dollars).  

**Maintenance Overload**  
Instead of focusing on innovation, 48% of companies spend time diagnosing integration issues and providing resolution steps to customer success teams, while 46% dedicate resources to fixing integrations that break due to third-party API changes.  

**Aggressive 2024 Goals Demand Transformation**  
With half of B2B companies aiming to build **15+ integrations** and nearly 19% targeting **30+**, the industry faces a critical inflection point. Current practices—spanning slow development, poor performance, and fragmented maintenance—must evolve to meet these ambitious goals. Organizations that prioritize scaling integration capabilities will dominate in 2024.
