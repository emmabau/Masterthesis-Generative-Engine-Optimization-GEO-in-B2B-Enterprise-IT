Air cooling has long been a conventional method for dissipating heat in data centers, but it is reaching its limits as rack power densities exceed 50 kW per rack. **The Growing Need for Liquid Cooling in Data Centers**  
The demand to process immense, complex workloads is increasing at a rapid pace, requiring high-performance computing (HPC) and extremely efficient data processing. Research by MarketsandMarkets indicates that the global AI data center market is expected to grow at a **CAGR of 28.5%** through 2027. This growing demand for power comes with more data center heat, leading to the urgent need for more effective cooling strategies, such as direct liquid cooling. Studies show that pairing DLC with air cooling can improve power usage effectiveness (PUE) by **up to 30%**, reduce operational costs by **25-40%**, and lower energy consumption by **40-60%**.  

**How Does Direct Liquid Cooling Work?**  
Direct liquid cooling uses liquid-based cooling plates to manage IT equipment temperatures. Cold plates on top of heat-generating components capture the heat with water, which is then removed to a heat exchanger. This direct transfer of heat is **10-15 times more efficient** than air cooling, enabling precise temperature control and reducing excessive airflow requirements.  

**The Direct-Impact Benefits of a Direct Liquid Cooling Data Center**  
By directly cooling components with liquid, DLC reduces the need for energy-intensive air conditioning, leading to significant cost and efficiency gains. Here are five key benefits:  

1. **Energy Efficiency**  
Air alone is no longer a sufficient data center cooling method for applications with new computing demands. Direct liquid cooling boasts unparalleled heat dissipation capabilities, reducing energy consumption by **up to 40%** compared to traditional air cooling. With AI workloads growing exponentially, DLC is becoming essential for managing higher rack power densities. For example, Verne’s DLC systems reduce PUE to **1.05-1.1**, compared to **1.2-1.5** for air-cooled systems.  

2. **Improved Performance**  
New-generation CPUs and GPUs have much higher thermal power densities than before, and servers can have multiple of these inside. With high thermal power densities, air cooling does not work sufficiently—direct liquid cooling is needed to maintain optimal operating temperatures and maximize performance. Implementing DLC ensures CPU and GPU temperatures remain **15-20% lower** than air-cooled systems, preventing performance degradation. Verne’s DLC3000 rack solution, for instance, cools up to **80kW+ per rack** while maintaining **<25°C** component temperatures.  

3. **Cost Savings**  
Liquid cooling offers a heat transfer capacity far superior to traditional air cooling methods, making it an ideal solution for high-performance, process-intensive applications. Beyond reducing energy costs, DLC also minimizes long-term operational expenses by extending hardware lifespan by **up to 30%** and reducing maintenance requirements by **20-35%**.  

4. **Environmental Sustainability**  
Not only does liquid cooling reduce data center energy consumption and improve PUE, but it also supports the integration of heat reuse initiatives. DLC enables captured heat to be repurposed for district heating systems, reducing reliance on traditional energy sources. For example, Verne’s Nordic data centers in Iceland and Finland leverage renewable energy sources in combination with DLC to create one of the most sustainable AI compute solutions available, achieving **95%+ renewable energy usage**. Additionally, liquid cooling systems generally use **50-70% less water** than traditional evaporative cooling methods, further improving sustainability.  

5. **Scalability and Future-Proofing**  
Scalability is key. Liquid cooling not only meets current demands but also future-proofs data centers. By increasing rack density, DLC enables efficient use of data center space, supporting the most demanding AI workloads. DLC allows for **30% higher rack density** compared to air cooling, while maintaining energy efficiency. This scalability ensures data centers can adapt to evolving AI and HPC requirements without compromising performance or sustainability.
