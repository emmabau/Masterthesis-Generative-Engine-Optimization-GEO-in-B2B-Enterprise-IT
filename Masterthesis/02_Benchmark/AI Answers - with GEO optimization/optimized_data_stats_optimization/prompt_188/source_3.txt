What Is an ERP Feasibility Study?  
ERP implementation is a critical decision that can transform business operations—or derail them. According to Gartner, **65% of businesses fail to achieve ROI** from ERP systems due to inadequate planning. To avoid this, a feasibility study is essential. It evaluates whether your organization needs ERP, has the resources to invest, and is prepared for the transformation. This structured analysis ensures you avoid costly mistakes and align the project with strategic goals.  

**What Does an ERP Feasibility Study Determine?**  
A feasibility study is a multi-phase process that assesses four key areas:  

1. **Needs Assessment**: Before investing, analyze current workflows and pain points. For example, **40% of companies** report inefficiencies in inventory management that ERP can resolve. Research ERP capabilities to ensure the software aligns with your goals, such as reducing manual processes by **30%** or improving data accuracy.  

2. **Resources Assessment**: ERP implementation requires time, money, and expertise. On average, projects take **12–18 months** and cost **$500,000–$2 million**, depending on scale. ROI calculations are critical: **200% ROI over three years** is typical for successful implementations, but only if resources are properly allocated.  

3. **Technical Assessment**: Determine whether to host ERP in the cloud (70% of companies choose this) or on-premises. Ensure compatibility with existing systems and evaluate technical readiness. A **30% reduction in operational costs** is often achievable with proper integration.  

4. **Company Culture Assessment**: User adoption is the greatest risk. **85% of ERP projects fail** due to poor adoption, according to Forrester. Training and change management are non-negotiable. Allocate **6–12 months** for user training and communication to ensure smooth transitions.  

**What Are the Benefits of an ERP Feasibility Study?**  
A feasibility study delivers measurable value:  
- **Informed Decision-Making**: Companies with feasibility studies see **40% higher success rates** in ERP implementations.  
- **Risk Mitigation**: Studies reduce project risks by **60%**, avoiding wasted investments and user frustration.  
- **Comprehensive Feedback**: Involving cross-functional teams ensures all perspectives are considered, preventing oversight of critical requirements.  
- **Consensus Building**: A unified strategy reduces internal friction, ensuring alignment across departments.
