NIS2 Compliance: What It Means, Who's Affected, and How to Comply  
Jan 8, 2026  

The Network and Information Security Directive 2 (NIS2) represents a **114% expansion** in scope compared to its predecessor, NIS1, now covering **15+ sectors** instead of 7. This includes critical areas like energy, transport, finance, healthcare, and digital infrastructure, alongside important sectors such as food production, manufacturing, and postal services. NIS2 mandates **strict penalties**—up to **€10 million or 2% of global turnover** for essential entities and **€7 million or 1.4% of turnover** for important entities—ensuring uniform enforcement across all EU member states.  

**Key Changes and Impacts**  
NIS2 introduces a **risk-based framework** with 10 mandatory cybersecurity measures, including:  
1. **Real-time incident reporting** within **24 hours** for significant breaches.  
2. **Supply chain risk management**, requiring assessments of third-party vendors to mitigate vulnerabilities.  
3. **Governance accountability**, holding executives personally liable for cybersecurity failures.  

**Who Must Comply?**  
- **Essential Entities**: Large organizations with **≥250 employees** or **€50M+ turnover** in sectors like energy, healthcare, and finance.  
- **Important Entities**: Medium-sized firms with **≥50 employees** or **€10M+ turnover** in sectors like food production and digital services.  

**Why NIS2 Matters**  
Modern cyberattacks, such as **ransomware targeting critical infrastructure**, have evolved to exploit supply chains and automate attacks at scale. NIS2 addresses these threats by strengthening cross-border collaboration through the **EU-CyCLONe network** and mandating **mandatory cybersecurity training** for leadership.  

**Challenges and Solutions**  
- **Skills Shortages**: 71% of organizations report a lack of qualified cybersecurity professionals, according to recent studies.  
- **Supply Chain Complexity**: NIS2 requires **vendor risk assessments** and contractual clauses for incident notification, ensuring accountability across multi-tiered supply chains.
