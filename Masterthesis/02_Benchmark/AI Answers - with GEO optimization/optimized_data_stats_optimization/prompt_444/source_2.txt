MIT researchers handling U.S. sensitive personal data or government-related data must comply with the DOJ Final Rule (28 C.F.R. Part 202), effective **April 8, 2025**, with full enforcement beginning **October 6, 2025**—a 5-month grace period for compliance preparation. This rule restricts access to data for collaborators or contractors in **6 designated countries of concern**: China (including Hong Kong and Macau), Russia, Iran, North Korea, Cuba, and Venezuela.  

**Bulk thresholds** for restricted data are strictly defined:  
- **Genomic data**: ≥100 U.S. persons  
- **Epigenomic/proteomic/transcriptomic data**: ≥1,000 U.S. persons  
- **Biometric identifiers**: ≥1,000 U.S. persons  
- **Precise geolocation data**: ≥1,000 U.S. persons  
- **Personal financial/health data**: ≥10,000 U.S. persons  
- **Covered personal identifiers**: ≥100,000 U.S. persons  

These thresholds apply regardless of anonymization, encryption, or pseudonymization.  

**Prohibited transactions** include:  
- Data brokerage with countries of concern or covered persons  
- Access to bulk human "omic data (genomic, epigenomic, proteomic, transcriptomic) or biospecimens from which such data can be derived  

**Restricted transactions** involve vendor agreements, employment agreements, or investment agreements with covered persons.  

**Exempt activities** include:  
- Personal communications (e.g., email, phone calls)  
- Import/export of information  
- U.S. government activities or federal law mandates  
- Medical research under specific regulatory frameworks  

MIT must implement **DOJ-compliant security controls**, including:  
- Updating data use agreements with non-transfer clauses  
- Ensuring cloud storage, collaboration platforms, and code repositories exclude foreign vendors from countries of concern  
- Conducting **annual audits** (every 12 months) to verify compliance  

**Critical compliance steps**:  
1. **Pause all work** with collaborators/contractors from countries of concern or on DOJ’s Covered Persons List.  
2. **Email research-compliance-help@mit.edu** before sharing bulk U.S. health, financial, geolocation, or biometric datasets with foreign persons.  
3. **Document all data handling practices** and ensure COUHES protocols explicitly prohibit sharing regulated datasets with countries of concern.  

Failure to comply risks legal penalties and exposure of sensitive U.S. data. Immediate action is required to align with DOJ’s national security priorities.
