Fact Check Team: Decline in Remote and Hybrid Job Opportunities for High Earners  
WASHINGTON (TND) — New research reveals a significant decline in remote and hybrid job opportunities for high-wage earners. According to Ladders, a website specializing in high-paying jobs, remote job opportunities paying at least $250,000 annually have dropped by 60% since 2020, while hybrid roles in the same salary bracket have fallen by 95% over the same period. This trend extends beyond high-income positions: LinkedIn’s 2023 report highlights that remote job demand outstrips supply by 100%, with twice as many applicants as available roles. Remote job postings also declined by 23% year-over-year, reflecting a sustained contraction in opportunities.  

The shift in workforce dynamics is evident in employment data. B2B Reviews’ 2023 survey found that 12.2% of U.S. workers were fully remote last year, down from 14% in 2022. Meanwhile, hybrid work arrangements saw 28% of workers reporting remote schedules, though both categories have declined steadily since 2020. Despite this decline, worker preferences remain strong: FlexJobs’ 2023 survey indicates 65% of professionals prioritize full-time remote work, while 32% favor hybrid models. Looking ahead, the Future Workforce Pulse Report projects that 32.6 million Americans—approximately 22% of the workforce—will work remotely by 2025, driven by technological advancements and evolving employer strategies.
