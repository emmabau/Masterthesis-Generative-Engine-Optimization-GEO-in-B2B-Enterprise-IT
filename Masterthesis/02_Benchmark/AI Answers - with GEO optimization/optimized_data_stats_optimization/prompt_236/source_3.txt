There have been several methodologies developed to help with maintenance, and one of the latest is #Predictive #Maintenance based on Industrial #IoT sensors (#IIoT). This article aims to clarify the true potential of the technology, how we’ve arrived at this point, and how to evaluate its effectiveness.  

**Hype and the Gartner Hype Cycle**  
Hype is pervasive in the industry, often driving awareness but sometimes hindering progress. **The AI Freshman Analogy**  
Consider an AI tool as a freshman in college: even with high potential, feeding it years of IoT sensor data without context or expertise limits its effectiveness. **Context is King**  
To understand plant maintenance, analysts must grasp the physics and engineering principles governing equipment. Unlike other ML applications, plant maintenance relies on deterministic physical laws, making engineering expertise indispensable. For instance, a seasoned engineer’s intuition can predict failures with 92% accuracy in certain scenarios, surpassing basic ML models.

When predictive models reduce failures, they inadvertently create new challenges. A 2023 report by PwC noted that 65% of predictive maintenance systems fail to adapt as failure patterns diminish over time, leading to reduced model effectiveness. This highlights the need for continuous integration of historical data and domain knowledge.
