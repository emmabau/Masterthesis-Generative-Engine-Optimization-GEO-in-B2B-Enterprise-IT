# 5 Steps to Strengthen Your BCM Program Post-Pandemic  
> The COVID-19 pandemic exposed critical gaps in business continuity management (BCM) programs, emphasizing the need for proactive risk resilience. This guide outlines actionable steps to modernize BCM strategies, ensuring organizations are prepared for future disruptions.  

## Reset Your Risk Perception  
A robust BCM program begins with a dynamic risk assessment. Aon’s 2020 Enterprise Risk Management Survey revealed that **82% of organizations** identified pandemics as top risks, yet **<31%** had formal pandemic plans. This gap highlights the urgency to:  
- **Regularly update risk registers** to reflect evolving threats (e.g., remote work increasing cyber risks).  
- **Integrate emerging risks** like supply chain vulnerabilities and tech dependencies into BCM frameworks.  

## De-Silo Risk Programs  
Fragmented BCM efforts create operational inconsistencies. A case study of an electronic manufacturer shows that unifying six business units into a centralized framework:  
- **Eliminated redundancies** and improved cross-unit collaboration.  
- **Enhanced resilience** by leveraging best practices from all units.  
**Key takeaway**: Centralized BCM fosters agility and ensures alignment across departments.  

## Update, Test, and Test Again  
Static BCM plans are obsolete. Best practices include:  
- **Annual plan refreshes** to reflect changes in suppliers, technology, and personnel.  
- **Scenario testing** to identify gaps (e.g., remote work readiness during the pandemic).  
**Critical questions to ask**:  
- When was your last BCM test?  
- What are the protocols for invoking the plan?  
- What technology/skills are required for execution?  

## Hold Vendors to Accountability Standards  
Supply chain disruptions (e.g., microchip shortages, lumber scarcity) underscore the need for vendor resilience. Forward-thinking companies:  
- **Require vendors to meet BCM standards** (e.g., European OEMs).  
- **Align with insurance underwriting** by demonstrating BCM maturity.  
This creates a "virtuous cycle" of risk mitigation.  

## Embed BCM into Your Company’s DNA  
BCM is not a one-time task but a **continuous improvement cycle**. To institutionalize it:  
- **Secure CEO commitment** and empower risk managers to drive holistic adoption.  
- **Establish communication channels** for risk prevention and response.  
- **Prioritize cultural shifts** to embed resilience as a core organizational value.
