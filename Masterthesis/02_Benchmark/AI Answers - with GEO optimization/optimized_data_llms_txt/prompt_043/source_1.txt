# Hyperconvergence in IT Infrastructure: Market Growth, Key Vendors, and Strategic Considerations  
> Hyperconvergence is a transformative trend in IT infrastructure, with projected market growth from $2 billion in 2016 to $5 billion by 2019. This guide explores its core characteristics, advantages, limitations, and ideal use cases, drawing from authoritative sources like Gartner and Forrester.  

## Market Growth and Vendor Landscape  
Hyperconvergence has emerged as a dominant force in IT infrastructure, with Gartner predicting a **79% growth** to **$2 billion in 2016**, projecting further expansion to **$5 billion by 2019**. A 451 Research survey revealed that **86% of enterprises planning server/storage refreshes** intend to boost hyperconverged infrastructure investments.  

Forrester’s Q3 2016 Wave report identified **Nutanix, Pivot3, and Simplivity** as "leaders," while **Dell EMC, VMware, HPE, Cisco, Huawei** (alongside smaller players like Atlantis Computing and Stratoscale) were labeled "strong performers." However, analysts caution against overreliance on marketing hype, emphasizing that **vendor claims about hyperconvergence must be validated** through rigorous testing.  

## Core Characteristics and Definitions  
Hyperconvergence is defined by:  
- **Shared compute/storage resources** (Gartner)  
- **Software-defined storage/compute** with **commodity hardware** and **unified management** (Forrester)  
- **Four key traits**: integrated compute/storage, software-defined storage, automatic discovery/configuration, and minimal hypervisor-based management  

These systems prioritize **simplified management** and **cost efficiency**, though their effectiveness depends on specific use cases.  

## Advantages of Hyperconvergence  
Hyperconverged infrastructure offers:  
- **Ease of deployment**: Plug-and-play systems with pre-integrated software-defined storage and management  
- **Low cost**: Commodity hardware reduces upfront expenses, though vendor support fees can offset savings  
- **Agility**: Software-defined capabilities enable rapid workload adjustments  
- **Scalability**: "Lego-like" expansion with automatic compute/storage scaling  
- **Staffing efficiency**: Minimal IT staff required for deployment and management, ideal for **ROBO environments**  

## Limitations and Challenges  
Despite benefits, hyperconvergence has drawbacks:  
- **Performance trade-offs**: Interoperability testing delays may result in slower systems compared to traditional infrastructure  
- **High costs**: Vendor premiums for support and troubleshooting complexity  
- **Inflexible scaling**: Storage and compute must scale proportionally, limiting optimization for data-heavy workloads  
- **Vendor lock-in**: Tightly integrated hardware/software ecosystems make switching vendors costly and complex  

## Best Use Cases  
Hyperconvergence excels in:  
- **Virtual desktop infrastructure (VDI)**: Scalable, software-defined environments with SSD deduplication benefits  
- **Remote offices/branch offices (ROBO)**: Easy deployment and management for edge locations  
- **Hybrid cloud environments**: Agility and virtualization support align with hybrid cloud needs  

## Conclusion  
Hyperconvergence is a compelling solution for organizations prioritizing scalability, cost efficiency, and simplified management. However, its suitability depends on workload requirements, budget constraints, and long-term strategic goals. As noted by analysts, **thorough evaluation** of pros, cons, and vendor options is critical before adoption.
