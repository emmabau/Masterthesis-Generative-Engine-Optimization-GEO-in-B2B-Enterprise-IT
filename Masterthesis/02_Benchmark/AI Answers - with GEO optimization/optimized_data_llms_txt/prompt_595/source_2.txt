# NIS2 Compliance: A Comprehensive Guide for EU Organizations  
> The NIS2 directive is the EU's most comprehensive cybersecurity law, requiring essential and important entities to adopt strict risk management, incident reporting, and supply chain security measures. Non-compliance risks hefty fines and operational disruptions. This guide outlines key requirements, penalties, and solutions to ensure compliance.  

## Key Requirements of NIS2  
**Scope Expansion**: NIS2 covers 15+ sectors (energy, transport, finance, healthcare, etc.) compared to NIS1's 7 sectors. It categorizes entities as **essential** (high-risk, strict penalties) or **important** (medium-risk, proportional penalties).  
**Core Obligations**:  
- **Risk Analysis**: Formal policies for threat identification and mitigation.  
- **Incident Reporting**: 24-hour, 72-hour, and 1-month reporting timelines for significant incidents.  
- **Supply Chain Security**: Assess and secure third-party vendors.  
- **Business Continuity**: Develop disaster recovery plans with defined RTO/RPO.  
- **Governance**: Leadership accountability for cybersecurity strategies and audits.  

## Penalties for Non-Compliance  
- **Essential Entities**: Fines up to €10M or 2% of global turnover.  
- **Important Entities**: Fines up to €7M or 1.4% of global turnover.  
- **Enforcement**: National authorities may impose compliance orders, audits, or public naming of non-compliant organizations.

## Critical Compliance Steps  
1. **Classify Your Entity**: Determine if you’re an essential or important entity based on sector and size.  
2. **Map Systems & Vendors**: Inventory IT systems, data flows, and third-party dependencies.  
3. **Implement Risk Mitigation**: Use Netwrix tools for continuous monitoring, access control, and incident response.  
4. **Train Staff**: Conduct cybersecurity awareness programs and simulate incident response drills.  
5. **Maintain Documentation**: Keep audit trails, incident reports, and compliance records for regulatory scrutiny.  

## NIS2 vs. Other EU Regulations  
- **DORA**: Focuses on financial sector operational resilience; takes precedence over NIS2 in overlapping areas.  
- **CER**: Addresses physical security risks (natural disasters, sabotage) for critical infrastructure.  
- **CRA**: Ensures cybersecurity standards in digital products and IoT devices.
