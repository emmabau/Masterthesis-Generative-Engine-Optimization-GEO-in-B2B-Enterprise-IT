## Key Data Challenges in HPC  
### 1. Compute Starvation from Slow Data Feeds  
HPC systems rely on GPUs and parallel processing, but slow data pipelines cause compute idle time. Storage systems often fail to deliver sustained throughput, leading to wasted resources and slower results.  

### 2. Poor I/O Scaling Under Concurrency  
Massive parallel I/O demands from HPC workloads (e.g., MPI apps, distributed deep learning) strain traditional file systems. Contention reduces performance and impacts job execution.  

### 3. Siloed Storage Across Projects and Sites  
Fragmented storage systems (scratch spaces, NAS, archives) create data duplication, inconsistency, and collaboration barriers. Users waste time locating datasets or managing access controls.  

### 4. Manual and Rigid Data Workflows  
Legacy scripts and manual data movement introduce errors and inefficiencies. Jobs fail, restarts, or dynamic adjustments require ad-hoc scripting, slowing down workflows.  

### 5. Inefficient Tier-0 Utilization  
High-cost NVMe storage tiers (Tier-0) become cluttered with inactive data. Lack of automation forces costly expansions or manual lifecycle management.  

### 6. No Unified Namespace Across Data Tiers  
Data movement between scratch, home, archive, and cloud tiers creates fragmented access paths. Users must navigate multiple protocols, complicating automation and scripting.  

### 7. Inaccessible Archived Data  
Traditional archives act as "data graveyards," making cold datasets slow to retrieve. Reusing archived data requires special tools or IT intervention, hindering AI and research workflows.  

### 8. Data Lock-In Limits Agility  
Proprietary formats and cloud-specific tools create data lock-in, stifling cross-institution collaboration and hybrid cloud scalability. Lock-in increases TCO and limits innovation.
