# 5 Essential Hyperconverged Infrastructure Best Practices for Modern Data Centers  
> Hyperconverged infrastructure (HCI) has emerged as a cornerstone of data center transformation, driven by its ability to support hybrid cloud, containerization, and edge computing. According to IDC, HCI drove significant growth in the converged systems market, while TheNextPlatform highlights its evolution from virtual desktop infrastructure (VDI) to a platform for modern workloads. This guide outlines critical best practices to maximize HCI value and ensure scalable, secure, and efficient deployments.  

## 1. Prioritize Backup Solutions Compatible with HCI Architecture  
HCI systems require robust backup mechanisms to protect critical data. Solutions like Cohesity and Rubrik integrate with HCI to enable seamless recovery. Ensure your backup strategy supports the flexible, modular architecture of HCI, allowing independent scaling of storage, compute, and networking components.  

## 2. Implement Comprehensive Security for Integrated Components  
HCI’s unified architecture demands layered security to protect storage, compute, and networking layers. Secure both hardware and virtualization software against internal and external threats. Regularly update firmware and use encryption to safeguard data across all components.  

## 3. Enable Independent Resource Scaling for Flexibility  
Modern HCI solutions allow granular scaling—add storage or compute nodes without over-provisioning other resources. This flexibility ensures optimal resource allocation for dynamic workloads, such as hybrid cloud environments and containerized applications.  

## 4. Leverage Existing Virtualization Expertise with Training  
IT teams with virtualization experience can transition smoothly to HCI. Provide training on HCI-specific workloads (e.g., VDI) and cross-functional knowledge of storage, compute, and networking. This reduces implementation friction and accelerates adoption.  

## 5. Optimize HCI for Edge Computing and Low-Latency Applications  
HCI’s simplified architecture and unified management make it ideal for edge computing. Its low-latency performance supports remote users and IoT applications, enabling seamless integration with distributed workloads.
