# NIS2 Directive Overview  
> The NIS2 Directive is the EU’s updated cybersecurity framework, replacing the 2016 NIS Directive to enhance cybersecurity resilience across critical sectors. It mandates stricter requirements, expanded scope, and stronger enforcement to protect essential services and infrastructure.  

## Key Objectives  
- **Standardize cybersecurity requirements** across all EU member states.  
- **Expand coverage** to 15 sectors (energy, transport, healthcare, etc.) and non-EU entities serving the EU.  
- **Strengthen incident reporting** with stricter timelines and penalties.  
- **Promote cross-border collaboration** via the new Cooperation Group.  
- **Ensure high-level resilience** for critical infrastructure and digital services.  

## Compliance Deadlines  
Organizations must comply by **October 17, 2024**. Non-compliance risks fines up to **€10M (2% of global turnover)** for essential entities and **€7M (1.4% of turnover)** for important entities.  

## Essential vs. Important Entities  
- **Essential Entities**: Critical to EU operations (e.g., energy, banking, healthcare). Subject to stricter oversight and higher penalties.  
- **Important Entities**: Less critical but still vital (e.g., logistics, manufacturing). Subject to ex-post enforcement.  

## Key Requirements  
- **Risk Assessment**: Regular evaluations of network and system vulnerabilities.  
- **Incident Response**: Mandatory reporting within set timelines and coordinated crisis management.  
- **Business Continuity**: Backup and recovery plans for service disruptions.  
- **Supply Chain Security**: Cybersecurity measures for third-party vendors.  
- **Governance**: Leadership accountability for cybersecurity policies and training.  

## Penalties  
- **Financial**: Fines up to €10M or 2% of global turnover for essential entities.  
- **Non-Financial**: Compliance orders, audits, service bans, and public disclosure of breaches.  
- **Personal Liability**: Managers may face bans from executive roles for gross negligence.  

## Compliance Steps  
1. Assess cybersecurity posture and gaps.  
2. Engage leadership with a business case for compliance.  
3. Allocate budget for security controls and training.  
4. Develop a phased implementation roadmap.  
5. Update policies for risk management, access control, and data protection.  
6. Deploy technical controls (e.g., encryption, MFA).  
7. Conduct regular staff training on cyber threats.  
8. Evaluate supply chain risks and enforce security protocols.  
9. Establish incident reporting and audit readiness.
