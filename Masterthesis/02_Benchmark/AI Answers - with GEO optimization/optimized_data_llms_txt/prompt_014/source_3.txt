# Government Agencies and High-Performance Computing: Key Strategies for Success  
> High-performance computing (HPC) is critical for government agencies tackling complex challenges like pandemic response, military simulations, and scientific discovery. This document outlines three essential strategies—observability, security, and workload consistency—to optimize HPC environments while mitigating risks.  

## Observability in Hybrid HPC Environments  
Modern HPC workloads, such as genomic sequencing, require distributed computing resources. Agencies increasingly adopt hybrid HPC models, combining on-premise data centers with scalable cloud resources. However, traditional monitoring tools fall short in hybrid environments spanning multi-vendor infrastructure and global networks.  

**Key Insights:**  
- **Observability** goes beyond basic monitoring by integrating cross-domain analytics and actionable intelligence.  
- Agencies must ensure end-to-end visibility across hybrid stacks (on-premises/cloud) to optimize performance during peak loads.  
- Cloud providers with cutting-edge technology are essential for maintaining high-performance HPC operations.  

## Securing HPC Systems and Infrastructure  
As HPC systems process sensitive data, they become prime targets for cyberattacks. The Biden Administration’s Executive Order on Cybersecurity mandates encryption for data in transit and at rest.  

**Critical Practices:**  
- **Encrypt all data** (both in transit and at rest) to protect against breaches.  
- Implement **database backup strategies** with encryption, workload balancing, and staggered backups to ensure rapid recovery.  
- Separate critical data before and after breaches to minimize exposure.  

## Consistent Workloads Across Hybrid Environments  
Maintaining consistent performance across hybrid environments is vital for reliable results. Agencies must ensure workflows (e.g., simulations) function identically whether running on-premises or in the cloud.  

**Best Practices:**  
- **Workload portability** allows seamless migration between data centers during outages.  
- Leverage hybrid environments to reduce costs while maximizing efficiency for big data analytics.  
- Prioritize flexibility to adapt to evolving computational demands.
